**List of Walls**

The **list of walls** includes a variety of significant barriers built throughout history for political, military, economic, or symbolic reasons. These structures—ranging from ancient fortifications to modern border barriers—have been constructed by states and empires to define territory, deter invasions, control migration, and project authority. Some have become enduring cultural and political symbols, while others reflect contemporary geopolitical divisions and security concerns.

Walls have played a notable role in global history, often intersecting with issues of sovereignty, conflict, ideology, and diplomacy. Their construction and demolition frequently generate international attention, highlighting the tension between national security and the ideals of openness, cooperation, and human mobility.

---

### Historical and Political Context

The use of walls as physical demarcations of power dates back thousands of years. Ancient civilizations built walls to protect cities and mark the limits of empire. In the modern era, walls increasingly reflect political divisions, ideological conflicts, and state approaches to border control.

Walls can serve a variety of functions:

* **Defensive**: Fortifications built to repel military invasions.
* **Political**: Barriers that reflect or enforce political ideologies or disputes.
* **Border enforcement**: Structures aimed at controlling migration or smuggling.
* **Symbolic**: Walls used to convey strength, separation, or cultural identity.

As globalization and international migration have increased, the building of walls in the 21st century has prompted debate over national security, human rights, and the political message such structures send.

---

### Ancient and Historical Walls

#### **Great Wall of China**

* **Location:** Northern China
* **Construction period:** 7th century BCE – 17th century CE
* **Purpose:** Built by successive Chinese dynasties to deter invasions from northern nomadic tribes.
* **Political context:** Symbol of Chinese unity and strength; used historically to consolidate imperial control.

#### **Hadrian’s Wall**

* **Location:** Northern England
* **Construction period:** 122 CE
* **Purpose:** Built by the Roman Empire to mark the northern boundary and defend against tribal incursions.
* **Significance:** Represents Roman imperial reach and frontier policy.

#### **Walls of Constantinople**

* **Location:** Present-day Istanbul, Turkey
* **Construction period:** 4th–15th centuries
* **Purpose:** Defensive fortifications for the Byzantine capital.
* **Political relevance:** Withstood numerous sieges; critical in the defense of Christendom during the Middle Ages.

---

### Modern and Contemporary Walls

#### **Berlin Wall**

* **Location:** Berlin, Germany
* **Construction period:** 1961–1989
* **Purpose:** Erected by the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) to stop emigration to West Berlin.
* **Political context:** Cold War symbol of communist repression and division between East and West. Its fall in 1989 marked the decline of Soviet influence in Eastern Europe.

#### **Israeli West Bank Barrier**

* **Location:** West Bank and Israel
* **Construction began:** 2002
* **Purpose:** Israel claims it was built to prevent terrorist attacks; critics view it as de facto annexation and a restriction on Palestinian movement.
* **Controversy:** Subject to international debate and rulings, including a 2004 International Court of Justice advisory opinion declaring it contrary to international law.

#### **Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ)**

* **Location:** Between North and South Korea
* **Established:** 1953 (Armistice Agreement)
* **Nature:** Though not a wall in the traditional sense, the DMZ is one of the most heavily fortified borders in the world.
* **Political significance:** Symbol of the ongoing division and unresolved status of the Korean War.

#### **Mexico–United States Border Barrier**

* **Location:** Southern U.S. border
* **Construction began:** 1990s; expanded in the 2000s and 2010s
* **Purpose:** To prevent illegal immigration and drug trafficking.
* **Political context:** Became a major issue in U.S. domestic politics, especially during the 2016 and 2020 presidential campaigns.

#### **Ceuta and Melilla Fences**

* **Location:** Spanish enclaves in North Africa
* **Purpose:** Prevent unauthorized migration from Morocco into the European Union.
* **Political issues:** Highlight EU immigration challenges and African-European relations.

---

### Other Notable Walls

* **Green Line (Cyprus)** – A buffer zone dividing Greek Cypriots and Turkish Cypriots since 1974.
* **Moroccan Western Sahara Wall** – Separates Moroccan-controlled areas from Sahrawi rebel-held territory; subject of ongoing territorial dispute.
* **India–Pakistan Line of Control** – Includes fencing and barriers in the contested Kashmir region.
* **Hungary–Serbia Border Fence** – Built in 2015 during the European migrant crisis to curb refugee flows into Hungary.
* **Bangladesh–India Border Fence** – Constructed by India to curb cross-border smuggling and unauthorized migration.

---

### Cultural and Political Implications

Walls are often politically divisive and can carry strong symbolic meanings. To some, they represent protection and sovereignty; to others, exclusion, repression, and the erosion of human rights. The fall of the Berlin Wall is often invoked as a triumph of democratic ideals, while newer walls are frequently criticized for fostering xenophobia and undermining international norms.

International law does not explicitly prohibit the building of walls, but such structures must comply with humanitarian and human rights obligations. Their legality often depends on location, purpose, and impact on civilian populations.

---

### Conclusion

The **list of walls** underscores how physical barriers have historically been used to shape political geography, define power, and manage security challenges. From ancient empires to modern nation-states, walls have reflected the evolving nature of borders, conflict, and governance. As global interconnectedness increases, the persistence—and resurgence—of wall-building remains a contentious and highly symbolic political phenomenon.
