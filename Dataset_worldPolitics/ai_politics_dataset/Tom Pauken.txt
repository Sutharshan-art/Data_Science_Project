**Tom Pauken**

**Early Life and Education**
Thomas Michael Pauken (born September 16, 1949) is an American politician, public servant, and conservative activist notable for his leadership roles in Texas and national Republican politics. He was born in California but raised in Texas, earning a Bachelor of Arts in political science from California State University, Long Beach, in 1971 and a Master of Public Administration from the University of Texas at Austin in 1974.

**Early Career and Texas Republican Party Leadership**
Pauken’s political career began in the early 1970s as an organizer for the burgeoning Texas Republican Party. In 1976, at age 27, he was elected chairman of the Republican Party of Texas—a position he held until 1980. Under his leadership, the party made significant gains in urban and suburban areas, laying groundwork for the GOP’s eventual ascendancy in statewide offices. Pauken emphasized grassroots organizing, candidate recruitment, and issue‐based campaigns focused on tax reform and education.

**Reagan Administration Service (1981–1985)**
In 1981, President Ronald Reagan appointed Pauken as Administrator of the Regional Office for the Health Care Financing Administration (HCFA, now Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) for six Southwestern states. In this role, he oversaw federal health‐care financing programs during a period of contentious debate over budget cuts and regulatory restructuring. Pauken implemented reforms aimed at reducing fraud and improving program efficiency, earning praise from conservative policymakers for his cost‐containment efforts.

**Return to Texas and Workforce Development (1986–1991)**
After leaving HCFA in 1985, Pauken served briefly as deputy commissioner of the Texas Department of Human Services before being appointed Executive Director of the Texas Workforce Commission (TWC) in 1986 by Governor Bill Clements. At TWC, he led initiatives to modernize job training, expand employment services, and forge public–private partnerships. His tenure coincided with Texas’s economic diversification away from oil dependence, and he championed workforce programs that supported emerging high‐technology and service industries.

**Educational Governance and Think Tanks (1991–2003)**
In 1991, Pauken was appointed to the Texas A\&M University System Board of Regents by Governor Clements, serving two terms (1991–1995, reappointed 1996–2000). He advocated for increased accountability, expanded research capacity, and closer ties between the university and state economic development goals. Concurrently, he helped found the Texas Public Policy Foundation, a conservative think tank promoting limited government, free‐market policies, and educational reform.

**2006 Texas Gubernatorial Campaign**
In 2006, Pauken won the Republican nomination for governor of Texas, defeating former Texas Secretary of State Geoff Connor and businessman Jim Squier. His campaign emphasized property‐tax relief, tort‐reform enhancements, and support for public school choice. Pauken positioned himself as a “principled conservative,” frequently criticizing incumbent Democratic Governor Rick Perry’s pragmatic compromises as departures from core Republican values. In the general election, Perry’s incumbency and broad coalition—including moderate Republicans and traditional Democrats—secured his re‐election with 39.9% of the vote to Pauken’s 39.0%, making it one of the closest gubernatorial races in Texas history.

**2008 U.S. Senate Run and Later Activity**
Pauken briefly sought the Republican nomination for the U.S. Senate in 2008 but withdrew in favor of incumbent Senator Kay Bailey Hutchison. After the campaign, he returned to think‐tank work, including leadership roles at the Texas Public Policy Foundation and the Texas Conservative Coalition Research Institute, where he promoted policy research on health care, taxation, and regulatory reform.

**Political Philosophy and Impact**
Throughout his career, Pauken has been a prominent voice for traditional conservative principles: limited government, fiscal restraint, free enterprise, and strong national defense. He has argued against expansive welfare programs, advocated for market‐based education reforms, and supported robust support for veterans and military families. His leadership in the Texas GOP during the late 1970s helped transform Texas from a Democratic stronghold to a competitive two‐party state, setting the stage for decades of Republican dominance.

**Honors and Recognition**
Pauken has received awards from conservative organizations, including the Texas Conservative Coalition’s Conservative Leadership Award and the Texas Public Policy Foundation’s Leadership in State Policy Award. He remains active as a speaker and commentator on Texas and national politics, frequently publishing opinion pieces and participating in public policy forums.

**Personal Life**
Pauken resides in Austin, Texas, with his wife, Laura. They have two children. He continues to serve on nonprofit and policy‐advisory boards and is a member of the Council for National Policy, a conservative nonprofit forum.

**Legacy**
Tom Pauken’s career reflects the ascendance of conservative politics in Texas and his advocacy for principled governance. From party‐building in the 1970s to administrative reforms under Reagan and state leadership in workforce and education, his influence spans electoral politics, public administration, and policy research. His narrow loss in 2006 underscores both the strengths and electoral limits of doctrinal conservatism in a rapidly evolving political landscape.
