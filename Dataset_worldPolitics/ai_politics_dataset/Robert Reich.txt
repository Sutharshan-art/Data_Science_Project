**Robert Reich**

**Early Life and Education**
Robert Bruce Reich was born on June 24, 1946, in Scranton, Pennsylvania. The son of a factory worker and a social worker, he grew up in a working‐class household marked by his father’s struggle with Alzheimer’s disease and his family’s financial challenges. Reich graduated **magna cum laude** from Dartmouth College in 1968, where he studied political science and public policy. He earned a Rhodes Scholarship to Oxford University, receiving a Bachelor of Philosophy in PPE (Philosophy, Politics, and Economics) in 1970, and later obtained a Juris Doctor from Yale Law School in 1973.

**Early Career and Academic Work**
Reich began his career as a law clerk for Chief Judge Edward T. Gignoux of the U.S. District Court for the District of Maine. He then served as a staff member of the National Commission on the Public Service and as legislative assistant to Senator Edmund S. Muskie. From 1976 to 1980, Reich taught at the University of California, Berkeley’s School of Law (Boalt Hall) and concurrently served as Assistant to the Secretary of Labor in the Carter administration. He was a professor of public policy at Harvard University’s Kennedy School of Government from 1981 to 1994, specializing in labor economics, welfare policy, and the politics of bureaucracy.

**Clinton Administration (1993–1997)**
In January 1993, President Bill Clinton appointed Reich as the 22nd United States Secretary of Labor. Serving until 1997, Reich sought to modernize workforce policy in the face of globalization and technological change. Key initiatives included:

* **Welfare Reform Negotiations**: Reich participated in early discussions that eventually led to the Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act of 1996, balancing his advocacy for a social safety net with support for work‐based welfare.
* **Workforce Training**: He expanded the Job Training Partnership Act to emphasize skill development for displaced workers, championing apprenticeship and community‐college partnerships.
* **Minimum Wage Increase**: Reich successfully lobbied for the first federal minimum wage raise in seven years, from \$4.25 to \$5.15 per hour.
* **Labor‐Management Partnerships**: He promoted cooperative labor‐management initiatives to improve productivity and protect worker rights in the emerging global economy.

**Post‐Government Career**
After leaving the Cabinet, Reich taught at Brandeis University’s Heller School for Social Policy and Management and at the University of California, Berkeley. He founded the Institute for New Economic Thinking in 2009 and served as its inaugural president. Between 2006 and 2013, Reich was a professor of public policy at the University of California, Berkeley, where he directed the Blum Center for Developing Economies and advised California governors on budget and economic issues.

**Writings and Media**
A prolific author, Reich has written over a dozen books on economics, inequality, and democratic governance. Notable works include *The Work of Nations* (1991), which analyzed the impact of globalization on labor markets; *Saving Capitalism* (2015), co‐authored with Stephen B. Cohen, critiquing the erosion of democratic accountability in economic policy; and *The Common Good* (2018), exploring the moral imperatives of a just society. Reich is also known for his frequent commentary in *The New York Times*, *The Washington Post*, and as a contributor to MSNBC. His documentary *Inequality for All* (2013) examined widening economic disparities and earned critical acclaim for bringing complex policy debates to a broad audience.

**Political and Policy Perspectives**
Reich identifies as a progressive Democrat and has been a vocal critic of income and wealth inequality, deregulation, and unrestrained corporate power. He argues for:

* **Stronger Social Safety Nets**: Including universal healthcare and expanded unemployment benefits.
* **Progressive Taxation**: To ensure higher‐income households and corporations pay a fair share.
* **Labor Rights**: Revitalizing collective bargaining and strengthening unions to counterbalance corporate influence.
* **Investment in Education and Infrastructure**: As engines of broadly shared economic growth.

**Role in Public Debate**
Reich’s public engagement spans academic conferences, congressional testimony, and social media campaigns. During the 2016 and 2020 presidential cycles, he advised and endorsed progressive candidates, including Senator Elizabeth Warren and Senator Bernie Sanders, on economic policy. His TED talks and online courses on “Rewriting the Rules of the American Economy” have reached millions, shaping discourse on the role of government in the 21st‐century economy.

**Honors and Recognition**
Reich has received numerous honorary degrees and awards, including the Yip Harburg Award for social justice in 2014. He is a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and has served on corporate and nonprofit boards, such as Hewlett‐Packard and The Broad Foundations.

**Personal Life**
Reich is married to Clare Dalton, a professor of evidence and procedure at Harvard Law School. They have three children. He continues to reside in Berkeley, California, and remains active in policy research, teaching, and media commentary.

**Legacy**
Robert Reich’s career bridges academic scholarship, government service, and mass public engagement. His advocacy for a more equitable economy and his efforts to translate complex policy issues into accessible narratives have made him a prominent figure in contemporary American political and economic debates. His work continues to influence policymakers and the broader public on the challenges of inequality, globalization, and democratic accountability.
