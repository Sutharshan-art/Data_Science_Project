**Tom Nichols**

Thomas Michael “Tom” Nichols (born April 14, 1960) is an American academic, author, and commentator specializing in international affairs, civil–military relations, and the impact of expertise on democratic governance. He is a professor at the Naval War College and an adjunct in the School of Advanced International Studies (SAIS) at Johns Hopkins University. Nichols is best known for his critique of the erosion of public respect for expertise, articulated in his 2017 book *The Death of Expertise*.

**Early Life and Education**
Nichols was born in the Bronx, New York City, and raised in metropolitan Boston. He earned a Bachelor of Arts in political science from Colorado College in 1981 and a Ph.D. in political science from Georgetown University in 1994. His doctoral dissertation, under the supervision of Graham T. Allison, examined U.S.–Soviet arms control negotiations.

**Academic and Government Career**

* **U.S. Government Service (1981–1994):** Beginning in 1981, Nichols joined the U.S. Department of Defense, serving as a division chief in the Office of the Secretary of Defense’s policy planning staff. He contributed to nuclear arms control and strategic policy during the Reagan and George H. W. Bush administrations, including work on the Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty.
* **Naval War College (1994–present):** In 1994, Nichols joined the faculty of the U.S. Naval War College in Newport, Rhode Island. He teaches strategy, civil–military affairs, and Russian security affairs, and has won multiple teaching awards. He served on the Secretary of the Navy’s Advisory Panel on Naval Reserve and on the Defense Science Board’s Task Force on Nuclear Deterrence Skills.
* **Johns Hopkins SAIS (2009–present):** Nichols holds an adjunct professorship at SAIS, teaching courses on Russian foreign policy and the role of expertise in democratic societies. He was a Fulbright Scholar at St. Antony’s College, Oxford (2003–2004), lecturing on U.S. foreign policy.

**Major Publications and Ideas**

* **The Death of Expertise (2017):** Nichols argues that modern information technology, mass higher education, and populist politics have fostered a culture in which credentialed experts are distrusted and “opinion” is valued over informed analysis. He traces this trend to the 1960s campus revolts, the democratization of knowledge, and the rise of social media. The book sparked widespread debate about the role of expertise in public policy, science, and journalism.
* **The Russian Presidency (2003):** A study of Russian executive power under Boris Yeltsin and Vladimir Putin, examining institutional development and personalized authority in post‐Soviet Russia.
* **No Use: Nuclear Weapons and U.S. National Security (2013):** Co‐authored with Keith B. Payne, this work assesses the role of U.S. nuclear weapons in deterrence and global strategy, arguing for a re‐examination of declaratory policy and extended deterrence.
* **Countering Russian Information Warfare (2018):** Nichols has published on disinformation and hybrid warfare, emphasizing the need for Western democracies to bolster resilience against state‐sponsored influence campaigns.

**Commentary and Public Engagement**
Nichols is a frequent contributor to *The Atlantic*, *Foreign Affairs*, *The New York Times*, and *The Washington Post*. He appears regularly on television and radio—including CNN, NPR, and the BBC—as a foreign‐policy and national‐security analyst. Following the 2016 U.S. presidential election, he became a vocal critic of anti‐intellectualism, spreading his arguments through interviews, op‐eds, and public lectures.

**Political and Historical Context**
Nichols’s career spans the late Cold War, the post–Cold War unipolar moment, and the emergence of multipolar tensions with Russia and China. His insider perspective on arms control during the INF negotiations placed him at the heart of superpower détente. Later, his scholarship on Russian politics deepened as Putin consolidated power and reasserted Russia on the world stage. His concerns about expertise emerged amid rising populism—embodied in Brexit, Trumpism, and global anti‐establishment movements—and the information revolution’s democratizing yet destabilizing effects.

**Awards and Honors**
Nichols has received fellowships from the Guggenheim Foundation, the Rockefeller Foundation, and the Kennan Institute at the Wilson Center. He holds the Massachusetts Medal of Liberty for his contributions to public understanding of national security. In 2018, he was named one of *Politico*’s “Top 50 Thinkers” for influencing policy debates on expertise and democracy.

**Personal Life**
Nichols resides in Rhode Island with his wife, Anne; they have two children. He is a member of the Council on Foreign Relations and serves on the advisory board of the Journal of Strategic Studies.

**Legacy**
Tom Nichols has become a leading voice on the challenges that modern democracies face in distinguishing informed expertise from political opinion. His work on civil–military relations, arms control, and Russian foreign policy remains influential in academic and policy circles. *The Death of Expertise* has galvanized discussions about education, media, and public trust in institutions, contributing to debates on the resilience of democratic governance in the Information Age.
