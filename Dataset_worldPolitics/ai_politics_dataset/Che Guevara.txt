**Che Guevara**

**Ernesto "Che" Guevara** (June 14, 1928 – October 9, 1967) was an Argentine Marxist revolutionary, physician, author, guerrilla leader, and major figure in the Cuban Revolution. A symbol of anti-imperialism and radical leftist movements, Guevara played a key political and military role in shaping revolutionary strategy in Latin America and beyond during the Cold War. His legacy remains deeply polarizing, regarded by supporters as a champion of the oppressed and by critics as a proponent of authoritarianism and violent revolution.

---

### Early Life and Political Awakening

Ernesto Guevara was born in Rosario, Argentina, to a middle-class family. He studied medicine at the University of Buenos Aires but interrupted his studies to travel across Latin America. These travels, documented in his later memoir *The Motorcycle Diaries*, exposed him to widespread poverty, inequality, and U.S. influence in the region. These experiences radicalized Guevara and led to his belief that systemic change could only come through armed revolution.

His political worldview was shaped by Marxist theory and anti-imperialist thought, influenced by thinkers such as Karl Marx, Vladimir Lenin, and Mao Zedong. Guevara viewed capitalist exploitation and U.S. imperialism as root causes of Latin America's social and economic underdevelopment.

---

### Role in the Cuban Revolution

In 1955, Guevara met Fidel Castro in Mexico and joined the 26th of July Movement, a revolutionary group committed to overthrowing the U.S.-backed Cuban dictator Fulgencio Batista. As a key member of the guerrilla force that landed in Cuba in 1956, Guevara rose to prominence as a skilled military leader and strategist.

During the Cuban Revolution (1956–1959), he commanded rebel forces in several decisive battles, including the Battle of Santa Clara, which contributed to Batista’s eventual flight and the movement’s victory in January 1959. Guevara's leadership and ideological commitment earned him the position of a trusted confidant within Castro’s inner circle.

---

### Government and International Roles

After the revolution, Guevara held several high-ranking positions in the new Cuban government. He served as President of the National Bank, Minister of Industries, and key architect of Cuba's planned economy. He played a central role in shifting Cuba toward a Soviet-style model, promoting agrarian reform, collectivization, and rapid industrialization.

Guevara also became a leading figure in Cuba’s foreign policy, advocating for global anti-imperialist struggle. He represented Cuba at the United Nations and other international forums, where he criticized U.S. foreign policy, supported decolonization movements, and promoted armed struggle as a means of liberation in the developing world.

One of his most famous public speeches, delivered at the UN General Assembly in 1964, denounced U.S. intervention in Latin America and Africa, asserting Cuba's alignment with global revolutionary movements.

---

### Revolutionary Campaigns Abroad

In the mid-1960s, Guevara left Cuba to foment revolutionary movements abroad. He first attempted to support rebels in the Congo (present-day Democratic Republic of the Congo), but the campaign failed due to poor coordination and internal divisions among local fighters.

Undeterred, Guevara later launched a guerrilla insurgency in Bolivia in 1966, aiming to ignite a continent-wide revolution. Operating under a false identity, he sought to organize peasants and local revolutionaries into a fighting force against the Bolivian government, which was supported by the United States.

However, the Bolivian campaign failed to gain popular support and was met with effective counterinsurgency efforts. On October 8, 1967, Guevara was captured by Bolivian forces with assistance from the CIA. He was executed the following day in the village of La Higuera, becoming a martyr figure to leftist movements around the world.

---

### Political Thought

Che Guevara's political philosophy emphasized the moral imperative of revolution, internationalism, and the creation of a "new man" committed to collective good over individualism. His writings, including *Guerrilla Warfare* (1961) and *Socialism and Man in Cuba* (1965), promoted guerrilla struggle as a strategy for the oppressed and emphasized the role of armed struggle in achieving social justice.

Guevara believed that revolutionary violence was necessary to overthrow entrenched power structures and that the conditions for revolution could be created through proactive insurgency—a theory known as the "foco theory." This approach was influential among leftist movements across Latin America, Africa, and Asia.

---

### Legacy and Controversy

Che Guevara remains a highly controversial figure. To supporters, he symbolizes resistance to imperialism, capitalist oppression, and injustice. His image has become iconic worldwide, particularly through the famous Alberto Korda photograph, used as a symbol in political movements, protests, and popular culture.

Critics, however, highlight his authoritarian tendencies, support for one-party rule, and involvement in political executions and suppression of dissent in post-revolutionary Cuba. Human rights groups and historians have debated the extent of his involvement in repressive policies and whether his vision of socialism justified the use of violence.

In academic and political discourse, Guevara’s life continues to provoke debate over the ethics of revolutionary struggle, the role of violence in political change, and the tensions between ideals and outcomes in socialist governance.

---

### Conclusion

Che Guevara was a central figure in 20th-century revolutionary politics, embodying the aspirations and contradictions of leftist insurgency in the Cold War era. His life and legacy reflect the broader struggles over ideology, power, and justice that defined a turbulent period in global history. Whether viewed as a hero or a radical, Guevara's impact on political thought and revolutionary movements remains indelible.
