The **Cold War** (c. 1947–1991) was characterized by geopolitical rivalry between the United States and its allies (the Western Bloc) and the Soviet Union and its satellites (the Eastern Bloc). Though the superpowers never fought directly, they engaged in numerous **proxy conflicts**, civil wars, and limited interventions worldwide, each shaped by the competing ideologies of capitalism and communism.

---

## Early Cold War Crises (1945–1953)

**Greek Civil War (1946–1949)**

* **Belligerents:** Greek government (backed by Britain and the U.S.) vs. Democratic Army of Greece (communist-led).
* **Context:** In the power vacuum after World War II, communist partisans sought control of Greece. The Truman Doctrine (1947) framed U.S. aid as essential to “contain” Soviet influence, marking the start of American interventionism.

**Berlin Blockade and Airlift (1948–1949)**

* **Belligerents:** Western Allies vs. Soviet Union
* **Context:** Stalin’s blockade of West Berlin aimed to force Allied withdrawal. The Western airlift to supply the city demonstrated U.S. commitment to resisting Soviet pressure without direct military confrontation.

## Major Asian Conflicts

**Korean War (1950–1953)**

* **Belligerents:** South Korea and UN forces (primarily U.S.) vs. North Korea and Chinese “volunteers” (with Soviet support).
* **Context:** North Korea’s invasion of the South triggered UN-sanctioned intervention. The war solidified the U.S. policy of “containment” in Asia and led to the permanent division of Korea at the 38th parallel.

**First Indochina War (1946–1954)**

* **Belligerents:** French colonial forces vs. Viet Minh (communist-led).
* **Context:** While French initially fought nationalist forces, the U.S. began funding France after 1950 to resist communist expansion. The 1954 Geneva Accords partitioned Vietnam, setting the stage for later conflict.

**Vietnam War (1955–1975)**

* **Belligerents:** South Vietnam (U.S. and allies) vs. North Vietnam (Soviet and Chinese support) and Viet Cong insurgents.
* **Context:** U.S. escalated military involvement under the domino theory. Widespread anti-war protest at home and military stalemate abroad led to U.S. withdrawal in 1973 and eventual communist victory in 1975.

## Middle East and Mediterranean

**Suez Crisis (1956)**

* **Belligerents:** Egypt vs. Israel, Britain, France
* **Context:** Egypt’s nationalization of the Suez Canal prompted a tripartite invasion. The U.S. and USSR jointly pressured Britain and France to withdraw, demonstrating superpower influence beyond Europe.

**Arab–Israeli Wars (1948, 1956, 1967, 1973)**

* **Belligerents:** Israel vs. Arab states (Egypt, Syria, Jordan, etc.)
* **Context:** Both superpowers vied for influence: the USSR backed Egypt and Syria, while the U.S. increasingly supported Israel, making the Middle East a Cold War theater.

**Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990)**

* **Belligerents:** Multiple sectarian militias, Syria, Israel, PLO
* **Context:** Soviet-supplied leftist and Palestinian factions clashed with U.S.- and Saudi-backed rightist Maronite forces. Syria’s intervention (1976) and later U.S. and Israeli involvement underscored Cold War fragmentation.

## Africa and Latin America

**Congo Crisis (1960–1965)**

* **Belligerents:** Congolese factions (Lumumba’s supporters backed by USSR vs. Tshombe’s secessionists backed by U.S. and Belgium)
* **Context:** After independence, internal power struggles attracted U.S.–Soviet rivalry. The assassination of Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba (1961) epitomized Cold War interventions in decolonization.

**Angolan Civil War (1975–2002)**

* **Belligerents:** MPLA (Soviet/Cuban-backed) vs. UNITA (U.S. and South African-supported) vs. FNLA
* **Context:** Angola’s post-independence power vacuum drew massive foreign intervention: Cuban troops and Soviet arms versus U.S. covert support and South African incursions.

**Mozambican Civil War (1977–1992)**

* **Belligerents:** FRELIMO government (Marxist, Soviet-aligned) vs. RENAMO rebels (backed by Rhodesia and apartheid South Africa)
* **Context:** The conflict reflected regional Cold War dynamics, with anti-communist insurgents receiving cross-border support to destabilize Marxist regimes.

**Nicaraguan Revolution and Contra War (1978–1990)**

* **Belligerents:** Sandinista government (Soviet/Cuban-backed) vs. U.S.-supported CONRA rebels
* **Context:** After overthrowing the Somoza dictatorship, the Sandinistas implemented socialist reforms. The Reagan administration funded Contras to “roll back” communism, leading to the Iran–Contra scandal.

**Guatemalan Civil War (1960–1996)**

* **Belligerents:** Military government (U.S.-backed) vs. leftist insurgents
* **Context:** U.S. intervention in 1954 coup preceded decades of anti-communist campaigns. The conflict featured U.S.-trained counterinsurgency forces and was marked by widespread human rights abuses.

## South Asia and Central Asia

**Soviet–Afghan War (1979–1989)**

* **Belligerents:** Soviet Union and Afghan government vs. Mujahideen (U.S., Pakistani, Saudi-supported)
* **Context:** Following a communist coup, the USSR intervened to prop up Kabul. The U.S. CIA’s Operation Cyclone funneled arms to insurgents, making Afghanistan a pivotal Cold War proxy battleground.

---

## European and Intra-Bloc Conflicts

**Hungarian Revolution (1956)**

* **Belligerents:** Hungarian reformists vs. Soviet forces
* **Context:** Budapest’s uprising against the Stalinist regime was crushed by a massive Soviet intervention, reaffirming the Brezhnev Doctrine that Eastern Bloc states must remain within Soviet orbit.

**Prague Spring (1968)**

* **Belligerents:** Czechoslovak reformers vs. Warsaw Pact invasion (led by USSR)
* **Context:** Alexander Dubček’s “socialism with a human face” reforms prompted a coordinated invasion by Warsaw Pact states, highlighting limits of liberalization within the communist bloc.

---

## Political and Historical Impact

These conflicts illustrate how the Cold War shaped global geopolitics:

* **Containment vs. Expansion:** U.S. doctrine of containing communism led to interventions in Asia, Africa, and Latin America.
* **Alliance Dynamics:** NATO and Warsaw Pact structures defined military aid flows and intervention authorizations.
* **Decolonization and Ideology:** Newly independent states often became proxy battlegrounds, with superpower patronage influencing internal politics and postcolonial trajectories.

The end of the Cold War in 1991, marked by the Soviet Union’s dissolution, led to the cessation of most superpower proxy wars, though regional legacies—unresolved grievances, armed factions, and weapon stockpiles—continue to affect contemporary conflicts.
