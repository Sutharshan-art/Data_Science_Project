**2006 United States House of Representatives Elections in Florida**

The **2006 United States House of Representatives elections in Florida** were held on **November 7, 2006**, as part of the national midterm elections. All **25 congressional districts** in Florida were contested, with voters selecting representatives to serve two-year terms in the **110th United States Congress**. The election took place against a backdrop of growing dissatisfaction with the **Bush administration**, the ongoing **Iraq War**, and concerns over government ethics and corruption—factors that contributed to a broader national shift in favor of the **Democratic Party**.

In Florida, the election results reflected the national trend, though the overall delegation remained narrowly in Republican control. The **Republican Party** won **16 seats**, a loss of one from the previous cycle, while the **Democratic Party** secured **9 seats**, gaining one. The outcome included closely watched races and several competitive open-seat contests, most notably in **District 13**, which became embroiled in controversy over voting machine irregularities and would remain unsettled for months after the election.

---

### Political Context

The 2006 midterms were widely seen as a referendum on **President George W. Bush** and the Republican-controlled **Congress**. Public opinion had turned increasingly negative due to the protracted war in Iraq, the handling of **Hurricane Katrina**, and a series of high-profile political scandals, including the resignation of Rep. **Mark Foley** (R-FL), which directly impacted the races in Florida.

Nationally, Democrats aimed to regain control of the House of Representatives, which they had lost in the 1994 "Republican Revolution." In Florida, while Republicans had historically dominated congressional politics, demographic changes, suburban discontent, and shifting attitudes on foreign and domestic policy created opportunities for Democratic gains, particularly in competitive and open-seat districts.

---

### Statewide Overview

Prior to the election, Florida’s congressional delegation consisted of **17 Republicans** and **8 Democrats**. The GOP had benefited from redistricting following the 2000 census and had maintained a strong presence in both urban and rural areas. However, several incumbent retirements and ethical controversies, including the **Foley scandal**, created vulnerabilities.

Following the 2006 elections, the delegation balance shifted to **16 Republicans** and **9 Democrats**. While not a dramatic change, it marked the first time since 2000 that Democrats had gained a seat in Florida’s delegation.

---

### Notable Races

#### District 13 – Sarasota County and Manatee County

One of the most contested and controversial races of the cycle occurred in **Florida’s 13th district**, where incumbent Republican **Katherine Harris** vacated the seat to run unsuccessfully for U.S. Senate. The race featured Republican **Vern Buchanan**, a businessman, and Democrat **Christine Jennings**, a former bank executive.

Buchanan was initially declared the winner by a narrow margin of 369 votes. However, the election was marred by technical issues with **electronic voting machines**, particularly in Sarasota County, where over **18,000 undervotes** were recorded in the congressional race—an unusually high number. Jennings contested the results, leading to legal challenges and a House investigation. The seat was ultimately awarded to Buchanan, but the controversy raised broader concerns about election technology and voter disenfranchisement.

#### District 16 – Mark Foley Scandal

Another closely watched race was in **District 16**, where Republican incumbent **Mark Foley** resigned in September 2006 amid revelations that he had sent sexually explicit messages to congressional pages. Although Foley’s name remained on the ballot due to the timing of his resignation, votes cast for him were legally counted for his replacement, state Rep. **Joe Negron**.

Democrat **Tim Mahoney** won the race with 50% of the vote, flipping a previously safe Republican seat. The scandal and its fallout were emblematic of the broader concerns over congressional ethics that helped fuel Democratic momentum nationwide.

#### District 22 – E. Clay Shaw Jr. Defeated

In South Florida’s **22nd district**, long-serving Republican **E. Clay Shaw Jr.** was defeated by Democrat **Ron Klein**, a state senator. Klein’s victory was part of the Democratic surge in moderate-to-conservative districts, particularly in urban and suburban areas where opposition to the Iraq War and economic concerns resonated with swing voters.

---

### Voter Turnout and Electoral Trends

Voter turnout in Florida for the 2006 midterms was approximately **44%**, a typical figure for non-presidential elections but higher than in previous cycles, reflecting heightened political engagement. Suburban and independent voters played a decisive role in many swing districts, while African American and Hispanic voters provided strong support for Democratic candidates in key areas such as South Florida and Central Florida.

The results reflected both national dissatisfaction with Republican leadership and the growing competitiveness of Florida’s political landscape. Though the GOP maintained an edge, the elections underscored vulnerabilities in their coalition and set the stage for increased Democratic ambitions heading into the **2008 presidential election**.

---

### Aftermath and Legacy

The 2006 House elections in Florida were part of a broader national realignment that saw Democrats retake control of both chambers of Congress for the first time in over a decade. In Florida, while Republicans retained a majority of the state’s delegation, the Democratic gains and high-profile contests signaled a more competitive political environment.

Issues such as voting technology, ethics reform, and Iraq policy remained prominent in the post-election discourse. The controversy surrounding District 13 highlighted systemic weaknesses in Florida’s election infrastructure, prompting calls for reform and contributing to a national dialogue about electoral integrity.

Overall, the 2006 elections demonstrated the fluidity of Florida’s congressional politics and foreshadowed the state’s pivotal role in future national contests.
