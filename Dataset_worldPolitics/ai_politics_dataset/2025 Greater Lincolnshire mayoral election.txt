
**2025 Greater Lincolnshire Mayoral Election**

The **2025 Greater Lincolnshire mayoral election** was held on **1 May 2025** to elect the inaugural **Mayor of the Greater Lincolnshire Combined County Authority (GLCCA)**. The election formed part of the implementation of a devolution agreement between the UK central government and local authorities in Lincolnshire, North Lincolnshire, and North East Lincolnshire. The new mayoral role provides leadership and oversight on strategic issues including transport, economic development, skills training, housing, and infrastructure.

---

### Background and Political Context

The election followed a devolution agreement announced in late 2023 and formally approved in 2024. This agreement established the Greater Lincolnshire Combined County Authority, bringing together the administrative county of Lincolnshire and the two unitary authorities of North Lincolnshire and North East Lincolnshire under a single regional framework. The authority was given powers to manage multi-year funding allocations and develop localized strategies for economic growth and public services.

The mayoral role is part of the UK government’s wider strategy to deliver “Levelling Up” through regional devolution. Similar combined authorities and metro mayors had already been established in other parts of England, such as Greater Manchester, West Yorkshire, and the West Midlands. The 2025 election marked a milestone for Lincolnshire, traditionally a rural and conservative region, in aligning with the nationwide model of devolved leadership.

---

### Electoral System and Voter Turnout

The election was conducted using the **first-past-the-post** voting system, under which the candidate with the most votes wins outright. Unlike other mayoral contests that previously used supplementary vote systems, the use of first-past-the-post was standardised across England in recent electoral reforms.

Approximately 829,000 people were eligible to vote in the combined area. Voter turnout was estimated at **29.9%**, consistent with other local elections in England held on the same day.

---

### Candidates

Six candidates stood for the position:

* **Andrea Jenkyns** (Reform UK): A former Member of Parliament who had previously served in ministerial roles before defecting from the Conservative Party.
* **Rob Waltham** (Conservative Party): Leader of North Lincolnshire Council and a long-standing figure in local government.
* **Jason Stockwood** (Labour and Co-operative Party): A business leader and social entrepreneur known for his work in tech and community investment.
* **Marianne Overton** (Lincolnshire Independents): A regional political figure with a focus on rural affairs and local governance.
* **Sally Horscroft** (Green Party): An environmental campaigner and former parliamentary candidate.
* **Trevor Young** (Liberal Democrats): Council leader and advocate for community-led planning and services.

The field represented a broad political spectrum, including mainstream parties, smaller regional movements, and independents.

---

### Results

* **Andrea Jenkyns (Reform UK)** – 104,133 votes (42.0%)
* **Rob Waltham (Conservative)** – 64,585 votes (26.1%)
* **Jason Stockwood (Labour and Co-operative)** – 30,384 votes (12.3%)
* **Marianne Overton (Lincolnshire Independents)** – 19,911 votes (8.0%)
* **Sally Horscroft (Green)** – 15,040 votes (6.1%)
* **Trevor Young (Liberal Democrat)** – 13,728 votes (5.5%)

Andrea Jenkyns was elected as the first Mayor of Greater Lincolnshire, securing a clear margin over her closest rival.

---

### Campaign Themes

The mayoral campaigns focused on several regional priorities:

* **Economic regeneration**: Candidates proposed investment in infrastructure, digital connectivity, and small business support.
* **Transport integration**: The need for better public transport links between urban and rural areas was a key issue.
* **Skills and education**: Plans for new vocational training hubs and partnerships with local employers were presented.
* **Environmental planning**: Green and independent candidates emphasized sustainable development, flood resilience, and green energy.
* **Political identity**: The contest highlighted broader dissatisfaction with traditional party politics, with Reform UK presenting itself as an alternative voice.

Jenkyns campaigned on a platform of direct accountability, reducing bureaucracy, and advocating for greater autonomy from Westminster.

---

### Political and Strategic Significance

Jenkyns’ victory represented a breakthrough for Reform UK in a major regional election and a shift in Lincolnshire’s traditionally Conservative political landscape. It was viewed as a significant test case for public reception to populist and anti-establishment platforms in local governance. The result also reflected voter disaffection with existing party structures, particularly on issues like immigration, rural services, and infrastructure neglect.

Despite low turnout, the election was viewed as a key development in England’s ongoing devolution journey. It expanded the geographic reach of directly elected regional governance beyond urban centers and into a predominantly rural county.

---

### Role and Responsibilities of the Mayor

The mayor will chair the Greater Lincolnshire Combined County Authority and coordinate with local leaders across the three constituent councils. Key responsibilities include:

* Overseeing a multi-million-pound investment fund
* Developing a long-term transport strategy
* Managing adult education budgets and skills programs
* Promoting housing and regeneration projects
* Representing the region in discussions with central government and other combined authorities

The mayor will serve a four-year term, with the next election expected in **May 2029**.

---

### Outlook and Challenges

As the first individual to hold this office, the mayor faces the immediate challenge of establishing institutional structures, building inter-council cooperation, and meeting campaign expectations. There is particular pressure to demonstrate the benefits of devolution in tangible improvements to local services and economic opportunity.

The success of the Greater Lincolnshire mayoral model may influence future devolution deals across rural England. It will also serve as a test of Reform UK’s ability to transition from protest movement to administrative leadership.

The 2025 Greater Lincolnshire mayoral election thus marked a significant moment in the evolution of local democracy and regional autonomy in the United Kingdom.
