**Velika Kladuša**

**Overview**
Velika Kladuša is a town and municipality located in the northwestern part of Bosnia and Herzegovina, near the border with Croatia. Situated in the Una-Sana Canton of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina, it has historically held strategic significance due to its geographic position. Known for its complex political history and role in regional conflicts, Velika Kladuša has been a focal point of administrative, military, and ideological developments in the Balkans, particularly during the dissolution of Yugoslavia in the 1990s.

**Historical Background**
The area of Velika Kladuša has been inhabited since antiquity, with archaeological evidence pointing to Illyrian and Roman settlements. During the Middle Ages, it became part of the medieval Kingdom of Bosnia and later fell under Ottoman rule in the 16th century. Under the Ottomans, Velika Kladuša developed into a fortified town and administrative center. The name "Kladuša" is believed to derive from the Slavic word *klada*, meaning "log" or "block," reflecting its early defensive architecture.

Following the Austro-Hungarian occupation of Bosnia and Herzegovina in 1878, Velika Kladuša became integrated into the administrative and legal structures of the Habsburg Empire, facilitating modern infrastructural and institutional development. After World War I, the region was incorporated into the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats, and Slovenes (later Yugoslavia), where it remained a relatively rural and agrarian community.

**World War II and Socialist Yugoslavia**
During World War II, Velika Kladuša was the site of partisan resistance as well as conflict between fascist and communist-aligned forces. After the establishment of socialist Yugoslavia under Josip Broz Tito, the town was integrated into the Socialist Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina within the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia.

In the post-war period, Velika Kladuša gained a reputation for agricultural development, most notably through the Agrokomerc food-processing company, founded in 1969. Agrokomerc, under the leadership of Fikret Abdić, became one of the largest food production enterprises in the Balkans, employing thousands and contributing significantly to the local economy. The success of the company gave Velika Kladuša a unique political and economic standing within the Yugoslav federation.

**War in Bosnia and Political Division (1992–1995)**
With the outbreak of the Bosnian War in 1992, Velika Kladuša became a focal point of internal political divisions. Fikret Abdić, a prominent businessman and politician, broke with the central Bosnian government in Sarajevo and declared the formation of the Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia in 1993, with Velika Kladuša as its de facto capital.

This self-declared autonomous province was not recognized by the international community or the central Bosnian authorities. Abdić’s forces aligned with Serb and Croat factions at various times during the war, leading to direct conflict with the Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina. In 1995, the Fifth Corps of the Bosnian Army reasserted control over the area, ending the existence of the autonomous province and exiling Abdić.

**Post-War Period and Return of Fikret Abdić**
Following the Dayton Peace Accords and the end of hostilities in late 1995, Velika Kladuša was reintegrated into the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina. However, the legacy of wartime divisions persisted. Fikret Abdić was convicted of war crimes in Croatia in 2002 and sentenced to 15 years in prison, later reduced to 13. After serving two-thirds of his sentence, he was released in 2012 and returned to politics.

In a surprising political comeback, Abdić was elected mayor of Velika Kladuša in 2016, demonstrating his enduring support base among segments of the local population. His re-election reflected broader challenges in Bosnian post-war governance, where ethnic and political divisions continue to dominate local and national politics.

**Contemporary Political and Social Context**
Today, Velika Kladuša is part of the Una-Sana Canton within the Federation entity of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Its local politics remain shaped by the legacy of the 1990s conflict and the influence of figures like Abdić. The municipality has also been affected by broader regional issues, including economic underdevelopment, emigration, and, more recently, the European migrant crisis. Since 2018, Velika Kladuša has become a crossing point for migrants attempting to enter the European Union via Croatia, leading to tensions over humanitarian resources and security.

**Governance and Administration**
Velika Kladuša has a municipal government led by a mayor and a local council. Political power has shifted among parties and independent actors, with periodic tensions between municipal and cantonal authorities. Governance challenges include public service delivery, economic revitalization, and the management of migration-related pressures.

**Conclusion**
Velika Kladuša’s history encapsulates the broader political evolution of Bosnia and Herzegovina—from imperial frontiers and socialist development to nationalist conflict and contested post-war recovery. Its local dynamics reflect national struggles over identity, governance, and reconciliation in a complex multiethnic society. As Bosnia continues its path toward European integration, towns like Velika Kladuša remain key to understanding the enduring legacies of the Yugoslav wars and the challenges of building democratic institutions in post-conflict environments.
