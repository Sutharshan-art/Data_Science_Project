**Operation Tekbir ’95**

**Operation Tekbir ’95** was a military offensive launched by the Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina (ARBiH) between 15 and 28 June 1995, aimed at breaking the Bosnian Serb (VRS) encirclement of Sarajevo during the final months of the Bosnian War. Despite early territorial gains, the operation failed to achieve its primary objective of relieving the besieged city.

## Historical and Political Context

Following Bosnia and Herzegovina's declaration of independence from Yugoslavia in April 1992, Bosnian Serb forces, supported by the Republika Srpska political leadership, initiated the siege of Sarajevo. The siege became one of the longest and most devastating in modern European history, characterized by widespread civilian suffering and international condemnation. Multiple attempts by the ARBiH to lift the siege had previously failed due to entrenched Bosnian Serb positions and superior firepower.

By mid-1995, shifting dynamics on various fronts—including significant Croatian military actions and escalating NATO involvement—provided a strategic context for renewed attempts to break the siege militarily.

## Objectives of the Operation

* **Primary Objective**: To breach the Bosnian Serb siege lines around Sarajevo, restoring supply routes and allowing humanitarian aid into the city.
* **Secondary Objectives**: Capturing strategic high ground near Ilijaš and Semizovac to secure routes for military reinforcement and resupply.

## Forces and Leadership

* **ARBiH Forces**: Primarily units of the ARBiH 1st Corps, based in Sarajevo, supported by elements of the 3rd, 4th, and 7th Corps. ARBiH forces numbered approximately 46,000 troops, equipped with limited armor and artillery.
* **VRS Forces**: Primarily the Sarajevo-Romanija Corps, supplemented by additional reinforcements from the Herzegovina Corps, totaling around 17,500 soldiers with substantial heavy weaponry, including artillery and armored vehicles.
* **Leadership**: ARBiH forces were under the command of General Rasim Delić, while Bosnian Serb forces were commanded by General Ratko Mladić.

## The Offensive

The ARBiH launched Operation Tekbir ’95 on 15 June 1995, initially achieving tactical successes by capturing key positions such as Debelo Brdo, Čemerska, and Route Viper, threatening the Bosnian Serb defensive perimeter around Sarajevo. These early victories briefly raised hopes that the siege could finally be broken.

However, the VRS quickly mounted a powerful counteroffensive, effectively utilizing superior firepower, artillery, and armor. Bosnian Serb forces recaptured lost positions and inflicted significant casualties on advancing ARBiH units. By late June, heavy fighting resulted in approximately 1,000 ARBiH casualties, with around 3,000 wounded. Bosnian Serb forces suffered fewer losses, with approximately 200 killed and 600 wounded.

The ARBiH offensive stalled amid these losses, logistical challenges, and entrenched resistance from VRS forces, resulting in a tactical setback. By 28 June 1995, the ARBiH halted the operation without achieving its strategic goal of relieving Sarajevo.

## Consequences and Strategic Impact

* **Humanitarian Crisis**: The failure to break the siege prolonged civilian suffering in Sarajevo, exacerbating shortages of food, medicine, and basic necessities.
* **Political Implications**: Operation Tekbir ’95 highlighted the limitations faced by the ARBiH in conducting large-scale offensives without external support, leading to renewed international calls for more aggressive NATO intervention.
* **Military Lessons**: The operation underscored the critical importance of coordinated use of heavy weaponry, air power, and combined arms in modern warfare. The Bosnian military subsequently sought closer cooperation with Croatian forces and NATO, paving the way for later military successes.

## Broader Historical Context

Operation Tekbir ’95 was part of a broader military and diplomatic shift that defined the final stage of the Bosnian War. The failure of unilateral Bosnian offensives underscored the necessity for international intervention. NATO's Operation Deliberate Force commenced shortly after, significantly weakening the military capabilities of Bosnian Serb forces and influencing their decision to engage seriously in peace negotiations.

These events culminated in the signing of the Dayton Peace Accords in November 1995, effectively ending the Bosnian War and laying the groundwork for peace and reconstruction in Bosnia and Herzegovina.

## Legacy

While Operation Tekbir ’95 did not succeed militarily, its significance lies in the broader context of the conflict, demonstrating both the resilience of the ARBiH and the limits of conventional military action without adequate logistical and international support. It remains an important case study in military strategy, humanitarian intervention, and the political dynamics of the late 20th-century Balkan conflicts.
