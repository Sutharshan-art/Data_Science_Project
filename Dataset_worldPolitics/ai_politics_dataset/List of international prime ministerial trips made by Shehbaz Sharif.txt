Shehbaz Sharif became Pakistan’s 23rd Prime Minister on 11 April 2022, inheriting a challenging economic and security environment. From that date through mid-2024, he undertook a series of official visits abroad aimed at securing financial assistance, expanding trade ties, and strengthening diplomatic partnerships. Below is a summary of his principal international prime-ministerial trips, listed chronologically with their political context.

---

## 1. Saudi Arabia (25–27 April 2022)

**Context:** Sharif’s first foreign visit underscored Pakistan’s historic reliance on Gulf support. He met King Salman and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman in Riyadh, seeking Saudi investment and relief on deferred oil payments amid acute foreign-exchange shortages. Talks yielded a package of deferred oil payments and pledges for sovereign–wealth fund investments in energy and infrastructure.

## 2. United Arab Emirates (28–29 April 2022)

**Context:** Continuing Gulf outreach, Sharif attended bilateral talks with President Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed in Abu Dhabi. The UAE agreed to defer \$2.5 billion of annual oil payments and explore equity stakes in Pakistan’s sovereign wealth fund. The visit reaffirmed Pakistan’s strategic energy and labor-remittance ties with the Emirates.

## 3. China (6–8 May 2022)

**Context:** Pakistan’s “all-weather” ally received Sharif in Beijing for a package of concessional loans and new investment pledges under the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC). Meetings with President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Keqiang focused on financial support to stabilize Pakistan’s balance of payments and propel stalled CPEC energy and infrastructure projects.

## 4. Saudi Arabia (Jeddah Economic Forum, 14–16 June 2022)

**Context:** Invited to speak at the Future Investment Initiative, Sharif used the forum to court private investors and global financial institutions. He launched a “Pakistan Investment Roadshow” highlighting tax incentives, infrastructure concessions, and public–private partnership opportunities, part of his broader IMF-backed economic reform agenda.

## 5. United States (20–23 September 2022)

**Context:** Sharif attended the UN General Assembly in New York, delivering Pakistan’s national statement and holding bilateral meetings with U.S. lawmakers and business leaders. His discussions with Treasury and State Department officials secured assurances of continuing U.S. economic support—critical for Pakistan’s IMF program—and addressed counter-terrorism cooperation and regional stability vis-à-vis Afghanistan.

## 6. Switzerland (World Economic Forum, Davos; 16–20 January 2023)

**Context:** At Davos, Sharif joined global leaders to promote Pakistan as an emerging investment destination. In panel discussions on climate resilience and South Asia’s economic integration, he highlighted Pakistan’s reforms in energy, digitalization, and governance, seeking to attract green-technology and fintech investment.

## 7. Turkey (11–12 March 2023)

**Context:** Visiting Ankara at President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s invitation, Sharif signed agreements on defense cooperation, renewable energy, and tourism. The two sides agreed to upgrade their “Strategic Cooperation Council” framework, reflecting Pakistan’s pivot to diversify security partnerships beyond traditional Western alliances.

## 8. Qatar (7–8 May 2023)

**Context:** Sharif’s trip to Doha secured commitments for additional LNG cargoes at discounted rates and discussed Qatar Investment Authority funding for Pakistan’s liquefied natural gas infrastructure. Meetings with Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani and investment officials focused on energy security and sovereign‐wealth–fund partnerships.

## 9. United States (UN General Assembly, New York; 17–21 September 2023)

**Context:** Returning to the UN, Sharif reiterated Pakistan’s support for the global Sustainable Development Goals and climate action. He held side-line meetings with IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva to review Pakistan’s Extended Fund Facility and with U.S. congressional leadership to reaffirm bipartisan support for Pakistan’s security assistance.

## 10. China (Belt and Road Forum, Beijing; 25–26 October 2023)

**Context:** Sharif attended the Belt and Road Forum, emphasizing Pakistan’s CPEC achievements and future “CPEC-Plus” proposals to include Central and Southeast Asian connectivity. He secured pledges for CPEC Phase II funding, focusing on renewable energy, digital infrastructure, and special economic zones.

## 11. Saudi Arabia (Riyadh Summit, 20–22 December 2023)

**Context:** Invited to the Gulf Cooperation Council–Islamic Summit in Riyadh, Sharif discussed Islamic-world economic cooperation and the Palestinian refugee crisis. Bilateral meetings yielded an enhanced deferred oil‐payment facility and exploration of Islamic-finance instruments for Pakistan’s debt restructuring.

## 12. Turkmenistan (Central Asia–Pakistan–China Economic Corridor discussions, 5–6 February 2024)

**Context:** Sharif’s visit to Ashgabat advanced the proposed TAPI (Turkmenistan–Afghanistan–Pakistan–India) gas pipeline talks and “CPEC-Central Asia” linkages. Meetings with President Serdar Berdimuhamedow addressed transit fees, security guarantees, and multilateral development bank financing.

---

### Political Significance

These trips illustrate Sharif’s strategic priorities:

* **Economic Stabilization:** Pursuit of deferred energy payments, concessional loans, and foreign direct investment to buttress IMF‐backed reforms.
* **Diversified Diplomacy:** Engagements beyond traditional Western partners—deepening ties with Gulf and Turkic states and broadening regional connectivity initiatives.
* **Security Cooperation:** Strengthening counter-terrorism and defense partnerships in an evolving regional security environment post-U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan.
* **Global Advocacy:** Leveraging multilateral forums (UNGA, Davos, Belt and Road Forum) to project Pakistan’s reform agenda and climate vulnerabilities.

Collectively, these foreign visits by Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif reflect a concerted diplomatic push to navigate Pakistan through economic crisis and complex regional geopolitics in the early 2020s.
