**Joe Biden** (Joseph Robinette Biden Jr.; born November 20, 1942) is an American politician serving as the 46th President of the United States since January 20, 2021. A member of the Democratic Party, Biden previously served as Vice President from 2009 to 2017 under President Barack Obama and represented Delaware in the U.S. Senate from 1973 to 2009. His nearly five decades in federal office span eras of Cold War détente, partisan realignment, and deep social and economic transformation.

### Early Life and Senate Career

Born in Scranton, Pennsylvania, Biden moved with his family to Delaware as a child. He graduated from the University of Delaware in 1965 and earned his J.D. from Syracuse University College of Law in 1968. After serving on the New Castle County Council, he was elected to the U.S. Senate in 1972 at age 29. In the Senate, Biden developed a reputation for work on foreign relations, criminal justice, and judicial issues. He served as Chairman and then Ranking Member of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, influencing U.S. policy on the Balkans, Iraq, and arms control, and sponsored the 1994 Violence Against Women Act.

### Vice Presidency (2009–2017)

Biden was selected as Barack Obama’s running mate in 2008. As Vice President, he chaired the Middle Class Task Force, helped shepherd the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009 through Congress, and represented the administration in negotiations on the Affordable Care Act. He played leading roles in U.S. policy on Iraq during the troop drawdown, oversaw U.S. engagement in Ukraine after Russia’s 2014 annexation of Crimea, and led efforts to combat gender-based violence globally.

### 2020 Presidential Campaign

Launching his bid in April 2019, Biden positioned himself as the Democratic standard-bearer for restoring stability and unity after the tumultuous Trump presidency. His platform emphasized managing the COVID-19 pandemic, expanding healthcare access, reversing environmental rollbacks, promoting economic relief for working families, and addressing racial inequities. Biden captured the Democratic nomination and defeated incumbent President Donald Trump in the November 2020 election, receiving over 81 million votes—the most in U.S. history—and forming a coalition of suburban voters, Black and Latino communities, and older Americans.

### Presidency (2021–Present)

#### COVID-19 Response and Economic Recovery

In office amid a resurgent pandemic, Biden signed a series of executive orders on his first day, including a national mask mandate on federal property. He championed the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021, a \$1.9 trillion relief package providing direct stimulus payments, extended unemployment benefits, and funding for vaccine distribution. Job growth rebounded as vaccine rollout accelerated, with the economy adding over 6 million jobs in 2021.

#### Infrastructure and Climate

Biden secured bipartisan passage of the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (2021), allocating \$1.2 trillion toward roads, bridges, broadband, and clean energy. He rejoined the Paris Agreement on climate change and unveiled the Build Back Better agenda—though negotiations stalled in Congress—seeking expanded childcare, paid leave, and renewable-energy incentives.

#### Foreign Policy and Global Affairs

Restoring U.S. alliances was a central theme of Biden’s foreign policy. At the 2021 G7 summit, he pledged to counter “autocratic” influence. He upheld NATO’s Article 5 deterrence, imposed sanctions on Russia for cyberattacks and election interference, and led the U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan in August 2021—ending a 20-year war but drawing widespread criticism over its execution and the Taliban’s rapid takeover.

#### Inflation and Economic Challenges

By late 2021 and into 2022, inflation surged to multi-decade highs, driven by supply-chain disruptions, energy-price shocks following Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, and robust consumer demand. The administration’s measures—such as the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, which provided funding for clean energy and prescription-drug negotiation—aimed to address long-term cost pressures, though critics from both parties blamed fiscal stimulus and regulatory overreach for exacerbating inflationary trends.

#### Social and Domestic Policy

Biden has advanced efforts on voting rights, LGBTQ protections, and policing reform, though key legislative initiatives—such as the John Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act and the George Floyd Justice in Policing Act—lacked sufficient Senate support. The administration’s student-debt relief plans and executive actions on asylum policies have faced legal challenges, reflecting polarized congressional gridlock.

### Political Context and Legacy

Biden’s presidency unfolds amid heightened partisan polarization, shifting global power dynamics, and pressing challenges—climate change, pandemics, and technological change. His emphasis on coalition-building and incremental progress contrasts with the more confrontational style of his predecessor. As a political centrist who has evolved on issues such as criminal-justice reform and same-sex marriage, Biden continues to navigate intra-party divides between moderate and progressive factions.

### Electoral History and Approval

Biden’s narrow victory in 2020—with a 306-to-232 Electoral College margin—underscored deep national divisions. His approval ratings have fluctuated, reflecting public sentiment on inflation, immigration, and foreign-policy crises. Congressional midterm elections in 2022 saw Democrats retain control of the Senate but lose the House, complicating his legislative agenda.

---

Joe Biden’s long public-service career—from Senate freshman amid Watergate to his first term as president—embodies significant shifts in American politics: the end of the Cold War, the rise of globalization, and the fracturing of bipartisan consensus. His ongoing presidency tests the capacity of traditional political institutions to address contemporary crises while bridging an increasingly divided electorate.
