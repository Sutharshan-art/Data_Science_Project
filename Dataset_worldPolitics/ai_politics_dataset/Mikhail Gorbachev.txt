**Mikhail Gorbachev**

**Mikhail Sergeyevich Gorbachev** (2 March 1931 – 30 August 2022) was a Soviet and Russian political leader who served as the **last General Secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union** from 1985 to 1991 and as the **first and only President of the Soviet Union** from 1990 until its dissolution in 1991. Widely recognized as a transformative figure in 20th-century politics, Gorbachev introduced sweeping reforms that led to the end of the Cold War, the liberalization of Soviet society, and ultimately, the collapse of the Soviet Union.

Gorbachev’s tenure was marked by his policies of **glasnost** (openness) and **perestroika** (restructuring), which aimed to revitalize the Soviet system through increased transparency and economic reform. These policies, however, also triggered political upheaval, loss of control over Eastern Europe, and internal dissent, all of which contributed to the fragmentation of the Soviet state.

---

### Early Life and Political Rise

Gorbachev was born in **Privolnoye**, a rural village in the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic. Coming from a peasant background, he joined the **Komsomol** (Communist Youth League) in his teenage years and later the **Communist Party of the Soviet Union (CPSU)**. He studied law at **Moscow State University**, where he met his future wife, **Raisa Titarenko**, and began building connections within the party elite.

After rising through the ranks of regional party structures in Stavropol, Gorbachev gained attention for his pragmatic style and ability to navigate bureaucratic politics. In 1978, he was brought to Moscow and quickly advanced within the CPSU hierarchy. By 1980, he had become the youngest full member of the **Politburo**, the ruling body of the Soviet Union.

Following the deaths of three successive Soviet leaders—Leonid Brezhnev (1982), Yuri Andropov (1984), and Konstantin Chernenko (1985)—Gorbachev emerged as a compromise candidate and was appointed **General Secretary** in March 1985.

---

### Reforms: Glasnost and Perestroika

Upon assuming power, Gorbachev confronted a stagnant economy, technological backwardness, and growing discontent both at home and abroad. His signature policies sought to modernize the Soviet system and respond to mounting pressure for change.

* **Perestroika** involved restructuring the Soviet economy by introducing limited market mechanisms, decentralizing control, and encouraging private enterprise.
* **Glasnost** aimed to foster greater transparency, free expression, and political participation, reversing decades of repression.

While initially popular, these reforms exposed deep systemic problems. The economy declined further, shortages increased, and political liberalization emboldened nationalist movements and reformist factions within the USSR and Eastern Europe.

---

### Foreign Policy and the End of the Cold War

Gorbachev’s foreign policy, known as **“new thinking,”** rejected Cold War confrontation in favor of diplomacy, arms reduction, and global cooperation. He engaged in a series of high-profile summits with U.S. President **Ronald Reagan**, culminating in the **Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty** of 1987, a milestone in arms control.

Gorbachev also oversaw the withdrawal of Soviet troops from **Afghanistan** and signaled a non-interventionist stance toward Eastern Europe. In 1989, this approach facilitated the peaceful collapse of Communist regimes across the Warsaw Pact, including the fall of the **Berlin Wall**, without Soviet military intervention.

While earning Gorbachev the **Nobel Peace Prize in 1990**, these developments also weakened Soviet influence and emboldened demands for autonomy within the USSR.

---

### Collapse of the Soviet Union

As economic conditions worsened and nationalist tensions rose, Gorbachev struggled to balance reform and unity. In 1990, he became the first President of the Soviet Union, a newly created position designed to centralize authority. However, his power was increasingly undermined by rivals such as **Boris Yeltsin**, who championed Russian sovereignty and liberal economic reforms.

In August 1991, Gorbachev was briefly placed under house arrest during a failed coup by hardline communists opposed to his reforms. Though he returned to power, the event accelerated the collapse of central authority. By December 1991, the USSR was formally dissolved, and Gorbachev resigned as president.

---

### Later Life and Legacy

Following his resignation, Gorbachev remained active in global affairs through the **Gorbachev Foundation** and **Green Cross International**, promoting disarmament, environmentalism, and democratic governance. He ran unsuccessfully for President of Russia in 1996, receiving less than 1% of the vote—a reflection of his unpopularity in post-Soviet Russia, where many blamed him for economic decline and national humiliation.

Internationally, Gorbachev remained a respected figure, frequently consulted on global security and democratic reform. His life symbolizes the profound shifts of the late 20th century—from superpower rivalry to post-Cold War cooperation—and his role in ending the Cold War remains widely acknowledged.

---

### Assessment

Mikhail Gorbachev is one of the most consequential figures of modern history. His reforms fundamentally altered the Soviet political landscape and contributed to the end of communist rule in Europe. While his leadership is credited with averting violent conflict during the USSR's dissolution, he is also criticized for economic mismanagement and political naivety.

To supporters, Gorbachev was a courageous reformer who gave the Soviet people a taste of freedom and helped end decades of global tension. To critics, he presided over the disintegration of a superpower. His mixed legacy reflects the complexity of a leader who sought to save his system by transforming it—and, in doing so, set in motion its collapse.
