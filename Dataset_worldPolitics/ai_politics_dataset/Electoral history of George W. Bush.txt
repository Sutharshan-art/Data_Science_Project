**Electoral History of George W. Bush**

George Walker Bush (born July 6, 1946) served as the 43rd Governor of Texas (1995–2000) and the 43rd President of the United States (2001–2009). His electoral career spans a failed congressional bid in the 1970s, two successful gubernatorial campaigns, and two presidential elections, each reflecting broader political realignments at the state and national levels.

## 1978 Congressional Primary

* **Office Sought:** U.S. House of Representatives, Texas’s 19th Congressional District
* **Context:** Bush’s first foray into elective politics, at age 31, challenged incumbent Democrat Kent Hance in the Republican primary. Texas remained heavily Democratic, and the Republican Party was rebuilding after the Watergate scandal.
* **Result:** Bush finished third in a five-candidate primary, receiving 11.6 % of the vote; Hance won renomination and the general election.

## Gubernatorial Elections in Texas

### 1994 Republican Primary

* **Office Sought:** Governor of Texas
* **Context:** After business and political involvement, Bush entered the 1994 gubernatorial race to succeed Democratic incumbent Ann Richards. He campaigned as a “compassionate conservative,” focusing on education reform, tort reform, and tax cuts.
* **Result:** In a crowded nine-candidate primary, Bush led with 58.4 % of the vote—avoiding a runoff—and secured the Republican nomination outright.

### 1994 General Election

* **Date:** November 8, 1994
* **Opponent:** Ann Richards (incumbent Democrat)
* **Context:** The “Republican Revolution” of 1994, driven by Newt Gingrich’s Contract with America, bolstered GOP prospects nationwide and in Texas. Richards faced criticism over taxes and state spending.
* **Result:** Bush defeated Richards 53.5 % to 45.9 %, becoming the first Republican governor of Texas since Reconstruction.

### 1998 Gubernatorial Reelection

* **Date:** November 3, 1998
* **Opponent:** Garry Mauro (Democrat), tax commissioner
* **Context:** Bush governed during a period of robust economic growth and educational initiatives. He leveraged incumbency, personal popularity, and a booming state economy.
* **Result:** Bush won resoundingly, 68.6 % to 30.6 %, carrying all 254 counties—the largest margin in Texas gubernatorial history.

## 2000 Presidential Campaign

### Republican Primaries

* **Launch:** June 1999
* **Context:** Bush announced his candidacy as the early frontrunner, leveraging his Texas governorship and political lineage. He faced little serious primary opposition, briefly competing with Senator John McCain before securing the nomination.
* **Results:**

  * **Iowa Caucuses (January 24, 2000):** Bush narrowly lost to McCain (48 %–30 %), but rebounded in subsequent contests.
  * **New Hampshire Primary (February 1):** Bush won decisively with 48 % to McCain’s 30 %.
  * **Super Tuesday (March 7):** Captured a majority of delegates by winning Georgia, Virginia, and other Southern states.

### Republican National Convention

* **Date:** July 31–August 3, 2000, Philadelphia
* **Result:** Bush was nominated by acclamation; he selected Dick Cheney as his running mate to strengthen his national security credentials.

### 2000 General Election

* **Date:** November 7, 2000
* **Opponents:** Al Gore–Joe Lieberman (Democrat), Ralph Nader–Winona LaDuke (Green)
* **Context:** The race was exceptionally close, centered on the economy’s “soft landing,” Bush’s education agenda (“No Child Left Behind”), and the vice presidency. The election hinged on Florida’s 25 electoral votes, triggering a recount and **Bush v. Gore** Supreme Court decision.
* **Result:** Bush won 47.9 % of the popular vote to Gore’s 48.4 %, but secured the Electoral College 271–266 after Florida’s 25 votes were awarded to him by the Court. Nader drew 2.7 % nationally, with stronger showings in key states.

## 2004 Presidential Campaign

### Republican Primaries

* **Launch:** June 2003
* **Context:** As incumbent, Bush sought reelection amid the ongoing global War on Terror and the Iraq War’s early years. Polls showed high approval ratings post–9/11, deterring serious primary challengers.
* **Result:** Bush faced token opposition and won over 98 % of primary votes, securing the nomination early.

### Republican National Convention

* **Date:** September 1–4, 2004, New York City
* **Result:** Bush was renominated; he chose incumbent Vice President Dick Cheney to emphasize continuity in national security.

### 2004 General Election

* **Date:** November 2, 2004
* **Opponents:** John Kerry–John Edwards (Democrat), Michael Badnarik (Libertarian), Ralph Nader–Peter Camejo (independent)
* **Context:** Campaign themes included national security, Iraq War rationale, and social issues. Bush’s “stay the course” message contrasted with Kerry’s emphasis on domestic concerns and “unity.”
* **Result:** Bush won 50.7 % of the popular vote to Kerry’s 48.3 % and captured 286 electoral votes to Kerry’s 251. He reclaimed Ohio’s 20 electoral votes—decisive in the Electoral College—even as he lost the popular vote margin in swing states.

## Summary

George W. Bush’s electoral history reflects a trajectory from early defeat in a Democratic stronghold to dominant statewide victories in Texas and narrow but consequential national wins. His campaigns were shaped by shifting partisan tides—the Republican resurgence in the South, post–9/11 political realignment, and the growing influence of third‐party voters. Bush’s two presidential victories underscore the significance of the Electoral College and regional coalitions in U.S. politics.
