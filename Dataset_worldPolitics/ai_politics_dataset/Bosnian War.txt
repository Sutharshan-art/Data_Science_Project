**Bosnian War**

The **Bosnian War** was an armed conflict that took place in Bosnia and Herzegovina between April 1992 and December 1995. It was one of the most devastating wars in Europe since World War II, characterized by intense ethnic violence among the country’s three main ethnic groups: Bosniaks (Bosnian Muslims), Bosnian Serbs, and Bosnian Croats. The war resulted in widespread atrocities, including genocide, ethnic cleansing, and war crimes, profoundly impacting the region’s political landscape and international relations.

---

### Historical and Political Context

The roots of the Bosnian War lie in the complex historical, ethnic, and political tensions within the former Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (SFRY). Bosnia and Herzegovina, a multiethnic republic within Yugoslavia, was home to a diverse population including Bosniaks, Serbs, and Croats.

The breakup of Yugoslavia in the early 1990s, driven by nationalist movements and the weakening of federal authority, set the stage for conflict. Slovenia and Croatia declared independence in 1991, leading to armed conflicts. Bosnia and Herzegovina held an independence referendum in February–March 1992, which was largely boycotted by Bosnian Serbs. Following the referendum’s approval, Bosnia declared independence, triggering opposition and armed resistance from Bosnian Serb forces backed by the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (Serbia and Montenegro).

---

### Outbreak of War

The war began in April 1992, with Bosnian Serb paramilitary and regular forces launching attacks on Bosniak and Croat-populated areas, aiming to carve out ethnically homogenous territories to eventually join with Serbia. This campaign included widespread ethnic cleansing, forced displacement, mass killings, and the establishment of concentration camps.

Bosniak and Bosnian Croat forces resisted the aggression, but the Bosnian Army (ARBiH) was initially less equipped and fragmented. International recognition of Bosnia’s sovereignty came early, but effective intervention was limited in the initial years.

---

### Key Phases and Events

The war evolved through several key phases:

* **Siege of Sarajevo (1992–1996):** One of the longest sieges in modern history, Bosnian Serb forces encircled the capital Sarajevo, subjecting it to constant shelling and sniper fire, causing thousands of civilian casualties.

* **Ethnic Cleansing and Massacres:** Large-scale atrocities occurred, including the systematic killing of civilians in towns like Prijedor and Foča. The most infamous atrocity was the **Srebrenica massacre** in July 1995, where more than 8,000 Bosniak men and boys were executed by Bosnian Serb forces.

* **Croat-Bosniak Conflict (1992–1994):** Initially allies, Bosniaks and Croats later fought over territorial control, adding complexity to the conflict until the Washington Agreement (1994) restored their cooperation.

* **NATO Intervention:** In response to escalating violence and humanitarian crises, NATO launched air strikes against Bosnian Serb targets in 1995, pressuring parties toward peace negotiations.

---

### International Involvement and Peace Process

The United Nations deployed peacekeepers and imposed arms embargoes, though these measures had limited effectiveness in stopping the violence. The international community faced criticism for failing to prevent atrocities and protect civilians.

The war formally ended with the **Dayton Peace Agreement** in December 1995, brokered by the United States. The agreement established Bosnia and Herzegovina as a single state composed of two autonomous entities: the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina (mainly Bosniaks and Croats) and the Republika Srpska (mainly Serbs). It created a complex power-sharing system designed to maintain peace and accommodate ethnic divisions.

---

### Humanitarian Impact and Legacy

The Bosnian War caused approximately 100,000 deaths, with millions displaced internally or as refugees. War crimes, including genocide, ethnic cleansing, rape, and torture, were documented extensively.

The International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY) was established to prosecute individuals responsible for these crimes, leading to convictions of prominent political and military leaders.

The war left Bosnia and Herzegovina deeply divided ethnically, politically, and socially. The legacy of trauma, displacement, and unresolved grievances continues to affect the country’s development and regional relations.

---

### Conclusion

The Bosnian War was a complex and brutal conflict rooted in ethnic nationalism, territorial ambitions, and the collapse of Yugoslavia. It involved widespread human rights violations and challenged international norms on sovereignty, intervention, and justice. The war’s resolution and ongoing peace efforts illustrate the difficulties of reconciling divided societies and rebuilding multiethnic states after violent conflict.
