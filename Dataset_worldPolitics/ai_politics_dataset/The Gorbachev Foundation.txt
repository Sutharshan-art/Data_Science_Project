**The Gorbachev Foundation**

The Gorbachev Foundation, formally the International Foundation for Socio-Economic and Political Studies, is a Moscow-based non-profit think tank established in November 1991 by Mikhail Gorbachev, the last leader of the Soviet Union. Dedicated to the study of political reform, economic transition, and global governance, the Foundation has played a significant role in documenting the perestroika era, promoting democratic values, and fostering dialogue on international security and sustainable development.

**Historical and Political Context**
By the late 1980s, General Secretary Mikhail Gorbachev had launched his policies of perestroika (restructuring) and glasnost (openness) in an effort to revitalize the stagnating Soviet system. While these reforms ultimately contributed to the Soviet Union’s dissolution in December 1991, they also paved the way for unprecedented political liberalization and global engagement. Against this backdrop, Gorbachev sought to institutionalize the legacy of reform by creating a foundation that would continue to study and promote the principles of democratization, social justice, and international cooperation beyond his tenure in office.

**Founding and Early Years**
The Foundation was officially incorporated on 10 November 1991, two months before the formal end of the USSR. As founder and chairman, Gorbachev endowed it with architectural landmarks—the former Communist Party Central Committee guest house on Ulitsa Novaya Ploshchad in central Moscow—providing a symbolic bridge between the Soviet past and a reformist future. Early activities centered on collecting archival materials from the Gorbachev era, publishing memoirs and documents related to perestroika, and organizing conferences on the transition from socialism to market economies.

**Mission and Objectives**
The Gorbachev Foundation’s charter defines its mission as:

1. **Preservation and study** of historical records from the late‐Soviet period, especially policies of perestroika and glasnost.
2. **Analysis and promotion** of democratic governance, human rights, and the rule of law within Russia and internationally.
3. **Policy research** on economic development, environmental sustainability, and social welfare in post‐socialist states.
4. **Facilitation of dialogue** among political leaders, scholars, and civil society on conflict resolution, nuclear disarmament, and global security.

**Organizational Structure**
The Foundation is governed by a board of trustees chaired by Gorbachev until his death in 2022, and thereafter by a rotating executive committee. It maintains several research departments—Political Reform, Economic Policy, International Affairs, and Environmental Studies—each led by senior scholars. An advisory council, comprising former politicians, academics, and public intellectuals from multiple countries, provides strategic guidance.

**Key Activities and Programs**

* **Gorbachev Readings:** An annual international conference held in Moscow, convening former heads of state, Nobel laureates, and experts to discuss pressing global challenges.
* **Policy Papers and Journals:** The Foundation publishes the quarterly *Perestroika Review* and monographs on topics such as market transition, Russian constitutional reform, and climate policy.
* **Archival Projects:** In collaboration with the State Archive of the Russian Federation, the Foundation has digitized thousands of documents—including Politburo minutes, correspondence, and public speeches—making them available to researchers worldwide.
* **Educational Outreach:** Through fellowships and scholarships, the Foundation supports graduate students and young professionals from Russia and former Soviet republics to study democratic governance and public administration.

**International Partnerships**
The Gorbachev Foundation has established formal ties with leading global think tanks and universities, including the Brookings Institution (USA), the German Marshall Fund, the Centre for European Policy Studies (Belgium), and the Shanghai Academy of Social Sciences. These partnerships facilitate joint research initiatives, exchange programs, and comparative studies on post‐communist transitions and contemporary geopolitical trends.

**Role in Russian Civil Society**
In post‐Soviet Russia, the Foundation occupies a unique position as a bridge between the political establishment and independent civil society. While received with some skepticism by hard‐line political figures for its liberal agenda and critical stance on democratic backsliding, it remains one of the few institutions in Russia dedicated to sustained analysis of governance and human rights. Its events are attended by opposition figures, government officials, and foreign diplomats alike.

**Funding and Transparency**
As a non‐commercial entity, the Gorbachev Foundation’s budget is financed through a combination of private endowments, grants from international foundations (e.g., Ford, Friedrich Ebert, and Erasmus+), and revenue from publications and event sponsorships. The Foundation publishes annual financial statements and program reports on its website, adhering to standards of nonprofit transparency.

**Challenges and Criticism**
The Foundation has faced challenges amid tightening regulations on NGOs in Russia, particularly laws on “foreign agents” that require organizations receiving overseas funding to register under that label. Although the Gorbachev Foundation has not been officially designated as such, its critics in nationalist and security circles accuse it of promoting Western values. Internally, debates continue over balancing historical commemoration with forward‐looking policy advocacy in an evolving Russian political climate.

**Legacy and Impact**
Over three decades, the Gorbachev Foundation has contributed significantly to scholarship on the end of the Cold War, the nature of Soviet reform, and the trajectory of post‐communist societies. By preserving primary sources and fostering international dialogue, it has helped shape academic understanding and public discourse on democratization and global security. As Russia’s political landscape continues to evolve, the Foundation remains a testament to Mikhail Gorbachev’s vision of openness, peace, and international partnership.
