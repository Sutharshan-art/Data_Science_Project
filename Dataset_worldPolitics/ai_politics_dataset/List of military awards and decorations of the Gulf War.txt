**List of Military Awards and Decorations of the Gulf War**

The Gulf War (August 1990 – February 1991), fought to expel Iraqi forces from Kuwait under United Nations mandate, involved a U.S.-led coalition of more than 30 nations. In recognition of service during Operations Desert Shield and Desert Storm, participating countries instituted or authorized a variety of campaign medals, service ribbons, and foreign decorations. These awards reflect both the military efforts and the political alliances forged during and after the conflict.

---

## United States

* **Southwest Asia Service Medal (SASM)**
  Established by Executive Order in 1991, the SASM recognized U.S. service members who served in designated areas of Southwest Asia between 2 August 1990 and 31 August 1991. Campaign stars denoted participation in three phases: Defense of Saudi Arabia, Liberation and Defense of Kuwait, and Cease‐Fire operations.

* **Kuwait Liberation Medal (Kingdom of Saudi Arabia)**
  Authorized by Saudi Arabia in 1991 and by the U.S. Secretary of Defense in 1999, this medal honored coalition forces who served in Saudi Arabian territory or airspace during specified periods. Initially declined due to foreign decoration policies, it was later approved for U.S. wear.

* **Kuwait Liberation Medal (Government of Kuwait)**
  Established in April 1991, Kuwait issued this medal to coalition personnel who participated in liberating or defending Kuwait between 2 August 1990 and 31 August 1993. The U.S. government authorized its acceptance and wear in 1995.

* **Prisoner of War Medal**
  Though predating the Gulf War, this medal was awarded to those captured or missing in action during Desert Storm, including the few U.S. and allied personnel held by Iraqi forces.

* **National Defense Service Medal**
  Retroactively applied to the Gulf War period, this medal recognized all U.S. military personnel on active duty from 2 August 1990 to 30 November 1995.

---

## United Kingdom

* **Gulf Medal**
  Instituted by Royal Warrant in 1992, the Gulf Medal honored British forces deployed in Operations Granby (UK contribution to Desert Storm) and subsequent maritime and air operations. A clasp inscribed “15 JAN. – 28 FEB. 1991” denoted direct participation in combat operations; no clasp indicated service during the buildup phase (2 August 1990–14 January 1991).

* **Kuwait Liberation Medal (Government of Kuwait)**
  Recognized in UK honors regulations, this decoration was awarded to British personnel who met the Kuwaiti government’s criteria. It was worn after British medals in the order of precedence.

---

## Kuwait

* **Medal for Kuwait Liberation**
  Established 16 April 1991, this medal was awarded to coalition forces and Kuwaiti resistance members who directly participated in operations to liberate Kuwait. It featured a central map of Kuwait and Arabic inscriptions; national policy determined eligibility for foreign wear.

---

## Saudi Arabia

* **Order of King Abdulaziz** (Special Class)
  Conferred on select senior coalition leaders (e.g., President George H. W. Bush, Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher) as a diplomatic honor, reflecting Saudi recognition of strategic leadership.

* **Medal for the Liberation of Kuwait**
  A distinct Saudi decoration for service in Saudi Arabia and coalition operations, its design incorporated national emblems and the dates of the Gulf War.

---

## Other Coalition Nations

* **France – Médaille du Golfe PERSIAN (Gulf War Medal)**
  Instituted in 1991 to honor French forces in Opération Daguet. Awarded with a ribbon bearing national colors and an emirate emblem.

* **Canada – Gulf and Kuwait Medal**
  Established by royal proclamation in 1992, this Canadian decoration recognized service members who served in the Gulf region between 2 August 1990 and 31 March 1991.

* **Australia – Australian Active Service Medal 1945–75 (with “Kuwait” clasp)**
  Although primarily for Vietnam, a special “Kuwait” clasp was authorized for Australian service in the Gulf War period, acknowledging expeditionary operations under UN mandate.

* **Italy – Medal for the Operations in the Gulf**
  Awarded to Italian personnel in Operation Provide Comfort and subsequent missions, reflecting Italy’s logistical and humanitarian contributions.

* **Egypt – Medal for the Liberation of Kuwait**
  Conferred upon Egyptian forces who joined the coalition, underscoring Cairo’s strategic alignment with Western-led efforts.

* **Other Nations**
  Belgium, the Netherlands, Spain, and several Gulf Cooperation Council states issued their own campaign medals or clasps for participation in mine-clearing, air operations, and naval escorts, reflecting national policies on foreign deployments.

---

## Political and Historical Context

1. **Diplomatic Significance:** Postwar decorations often carried diplomatic weight, symbolizing gratitude and reinforcing bilateral ties—particularly Middle Eastern awards to Western leaders and forces.
2. **Coalition Cohesion:** Uniform recognition across multiple nations helped maintain solidarity among diverse forces from NATO members, Arab states, and non-aligned countries, reflecting the UN’s broad mandate.
3. **Domestic Policies:** Variations in acceptance and wear of foreign medals (e.g., initial U.S. hesitation on Saudi awards) highlighted national regulations governing precedence and eligibility for foreign decorations.
4. **Legacy:** The Gulf War’s awards set precedents for subsequent multinational operations—such as peacekeeping in the Balkans and coalition actions in Afghanistan and Iraq—where campaign medals and coalitional decorations continue to play roles in morale and diplomatic signaling.

This **List of military awards and decorations of the Gulf War** illustrates how governments recognized service and sacrifice during a pivotal post–Cold War conflict, weaving military honors into broader patterns of alliance politics and national commemoration.
