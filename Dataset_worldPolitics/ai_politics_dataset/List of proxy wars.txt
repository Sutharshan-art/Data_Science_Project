**List of Proxy Wars**

A **proxy war** is a conflict where two or more external powers use third parties as substitutes for direct engagement. Rather than confronting each other militarily, the rival powers support opposing sides in a regional or internal conflict, often supplying arms, training, funding, intelligence, or political backing. Proxy wars have been a hallmark of international politics, especially during periods of bipolar or multipolar rivalry, and they frequently occur in states experiencing civil unrest, decolonization, or weak central authority.

This list of proxy wars highlights significant conflicts throughout modern history in which major powers have competed indirectly, shaping the course of local wars to serve broader strategic, ideological, or geopolitical interests.

---

### Historical and Political Context

Proxy wars are not unique to the modern era, but they became particularly prominent during the **Cold War (1947–1991)**, when the United States and the Soviet Union avoided direct confrontation to prevent nuclear escalation. Instead, both superpowers sought to expand their influence and contain the other's ideology—capitalism or communism—through regional conflicts.

Beyond the Cold War, proxy wars have also occurred during the **post-colonial era**, **inter-imperial rivalries**, and the **post-9/11 period**, reflecting changing alliances and global power dynamics. Non-state actors, such as insurgent groups, militias, and terrorist organizations, have increasingly featured in these conflicts, further complicating the traditional state-to-state framework.

---

### Major Proxy Wars by Era

#### **Spanish Civil War (1936–1939)**

**Proxies:** Spanish Republicans (supported by the Soviet Union) vs. Nationalists (supported by Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy)
This conflict served as a precursor to World War II and featured significant international involvement. While officially a civil war, foreign powers provided weapons, volunteers, and tactical support, turning it into a proxy battleground between fascist and communist ideologies.

---

### Cold War Proxy Wars (1947–1991)

#### **Korean War (1950–1953)**

**Proxies:** South Korea (backed by the United States and UN allies) vs. North Korea (supported by China and the Soviet Union)
The Korean War was the first major armed conflict of the Cold War, resulting in millions of deaths and the continued division of the Korean Peninsula.

#### **Vietnam War (1955–1975)**

**Proxies:** South Vietnam (U.S.-backed) vs. North Vietnam (supported by the USSR and China)
This prolonged conflict symbolized Cold War rivalry in Southeast Asia. The United States committed extensive military resources to prevent a communist takeover, while North Vietnam received substantial aid from the Eastern Bloc.

#### **Laotian Civil War (1959–1975)**

**Proxies:** Royal Lao Government (supported by the U.S.) vs. Pathet Lao (backed by North Vietnam and the Soviet Union)
Often overshadowed by the Vietnam War, this conflict was deeply entangled in regional Cold War dynamics and included covert CIA operations.

#### **Angolan Civil War (1975–2002)**

**Proxies:** MPLA (backed by the Soviet Union and Cuba) vs. UNITA (supported by the U.S., South Africa, and others)
Following decolonization from Portugal, Angola descended into a complex civil war that became a major Cold War proxy conflict in Africa.

#### **Afghan War (1979–1989)**

**Proxies:** Afghan Mujahideen (supported by the U.S., Pakistan, Saudi Arabia) vs. Soviet-backed Afghan government
The Soviet invasion of Afghanistan prompted a massive U.S.-led campaign to support Islamist fighters, who would later evolve into various militant groups with global implications.

#### **Nicaraguan Contra War (1981–1990)**

**Proxies:** Sandinista government (supported by Cuba and the Soviet Union) vs. Contras (funded and trained by the United States)
The Reagan administration's support for the Contras against Nicaragua’s leftist government led to major political controversies, including the Iran-Contra affair.

---

### Post-Cold War and 21st-Century Proxy Wars

#### **Syrian Civil War (2011–present)**

**Proxies:** Syrian government (backed by Russia and Iran) vs. various rebel groups (supported by the U.S., Turkey, Gulf States); involvement of ISIS and Kurdish forces added further complexity
The Syrian conflict became a focal point for regional and global rivalries, featuring overlapping proxy battles involving both state and non-state actors.

#### **Yemeni Civil War (2014–present)**

**Proxies:** Houthi rebels (supported by Iran) vs. Yemeni government forces (backed by a Saudi-led coalition, with U.S. and Western support)
Often cited as a major Middle East proxy war, the Yemen conflict reflects the broader Saudi-Iranian rivalry for regional dominance.

#### **Libyan Civil War (2014–2020)**

**Proxies:** Government of National Accord (supported by Turkey and Qatar) vs. Libyan National Army (backed by Egypt, UAE, Russia)
Libya’s post-Gaddafi instability turned into a multi-sided civil war involving external backers with competing regional interests.

#### **Ukraine Conflict (2014–present)**

**Proxies:** Ukraine (supported by NATO and Western countries) vs. Russian-backed separatists; direct Russian invasion in 2022 intensified the conflict
Initially viewed as a hybrid war with proxy elements, the conflict evolved into a conventional war after Russia’s 2022 invasion, though proxy dimensions persist in the wider geopolitical contest.

---

### Characteristics of Proxy Wars

* **Indirect Engagement:** Major powers avoid direct conflict by supporting aligned factions.
* **Asymmetric Warfare:** Involvement of guerrilla fighters, militias, and insurgent groups.
* **Regional Destabilization:** Often prolong conflicts and exacerbate humanitarian crises.
* **Deniability:** External sponsors may deny or downplay their involvement for strategic or diplomatic reasons.

---

### Conclusion

The list of proxy wars illustrates how global power struggles are often fought through indirect means, with devastating consequences for the affected regions. From Cold War superpower confrontations to modern regional rivalries, proxy wars remain a persistent feature of international relations. While they offer powerful states a way to exert influence without direct confrontation, they often lead to protracted violence, displacement, and geopolitical entanglement. As international power dynamics evolve, proxy conflicts continue to challenge peace and stability across the globe.
