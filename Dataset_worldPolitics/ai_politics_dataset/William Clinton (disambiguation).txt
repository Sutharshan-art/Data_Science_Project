## William Jefferson Clinton (born 1946)

Commonly known as Bill Clinton, William Jefferson Clinton served as the 42nd President of the United States from 1993 to 2001. A member of the Democratic Party, he was previously Governor of Arkansas (1979–1981, 1983–1992) and Attorney General of Arkansas (1977–1979). His presidency oversaw a period of economic expansion and the implementation of the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA), but was also marked by the 1998–1999 impeachment crisis stemming from charges of perjury and obstruction of justice. After leaving office, Clinton continued to engage in global humanitarian work through the Clinton Foundation and remained active in Democratic Party politics.

## William de Clinton, 1st Earl of Huntingdon (1304–1354)

William de Clinton was an English nobleman and royal administrator during the reign of King Edward III. Born circa 1304, he was a trusted supporter of the young king and was created Earl of Huntingdon in 1337. Clinton held several high offices, including Lord High Admiral (1342–1344) and Lord Privy Seal. He participated in the Hundred Years’ War, commanding naval expeditions against France, and was instrumental in maritime reorganization that strengthened England’s naval capabilities. His earldom became extinct upon his death in 1354, as he left no legitimate heirs.

## General Sir William Henry Clinton (1769–1846)

William Henry Clinton was a senior British Army officer and Member of Parliament in the late Georgian and early Victorian eras. Born into the prominent Clinton family, he entered the army in 1786 and served in the Flanders Campaign against Revolutionary France. Rising through the ranks, he commanded troops in the Peninsular War under the Duke of Wellington and later held garrison posts in North America during the War of 1812. Elected Member of Parliament for Boroughbridge (1808–1812) and later for Sandwich (1818–1820), Clinton advocated for military reform and veterans’ welfare before retiring with the rank of full General in 1841.

## William Clinton (Member of Parliament for Nottingham, 1572)

William Clinton represented Nottingham as a Member of Parliament in the Parliament of England’s session of 1572, during the reign of Queen Elizabeth I. Little is recorded of his parliamentary activity, but MPs of this period typically addressed local grievances, taxation measures, and national security concerns—particularly the threat posed by Catholic conspiracies and tensions with Spain. Clinton’s election reflects Nottingham’s growing importance as a market and administrative center in the Elizabethan era.
