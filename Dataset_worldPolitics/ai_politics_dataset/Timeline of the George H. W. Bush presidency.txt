**Timeline of the George H. W. Bush Presidency (1989–1993)**

George Herbert Walker Bush served as the 41st President of the United States from January 20, 1989, to January 20, 1993. His administration presided over the end of the Cold War, the first Gulf War, significant domestic legislation, and an economic recession that shaped the 1992 election.

---

### 1989: Transition and Early Initiatives

* **January 20**: Inauguration—Bush is sworn in, promising a “kinder, gentler nation” and continuity with Ronald Reagan’s policies.
* **March**: Nomination battles—Senate rejects John Tower for Defense Secretary; Bush turns to Dick Cheney, who is confirmed.
* **May**: U.S.–Soviet Summit in Malta—Bush and Mikhail Gorbachev declare an end to Cold War hostilities.
* **June–July**: Domestic priorities—Bush signs the Clean Air Act Amendments of 1990 (negotiated mid-year) and the Immigration Act of 1990, responding to environmental and demographic concerns.
* **September**: Tiananmen Square—Bush condemns China’s crackdown, imposes an arms embargo, and freezes high-level exchanges.
* **November–December**: Budget battles—Bush negotiates the Budget Enforcement Act and raises taxes despite his “no new taxes” pledge, setting the stage for future political vulnerability.

### 1990: Bipartisan Legislation and Gulf Crisis

* **February**: Budget summit—“Pay-as-you-go” rules are enacted to control deficits.
* **March–May**: Cabinet and policy—Bush signs the Americans with Disabilities Act, a landmark civil-rights act, and the Immigration Act increases annual visas and diversity programs.
* **July**: Clean Air Act—Bush signs comprehensive amendments, targeting acid rain, urban smog, and toxic emissions.
* **August 2**: Kuwait invasion—Saddam Hussein’s Iraq invades Kuwait, triggering a global diplomatic crisis.
* **August–December**: Operation Desert Shield—Bush builds a UN-mandated coalition to deter further Iraqi aggression, deploying U.S. forces to Saudi Arabia and securing sanctions under UN Resolution 661.

### 1991: Gulf War and Soviet Collapse

* **January 16–28**: Operation Desert Storm—Bush wins congressional approval for military force; a massive air campaign and a swift ground offensive liberate Kuwait.
* **March**: Humanitarian interventions—U.S. enforces no-fly zones over Kurdish and Shiite areas in Iraq to protect civilians.
* **July–September**: START I—Bush and Gorbachev sign the Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty, reducing strategic nuclear arsenals.
* **August 19–21**: Soviet coup attempt—Bush condemns the hard-liners’ putsch, supports Boris Yeltsin, and helps ensure no U.S. intervention.
* **December 25**: Dissolution of the USSR—Gorbachev resigns; Bush proclaims the Cold War’s end and pledges support for newly independent republics.

### 1992: Domestic Challenges and Re-election Campaign

* **January–June**: Economic downturn—Unemployment peaks above 7 percent; Bush’s approval falls amid recession. He negotiates the 1992 Energy Policy Act but struggles with budget deficits.
* **April**: Los Angeles riots—Following the Rodney King verdict, Bush deploys federal aid and urges reconciliation.
* **August**: Republican National Convention—Bush, attacked as out of touch on the economy, secures renomination with Dan Quayle as running mate.
* **November 3**: Election defeat—Bill Clinton wins 43 percent of the vote to Bush’s 37 percent, with Ross Perot’s independent candidacy siphoning support. Bush’s concession speech underscores policy disagreements over deficits and domestic priorities.

### 1993: Transition to the Clinton Administration

* **January 7**: Final State of the Union—Bush warns of emerging threats and lauds the Gulf Coalition’s success.
* **January 15**: Bush records a farewell radio address, highlighting achievements in foreign policy and cautioning against new isolationism.
* **January 20**: Departure—Bush leaves office with a legacy of foreign-policy triumphs overshadowed by domestic economic woes; Clinton is inaugurated.

---

**Historical and Political Context**
Bush entered office as the Soviet bloc unraveled and U.S. foreign policy shifted from containment to cooperative security. His swift management of the Gulf crisis demonstrated a new model of UN-backed collective action. Domestically, Bush achieved landmark legislation on environmental protection, disability rights, and immigration but broke his “read my lips: no new taxes” pledge to address growing deficits. An economic recession and rising unemployment eroded his popularity, contributing to his 1992 electoral defeat. Bush’s presidency thus straddled two eras: the closing of the Cold War and the emerging post–Cold War challenges of balancing international leadership with pressing domestic concerns.
