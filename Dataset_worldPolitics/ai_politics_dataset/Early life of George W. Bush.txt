**Early Life of George W. Bush**

**George Walker Bush** (born July 6, 1946) is an American politician who served as the 43rd President of the United States (2001–2009). The eldest son of President George H. W. Bush and First Lady Barbara Bush, his upbringing and formative experiences shaped his later political career.

## Family Background and Birth

George W. Bush was born in New Haven, Connecticut, where his father was a student at Yale Law School. His paternal grandfather, Prescott Bush, was a U.S. Senator from Connecticut (1952–1963), and his father, George H. W. Bush, served as U.S. Ambassador to the United Nations (1971–1973), Director of Central Intelligence (1976–1977), Vice President under Ronald Reagan (1981–1989), and President (1989–1993). His mother, Barbara Pierce Bush, was a great-granddaughter of U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt’s maternal grandfather’s sister-in-law, embedding Bush in a lineage of American political elites.

Bush’s birth occurred during the early Cold War, a period marked by U.S.–Soviet rivalry and the onset of American suburbanization. His family’s political prominence and the era’s emphasis on public service set expectations for his future role in government.

## Childhood and Early Education

Shortly after his birth, the Bush family moved to Midland, Texas, where George H. W. Bush entered the oil business with the Zapata Petroleum Corporation. Midland’s burgeoning oil economy and conservative cultural milieu influenced young George’s worldview, instilling values of entrepreneurship and community service common in post–World War II America.

Bush attended school in Midland through eighth grade, excelling in athletics—especially baseball—and demonstrating early leadership as a student council member. In 1952, the family moved to Houston briefly when his father’s career advanced, before relocating to Greenwich, Connecticut, in 1955, following his grandfather Prescott Bush’s return to private practice.

## Preparatory School and Youth Activities

From 1959 to 1964, Bush attended **Greenwich Country Day School** and **Fairfield Country Day School**, then enrolled as a boarder at **Phillips Academy** in Andover, Massachusetts. At Andover, he played baseball and football, served as class president, and first expressed interest in politics by engaging in student government debates. Andover’s rigorous academic environment and tradition of preparing future leaders in an era of civil-rights upheaval honed his confidence and reinforced his establishment roots.

## College Years and Military Service

In 1964, amid the Civil Rights Act’s passage and escalating U.S. involvement in Vietnam, Bush enrolled at **Yale University**, his father’s alma mater. As a member of the **Delta Kappa Epsilon** fraternity, he played first-base on the varsity baseball team and earned a bachelor’s degree in history in 1968. His senior thesis explored the international response to the Spanish Civil War, reflecting his interest in foreign affairs.

Upon graduation, Bush commissioned as a second lieutenant in the **Texas Air National Guard**, serving as a pilot of an F-102 Delta Dagger interceptor. His service—later scrutinized amid the broader controversy over draft deferments during the Vietnam War—ended honorably in 1974. The National Guard route was a common alternative to active-duty service for many young men during the era of conscription.

## Early Career in Oil and Business

After military service, Bush returned to Texas in the mid-1970s, joining the **Arbusto Energy** oil exploration company in Midland, co-founded with friends from Yale. Although Arbusto faced financial difficulties during the 1970s energy downturn, Bush’s tenure coincided with the Carter administration’s deregulation of oil prices and OPEC shocks, offering lessons in market volatility and government policy’s impact on industry.

In 1984, Arbusto merged into **Spectrum 7**, where Bush became vice president. Two years later, in the context of President Ronald Reagan’s tax cuts and deregulatory agenda, Spectrum 7 merged with **Harken Energy**, and Bush joined its board. His business connections to oil markets and political fundraising foreshadowed his entry into electoral politics.

## Early Political Involvement

Bush’s political activism began with volunteer work for his father’s 1970 Senate campaign in Texas and as a campaign aide for **Presidential candidate Gerald Ford** in 1976. In 1979, he chaired the **Finance Committee** for Ronald Reagan’s primary campaign in Texas, raising substantial funds and building a network within the emerging conservative coalition. These roles leveraged his family name and business ties during an era when money and personal connections were increasingly central to Republican politics.

In 1978, Bush made an unsuccessful bid for the U.S. House of Representatives in Texas’s 19th congressional district. His campaign emphasized his military service, business experience, and commitment to conservative values—tax cuts, strong national defense, and anti-communism—reflecting the ideological realignment of the Republican Party under Reagan.

## Preparations for Public Office

By the early 1980s, Bush had relocated to Houston and engaged in civic institutions, serving on the boards of the **Houston Chronicle** and the **Texas Rangers Baseball Club**, and establishing the **George W. Bush Foundation** for community development. These roles enhanced his public visibility and administrative credentials as the Reagan Revolution reshaped American political landscapes.

Bush’s early life thus combined elite education, military service, business leadership, and nascent political engagement. These experiences—set against the context of Cold War tensions, civil-rights transformations, and Republican ascendancy—provided the foundation for his successful run for Governor of Texas in 1994 and, ultimately, his presidential campaigns in 2000 and 2004.
