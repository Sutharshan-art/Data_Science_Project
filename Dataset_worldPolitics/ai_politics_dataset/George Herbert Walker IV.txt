**George Herbert Walker IV** (born July 12, 1969) is an American financier and executive who has served as CEO and president of Neuberger Berman since 2018. A great‐grandson of George Herbert Walker, founder of G. H. Walker & Co., and a nephew of George H. W. Bush, Walker has combined a prominent family pedigree with a career at the intersection of global finance and public policy. His leadership in asset management and his participation in economic and civic organizations reflect broader trends in the role of private‐sector executives in shaping political and regulatory environments.

**Early Life and Education**
Walker was born in New York City to George Herbert Walker III and Carol Banta Walker. Raised in an environment steeped in both finance and public service, he attended Phillips Exeter Academy before matriculating at Princeton University, where he graduated magna cum laude with an A.B. in Politics in 1991. His senior thesis examined the influence of financial markets on U.S. foreign‐policy decision‐making. Walker then earned an M.B.A. from Harvard Business School in 1995, focusing on international finance and monetary economics.

**Career at Goldman Sachs**
Upon completing his M.B.A., Walker joined Goldman Sachs in New York as an analyst in the Fixed Income, Currency and Commodities division. He rose through the ranks, becoming a partner in 2003. In 2006 he was appointed President of Goldman Sachs International, overseeing the firm’s European and Middle Eastern operations based in London. During his tenure, the firm expanded its presence in emerging‐market sovereign debt and played a key role in structuring bailout financing for Ireland and Greece amid the euro‐zone debt crisis. Walker’s leadership in navigating regulatory reforms—such as the European Union’s Markets in Financial Instruments Directive (MiFID) and the United Kingdom’s ring‐fencing of wholesale and retail banking—demonstrated the growing influence of senior bankers on policymaking forums.

**Neuberger Berman Leadership**
In April 2018, Walker became CEO and President of Neuberger Berman, an independent, employee‐owned asset manager with approximately \$400 billion under management. He steered the firm through the transition after its acquisition by a consortium led by Berkshire Hathaway in 2019, emphasizing investment in technology, sustainable‐finance strategies, and diversity initiatives. Under his direction, Neuberger Berman launched several ESG‐focused funds and expanded its footprint in Asia, particularly in China’s onshore mutual‐fund market. Walker also championed partnerships with central banks and sovereign‐wealth funds on infrastructure financing, reflecting a convergence of public needs and private capital.

**Political and Civic Engagement**
Though not an elected official, Walker has played an active role in political and civic affairs. He has hosted bipartisan gatherings of financial‐sector leaders and regulators, serving on advisory councils to the Federal Reserve Bank of New York and the U.S. Treasury Department. As a member of the Council on Foreign Relations, he has authored reports on the implications of U.S. fiscal‐monetary policy coordination and on the resilience of global financial networks to cyberattacks. Walker’s contributions to Republican and Democratic candidates—particularly on issues of financial regulation and trade policy—illustrate a pragmatic approach that emphasizes stability and economic growth over strict partisanship.

Walker chairs the board of trustees of his alma mater’s School of Public and International Affairs and serves on the executive committee of the Partnership for New York City, an organization linking business leaders with municipal policy initiatives. He is a founding sponsor of the “Global Finance and Diplomacy Forum,” which convenes senior officials and private‐sector executives to discuss financing for climate change mitigation and pandemic preparedness.

**Family Legacy and Influence**
As a member of the Walker–Bush family, Walker has often been called upon to advise on the nexus between finance and government. He has spoken at events honoring the legacies of Presidents George H. W. Bush and George W. Bush, and his perspective on executive leadership has drawn on lessons from his forebears’ emphasis on public service. Yet his career reflects a shift in which financial executives wield direct influence on regulatory frameworks and international economic agreements—an evolution from earlier eras when policymaking was more squarely the province of government officials.

**Recognition and Honors**
Walker was named one of “America’s 50 Most Influential Business Leaders” by *Financial Times* in 2020. He has received honorary doctorate degrees from Princeton University and the University of Edinburgh for his contributions to global finance and his support for higher‐education access programs. In 2022, he was appointed to the U.S. Department of State’s Advisory Committee on International Economic Policy, underscoring his role in advising on transnational financial issues.

**Personal Life**
Walker resides in New York City with his wife, Elizabeth Stanton, and their three children. An amateur marathon runner and violinist, he is also active in philanthropic efforts supporting economic‐literacy education and the restoration of public libraries. His blend of financial acumen, civic engagement, and familial tradition positions George Herbert Walker IV as a leading figure in the contemporary convergence of business leadership and public policy.
