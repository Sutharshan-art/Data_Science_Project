**Margaret Beckett**

**Margaret Mary Beckett** (née Jackson; born 15 January 1943) is a British Labour Party politician who has served as the Member of Parliament (MP) for Derby South since 1983, having previously represented Lincoln from 1974 to 1979. She is the longest-serving female MP in the history of the House of Commons and has held a variety of senior positions in government and opposition over the course of a political career spanning five decades.

Beckett served in the Cabinets of both Tony Blair and Gordon Brown, notably as the first woman to hold the post of **Foreign Secretary** (2006–2007). She was also **Deputy Leader of the Labour Party** from 1992 to 1994 and served briefly as **acting Leader of the Labour Party** following the sudden death of John Smith in 1994. Her political career has been marked by involvement in foreign policy, trade, and environmental affairs, and she remains a significant figure in the history of post-war British politics.

---

### Early Life and Political Beginnings

Margaret Beckett was born in Ashton-under-Lyne, Lancashire, and educated at the Manchester High School for Girls and later at the University of Manchester Institute of Science and Technology, where she trained as a metallurgist. Before entering politics, she worked as a researcher at the University of Manchester and later as a microelectronics engineer.

Beckett became politically active in the Labour Party during the 1970s and was elected MP for Lincoln in the February 1974 general election. She was appointed Parliamentary Under-Secretary at the Department of Education and Science in 1976 under Prime Minister James Callaghan. After losing her seat in the 1979 general election, Beckett returned to Parliament in 1983, winning the newly created seat of Derby South.

---

### Rise Through Labour Ranks

During Labour's years in opposition throughout the 1980s and early 1990s, Beckett emerged as a key figure within the party. Known for her loyalty to the party leadership and pragmatic approach to policy, she held various frontbench positions under Neil Kinnock and later John Smith. In 1992, following Labour's fourth consecutive general election defeat, she was elected Deputy Leader of the Labour Party and served as **Shadow Leader of the House of Commons** and later **Shadow President of the Board of Trade**.

Following the death of John Smith in 1994, Beckett became **acting Leader of the Labour Party** and **Leader of the Opposition** until the election of Tony Blair. Although she contested the leadership, she was defeated by Blair and subsequently returned to the front bench in senior roles.

---

### Cabinet Roles Under Blair and Brown

With Labour’s landslide victory in the 1997 general election, Beckett was appointed **President of the Board of Trade and Secretary of State for Trade and Industry**, becoming the first woman to hold the post. Her tenure focused on industrial competitiveness, regulatory reform, and efforts to modernize British industry during the early years of New Labour’s governance.

In 1998, she was moved to the role of **Leader of the House of Commons**, where she oversaw parliamentary procedure and reforms. In 2001, she became **Secretary of State for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs**, a position she held for five years, managing crises such as the outbreak of foot-and-mouth disease and working on climate change and rural development policies.

In 2006, Beckett was appointed **Foreign Secretary** in Tony Blair’s final Cabinet reshuffle, becoming the first woman to serve in that role. Her tenure coincided with complex international issues including the aftermath of the Iraq War, tensions with Iran over its nuclear program, and Britain’s diplomatic engagement with the European Union. She also supported multilateralism and the use of soft power in British foreign policy.

Though respected for her competence and experience, Beckett’s appointment drew some criticism due to her lack of prior experience in foreign affairs. She was replaced in 2007 when Gordon Brown succeeded Blair as Prime Minister.

---

### Later Political Career

Beckett continued to serve on the backbenches and held roles in the Labour opposition after 2010. In 2009, she was appointed to conduct a review of Labour’s election campaign strategy and remained involved in policy discussions during the leadership of Ed Miliband. She also served on the **National Security Strategy Committee** and **Foreign Affairs Committee** in the House of Commons.

In 2015, Beckett announced she would not seek reelection after the next general election, but later reversed that decision and continued to represent Derby South. She was appointed Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire (DBE) in 2013 for her services to politics.

---

### Political Legacy and Significance

Margaret Beckett is widely regarded as a trailblazer for women in British politics. As the first woman to serve as Foreign Secretary and one of the longest-serving MPs, she has played a significant role in shaping post-war Labour policy across multiple domains, including trade, the environment, and international relations.

Her career reflects the evolution of the Labour Party from its traditional roots to the centrist New Labour era and beyond. Though never leader of the party, her influence on policy, party structure, and government administration has been enduring. Beckett remains a symbol of political resilience and institutional knowledge within British parliamentary democracy.
