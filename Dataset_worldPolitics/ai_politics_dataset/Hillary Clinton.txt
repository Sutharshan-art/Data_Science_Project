**Hillary Rodham Clinton**

Hillary Diane Rodham Clinton (née Rodham; born October 26, 1947) is an American politician, diplomat, lawyer, and author who has played a prominent role in U.S. public life for more than four decades. She served as First Lady of the United States (1993–2001), U.S. Senator from New York (2001–2009), and U.S. Secretary of State (2009–2013). She was the Democratic Party’s presidential nominee in 2016, becoming the first woman to be nominated for president by a major U.S. political party.

---

## Early Life and Education

Born in Chicago and raised in Park Ridge, Illinois, Hillary Rodham was the eldest of three children in a middle-class Methodist family. She graduated **summa cum laude** from Wellesley College in 1969, where her senior thesis examined community organizing on Chicago’s Southwest Side. She then attended Yale Law School, earning her J.D. in 1973. At Yale, she met fellow law student William Jefferson Clinton, whom she married in October 1975.

---

## Early Career and Arkansas Years

After law school, Clinton joined the faculty of the University of Arkansas School of Law, teaching civil rights and family law. She co-founded the Arkansas Advocates for Children and Families, expanding legal services for low-income residents. In 1977, her husband was elected Governor of Arkansas, and she became an active partner in his administration. She served on the board of the Arkansas Children’s Hospital and led a commission on health care reform. Her policy work in the state bolstered the Clinton administration’s emphasis on centrist “Third Way” governance.

---

## First Lady of the United States (1993–2001)

As First Lady, Clinton took an unusually active policy role. In 1993 she was named chair of the Task Force on National Health Care Reform, which proposed universal coverage through an employer-mandated insurance model. Although the plan failed to pass Congress, her leadership established a new precedent for First Lady involvement in substantive policy. Throughout the 1990s she championed welfare reform, children’s health insurance, and global women’s rights initiatives. She also traveled extensively as an envoy for the administration, visiting 79 countries and advocating for humanitarian causes.

---

## U.S. Senate (2001–2009)

In 2000 Clinton won election to the U.S. Senate from New York, becoming the first former First Lady to hold elected office. She was reelected in 2006. Serving on the Senate Armed Services Committee, Finance Committee, and Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee, she was a strong supporter of the 2001 tax cuts but later backed the 2009 stimulus package. As a member of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, she supported military action in Afghanistan and, after the 2003 invasion of Iraq, voted to authorize continued troop presence while criticizing the Bush administration’s planning and execution.

---

## Secretary of State (2009–2013)

President Barack Obama appointed Clinton Secretary of State in January 2009. She led efforts to “reset” relations with Russia, secured United Nations sanctions against Iran over its nuclear program, and helped negotiate the New START treaty to reduce nuclear arsenals. Clinton oversaw the diplomatic response to the Arab Spring revolutions in Egypt, Libya, and Tunisia, supporting multilateral intervention in Libya to prevent mass atrocities. She also championed the “pivot to Asia” strategy, emphasizing expanded alliances and trade in the Asia-Pacific region. Clinton stepped down in February 2013 with high approval ratings for her tenure.

---

## Presidential Campaigns

Clinton made her first presidential bid in 2008, winning the New Hampshire primary but ultimately conceding the Democratic nomination to Barack Obama. She remained a leading figure in the party and was named Secretary of State in the Obama administration. In 2016 she became the Democratic nominee, campaigning on experience, economic fairness, and national security. She won the popular vote by nearly three million ballots but lost the Electoral College to Donald Trump. Her defeat prompted widespread analysis of campaign strategy, voter turnout, and the role of social media and foreign influence in U.S. elections.

---

## Policy Positions and Political Context

Clinton’s political stance has evolved over time. Early in her Senate career she supported free-trade agreements and tougher immigration enforcement; by 2016 she embraced comprehensive immigration reform, expanded background checks for firearms, and measures to reduce student-loan debt. She has advocated for paid family leave, affordable child care, and raising the federal minimum wage. On foreign policy, she combines interventionist credentials with support for diplomacy and multilateral institutions. Her career spans the end of the Cold War, the post-9/11 conflicts in Afghanistan and Iraq, the 2008 financial crisis, and the rise of populist movements, positioning her as a central figure in debates over globalization, trade, and American leadership abroad.

---

## Legacy and Continuing Influence

Clinton has authored numerous books—including memoirs, policy essays, and works on global women’s issues—and founded the Hillary Rodham Clinton Institute for Women, Peace, and Security at Georgetown University. She remains a sought-after public speaker and influential voice within the Democratic Party. Her career has broken multiple glass ceilings in American politics and reshaped expectations for the role of women in public office. Despite the controversies surrounding her email practices and the Clinton Foundation, she retains a significant base of support and continues to influence policy discussions on health care, equality, and international affairs.
