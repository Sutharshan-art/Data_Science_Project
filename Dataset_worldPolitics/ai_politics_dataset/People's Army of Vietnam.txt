**People's Army of Vietnam**

The **People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN)**, also known as the **Vietnamese People's Army (VPA)**, is the military force of the **Socialist Republic of Vietnam** and the armed wing of the **Communist Party of Vietnam (CPV)**. It was officially established on December 22, 1944, during the anti-colonial resistance against French rule, and has since played a central role in Vietnam’s modern history, particularly during the First Indochina War, the Vietnam War, and subsequent regional conflicts. The PAVN is tasked with defending Vietnam’s sovereignty and maintaining internal political stability, operating under the command of the Central Military Commission of the CPV.

---

### Origins and Founding

The People's Army of Vietnam traces its origins to the **Vietnamese Liberation Army**, formed in 1944 by revolutionary forces led by **Ho Chi Minh** and **General Võ Nguyên Giáp**. Initially composed of only a few dozen members, it emerged as the military force of the **Viet Minh**, a nationalist and communist-led movement resisting French colonial rule in Indochina.

The founding of the PAVN is celebrated annually on **December 22**, commemorating its role in the anti-colonial struggle. Its establishment reflected the CPV’s strategy of combining political mobilization with armed resistance in pursuit of national liberation and socialism.

---

### Role in the First Indochina War (1946–1954)

Following Japan’s defeat in World War II and the return of French colonial forces to Vietnam, the PAVN became the primary military force of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in its war against France. The **First Indochina War** lasted from 1946 to 1954, culminating in the decisive **Battle of Điện Biên Phủ**, where the PAVN inflicted a major defeat on French forces.

This victory led to the **Geneva Accords** of 1954, which temporarily divided Vietnam at the 17th parallel, with the PAVN controlling the North and the **Army of the Republic of Vietnam (ARVN)** established in the South. The PAVN’s success in this conflict established its reputation as an effective guerrilla and conventional military force and solidified its position as the military arm of the communist-led North Vietnamese state.

---

### Role in the Vietnam War (1955–1975)

The PAVN played a central role in the **Vietnam War**, also known as the **Second Indochina War**, which pitted North Vietnam and its communist allies—including the **National Front for the Liberation of South Vietnam (Viet Cong)**—against South Vietnam and its principal backer, the United States.

Throughout the conflict, the PAVN conducted operations across the demilitarized zone (DMZ), through the **Ho Chi Minh Trail**, and in coordination with the Viet Cong in the South. It employed both guerrilla warfare and conventional military strategies, engaging in major battles such as the **Tet Offensive (1968)** and the **Easter Offensive (1972)**.

The final **Spring Offensive** in 1975 led to the fall of Saigon and the reunification of Vietnam under communist control. The PAVN’s role in the war was critical to the CPV’s victory and the end of U.S. involvement in Southeast Asia.

---

### Post-1975 Conflicts and Regional Engagement

After reunification, the PAVN was involved in several major military engagements in the late 1970s and 1980s. In 1978, it invaded **Cambodia** to oust the **Khmer Rouge** regime, leading to a prolonged occupation and the establishment of a pro-Vietnamese government in Phnom Penh. This action was justified by Vietnam as a humanitarian intervention but led to international condemnation and a **border war with China** in 1979.

The brief but intense **Sino-Vietnamese War** further demonstrated the PAVN’s capabilities, although both sides claimed victory. The PAVN also faced insurgencies and border tensions throughout the 1980s, gradually shifting toward peacetime roles in the 1990s after Vietnam’s withdrawal from Cambodia and normalization of international relations.

---

### Structure and Political Role

The PAVN is subordinate to the **Communist Party of Vietnam**, with its top leadership integrated into the **Central Military Commission**, a powerful body within the CPV. The army is organized into regular units, regional commands, and specialized forces, including the **People's Navy**, **Air Force**, and **Border Defense Force**.

Political indoctrination, loyalty to the party, and ideological training remain key components of military service. The PAVN is also involved in civil development projects, disaster relief, and economic enterprises, reflecting its dual military-civil role in a socialist state.

---

### Modernization and Current Role

In recent decades, the PAVN has focused on **modernization** and professionalization. While still maintaining large ground forces, the emphasis has shifted to technological upgrades, particularly in the **naval and air sectors**, as Vietnam navigates complex territorial disputes in the **South China Sea**.

Vietnam’s military doctrine continues to prioritize defense and deterrence, with an emphasis on “people’s war” strategy—mobilizing the population in the event of foreign aggression. Although conscription remains in place, Vietnam has also invested in a professional officer corps and international military cooperation, including participation in **UN peacekeeping missions**.

---

### Conclusion

The People's Army of Vietnam has played a decisive role in the country's modern history, from anti-colonial struggles to regional conflicts and postwar reconstruction. As both a military and political institution, it has been central to the **Communist Party of Vietnam’s** authority and national strategy. Today, the PAVN remains a key pillar of Vietnamese state power, navigating evolving security challenges while maintaining its historic legacy as a revolutionary armed force.
