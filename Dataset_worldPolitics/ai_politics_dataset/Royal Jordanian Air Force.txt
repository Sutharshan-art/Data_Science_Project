**Royal Jordanian Air Force**

**Overview**
The Royal Jordanian Air Force (RJAF; Arabic: سلاح الجو الملكي الأردني‎, Ṣilāḥ al-Jaww al-Malakī al-ʾUrdunī) is the aerial warfare branch of the Jordanian Armed Forces. Established in 1948, the RJAF plays a key role in Jordan’s national defense, border security, and regional stability. It operates combat, transport, training, and reconnaissance aircraft, and contributes to multinational operations under the auspices of the Arab League and coalition partners.

**Historical Background**

* **Formation (1948–1950s):** In the aftermath of the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, the Emirate of Transjordan recognized the need for an air arm. With British assistance, it formed the “Arab Legion Air Force” in 1948, initially equipped with a handful of de Havilland Doves and Westland Lysanders for reconnaissance and liaison duties. In 1951, following Transjordan’s transition to the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan, the force was renamed the Royal Jordanian Air Force.
* **Cold War Expansion (1950s–1970s):** Amid regional tensions and pan-Arab conflicts, the RJAF expanded under U.S. and British assistance. It acquired Hawker Hunters, F-104 Starfighters, and Lockheed T-33 trainers. The force saw combat readiness during the Suez Crisis (1956) and provided air defense during the 1967 Six-Day War, though Jordan lost much of its operational strength following the defeat.
* **Modernization (1970s–1990s):** In response to the Black September conflict of 1970 and the 1973 Yom Kippur War, Jordan sought advanced aircraft. The RJAF introduced Northrop F-5 Freedom Fighters in the 1970s and McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom IIs in the 1980s. It also developed indigenous maintenance capabilities and established advanced pilot training programs in collaboration with the United States.

**Organizational Structure**

* **Command and Control:** The RJAF is headed by a Commander of the Air Force, subordinate to the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. It is organized into air wings, each comprising several squadrons.
* **Wings and Squadrons:** Main operational formations include the 1st Tactical Fighter Wing (F-16s), 2nd Fighter Wing (F-16s and F-5s), and the 10th Transport Wing (C-130 Hercules, CASA CN-235). A Helicopter Wing operates utility and attack helicopters such as UH-60 Black Hawks and AH-1 Cobras.
* **Training and Support:** The King Hussein Air College at Mafraq oversees basic and advanced pilot training. The Air Defense Directorate, operating surface-to-air missile systems and radar installations, falls under the Air Force’s umbrella.

**Equipment and Capabilities**

* **Combat Aircraft:** The RJAF’s combat fleet centers on General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcons, acquired under successive Foreign Military Sales (FMS) programs since 1997. Earlier platforms, like the F-104 and F-4, were retired by 2013. A modern radar and avionics upgrade program (Mid-Life Upgrade) has enhanced air-to-air and precision-strike capabilities.
* **Transport and Support:** C-130H Hercules and C-130J-30 aircraft provide strategic and tactical airlift. CASA CN-235 transports support paratroop operations and aerial surveillance. A fleet of Pilatus PC-21 and Slingsby T-67 Firefly trainers underpins pilot development.
* **Helicopters and UAVs:** The helicopter fleet includes UH-60L Black Hawks for utility and medevac roles and AH-1F Cobras for close air support. The RJAF has integrated unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) such as the Selex Falco and locally procured Hermes 450s for reconnaissance and border monitoring.

**Operational History**

* **Border Security and Counterinsurgency:** The RJAF has conducted air patrols along the Jordan–Syria and Jordan–Iraq borders, supporting ground forces against smuggling and militant incursions.
* **Coalition Participation:** Jordanian F-16s participated in the 1991 Gulf War air campaign under Operation Desert Storm, flying over 1,000 sorties targeting Iraqi Republican Guard positions. In the Global War on Terror, the RJAF has contributed reconnaissance and training missions against ISIS under Operation Inherent Resolve.
* **Humanitarian and Disaster Relief:** The transport fleet supports regional relief efforts, including earthquake response in Turkey (1999) and humanitarian airdrops during the Syrian refugee crisis.

**Political and Strategic Context**

* **Regional Stability:** Positioned at the crossroads of the Middle East, Jordan relies on the RJAF to guard its borders against spillover from Syrian and Iraqi conflicts. The Air Force’s capabilities bolster Jordan’s deterrent posture and reassure Gulf and Western partners of its contribution to coalition security.
* **Foreign Partnerships:** The United States, United Kingdom, and other NATO allies provide training, spare parts, and joint exercises (e.g., Eager Lion, Falcon Strike). These partnerships aim to enhance interoperability and foster military-to-military ties.
* **Domestic Significance:** As one of the largest branches of the Jordanian Armed Forces, the RJAF offers career opportunities and contributes to national unity. It also symbolizes Jordan’s modernizing aspirations under successive monarchs.

**Future Developments**
Jordan has expressed interest in acquiring fifth-generation fighters, advanced UAVs, and airborne early warning systems to address evolving threats. Modernization plans include potential procurement of F-35 Lightning II aircraft and expansion of the unmanned fleet. Investments in missile defense, network-centric operations, and infrastructure upgrades at King Hussein Air Base will shape the RJAF’s future capabilities.
