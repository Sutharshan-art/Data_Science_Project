**List of Military Operations**

A military operation is a coordinated action or series of actions undertaken by armed forces to achieve strategic or tactical objectives. Operations are typically assigned code names and may involve land, sea, air, space, or cyber domains. Below is a selection of notable operations organized by historical period, each reflecting the political imperatives and strategic contexts of its time.

---

## World War II (1939–1945)

* **Operation Fall Weiss (1939)**
  Germany’s invasion of Poland that triggered World War II, conceived to secure the eastern frontier and destroy Polish military resistance swiftly.

* **Operation Barbarossa (1941)**
  The German–Axis invasion of the Soviet Union, launched to eliminate Soviet power and gain strategic depth and resources, ultimately failing at Moscow.

* **Operation Torch (1942)**
  Allied landings in French North Africa, aimed at opening a second front against Axis forces and relieving pressure on the Soviet Union.

* **Operation Overlord (1944)**
  The Allied invasion of German-occupied Western Europe, beginning with D-Day landings in Normandy, designed to break Hitler’s Atlantic Wall and liberate France.

* **Operation Market Garden (1944)**
  An ambitious Allied airborne assault in the Netherlands intended to outflank the Siegfried Line; logistical challenges and fierce German resistance led to its partial failure.

---

## Early Cold War and Decolonization (1945–1960)

* **Operation Paperclip (1945–1959)**
  U.S. program to recruit German scientists (notably Wernher von Braun) for American missile and space projects, reflecting the emerging U.S.–Soviet technological rivalry.

* **Operation Ajax (1953)**
  Joint British–American covert operation to overthrow Iran’s Prime Minister Mohammad Mossadegh, reinstating the Shah and reshaping Middle East geopolitics.

* **Operation Vanguard (1956)**
  British and French intervention in Egypt alongside Israel following Nasser’s nationalization of the Suez Canal; ultimately curtailed by U.S. and Soviet diplomatic pressure.

---

## Vietnam and Regional Conflicts (1960–1980)

* **Operation Rolling Thunder (1965–1968)**
  Sustained U.S. bombing of North Vietnam aimed at diminishing its capacity to support the Viet Cong; its limited success underscored challenges of airpower in insurgency.

* **Operation Linebacker II (1972)**
  Intense U.S. “Christmas bombing” of North Vietnamese targets to compel Hanoi back to Paris peace talks, demonstrating air campaign escalation’s political leverage.

* **Operation Frequent Wind (1975)**
  Final phase of U.S. evacuation from Saigon as North Vietnamese forces closed in, emblematic of the Vietnam War’s ignominious end for the United States.

---

## Gulf Conflicts and Peace-Enforcement (1990–2001)

* **Operation Desert Shield (1990–1991)**
  Coalition buildup in Saudi Arabia to deter further Iraqi advances after Kuwait’s invasion, laying groundwork for offensive operations.

* **Operation Desert Storm (1991)**
  Allied air and ground offensive that liberated Kuwait, marking a new era of precision-guided munitions and coalition warfare under UN mandate.

* **Operation Restore Hope (1992–1993)**
  U.S.-led humanitarian mission in Somalia to secure relief corridors during civil war, later shifting to peace-enforcement amid local factionalism.

---

## Global War on Terror (2001–2021)

* **Operation Enduring Freedom (2001–2014)**
  U.S.-led invasion of Afghanistan to dismantle al-Qaeda and remove the Taliban from power, initiating the longest American war in history.

* **Operation Iraqi Freedom (2003–2011)**
  Coalition invasion aimed at toppling Saddam Hussein’s regime and eliminating alleged weapons of mass destruction; its aftermath reshaped Middle East security.

* **Operation Inherent Resolve (2014–present)**
  International campaign against ISIS in Iraq and Syria, combining airstrikes, special forces, and local partner training to reclaim territory and degrade the group.

---

## Peacekeeping and Humanitarian Interventions (1990–present)

* **Operation Uphold Democracy (1994–1995)**
  U.S.-led effort in Haiti to restore elected President Jean-Bertrand Aristide, illustrating post–Cold War emphasis on democratic stabilization.

* **Operation Unified Protector (2011)**
  NATO enforcement of a UN no-fly zone and arms embargo over Libya, contributing to the downfall of Muammar Gaddafi and raising debates over humanitarian intervention.

* **Operation Tomodachi (2011)**
  U.S. humanitarian assistance and disaster relief in Japan following the Tōhoku earthquake, tsunami, and nuclear crisis, underscoring military roles in disaster response.

---

## Contemporary and Future Operations

* **Operation Atlantic Resolve (2014–present)**
  U.S. rotational deployments to Eastern Europe reassuring NATO allies after Russia’s annexation of Crimea, reflecting renewed great-power tensions.

* **Operation Peace Spring (2019)**
  Turkish incursion into northeastern Syria against Kurdish forces, illustrating the complexity of coalition counter-terrorism and shifting alliances in the Syrian conflict.

---

**Political and Historical Significance**

Military operations are shaped by their geopolitical contexts:

1. **Great Power Competition:** World War II and Cold War operations reflect competing ideologies and the strategic imperatives of industrialized total war and nuclear brinkmanship.
2. **Decolonization and Proxy Wars:** Mid-twentieth-century conflicts (Korea, Vietnam) illustrate superpower engagements through local actors, foreshadowing modern insurgency warfare.
3. **Coalition Warfare under International Law:** Post-1990 interventions under UN or NATO mandates demonstrate evolving norms of collective security and humanitarian intervention.
4. **Non-Combat Roles:** Humanitarian and disaster-relief operations highlight militaries’ adaptive roles in soft-power projection and civil-military cooperation.

This **List of military operations** underscores how named campaigns serve both strategic ends and political narratives, framing public memory of conflict and informing doctrine for future challenges.
