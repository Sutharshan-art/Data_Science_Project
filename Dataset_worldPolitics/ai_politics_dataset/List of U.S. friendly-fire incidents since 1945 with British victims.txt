**List of U.S. Friendly-Fire Incidents Since 1945 with British Victims**

The **list of U.S. friendly-fire incidents since 1945 with British victims** comprises documented cases where British military personnel were unintentionally harmed or killed by United States armed forces during joint military operations or while operating in proximity. These incidents occurred during various multinational engagements, often involving NATO or coalition forces, and have sometimes resulted in diplomatic strain, military inquiries, and calls for improved coordination and interoperability.

Friendly-fire, or “blue-on-blue” incidents, are unintended engagements between allied or same-side forces and typically stem from misidentification, communication breakdowns, equipment failure, or operational confusion. While such incidents are statistically rare relative to the scale of modern warfare, their consequences—especially when involving high-profile allies like the United Kingdom and the United States—carry significant political and military implications.

---

### Historical and Political Context

Since the end of World War II, the United States and the United Kingdom have maintained a close military alliance, participating together in major conflicts including the Korean War, the Gulf War, operations in the Balkans, the Afghanistan War, and the Iraq War. Their strategic partnership, often described as the “special relationship,” includes extensive intelligence-sharing, joint training exercises, and coalition warfare.

Despite shared goals and integrated command structures, joint operations pose inherent risks, particularly in fast-paced combat zones with multiple national contingents. Technological disparities, differing rules of engagement, and human error can lead to tragic misidentifications. These incidents have at times sparked public outcry in the UK, prompted diplomatic protests, and led to joint investigations intended to prevent future occurrences.

### Notable Incidents

#### **Korean War (1950–1953)**

* **Date:** March 1951
* **Location:** Near Hill 187, Korea
* **Incident:** U.S. air support mistakenly attacked British Commonwealth forces, resulting in multiple injuries and casualties.
* **Context:** The rugged terrain and poorly coordinated air-ground communication contributed to this early Cold War friendly-fire incident.

#### **Gulf War (1991)**

* **Date:** February 26, 1991
* **Location:** Highway near the Kuwait-Iraq border
* **Incident:** A U.S. A-10 Thunderbolt II aircraft mistakenly fired on two British Scimitar armored vehicles from the Blues and Royals regiment, killing nine soldiers and injuring others.
* **Aftermath:** One of the most infamous friendly-fire episodes involving British troops, this incident led to significant criticism in the UK and a review of identification systems and coordination between coalition forces.

#### **Afghanistan War (2001–2021)**

* **Incident 1**

  * **Date:** September 2006
  * **Location:** Helmand Province
  * **Details:** A U.S. warplane mistakenly bombed British Royal Marine positions, killing one soldier and injuring several others.
  * **Response:** A joint inquiry highlighted challenges in real-time battlefield coordination and communication systems.

* **Incident 2**

  * **Date:** December 2007
  * **Location:** Southern Afghanistan
  * **Details:** U.S. forces mistakenly identified a British unit as hostile during a nighttime operation. Two British soldiers were killed.
  * **Aftermath:** Investigations focused on discrepancies in the reporting and tracking of friendly units.

#### **Iraq War (2003–2011)**

* **Incident 1**

  * **Date:** March 28, 2003
  * **Location:** Near Basra, Iraq
  * **Incident:** Lance-Corporal of Horse Matty Hull was killed when two U.S. A-10 aircraft attacked a British armored column.
  * **Repercussions:** The incident became widely known after the release of cockpit video footage. It prompted public outrage in the UK and led to increased scrutiny over U.S. engagement protocols and the mechanisms of military transparency.

* **Incident 2**

  * **Date:** April 6, 2003
  * **Location:** Near the town of Az Zubayr
  * **Details:** A British patrol was mistakenly engaged by U.S. forces, resulting in one fatality and multiple injuries.
  * **Outcome:** NATO forces reviewed identification and coordination procedures in mixed-nationality operational areas.

### Investigations and Protocol Reforms

In response to these incidents, both the United States and the United Kingdom have undertaken joint military reviews to improve identification technologies, real-time communication, and situational awareness. Notable measures include:

* **Improved Combat Identification Systems (CIDS)**: Including infrared markers, GPS integration, and blue force tracking.
* **Joint Training Exercises**: To simulate multinational coordination under combat conditions.
* **Memoranda of Understanding (MOUs)**: Establishing clearer protocols for air-ground coordination and incident response.

Additionally, legal frameworks for investigating and disclosing friendly-fire incidents have been a point of contention, particularly when families of victims seek greater transparency or redress.

---

### Political and Public Reaction

While military officials from both nations have typically described these events as tragic but unintentional, the political and emotional consequences have often been significant. British lawmakers have, at times, demanded formal apologies, compensation, or greater accountability from the U.S. government. These incidents have occasionally strained relations, particularly when perceived lapses in transparency or justice arise.

The media coverage of friendly-fire fatalities—such as in the case of Matty Hull—has played a major role in shaping public perception, often highlighting the dangers of modern coalition warfare and the need for continuous improvement in allied military coordination.

### Conclusion

The **list of U.S. friendly-fire incidents since 1945 with British victims** underscores the complexities and risks inherent in multinational military operations. While rare in relation to the overall scope of joint military engagements, these incidents have prompted enduring efforts to enhance interoperability, reduce miscommunication, and honor the sacrifices of those affected. They remain a sobering reminder of the challenges faced in coalition warfare, even among the closest of allies.
