**Introduction**
The 2023 Ecuadorian general election was a snap election held on 20 August 2023 to elect the President of Ecuador and all 137 members of the National Assembly. The election was precipitated by President Guillermo Lasso’s invocation of the constitutional “muerte cruzada” mechanism on 17 May 2023, which dissolved the National Assembly amid an impeachment threat and triggered new elections for both executive and legislative branches.

**Political Context and Crisis**
In early 2023, Ecuador faced mounting political instability. The opposition-controlled National Assembly initiated impeachment proceedings against President Lasso, accusing him of mismanagement and alleged links to corruption. Facing the prospect of removal, Lasso invoked Article 148(7) of the 2008 Constitution—known colloquially as “muerte cruzada”—to dissolve the legislature and call simultaneous presidential and legislative elections within six months. He also pledged not to seek re-election, framing his decision as a necessary step to restore stability and public confidence in governance.

**Security Environment and Candidate Assassination**
The campaign unfolded against a backdrop of escalating organized-crime violence driven by transnational drug cartels. On 9 August 2023, anti-corruption candidate Fernando Villavicencio was assassinated after a rally in Quito, prompting the government to impose a 60-day state of emergency and deploy the military to support policing efforts. Villavicencio had campaigned on exposing political-cartel connections, and his murder underscored both the personal risk faced by reformist candidates and the profound security challenges confronting the country.

**Electoral System under “Muerte Cruzada”**
Under the “muerte cruzada” provisions, snap elections were scheduled for 20 August 2023. The President is elected by popular vote, requiring either an absolute majority of valid votes or at least 40 percent with a 10 percent lead to win outright; otherwise, a runoff is held between the top two candidates. The National Assembly’s 137 members are chosen by closed-list proportional representation in multi-member districts corresponding to Ecuador’s 24 provinces, with seats apportioned based on population.

**Presidential Campaign and Major Contenders**
The field of eight presidential contenders was confirmed by the National Electoral Council in mid-July 2023. Key candidates included:

* **Luisa González** (Movimiento Construye): A former assemblywoman backed by the Citizen Revolution Movement and allies of ex-President Rafael Correa. She campaigned on expanding social-welfare programs, reversing austerity measures, and strengthening state oversight of strategic sectors.
* **Daniel Noboa** (Acción Democrática Nacional): A 35-year-old businessman and sitting assembly deputy whose late surge in opinion polls was attributed to a platform emphasizing private-sector growth, job creation, and an uncompromising “war on crime.”
* **Otto Sonnenholzner** (Avanza–SUMA): The former vice-president, running on a centrist ticket that prioritized economic liberalization and continuity of certain social programs.
* **Xavier Hervas** (RETÓ): A progressive independent and tech entrepreneur advocating for transparency, environmental protections, and participatory democracy.

Campaign debates focused heavily on security policy, economic recovery, corruption control, and the role of the state in providing social services. The assassination of Villavicencio intensified voters’ security concerns and shifted the discourse toward law-and-order proposals.

**First Round Results**
On 20 August, voter turnout reached 82.9 percent. No candidate met the requirements for an outright victory: Luisa González led with approximately 33 percent of the vote, followed by Daniel Noboa with around 24 percent. González’s strength was anchored in traditional left-leaning strongholds and urban poor districts, while Noboa drew support from middle-class and business-oriented voters concerned about security and economic stagnation.

**Runoff and Final Outcome**
A runoff was held on 15 October 2023, with turnout remaining high at 82.4 percent. Daniel Noboa won the presidency with 51.8 percent of valid votes against González’s 48.2 percent. Noboa’s campaign promises—such as deploying naval vessels as floating prisons, expanding military participation in policing, and offering fiscal incentives to spur private investment—resonated with voters seeking a decisive break from the political gridlock and rising crime rates of preceding years.

**Legislative Elections**
Simultaneously, the National Assembly elections produced a fragmented legislature. The Citizen Revolution bloc emerged as the largest single faction with 52 seats but lacked a majority. Movimiento Construye secured 29 seats, while Noboa’s Acción Democrática Nacional won 15. Various smaller parties and alliances filled the remaining seats, necessitating coalition-building to pass key legislation. The composition reflected regional and ideological cleavages, with rural-based parties and indigenous movements holding pivotal swing votes.

**Post-Election Dynamics and Significance**
Noboa’s victory was interpreted as a public endorsement of pro-business and security-focused policies after years of political deadlock and criminal violence. However, his narrow margin and the divided Assembly presented immediate challenges: forging alliances to enact reforms, navigating ongoing judicial investigations into corruption, and containing the influence of cartels. International observers noted that Ecuador’s institutions had withstood the strain of a simultaneous executive-legislative election amid a security crisis, underscoring both the resilience and the vulnerabilities of its democratic framework.

**Legacy and Outlook**
The 2023 snap election demonstrated the utility—and risks—of constitutional mechanisms like “muerte cruzada” in resolving institutional standoffs. It highlighted the extent to which criminal violence can disrupt electoral politics and the personal safety of candidates. As Daniel Noboa prepared to assume office on 25 November 2023, his administration faced the twin imperatives of restoring public security and stimulating economic growth, all while negotiating a complex multiparty legislature. The election thus stands as a defining moment in Ecuador’s recent history, illustrating the interplay of constitutional innovation, political crisis, and the persistent challenge of governance in the face of non-state violence.
