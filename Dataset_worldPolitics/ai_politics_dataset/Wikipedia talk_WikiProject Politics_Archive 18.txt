**Wikipedia talk\:WikiProject Politics/Archive 18**

**Overview**
“Wikipedia talk\:WikiProject Politics/Archive 18” is the eighteenth archived discussion page for WikiProject Politics on the English Wikipedia, preserving community deliberations and policy development conducted on the project’s main talk page between **January 1 and March 31, 2021**. WikiProject Politics is the collaborative effort responsible for improving the quality of political content—from biographies of officeholders to analyses of public policy—on Wikipedia. Archive 18 captures a three-month snapshot of discussions reflecting both the internal governance of the project and its engagement with contemporaneous global political events.

**Purpose of the Archive**
As part of Wikipedia’s archival system, “Archive 18” serves to:

1. **Preserve Historical Record:** Maintain a permanent record of consensus decisions, policy proposals, and task coordination.
2. **Improve Usability:** Streamline the live “Wikipedia talk\:WikiProject Politics” page by moving older threads to the archive, preventing menu clutter and improving navigability.
3. **Facilitate Research:** Provide researchers and new project members with access to past discussions on political content standards, article assessments, and event coverage.

**Organizational Structure**
Archive 18 retains the structure of the active talk page under topical headings. These include:

* **Project News and Announcements:** Summaries of editorial milestones and staffing changes.
* **Policy Proposals:** Drafts and debates on refining sourcing guidelines for politically sensitive topics.
* **Article Assessments:** Nominations for Good Article (GA) and Featured Article (FA) status in areas such as coronavirus pandemic policy and United States presidential transition.
* **Current Events Coordination:** Task forces formed to manage coverage of the U.S. Capitol riot (January 6 2021), the second impeachment of U.S. President Donald Trump, and rollout of COVID-19 vaccination programs worldwide.
* **Cross-Project Collaboration:** Discussions with WikiProject Medicine on COVID-19 content accuracy and with WikiProject Biography on BLP (Biographies of Living Persons) compliance during rapidly unfolding developments.

Each thread includes timestamps and signed comments, preserving the identity and rationale of participants.

**Key Discussions and Decisions**

1. **U.S. Capitol Riot Coverage:**

   * **Scope and Naming Conventions:** Debates centered on article titles—whether to use “2021 United States Capitol attack” versus “capitol riot”—to maintain neutrality and consistency with other political violence articles.
   * **Source Reliability:** Editors evaluated which mainstream media and government reports met WP\:RS (Reliable Sources) criteria for documenting the event.
   * **Image Use Policy:** Discussions on fair use of photographs depicting unrest within the Capitol, balancing encyclopedic value against privacy and copyright concerns.

2. **Second Impeachment of Donald Trump:**

   * **Article Organization:** Consensus on structuring the “Second impeachment of Donald Trump” article into sections on House proceedings, Senate trial, and public reaction.
   * **Template Updates:** Suggestions to modify the political-event infobox template to accommodate multiple impeachment events in a single presidency.

3. **COVID-19 Vaccination Program Coverage:**

   * **Global Coordination:** Task forces were formed to assign regional editors to maintain articles on vaccination rollout by country, ensuring up-to-date statistics and logistic overviews.
   * **Vaccine Policy Comparisons:** Debates arose over synthesizing public policy approaches—such as vaccine mandates and emergency use authorizations—while adhering strictly to WP\:STRUCTURE guidelines and avoiding original synthesis.

4. **Policy Refinements:**

   * **Sourcing Guidelines for Political Statements:** A proposal introduced enhanced guidance on attributing controversial political claims, requiring “headlining” according to WP\:VERIFY and avoiding undue weight.
   * **Conflict-of-Interest Disclosure:** Editors discussed extending the COI guideline (WP\:COI) to cover paid advocacy in think-tank reports cited in policy articles.

**Political Context**
Archive 18 reflects a period of intense political change and public health crisis:

* **U.S. Political Polarization:** The transition of presidential power in Washington, D.C., underscored deep partisan divides, influencing how editors negotiated neutral language in U.S. politics articles.
* **Global Pandemic Response:** National vaccination campaigns and debates over vaccine nationalism shaped the project’s collaboration with medical and policy-focused WikiProjects.
* **Rise of Disinformation Concerns:** Threads addressed the challenge of distinguishing verifiable policy analysis from propagandistic or fringe commentary, echoing broader societal efforts to combat misinformation.

These global events heightened the stakes of Wikipedia’s political coverage, as the encyclopedia sought to remain a trusted, neutral resource amid rapid news cycles.

**Significance and Legacy**
“Wikipedia talk\:WikiProject Politics/Archive 18” stands as a testament to Wikipedia’s capacity for self-governance in politically charged environments. By documenting how volunteer editors negotiated contentious topics—ranging from insurrection to pandemic policy—the archive provides insight into:

* **Consensus-Building Processes:** Demonstrating Wikipedia’s reliance on evidence-based discussion and policy invocation to resolve disputes.
* **Adaptability of Policies:** Showing how core Wikipedia policies (WP\:NPOV, WP\:VERIFY, WP\:BLP) were interpreted and refined in response to novel situations.
* **Community Resilience:** Highlighting the project’s ability to mobilize task forces and cross-project collaborations under tight deadlines and high public interest.

As future WikiProject Politics members consult Archive 18, they inherit institutional memory critical for navigating the ever-evolving intersection of online collaboration and real-world politics.
