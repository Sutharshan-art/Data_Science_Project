**Black January**

**Black January** (Azerbaijani: Qara Yanvar) refers to the violent crackdown that occurred on January 19–20, 1990, when Soviet troops entered Baku, the capital of Azerbaijan, and other parts of the country to suppress a growing independence movement. The intervention resulted in significant civilian casualties and became a pivotal event in Azerbaijan's struggle for independence from the Soviet Union. It remains a symbol of national resistance and is commemorated annually in Azerbaijan.

---

### Historical and Political Context

During the late 1980s, the Soviet Union experienced increasing nationalist and independence movements in its constituent republics. In Azerbaijan, tensions escalated amid ethnic conflicts, particularly relating to the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast, a predominantly Armenian region within Azerbaijan, which sought unification with Armenia. This dispute fueled interethnic violence and widespread unrest.

In the months leading up to January 1990, demonstrations and protests demanding greater sovereignty and an end to Soviet control gained momentum in Baku. The Soviet central government viewed these nationalist movements as threats to the unity of the USSR, particularly as the union was already facing multiple separatist pressures.

---

### Events of Black January

On the night of January 19–20, 1990, the Soviet government, under the leadership of Mikhail Gorbachev, authorized a military operation to restore order in Baku and suppress the Azerbaijani independence movement. Soviet troops and security forces entered the city, using tanks and armed personnel to disperse demonstrators and seize control of strategic locations.

The military intervention resulted in significant civilian casualties. Official Soviet figures reported dozens of deaths, while Azerbaijani sources estimate that over 130 civilians were killed, with many more injured and arrested. The troops’ actions included the use of live ammunition against unarmed protesters and widespread destruction of property.

---

### Aftermath and Impact

The events of Black January deeply shocked Azerbaijani society and galvanized public sentiment against Soviet rule. The brutality of the crackdown intensified demands for independence and united various political factions within Azerbaijan.

The incident is widely regarded as a turning point that accelerated Azerbaijan’s path toward sovereignty. In the months following Black January, Azerbaijani nationalist groups gained greater support, and calls for independence grew stronger.

On October 18, 1991, Azerbaijan formally declared independence from the Soviet Union, which was later recognized internationally after the USSR’s dissolution in December 1991.

---

### Political Significance

Black January exposed the Soviet government’s willingness to use military force against its own citizens to maintain control, undermining the legitimacy of Gorbachev’s reform policies. The crackdown strained relations between Azerbaijan and the central government, contributing to the broader unraveling of the Soviet Union.

The event also highlighted ethnic and national tensions in the Caucasus region, which later escalated into the First Nagorno-Karabakh War between Armenia and Azerbaijan.

---

### Commemoration and Legacy

Since Azerbaijan’s independence, January 20 has been officially observed as **National Mourning Day** (Qara Yanvar) to honor the victims of the crackdown. Memorials and ceremonies are held annually to remember those who lost their lives and to reaffirm the nation’s commitment to sovereignty and democratic values.

Black January remains a potent symbol of resistance against oppression and a foundational moment in modern Azerbaijani national identity.

---

### Conclusion

Black January represents a critical episode in the history of Azerbaijan and the collapse of the Soviet Union. The violent suppression of nationalist protests by Soviet forces in Baku underscored the tensions inherent in the Soviet system and accelerated Azerbaijan’s quest for independence. Its legacy endures as a somber reminder of the costs of political struggle and the resilience of national aspirations.
