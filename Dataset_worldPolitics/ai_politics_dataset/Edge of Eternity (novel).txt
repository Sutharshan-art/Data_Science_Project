**Edge of Eternity**

*Edge of Eternity* is a historical novel by British author Ken Follett, published in 2014. It is the third and final installment of the Century Trilogy, following *Fall of Giants* (2010) and *Winter of the World* (2012). Spanning the years 1961 to 2000, *Edge of Eternity* intertwines the lives of five interrelated families—American, German, Russian, English, and Welsh—against the backdrop of Cold War tensions, decolonization, and social upheaval.

## Historical and Political Context

The novel is set during some of the most tumultuous decades of the 20th century, and Follett uses his fictional characters to explore key political events and trends:

* **Cold War Confrontation (1961–1975)**

  * The construction of the Berlin Wall (1961) and the ensuing division of Germany into East and West set the stage for the novel’s opening chapters, in which Carla von Ulrich’s family flees East Berlin for the West.
  * The Cuban Missile Crisis (1962) underpins the growing nuclear standoff between the United States and the Soviet Union, shaping the worldview of American characters like George Jakes, a civil-rights activist turned Congressman.
  * Escalation of the Vietnam War (1964–1975) is depicted through George’s experiences in Washington, D.C., and his brother’s service as a U.S. Army officer, highlighting domestic protest movements and the war’s political repercussions.

* **Civil Rights and Social Change (1960s–1980s)**

  * George Jakes’s law practice is based on civil-rights cases in Mississippi, reflecting the struggle for African American voting rights and the passage of the Civil Rights Act (1964) and Voting Rights Act (1965).
  * Student protests of the 1960s, women’s liberation, and the emergence of counterculture influence characters such as Woody Hirsch and Reid O’Donnell, whose activism at Berkeley and anti-war demonstrations mirror real-world events at UC Berkeley and Columbia University.

* **Decolonization and Détente (1960s–1970s)**

  * The retreat of European powers from Africa and Asia is personified by Gus Dewar, an American diplomat posted in newly independent Uganda during Milton Obote’s regime (1962–1971). Dewar’s experiences underscore U.S. involvement in Cold War–era regime changes on the African continent.
  * Détente initiatives—such as the 1972 Strategic Arms Limitation Talks (SALT I) and President Nixon’s visit to China—provide a backdrop for characters engaged in diplomatic service in Moscow and Beijing, including Russian dissident Lev Peshkov’s brother, Vladimir.

* **Late Cold War and Collapse of the Soviet Bloc (1975–1991)**

  * The election of Jimmy Carter and the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan (1979) affect the careers of Washington insiders like George Jakes and Gus Dewar, illustrating the shift from détente to renewed superpower rivalry.
  * The ascendancy of Margaret Thatcher in the United Kingdom (1979) and Ronald Reagan in the United States (1981) is paralleled by the experiences of British characters such as Ben and Maud Cowell, who navigate the social and economic reforms of the Thatcher era.
  * Mikhail Gorbachev’s policies of *perestroika* and *glasnost* (mid-1980s) facilitate the defection of East German characters and the peaceful protests in Leipzig and Berlin that lead to the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989, depicted through Carla von Ulrich’s return to a reunifying Germany.

* **Post-Cold War Transition (1991–2000)**

  * The dissolution of the Soviet Union (1991) and the emergence of new democracies in Eastern Europe frame the final chapters, as Lev Peshkov assumes a role in the nascent Russian parliament and navigates the rise of oligarchic power.
  * The novel concludes with characters participating in or commenting on the Gulf War (1990–1991), the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA, 1994), and the dawn of the Internet age, reflecting broader trends of globalization and economic integration.

## Plot Overview

*Edge of Eternity* follows three generations of the five families as they intersect with historical events:

* **Carla von Ulrich** escapes East Berlin with her brother during the Wall’s construction and becomes involved in West German politics and commercial media.
* **George Jakes**, inspired by the 1963 March on Washington, rises from civil-rights lawyer to U.S. Congressman, multilateral diplomat, and presidential candidate.
* **Woody Hirsch**, a Jewish American student activist, publishes underground newspapers and later enters national politics in California.
* **Gus Dewar**, an American diplomat, witnesses coups and humanitarian crises in Africa, culminating in a senior State Department appointment.
* **Lev and Vladimir Peshkov**, brothers in the Soviet political system, diverge when Vladimir becomes a dissident, highlighting the spiritual and ethical costs of authoritarian rule.

Through alternating viewpoints, Follett weaves personal dramas—romance, betrayal, ambition—into the tapestry of global politics, illustrating how ordinary individuals both shape and are shaped by historical forces.

## Themes

* **Freedom versus Oppression**: The narrative contrasts authoritarian regimes (East Germany, the Soviet Union) with democratic societies (United States, West Germany), exploring the moral choices of characters on both sides of the Iron Curtain.
* **Power and Responsibility**: Several protagonists grapple with the ethical implications of wielding political power, whether legislating civil-rights laws or negotiating arms treaties.
* **Interconnectedness of History**: Follett emphasizes the global ripple effects of policy decisions—how U.S. intervention in Vietnam echoes in European student movements, and how Soviet reforms reverberate in African post-colonial politics.

## Reception and Impact

Critics praised *Edge of Eternity* for its ambitious scope and historical detail, though some noted its sprawling cast and occasional melodramatic episodes. The novel reached bestseller lists internationally and renewed public interest in late Cold War history. It has been used in academic courses as a supplementary text for understanding the era’s political dynamics, and Follett’s meticulous research garnered commendation from historians and political scientists alike for bringing complex geopolitical narratives to a broad readership.
