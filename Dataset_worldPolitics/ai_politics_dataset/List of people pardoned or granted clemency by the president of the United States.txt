**List of People Pardoned or Granted Clemency by the President of the United States**

The list of people pardoned or granted clemency by the president of the United States encompasses individuals who have received executive mercy in the form of a full pardon, commutation of sentence, remission of fines, or reprieve under the authority vested in the presidency by the U.S. Constitution. Article II, Section 2 grants the president the “Power to grant Reprieves and Pardons for Offenses against the United States, except in Cases of Impeachment.” This power applies exclusively to federal criminal convictions and does not extend to state-level offenses.

Presidential clemency has been used in a wide range of circumstances—from individual acts of mercy to sweeping political amnesties. The practice has evolved across administrations and is shaped by legal, humanitarian, political, and institutional considerations. While many clemency grants are routine and follow Department of Justice recommendations, others have been politically controversial and at times prompted congressional or public scrutiny.

### Types of Clemency

Presidential clemency can take several forms:

* **Pardon**: A full legal forgiveness of a federal crime, restoring civil rights and removing penalties.
* **Commutation**: A reduction of a sentence already imposed, without clearing the underlying conviction.
* **Reprieve**: A temporary delay in the imposition of a sentence, often used in capital cases.
* **Remission**: A cancellation or reduction of a fine or financial penalty.

The most common clemency actions involve pardons and commutations, and they may be issued at any time during or after a person’s conviction and sentencing.

### Early Use and Historical Precedents

The tradition of presidential pardons dates to George Washington, who granted the first pardons in 1795 to participants in the Whiskey Rebellion. Washington’s action set the precedent for using clemency to ease political tensions or promote national unity.

Thomas Jefferson issued pardons to those convicted under the Alien and Sedition Acts, which were widely regarded as politically motivated laws. James Madison, James Monroe, and Andrew Jackson followed similar patterns of using clemency for national reconciliation or to address judicial or legislative overreach.

Abraham Lincoln, during the Civil War, granted clemency to Confederate sympathizers and deserters, emphasizing national unity. His successor, Andrew Johnson, issued mass pardons as part of the post-war Reconstruction effort, including proclamations restoring rights to former Confederates.

### 20th Century Expansion and Controversies

In the modern era, presidential clemency began to draw greater attention due to increasing federal prosecutions, a growing bureaucracy, and the emergence of politically sensitive cases. The Department of Justice established the Office of the Pardon Attorney in 1893 to standardize the clemency review process.

Several landmark clemency decisions have shaped public debate:

* **Gerald Ford** pardoned former President Richard Nixon in 1974 for any crimes he may have committed related to the Watergate scandal. The pardon was issued preemptively and sparked significant controversy, although Ford defended it as a move to help the country heal.

* **Jimmy Carter** issued a blanket pardon in 1977 for Vietnam War draft evaders, fulfilling a campaign promise to promote reconciliation.

* **George H. W. Bush** issued pardons in 1992 to six officials involved in the Iran-Contra affair, a decision that critics said undermined legal accountability for executive actions.

* **Bill Clinton** issued 456 clemency actions, with his last-minute pardon of financier Marc Rich drawing allegations of influence-peddling and sparking congressional investigations.

* **George W. Bush** granted relatively few pardons and commutations, although he commuted the prison sentence of former White House aide Lewis “Scooter” Libby, convicted in the Valerie Plame CIA leak case.

* **Barack Obama** issued 1,927 clemency actions, including 1,715 commutations, the highest total since President Truman. His clemency program emphasized reducing lengthy sentences for nonviolent drug offenders under outdated sentencing laws.

* **Donald Trump** frequently bypassed the traditional clemency review process, granting pardons to political allies, public figures, and individuals with celebrity advocates. Notable recipients included Michael Flynn, Roger Stone, and Steve Bannon, all of whom had ties to his administration.

* **Joe Biden**, as of his third year in office, has issued a modest number of pardons and commutations, including efforts to address sentencing disparities and promote criminal justice reform.

### Political and Legal Context

While clemency is a constitutionally protected power, its use has occasionally raised ethical, legal, and political questions. Presidents are not required to provide justification for their decisions, and there is no judicial or legislative review of clemency grants. This has led some critics to call for reforms to increase transparency and establish clearer criteria for clemency.

The Department of Justice typically reviews clemency petitions and submits recommendations, but presidents are not bound by them. In some cases, high-profile pardons have been influenced by political connections, lobbying efforts, or public campaigns, fueling debates about fairness and accountability.

### Public Perception and Reform Proposals

Public reaction to clemency often varies depending on the political context, the nature of the offenses involved, and the perceived motivations of the president. Clemency has been celebrated as an act of compassion in cases of injustice but also condemned as a tool for favoritism or political expediency.

Proposals for reform include creating an independent clemency board, establishing clearer eligibility criteria, or limiting the timing of pardons, especially those issued during a president’s final days in office. Despite these proposals, presidential clemency remains a singular and largely unchecked expression of executive power.

### Legacy

Throughout U.S. history, presidential clemency has served as a vital instrument of justice, mercy, and political reconciliation. The individuals who have received pardons or commutations represent a broad spectrum of American society—from war resisters and civil rights activists to political figures and convicted criminals. The evolving use of this power continues to reflect broader trends in governance, criminal justice, and presidential leadership.
