**List of British Generals and Brigadiers**

This article provides an overview of notable officers holding the ranks of brigadier (formerly brigadier-general) and general in the British Army. These senior commanders have shaped military outcomes and influenced political affairs from the Napoleonic era through the present day.

---

## Rank Structure and Political Context

In the British Army, a **brigadier** commands a brigade (typically 3–5 battalions) and ranks above colonel and below major-general. The rank was styled “brigadier-general” until 1922. A **general** (full general) commands corps- or army-level formations, holds high staff appointments, or serves in the Ministry of Defence. Their appointments often intersect with political leadership—either through direct governmental roles (e.g., Secretary at War, Chief of the General Staff) or through influence on defence policy and civil-military relations.

---

## Notable Pre–20th Century Officers

* **Brigadier-General Sir John Moore (1761–1809)**
  Pioneer of light infantry tactics; his leadership at Corunna (1809) under Wellington influenced British Army doctrine.
* **General Sir Charles Napier (1782–1853)**
  Led the conquest of Sindh (1843), shaping imperial policy in British India.

---

## First World War (1914–1918)

### Generals

* **General Sir Douglas Haig (1861–1928)**
  Commander-in-Chief of the British Expeditionary Force (1915–1918); his strategies at the Somme and Passchendaele have been both criticized for high casualties and credited with wearing down German forces.
* **General Sir Henry Wilson (1864–1922)**
  Director of Military Operations (1915–1918); later Chief of the Imperial General Staff (CIGS), he influenced Allied coordination and postwar security arrangements.

### Brigadiers

* **Brigadier-General Oliver Leese (1877–1958)**
  Commanded the 4th Division on the Western Front; later rose to command Allied forces in Italy during the Second World War.
* **Brigadier-General Harold Alexander (1891–1969)**
  Commanded the 1st Canadian Division at Arras; subsequently became a four-star general in WWII and Governor General of Canada.

---

## Second World War (1939–1945)

### Generals

* **General Sir Alan Brooke, 1st Viscount Alanbrooke (1883–1963)**
  Chief of the Imperial General Staff (1941–1946); principal military adviser to Churchill and architect of British war strategy.
* **General Sir Bernard Montgomery (1887–1976)**
  Commander of the Eighth Army in North Africa and 21st Army Group in Northwest Europe; his victories at El Alamein and over Normandy breakout were politically significant for Allied morale.

### Brigadiers

* **Brigadier John Hackett (1910–1997)**
  Led 4th Brigade in North Africa; later a parliamentary under-secretary in the Ministry of Defence and author of *The Third World War*.
* **Brigadier Derek Horsford (1917–2007)**
  Commanded 161st Indian Infantry Brigade in Burma; later served on NATO planning staffs during the early Cold War.

---

## Cold War and Post-Imperial Era (1945–1990)

### Generals

* **General Sir John Hackett (1910–1997)**
  After WWII, served in Greece and Indochina; as NATO Deputy Supreme Allied Commander Europe (DSACEUR), he influenced alliance strategy against the Warsaw Pact.
* **General Sir Robert Fry (b. 1943)**
  Commanded British forces in the Gulf War (1991) and later became Deputy Supreme Allied Commander Atlantic, shaping maritime deterrence.

### Brigadiers

* **Brigadier Patrick Cordingley (b. 1939)**
  Led 7th Armoured Brigade (“Desert Rats”) in the Gulf War; his independent approach influenced post-Cold War expeditionary doctrine.
* **Brigadier Peter de la Billière (b. 1934)**
  Commanded the SAS (1984–1987) and later served as CINC UK Field Army; had pivotal roles in the Falklands and Gulf conflicts.

---

## Contemporary Officers (1990–Present)

### Generals

* **General Sir Mike Jackson (b. 1944)**
  Chief of the General Staff during the Kosovo conflict (1999) and later Commander-in-Chief, Land Command; known for his pragmatic approach to NATO operations.
* **General Sir Nick Carter (b. 1959)**
  Chief of the Defence Staff (2018–2021); oversaw operations against ISIS and managed UK–US military relations amid shifting global threats.

### Brigadiers

* **Brigadier Dan Lawrence-Smith (b. 1966)**
  Deputy Commander of Task Force Helmand in Afghanistan; later appointed Assistant Chief of the General Staff, contributing to Army transformation.
* **Brigadier James Martin (b. 1970)**
  Commander of 20th Armoured Brigade (“Iron Fist”) in Iraq; instrumental in developing UK counter-insurgency tactics.

---

## Political Influence and Legacy

British generals and brigadiers have profoundly influenced defence policy and international relations. As professional soldiers, their doctrinal innovations—ranging from Napoleonic light infantry tactics to modern counter-insurgency—shaped the Army’s organization. As senior advisers in government, they navigated civil-military tensions over force structure, procurement, and intervention mandates. Their honors (knighthoods, peerages) and post-service roles (parliamentary positions, NATO appointments) reflect the enduring link between military leadership and political decision-making in the United Kingdom.
