**List of Gulf War Military Equipment**

The Gulf War (1990–1991), fought between a U.S.-led coalition and Iraq following Iraq’s August 1990 invasion of Kuwait, showcased a wide array of modern military equipment. The conflict—codenamed **Operation Desert Shield** (defensive buildup) and **Operation Desert Storm** (combat phase)—was the first large‐scale use of precision-guided munitions and demonstrated the technological edge of coalition forces. Below is a representative list of principal military equipment deployed by both sides, organized by category, with attention to the political and strategic context.

---

## Coalition Equipment

### Aircraft

* **F-117A Nighthawk Stealth Fighter (United States)**

  * **Role:** Precision strike deep in Iraqi territory.
  * **Significance:** Its radar-evading design enabled the destruction of key command centers with minimal early warning, exemplifying U.S. high-technology dominance.

* **A-10 Thunderbolt II (United States)**

  * **Role:** Close air support (CAS) for ground forces.
  * **Significance:** Armed with a GAU-8/A Avenger 30 mm cannon, the “Warthog” effectively engaged Iraqi armored columns, underscoring the importance of dedicated CAS aircraft.

* **Panavia Tornado GR1 (United Kingdom, Italy, Saudi Arabia)**

  * **Role:** Low‐level interdiction and SEAD (Suppression of Enemy Air Defenses).
  * **Significance:** Equipped with JP233 runway-denial weapons and ALARM anti‐radar missiles, Tornados played a key role in establishing air superiority.

* **Dassault Mirage 2000 (France)**

  * **Role:** Air superiority and precision strike.
  * **Significance:** Demonstrated French involvement in coalition air operations, symbolizing NATO’s expanded reach beyond U.S. and British assets.

### Armored Fighting Vehicles

* **M1A1 Abrams Main Battle Tank (United States)**

  * **Role:** Armored breakthrough and exploitation.
  * **Significance:** Equipped with Chobham composite armor and a 120 mm smoothbore gun; Abrams outmatched Iraqi T‐72s, underscoring the coalition’s qualitative edge.

* **Challenger 1 (United Kingdom)**

  * **Role:** Main battle tank for British 1st Armoured Division.
  * **Significance:** Its rifled 120 mm L11A5 gun and mine protection highlighted British technological contributions to armored warfare.

* **AMX-30 (France)**

  * **Role:** Main battle tank in French Division Daguet.
  * **Significance:** Represented France’s determination to field its own forces under UN mandate, while illustrating varied coalition equipment.

### Artillery and Rocket Systems

* **M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System (MLRS) (United States)**

  * **Role:** Long‐range rocket artillery.
  * **Significance:** Delivered precision DBM (Dual-Purpose Improved Conventional Munition) rockets against dug-in targets, showcasing guided artillery’s emergence.

* **M109 Paladin Self‐Propelled Howitzer (United States)**

  * **Role:** Medium artillery support.
  * **Significance:** Provided sustained fire support for maneuvers in the open desert, emphasizing mobility and volume of fire.

### Naval and Missile Systems

* **BGM-109 Tomahawk Cruise Missile (United States, United Kingdom)**

  * **Role:** Long-range land attack.
  * **Significance:** Over 288 Tomahawks struck strategic targets in Iraq, demonstrating precision strike from stand-off platforms.

* **Aegis Combat System–Equipped Cruisers and Destroyers (United States)**

  * **Role:** Air defense and sea control.
  * **Significance:** Protected carrier battle groups from potential Iraqi air or missile attacks, underscoring integrated naval air defense.

## Iraqi Equipment

### Aircraft

* **Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-29 Fulcrum**

  * **Role:** Air superiority fighter.
  * **Significance:** While numerically limited, MiG-29s represented Iraq’s most capable fighters but achieved little success against coalition air patrols.

* **Sukhoi Su-25 Frogfoot**

  * **Role:** Close air support jet.
  * **Significance:** Conducted ground-attack missions against coalition positions, though suffered heavy losses due to coalition SEAD and air superiority.

### Armored Fighting Vehicles

* **T-72M Main Battle Tank**

  * **Role:** Core of Iraqi armored divisions.
  * **Significance:** Lacked modern armor and fire control compared to coalition tanks; massed T-72 formations were decisively engaged and destroyed.

* **T-55 and T-62**

  * **Role:** Older Soviet-designed tanks in reserve.
  * **Significance:** Supplemented T-72s but were vulnerable to 105 mm and 120 mm tank gun fire and precision artillery.

### Artillery and Missiles

* **BM-21 “Grad” Multiple Rocket Launcher**

  * **Role:** Area saturation rocket artillery.
  * **Significance:** Fired indiscriminately at Saudi and coalition lines, prompting coalition counter-battery operations.

* **Scud-B (R-17 Elbrus) Ballistic Missile**

  * **Role:** Long-range strike against Israel, Saudi Arabia, and coalition targets.
  * **Significance:** Scuds aimed to fracture the coalition by threatening Israel, though accuracy was poor and counter-measures (Patriot PAC-2) had mixed results.

### Air Defense

* **SA-2 Guideline, SA-3 Goa, and SA-6 Gainful Surface-to-Air Missile (SAM) Systems**

  * **Role:** High, medium, and low-altitude air defense.
  * **Significance:** Coalition “Wild Weasel” SEAD missions targeted radars, quickly neutralizing much of Iraq’s integrated air defense network.

* **ZSU-23-4 Shilka**

  * **Role:** Self-propelled anti-aircraft gun.
  * **Significance:** Provided point defense for mobile formations but was neutralized by air superiority and SEAD.

## Political and Strategic Implications

* **Coalition Cohesion and UN Mandate:** The diversity of coalition equipment—from U.S. stealth fighters to French AMX-30 tanks—reflected a unified international response under United Nations Security Council Resolutions 660 and 678.
* **Technological Asymmetry:** The Gulf War underscored the Western bloc’s technological dominance in precision strike, night vision, and armor protection, shaping post-Cold War military doctrine and accelerating modernization programs worldwide.
* **Lessons Learned:** Iraqi use of Scud missiles precipitated advances in missile defense (e.g., Patriot system development). Coalition reliance on high-technology platforms and real-time intelligence (e.g., AWACS, JSTARS) informed subsequent interventions in Bosnia, Kosovo, and Afghanistan.
* **Post-War Military Reforms:** The effectiveness of coalition equipment led to widespread procurement of precision-guided munitions, C4ISR networks, and all-weather attack capabilities among U.S. allies and former Warsaw Pact states transitioning to NATO standards.
