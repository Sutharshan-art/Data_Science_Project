**Lauren Bush Lauren** (née Lauren Pierce Bush; born June 25, 1984) is an American fashion designer, entrepreneur, and humanitarian advocate whose work in combating global hunger has intersected with broader political and policy debates on food security and sustainable development. A member of the Bush political family, she leveraged her public profile to found FEED Projects in 2007, a social enterprise that partners with international organizations and governments to distribute meals to children in need.

**Early Life and Education**
Born in Dallas, Texas, Lauren Pierce Bush is the eldest grandchild of President George H. W. Bush and First Lady Barbara Bush, and the niece of President George W. Bush and Florida Governor Jeb Bush. She spent her childhood between Texas and Washington, D.C., where her family’s political career shaped her awareness of public service. Bush attended Buckingham Browne & Nichols School in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and graduated cum laude from Princeton University in 2006 with a degree in anthropology, having written her senior thesis on food and identity among indigenous Mexican communities.

**Entrance into Public Life**
While an undergraduate, Bush interned at the U.S. Department of Agriculture and the International Rescue Committee, experiences that deepened her interest in food policy and international development. In 2005 she represented the United States as a delegate to the United Nations’ Millennium Summit on the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs), advocating for the inclusion of hunger reduction as a global priority. Her lineage and early public engagements drew media attention, situating her within discussions over how political families can mobilize private-sector resources for public purposes.

**Founding of FEED Projects**
In 2007, Bush co-founded FEED Projects with philanthropist Ellen Gustafson. FEED operates on a “buy one, give one” model: proceeds from the sale of socially conscious fashion and accessories fund meals in partnership with the United Nations World Food Programme (WFP) and other food-aid agencies. The organization’s launch coincided with expanded U.S. government commitments to food security—such as the 2008 reauthorization of the U.S. Farm Bill—and resonated with bipartisan interest in public–private partnerships to address global hunger.

Under Bush’s leadership as CEO, FEED expanded its footprint to more than 50 countries by 2015, distributing over 100 million school meals. She positioned FEED as a complement to official U.S. foreign assistance, emphasizing that grassroots consumer engagement could reinforce diplomatic and development strategies. In Congressional testimony before the House Committee on Agriculture in 2011, Bush highlighted the importance of integrating civil-society initiatives with U.S. programs such as Feed the Future, launched by the Obama administration in 2010.

**Advocacy and Policy Engagement**
Bush has used her platform to speak at international forums on the political dimensions of food security. At the 2012 Clinton Global Initiative, she announced a partnership between FEED and the WFP to support school-feeding programs in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. In 2013, she was appointed a WFP ambassador against hunger, engaging with national governments in Ethiopia and Guatemala to design locally appropriate school-feeding models. Her advocacy has underscored the links between child nutrition, educational attainment, and long-term economic development—a nexus central to U.S. and multilateral development policies.

**Intersection with Bush Family Politics**
Although Lauren Bush Lauren has remained formally nonpartisan, her family’s Republican legacy and her service under both Bush-family and subsequent Democratic-led administrations illustrate the bipartisan consensus on global hunger. During her uncle George W. Bush’s presidency, the landmark 2006 reauthorization of the President’s Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR) included provisions for nutrition support, reflecting converging interests in health and food policy. In the 2016 presidential campaign—when her aunt-in-law Hillary Clinton ran as the Democratic nominee—Bush Lauren publicly defended the Clinton Global Initiative’s school-feeding commitments while emphasizing FEED’s autonomous, apolitical mission.

**Transition to Private Sector and Continued Philanthropy**
In 2018, Bush Lauren stepped down as FEED’s CEO to focus on family and to launch a sustainable apparel line that adheres to the same social-impact model. Her new venture partnered with USAID and state governments to source ethically grown cotton in West Africa, blending commercial incentives with economic-development objectives. She continues to serve on FEED’s board and as an adviser to the WFP, testifying before the Senate Foreign Relations Committee in 2020 on the importance of maintaining U.S. leadership in global food assistance.

**Recognition and Legacy**
Bush’s fusion of fashion entrepreneurship and humanitarian advocacy has earned her honors from the World Economic Forum, the Clinton Global Initiative, and the National Council of Negro Women. Her work exemplifies how individuals with political pedigree can mobilize market-based solutions to complement governmental development efforts. As debates over the future of U.S. foreign aid and the role of private actors in sustainable development persist, Lauren Bush Lauren’s career highlights the potential—and the challenges—of public-private partnerships in addressing one of the world’s most intractable political issues: hunger.
