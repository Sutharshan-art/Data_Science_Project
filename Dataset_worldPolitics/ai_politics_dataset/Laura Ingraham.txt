**Laura Ingraham** (born June 19, 1963) is an American conservative television host, radio personality, author, and political commentator. Rising to prominence in the late 1990s, she has become a leading voice in the conservative movement, known for her combative style, advocacy of traditional values, and critique of liberal policies. Ingraham’s career spans print journalism, talk radio, and television, reflecting broader shifts in American media and conservative politics over the past three decades.

**Early Life and Education**
Born Laura Anne Preski in Glastonbury, Connecticut, Ingraham was raised by her mother and stepfather after her parents’ separation. A guardian ad litem following a childhood car accident that left her with a pronounced limp, she attended high school in Glastonbury before enrolling at Dartmouth College. There, she served as president of the student body and contributed to the *Dartmouth Review*, a conservative campus newspaper. Ingraham graduated magna cum laude in 1985 and earned her Juris Doctor from the University of Virginia School of Law in 1991.

**Legal and Early Media Career**
After law school, Ingraham clerked for Judge Ralph K. Winter of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit and worked briefly at a Washington, D.C., law firm. In 1994 she joined the staff of Vice President Dan Quayle as a speechwriter during the Clinton administration’s early years. Disenchanted with mainstream political culture, she transitioned to media: in 1998 she became a writer and commenter for *National Review*, contributing essays on culture, education, and judicial activism. Her outspoken essays opposing affirmative action and liberal judicial philosophies caught the attention of talk-radio producers.

**Rise in Talk Radio**
In 2001 Ingraham launched a late-night talk-radio show syndicated by Westwood One. Her blunt, often provocative style—criticizing what she termed the “liberal media” and championing conservative positions on immigration, taxes, and social issues—attracted a growing audience. By the mid-2000s her show achieved top-10 ratings among talk-radio hosts. Ingraham’s embrace of populist themes, such as opposition to high-skilled immigration and defense of traditional marriage, mirrored a rightward shift within the Republican base during the George W. Bush era.

**Television Breakthrough and Fox News**
In 2007 Ingraham joined Fox News Channel as a contributor, offering commentary on *The O’Reilly Factor* and *Hannity*. In 2017 she launched *The Ingraham Angle* in prime time, providing political analysis and cultural critique. The show quickly became known for its outspoken defense of President Donald Trump’s agenda on immigration, deregulation, and judicial appointments. Ingraham’s segment on the administration’s travel bans and her support for conservative judicial nominees positioned her as a key media ally of the Trump White House.

**Political Views and Advocacy**
Ingraham’s political positions center on cultural conservatism and economic nationalism. She vehemently opposes illegal immigration and has called for stricter border controls and enforcement. Skeptical of global trade deals, she criticizes outsourcing and champions “America First” economic policies. On social issues, she defends free speech on campus and opposes what she deems “political correctness” and “social justice” movements. Ingraham supported the repeal of the Affordable Care Act, tax cuts enacted in 2017, and the confirmation of conservative Supreme Court justices, reflecting mainstream Republican priorities in the 2010s and early 2020s.

**Controversies and Public Backlash**
Ingraham’s combative tone has provoked significant controversy. Her criticisms of public figures have led to defamation lawsuits and advertiser boycotts. Notably, in 2018 comments she made about a high-school survivor of the Parkland shooting sparked a temporary advertiser pullback from her program. Ingraham has also faced criticism for her commentary on racial issues and immigration, which opponents characterize as inflammatory. Despite these controversies, her ratings and influence within conservative media have remained strong.

**Role in Contemporary Politics**
As a leading conservative voice on television and radio, Ingraham has helped shape Republican messaging, particularly around immigration and cultural issues. During the 2016 and 2020 presidential elections, she campaigned for Donald Trump in rallies and provided sympathetic media coverage, amplifying his policy priorities. Her platforms have also become venues for up-and-coming conservative figures, reflecting the increasing integration of broadcast media and political activism within the right-wing ecosystem.

**Author and Cultural Commentator**
In addition to her broadcasting career, Ingraham has authored several books, including *Power to the People* (2007) and *The Obama Diaries* (2010), a satirical take on the first Black president’s administration. Her writings expand on themes addressed in her shows, arguing for strengthened national sovereignty, family values, and free-market principles. Ingraham also submits op-eds to major newspapers and contributes to online conservative outlets, extending her reach beyond radio and television.

**Legacy and Influence**
Laura Ingraham’s trajectory—from Ivy League conservative journalist to national talk-radio and cable-news host—parallels the rise of opinion-driven media in American politics. Her blend of cultural commentary and policy advocacy exemplifies the media strategies that have mobilized the Republican base since the early 2000s. While polarizing, she remains a central figure in conservative discourse, influencing debates on immigration, national identity, and the intersection of media and politics.
