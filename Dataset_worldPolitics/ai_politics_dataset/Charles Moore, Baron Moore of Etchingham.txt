**Charles Moore, Baron Moore of Etchingham**

Charles Hilary Moore, Baron Moore of Etchingham (born 31 October 1956), is a British journalist, author, and life peer known for his influential role in British conservative political commentary and media. He served as editor of several major British publications, including *The Spectator*, *The Sunday Telegraph*, and *The Daily Telegraph*, and is widely recognized for his political biographies, particularly his authorized three-volume biography of former Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher.

Moore has played a significant role in shaping right-of-centre political discourse in the United Kingdom, advocating for free-market policies, Euroscepticism, and cultural conservatism. He was elevated to the House of Lords in 2020 as a Conservative life peer.

---

### Early Life and Education

Charles Moore was born in Hastings, East Sussex, and grew up in the nearby village of Etchingham. He was educated at Eton College and later studied History at Trinity College, Cambridge. While at Cambridge, Moore became active in conservative politics and student journalism, serving as president of the Cambridge University Conservative Association.

His early involvement with the Conservative Party and interest in British political traditions laid the groundwork for his later career as a commentator and editor known for articulating conservative viewpoints.

---

### Journalism and Editorial Career

Moore began his journalism career as a political columnist and feature writer. In 1984, at the age of 27, he became editor of *The Spectator*, a leading centre-right weekly magazine. Under his leadership, *The Spectator* expanded its influence in British political and intellectual circles, becoming a key platform for conservative thought during the late Thatcher and Major years.

In 1992, Moore became editor of *The Sunday Telegraph*, followed by *The Daily Telegraph* in 1995. During his tenure, he reinforced the paper's reputation as a principal organ of British conservatism, advocating for limited government, traditional values, and Euroscepticism. He stepped down as editor in 2003 but remained active as a columnist and commentator.

Moore's influence extended beyond print journalism. He was a prominent media figure during pivotal moments in British politics, including debates over the European Union, immigration, and social policy. He has consistently voiced criticism of what he perceives as left-wing orthodoxy in public institutions and the media.

---

### Political Views and Public Engagement

A long-standing figure on the British right, Moore has been closely associated with Thatcherism and its legacy. He has championed deregulation, privatization, and a strong national identity, while opposing what he sees as the erosion of traditional British values.

He was a vocal critic of the European Union and supported Brexit in the 2016 referendum. His columns often reflect a sceptical view of supranational governance and a preference for British constitutional sovereignty.

Moore has also expressed concern over political correctness, the influence of progressive activism in public discourse, and the decline of religious and cultural institutions in British life. While often polarizing, his commentary has made him a prominent voice in debates over British identity, free speech, and the future of conservatism.

---

### Biographer of Margaret Thatcher

In 1997, Moore was selected by Margaret Thatcher to write her official biography. The result was a comprehensive three-volume work: *Margaret Thatcher: The Authorized Biography*, published between 2013 and 2019. The biography was widely praised for its depth, access to private papers, and balanced assessment of Thatcher’s political and personal life.

The biography covered Thatcher’s rise to power, her tenure as Prime Minister (1979–1990), and her post-premiership years, placing her legacy in the broader context of late 20th-century British and global politics. The work is considered a landmark in contemporary political biography and a key contribution to the historiography of modern Britain.

---

### Peerage and House of Lords

In October 2020, Charles Moore was appointed a life peer in the House of Lords by Prime Minister Boris Johnson. He took the title **Baron Moore of Etchingham**, after his hometown in East Sussex. His appointment was part of a broader list of political honours and was seen as recognition of his contributions to journalism and conservative political thought.

As a peer, Moore has continued to participate in debates on media freedom, public broadcasting, and constitutional matters. He has voiced criticism of the BBC and called for reforms to enhance impartiality and accountability in publicly funded media.

---

### Legacy and Influence

Charles Moore’s career spans several decades of significant political and cultural change in Britain. As an editor, columnist, and biographer, he has helped shape conservative intellectual discourse and influenced generations of readers and policymakers. Though a controversial figure to some, he is regarded by many as a leading voice in articulating and defending British conservatism in the post-Thatcher era.

His writings and public interventions continue to engage debates about Britain's national identity, its political future, and the role of media in democratic society. As both a historian and a commentator, Moore occupies a unique position in the British public sphere, bridging political journalism and historical analysis.
