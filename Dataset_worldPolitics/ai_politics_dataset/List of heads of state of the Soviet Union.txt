**List of Heads of State of the Soviet Union**

The head of state of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics evolved through three distinct offices between its creation in 1922 and its dissolution in 1991. Although real power lay with the General Secretary of the Communist Party, these ceremonial positions reflected the Soviet constitutional order and its changes over time.

**Chairman of the Central Executive Committee (1922–1938)**

* **Mikhail I. Kalinin** (1922–1938)
  A veteran Bolshevik and People’s Commissar, Kalinin was elected at the First Congress of Soviets in December 1922. He served through Lenin’s final years and Stalin’s early consolidation of power, presiding over the young federation’s highest legislative body.

**Chairmen of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet (1938–1990)**
With the 1936 Constitution, the Supreme Soviet replaced the Congress of Soviets, and its Presidium became the collective head of state.

1. **Mikhail I. Kalinin** (1938–1946)
   Continued in the new role, guiding official sessions through World War II and overseeing wartime legislative functions.

2. **Nikolai M. Shvernik** (1946–1953)
   Oversaw postwar reconstruction and the early Cold War period; his term ended shortly before Stalin’s death.

3. **Kliment Y. Voroshilov** (1953–1960)
   A Marshal of the Soviet Union and Old Bolshevik, Voroshilov served during the power struggle following Stalin’s passing and the denunciation of Stalin’s cult.

4. **Leonid I. Brezhnev** (1960–1964)
   Before becoming General Secretary, Brezhnev held the head-of-state post during the Khrushchev Thaw’s opening years.

5. **Anastas I. Mikoyan** (1964–1965)
   A survivor of Stalinist purges, Mikoyan’s brief tenure coincided with Khrushchev’s removal from power.

6. **Nikolai N. Podgorny** (1965–1977)
   Served during the Brezhnev Era of stability and economic stagnation, presiding as the Party consolidated its rule.

7. **Leonid I. Brezhnev** (1977–1982)
   Reassuming the state role while retaining Party leadership, Brezhnev’s second term spanned the late détente period.

8. **Vasily V. Kuznetsov (Acting)** (1982–1983)
   As First Deputy Chairman, Kuznetsov served three brief stints following the deaths of Brezhnev, Andropov, and Chernenko.

9. **Yuri V. Andropov** (1983–1984)
   Former KGB chairman, Andropov combined the head-of-state and Party leadership roles until his death.

10. **Konstantin U. Chernenko** (1984–1985)
    His short dual tenure saw continued economic stagnation and the beginning of generational leadership turnover.

11. **Andrei A. Gromyko** (1985–1988)
    A longtime Foreign Minister, Gromyko presided during the first years of Mikhail Gorbachev’s reform policies.

12. **Mikhail S. Gorbachev** (1988–1990)
    As Party leader, Gorbachev’s election as Chairman reflected his drive for perestroika and glasnost within existing institutions.

**President of the Soviet Union (1990–1991)**

* **Mikhail S. Gorbachev** (1990–1991)
  In March 1990, reforms established a separately elected presidency to strengthen state authority over the Party apparatus. As the first and only President of the USSR, Gorbachev oversaw the final phase of political liberalization, the failed August 1991 coup, and the Union’s eventual dissolution in December 1991.

**Political Significance**
Although the heads of state held largely ceremonial duties, their offices marked constitutional shifts:

* **1922–1938:** The Central Executive Committee chairmanship institutionalized Bolshevik federalism and legislative authority after the Revolution.
* **1938–1990:** The Presidium of the Supreme Soviet embodied collective state leadership, even as real power resided in the Communist Party.
* **1990–1991:** Creation of the presidency represented a major attempt to redefine Soviet governance by separating Party and state ahead of systemic collapse.

This list thus traces the evolution of Soviet state structures from revolutionary inception through authoritarian consolidation to the final reform efforts under Gorbachev.
