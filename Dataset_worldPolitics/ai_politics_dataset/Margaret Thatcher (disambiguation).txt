**Margaret Thatcher (disambiguation)**

**Margaret Thatcher** (1925–2013) was a British stateswoman who served as **Prime Minister of the United Kingdom** from 1979 to 1990 and leader of the **Conservative Party** from 1975 to 1990. As the first woman to hold either office, she remains one of the most prominent political figures in modern British history. Her political philosophy and economic policies, often referred to as **Thatcherism**, transformed the political and economic landscape of the UK and influenced conservative movements globally.

Due to her lasting legacy and the widespread influence of her name, "Margaret Thatcher" may also refer to a number of people, places, cultural references, and historical topics associated with or inspired by her life and political career. This disambiguation page provides an overview of those references, with a particular emphasis on the historical and political contexts in which they arose.

### Primary Figure

**Margaret Thatcher (1925–2013)**:
Born Margaret Hilda Roberts in Grantham, Lincolnshire, Thatcher trained as a chemist and later as a barrister before entering politics. Elected MP for Finchley in 1959, she rose within the ranks of the Conservative Party and was appointed Education Secretary in 1970. In 1975, she became party leader and, in 1979, led the Conservatives to victory, becoming the UK's first female Prime Minister. Her government prioritized deregulation, privatization, reduced state intervention, and a strong anti-communist foreign policy. She was re-elected in 1983 and 1987, before resigning in 1990 following internal party challenges. Her tenure remains one of the most controversial and impactful in British political history.

### People

* **Carol Thatcher**: A British journalist, author, and media personality, Carol is the daughter of Margaret Thatcher and businessman Denis Thatcher. She gained public attention not only as a writer but also through her appearances in British television. Her 2008 biography, *A Swim-on Part in the Goldfish Bowl*, provides personal insights into her mother’s political career.

* **Denis Thatcher (1915–2003)**: Margaret Thatcher’s husband and businessman. Though not a political figure himself, Denis played a supporting role during his wife's time in office and became a symbol of the prime ministerial spouse during the late 20th century.

### Political Ideology and Policies

* **Thatcherism**: A term used to describe the political ideology, economic policy, and style of governance associated with Margaret Thatcher. It emphasizes free markets, individualism, monetarism, and a reduced role for the state. Thatcherism was particularly influential in the restructuring of British industry, the curbing of trade union power, and the introduction of market-based reforms to the public sector.

* **Thatcherite**: A term used to describe politicians or public figures who adhere to the principles of Thatcherism. Within the Conservative Party, this label has often denoted the party’s economically liberal and socially conservative wing.

### Cultural and Historical References

* **"The Iron Lady"**: A nickname given to Margaret Thatcher by a Soviet journalist in 1976, reflecting her staunch anti-communist stance. The term was later adopted widely in domestic and international media to symbolize her resolute leadership style.

* **The Iron Lady (film)**: A 2011 biographical film starring Meryl Streep as Margaret Thatcher. The film depicts her rise to power, years in office, and later life, with a focus on her personal struggles and political legacy. It contributed to renewed public debate about Thatcher’s impact and memory.

* **Margaret Thatcher Square**: A public space in **Zagreb, Croatia**, named in her honor, reflecting her support for the independence of Eastern European states after the Cold War.

* **Statues and Memorials**: Numerous statues and plaques commemorate Margaret Thatcher, including one in her hometown of Grantham and another in the UK Parliament. These memorials have at times been the subject of controversy, reflecting the polarized views on her legacy.

### Related Topics

* **Fall of the Soviet Union**: Thatcher played a significant diplomatic role in the late Cold War period. While strongly anti-communist, she also developed a pragmatic working relationship with Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev, famously declaring, “We can do business together.” Her stance helped shape Western engagement with the USSR during its final years.

* **Falklands War**: A defining moment of her premiership, the 1982 conflict between the UK and Argentina over the Falkland Islands boosted Thatcher’s domestic popularity and reinforced her image as a determined and patriotic leader.

* **UK Miners' Strike (1984–85)**: One of the most contentious episodes of Thatcher's domestic policy era, the strike marked a major confrontation between the government and trade unions. Its outcome weakened union power and reshaped labor relations in Britain.

### Legacy and Public Perception

Margaret Thatcher's name remains a shorthand for a distinctive and influential period in British political life. Revered by supporters as a principled reformer who revitalized the British economy, and reviled by critics for social inequality and industrial decline, her legacy is deeply contested. Her policies continue to influence political debate in the UK and beyond, and her name is frequently invoked in discussions of leadership, economic policy, and ideological direction.

“Margaret Thatcher” thus refers not only to a historical individual but to a broader constellation of ideas, policies, and cultural symbols that have left an indelible mark on modern political history.
