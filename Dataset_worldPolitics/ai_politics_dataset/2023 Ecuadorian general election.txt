**2023 Ecuadorian General Election**

The **2023 Ecuadorian general election** was held in two rounds on **20 August 2023** and **15 October 2023**. These were snap elections, prompted by President **Guillermo Lasso**'s invocation of the **muerte cruzada** mechanism on **17 May 2023**, which dissolved the National Assembly and advanced both legislative and presidential elections. With Lasso opting not to seek re-election amid impeachment proceedings, the vote aimed to elect successors for the remainder of the 2021–2025 term and featured prominent candidates from opposing ideological fronts.

---

### Historical and Political Context

Ecuador had endured years of political instability marked by economic volatility, high levels of violent crime, and corruption scandals. President Lasso’s use of constitutional powers to dissolve the legislature reflected fractious relations with Congress and rising public disenchantment with institutional governance. The **muerte cruzada** act reset the political landscape, compelling all parties to rapidly organize campaigns in a short time frame.

Security issues loomed large following a surge in gang-related violence and the **assassination of candidate Fernando Villavicencio** eleven days before the first round—a chilling reenactment of political violence not seen since the 1970s.

---

### Electoral System and First Round

Ecuador uses a two-round system for presidential elections: a first round majority threshold of over **50%**, or at least **40%** with a **10-point lead**, leads to victory; otherwise, the top two candidates enter a runoff.

On **20 August**, turnout exceeded **82%**. **Luisa González**, backed by the **Citizen Revolution Movement**, led with approximately **33–34%**, consolidating support from former-President **Rafael Correa’s** political base. **Daniel Noboa**, a 35-year-old businessman from the center-right **National Democratic Action**, surprised many by finishing second with around **24%**, propelled by strong debate performances and a centrist platform. The remaining field, including populists, a former vice president, indigenous candidates, and independents, failed to reach the second round.

---

### Reference Questions and Legislative Vote

The first-round ballot also included two popular referendums: one to ban oil exploitation in Yasuní National Park, and another to prohibit metallic mining in the Chocó Andino region. Both environmental measures passed with strong public support. Overseas ballot-counting challenges even prompted a partial annulment and rescheduled votes.

Simultaneously, the election determined the **137-seat National Assembly**. The **Citizen Revolution Movement** emerged strongest with nearly **40%** of the vote and secured a plurality of seats. Other notable parties included **Movimiento Construye**, **National Democratic Action**, and the **Christian Social Party**, reflecting a fragmented legislature set to govern a divided electorate.

---

### Runoff and Results

The **second-round runoff** on **15 October** saw Noboa narrowly defeat González with **51.8%** of the vote to her **48.2%**, becoming **Ecuador’s youngest president** at age 35.

Noboa’s victory represented a centrist alternative appealing to voters concerned about crime, economic stagnation, and political extremism. Gonzalez’s narrow loss reflected the enduring strength of leftist populism yet underscored the limits of its wider appeal amid national insecurity.

---

### Campaign Themes

* **Security**: The escalation in violence and high-profile killings made law and order central to voter concerns. Noboa’s firm stance on cracking down on gangs contrasted with González’s calls for social reforms.
* **Economy**: Noboa promised pro-business policies, foreign investment, and job creation, while González emphasized wealth redistribution and maintaining subsidies.
* **Environment**: Referendum victories suggested strong public support for conservation, posing challenges to both candidates as policy implementation followed.
* **Corruption and Governance**: González campaigned on restoring Correa-era ideals and combating entrenched corruption. Noboa, as a political newcomer, portrayed a technocratic, ethical alternative.

---

### Legislative Outcome and Coalition Dynamics

The legislature’s fragmented makeup meant Noboa needed coalition-building with centrist and conservative parties—particularly **National Democratic Action**, the **Christian Social Party**, and possibly **Movimiento Construye**—to pass reforms. The **Citizen Revolution Movement** retained significant influence and later positioned itself as opposition, impacting governance and policymaking dynamics.

---

### Immediate Challenges and Legacy

Assuming office on **25 November 2023**, President Noboa inherited a nation plagued by staggering crime rates, economic hardship exacerbated by the global COVID recovery, and political polarization. He initiated aggressive anti-cartel campaigns, implemented states of emergency, and controversially authorized a raid on the Mexican embassy to detain a fugitive former vice president—moves that drew international scrutiny and suspicion of executive overreach.

Despite criticism, public support for Noboa remained strong in early 2024, driven by a demand for stability and order. Environmental referendums signaled public engagement with sustainable policymaking, yet tensions between development and conservation began to emerge.

---

### Significance for Ecuador and the Region

The 2023 election was a watershed in Ecuadorian history, breaking cycles of traditional party dominance and reflecting broader Latin American polarization. Noboa’s unexpected rise and moderate win disrupted expectations and challenged the durability of populist politics in the Andean region. Simultaneously, González’s narrow defeat affirmed that leftist currents remain potent, with potential resurgence under future Correa-aligned leadership.

Overall, the election underscored voters’ priorities: security, economic incentives, and stable governance superseded ideological allegiance, shaping Ecuador’s political trajectory and offering a blueprint for other democracies grappling with volatility.
