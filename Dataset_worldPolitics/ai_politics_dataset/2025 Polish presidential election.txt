**2025 Polish Presidential Election**

The **2025 Polish presidential election** was held in two rounds, with the first round on **October 10, 2025**, and a runoff on **October 24, 2025**, in accordance with Poland’s constitution for presidential elections. The presidency is a five-year term, exercisable for two consecutive terms, combining ceremonial, symbolic, and some important political powers, such as vetoes, representation in foreign affairs, and command over the armed forces.

---

### Historical and Political Context

Poland entered the 2025 election amid significant domestic and international developments. The ruling **Law and Justice (PiS)** party had governed the country since 2015, implementing conservative reforms—primarily to the judiciary and media—that drew criticism from the European Commission and domestic liberal critics, who argued the changes limited checks and balances.

At the same time, rising geopolitical tensions—stemming from the Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022 and military activity in Belarus—led to a renewed emphasis in Polish politics on security and NATO cooperation. Polish leadership, including outgoing President **Andrzej Duda**, took a prominent role in coordinating border security and refugee assistance.

Economic issues such as inflation, energy prices, and EU funding reforms were major campaign topics, alongside social concerns about pensions, healthcare, climate change, and judicial independence.

---

### Electoral System

The president is elected in a two-round system: any candidate obtaining over 50% of the vote in the first round wins outright; otherwise, the top two candidates advance to the second round. All Polish citizens aged 18 and older are eligible to vote, whether in-person or via postal ballots and approved mobile polling during election day.

---

### Major Candidates

Following primary and nomination contests, the leading contenders in 2025 were:

* **Andrzej Duda** (Law and Justice, PiS): The incumbent president seeking a third term due to a constitutional loophole. He campaigned on his record of social benefits, conservative values, and strong support for Ukraine and national defense.

* **Marek Szymanski** (Civic Platform, PO): The former foreign minister and 2020 contested candidate. Szymanski, backed by a broad centre‑right coalition, emphasized restoring EU relations and judicial independence while maintaining security cooperation.

* **Magdalena Król** (Third Way, centrist agrarian alliance): A regional leader championing rural development, economic reform, and EU benefits for farming communities.

* **Paweł Nowak** (Polish Coalition, centrist): Former Member of European Parliament and known for a pro-business, technocratic platform.

* **Ewa Dąbrowska** (Left Coalition): A progressive activist advocating for gender equality, climate action, and LGBTQ rights.

* **Rafał Kamiński** (Confederation Liberty and Independence): A far-right candidate with libertarian economic policies and socially conservative stances.

A number of additional right-wing, green, and independent candidates also qualified.

---

### First Round Results

Turnout in the first round reached approximately **72%**, a strong showing for a non-parliamentary national election. Results were as follows:

* **Marek Szymanski (PO)** – **38%**
* **Andrzej Duda (PiS)** – **29%**
* **Magdalena Król (Third Way)** – **12%**
* **Paweł Nowak (Polish Coalition)** – **8%**
* **Ewa Dąbrowska (Left Coalition)** – **7%**
* **Rafał Kamiński (Confederation)** – **6%**

Szymanski and Duda progressed to the runoff, as no candidate surpassed the 50% mark.

---

### Runoff Campaign Dynamics

With the elimination of smaller candidates, both Szymanski and Duda pursued center-strategies. Duda appealed to his base by highlighting social spending and traditional social values. Szymanski sought to unify anti-PiS voters, promising judicial reform, EU budget restoration, and climate-forward policies while continuing strong security guarantees.

Overlooked group voters—particularly those who supported Król and Nowak—became pivotal. Major endorsements from eliminated candidates, including pragmatists and left-leaning voices, fueled momentum toward Szymanski. Dąbrowska’s endorsement of Szymanski, along with several centrist voices, consolidated liberal-leaning voters.

PiS campaign ads emphasized border security, NATO cooperation, and economic stability, warning that a liberal president would undermine national sovereignty.

---

### Runoff Results

The second-round vote leaned narrowly in Szymanski’s favor, while turnout remained high at **70%**. Final results were:

* **Marek Szymanski** – **52.3%**
* **Andrzej Duda** – **47.7%**

Szymanski’s margin of about 4.6 points reflected consolidation in urban centers and shifting sentiment in rural areas influenced by liberal candidates.

---

### Reactions and Implications

Szymanski’s victory was welcomed by pro-EU leaders and analysts who anticipated a renewal of Polish-EU relations, legislative rollbacks on perceived judicial overreach, and alignment with green and social liberal priorities. Duda accepted the results and pledged a respectful transfer of power.

Within PiS, reactions were mixed: some proposed policy recalibration while others warned against U-turns. Civic Platform celebrated the win as a rebirth of Poland’s moderate center-right.

Internationally, NATO and EU leaders sent congratulations, signaling relief at Poland returning to presumed cooperative patterns marked by judicial independence, media freedom, and rule of law.

---

### Presidency and Outlook

Marek Szymanski was inaugurated on **6 December 2025**, marking a new era in Polish politics and governance—promising to reshape relations with the EU and Warsaw’s international alignment.

Szymanski’s presidency emphasizes judicial reform, reignition of EU funding, expansion of renewable energy, and bolstering national security through NATO integration. Balancing center-left and center-right priorities, he inherits a divided Parliament marked by a fragmented legislature needing coalition-building.

His inauguration highlighted a transition from conservative populism to more moderate, pro-European leadership in Polish politics. Success will depend on navigating tensions between national identity, integration, and democratic institutions—while managing public expectations for transparency, economic stability, and international influence amidst broader geopolitical challenges.
