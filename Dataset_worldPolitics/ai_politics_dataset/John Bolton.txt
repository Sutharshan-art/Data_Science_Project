**John Robert Bolton** (born November 20, 1948) is an American attorney, diplomat, and Republican political figure who has held senior national security and foreign policy roles in four administrations. Known for his hawkish views and emphasis on American sovereignty, Bolton has been a prominent advocate for assertive U.S. engagement abroad, particularly in opposing international institutions and promoting regime change for hostile states.

### Early Life and Education

Born in Baltimore, Maryland, Bolton graduated from Yale University (B.A., 1970) and Yale Law School (J.D., 1974). He began his career as a lawyer in Los Angeles before entering public service under President Ronald Reagan, joining the State Department as an attorney-adviser in 1985.

### Reagan and George H. W. Bush Administrations

Under President Reagan, Bolton served as Deputy Assistant Attorney General for the Office of Legal Counsel (1986–1989), advising on executive branch powers and international law. In 1989, President George H. W. Bush appointed him Assistant Secretary of State for International Organization Affairs (1989–1993). In that role, Bolton led U.S. delegations to the United Nations and other multilateral bodies, where he advocated U.S. withdrawal from or reform of international agreements he viewed as contrary to U.S. interests—foreshadowing his later opposition to the International Criminal Court and the UN Human Rights Commission.

### National Security Council and Arms Negotiations

In the George W. Bush administration, Bolton served as Under Secretary of State for Arms Control and International Security (2001–2005). He helped negotiate the Proliferation Security Initiative, aimed at interdicting illicit shipments of weapons of mass destruction, and oversaw U.S. withdrawal from the 1972 Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty in 2002. His tenure coincided with the U.S.-led invasions of Afghanistan and Iraq, where Bolton supported preemptive action to counter perceived threats.

### U.S. Ambassador to the United Nations (2005–2006)

In August 2005, President Bush appointed Bolton U.S. Permanent Representative to the United Nations. Confirmed by voice vote after contentious debate, he took a hard line against UNSC “activism” and what he characterized as anti-American bias within UN bodies. Bolton opposed the International Criminal Court and supported unilateral U.S. action when multilateral consensus failed. His tenure saw the U.S. abstain on a Security Council resolution condemning deportations of Palestinians by Israel and threatened U.S. withdrawal from UN agencies—reflecting an assertive “America First” interpretation of national sovereignty within a multilateral framework. He resigned in December 2006 amid a broader reshuffle of Bush’s foreign policy team.

### Post–Bush Administration and Advocacy

After leaving government, Bolton became a senior fellow at the American Enterprise Institute, a Fox News contributor, and a lobbyist. He authored several books—including *Surrender Is Not an Option* (2007)—arguing for robust U.S. global leadership and restoration of American deterrence. He testified before congressional committees supporting regime change in Iran and continued to critique the Obama administration’s Iran nuclear negotiations.

### National Security Advisor Nomination (2018)

In March 2018, President Donald Trump named Bolton National Security Advisor. Following confirmation by the Senate, Bolton succeeded H.R. McMaster in April 2018. As a member of Trump’s cabinet, he coordinated foreign policy across the National Security Council, advocated for maximum pressure on Iran through sanctions, supported the U.S. withdrawal from the Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty, and encouraged stronger stances against the International Criminal Court. He also played a role in negotiations on North Korean denuclearization and pushed for the U.S. recognition of Israel’s annexation of the Golan Heights.

### Departure and Controversy

Bolton’s tenure was marked by internal White House disputes over trade policy, Middle East peace plans, and the handling of the Ukraine pressure allegations that led to President Trump’s first impeachment. He left the administration in September 2019, reportedly at odds with the President over foreign policy direction and the administration’s management style.

### Political Positions

* **Unilateralism and Sovereignty:** Bolton has consistently prioritized U.S. sovereignty over treaty commitments and international court jurisdiction, opposing the International Criminal Court and many UN human rights mechanisms.
* **Regime Change:** He has advocated for regime change in North Korea, Iran, and Venezuela, viewing authoritarian governments as existential threats needing direct U.S. action.
* **Strong Defense Posture:** Bolton supports robust defense spending, missile defense deployments, and preemptive military options to deter adversaries.
* **Skepticism of Multilateralism:** While recognizing practical benefits of certain alliances—NATO, the Five Eyes intelligence partnership—he opposes what he sees as overreach by international organizations into U.S. decision‐making.

### Legacy and Impact

John Bolton’s influence lies in his forceful advocacy for American unilateral action, skepticism toward international institutions, and willingness to champion controversial interventions. His tenure in multiple administrations helped shape post–9/11 U.S. policy on arms control, the UN, and Middle East strategy. Critics accuse him of brinkmanship that heightens global tensions, while supporters praise his clarity in defending U.S. interests. Bolton remains a leading voice in debates over America’s role in a changing international order.
