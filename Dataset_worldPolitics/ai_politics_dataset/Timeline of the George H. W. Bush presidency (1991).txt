**Timeline of the George H. W. Bush Presidency (1991)**

George H. W. Bush’s third year in office, 1991, was dominated by the Gulf War, the dissolution of the Soviet Union, and significant domestic and diplomatic initiatives. This timeline highlights the principal events and political context from January 1 to December 31, 1991.

---

**January–March 1991**

* **January 15:** United Nations Security Council Resolution 678 takes effect, authorizing member states to “use all necessary means” to enforce Iraq’s withdrawal from Kuwait by January 15.
* **January 16:** President Bush addresses Congress, requesting authorization for use of U.S. military force to expel Iraqi forces from Kuwait; Congress votes overwhelmingly in favor.
* **January 17:** Operation Desert Storm begins with a massive aerial bombing campaign against Iraqi military targets. Bush’s public rationale emphasizes collective security and enforcement of international law under UN auspices.
* **February 23–24:** Ground offensive (Operation Desert Sabre) commences; U.S.-led coalition forces advance rapidly into Kuwait and southern Iraq.
* **February 28:** President Bush announces a cease-fire after just 100 hours of ground combat. Iraq accepts UN terms; Kuwait is liberated. Bush’s speech highlights the “coalition of the willing” and signals a U.S.-led enforcement of a new world order following the Cold War.

**March–April 1991**

* **March 3:** Bush addresses a joint session of Congress, commending military success and calling for reconstruction of Kuwait and continued vigilance in the Gulf.
* **March 5:** The administration imposes “no-fly” zones over northern and southern Iraq to protect Kurdish and Shiite populations, reflecting emerging humanitarian concerns.
* **March 25:** The Bush administration launches Operation Provide Comfort, deploying humanitarian aid and protective forces to Kurdish refugees in northern Iraq.
* **April 2:** President Bush hosts Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev in Houston, Texas. The third (and final) Bush–Gorbachev summit reinforces START I progress and underscores the United States’ commitment to peaceful post–Cold War relations.

**May–July 1991**

* **May 30:** The Bush administration signs the Rome Statute proposals into U.S. law, signaling tentative support for an international criminal court, though full ratification will never occur.
* **June 12:** The Supreme Court rules in *Rust v. Sullivan* that the government may restrict abortion counseling in federally funded clinics, reflecting the administration’s conservative social policy influence.
* **July 31:** President Bush and Gorbachev sign the Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty (START I) in Moscow, agreeing to reduce strategic offensive arms by approximately one-third. Bush frames START I as a cornerstone of the post–Cold War security framework.

**August–September 1991**

* **August 19–21:** An attempted coup in the Soviet Union by hard‐line communists briefly deposes Gorbachev. President Bush, cautious to avoid escalation, condemns the coup, calls for Gorbachev’s restoration, and offers diplomatic support to Russian President Boris Yeltsin. His restraint helps prevent U.S. intervention while bolstering democratic forces.
* **August 28:** The United States recognizes the independence of the three Baltic republics—Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania—rejecting Soviet attempts to suppress their sovereignty.
* **September 11:** President Bush delivers a major address to the United Nations General Assembly, urging continued support for emerging democracies and the rule of law in the post-Soviet space.

**October–December 1991**

* **October 1:** President Bush signs the National and Community Service Trust Act, establishing AmeriCorps and promoting domestic volunteerism as a response to social challenges.
* **November 19–20:** Leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus meet in Belavezha Forest (Belarus) and sign the Belavezha Accords, declaring that the USSR has effectively ceased to exist and establishing the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS).
* **December 8:** Bush welcomes the Belavezha signatories at the Malta Summit, emphasizing U.S. support for a peaceful transition and the sovereignty of newly independent states.
* **December 25:** Mikhail Gorbachev resigns as President of the Soviet Union. President Bush issues a statement expressing confidence in the peaceful dissolution of the USSR and pledging U.S. assistance to successor states.
* **December 26:** The Supreme Soviet formally dissolves the Soviet Union. Bush proclaims December 26 as the end of the Cold War, declaring that the emergence of independent states opens a new era of freedom and cooperation.

---

**Political and Historical Context**
In 1991, President Bush navigated the transition from Cold War confrontation to a unipolar moment in which the United States emerged as the sole superpower. The swift Gulf War victory under UN authorization demonstrated a new model of collective security. Concurrently, Bush’s calibrated response to the Soviet coup and his support for the independence of former Soviet republics underscored a diplomatic strategy balancing restraint with principled support for democracy.

Domestically, Bush’s legislative initiatives—on civic service, social policy, and adherence to conservative judicial philosophy—reflected his “kinder, gentler” vision while responding to budgetary constraints and the emerging economic recession. The signing of START I and recognition of Baltic independence cemented a shift away from ideological rivalry toward cooperative security arrangements.

The events of 1991 thus defined Bush’s legacy: managing the end of an era without triggering new conflicts, while laying the groundwork for U.S. involvement in a globally interconnected post–Cold War order.
