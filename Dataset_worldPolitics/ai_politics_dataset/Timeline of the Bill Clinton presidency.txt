**Timeline of the Bill Clinton Presidency**

The presidency of William Jefferson “Bill” Clinton spanned from January 20, 1993, to January 20, 2001. His two terms were marked by major domestic reforms, economic expansion, and significant foreign policy challenges, including military interventions and international trade negotiations.

**1993**

* **January 20:** Bill Clinton inaugurated as the 42nd President of the United States, succeeding George H. W. Bush.
* **January 22:** Signs the Family and Medical Leave Act, guaranteeing unpaid leave for family and medical reasons.
* **January 25:** Appoints Janet Reno as the first female Attorney General.
* **Spring:** Launches comprehensive health care reform led by First Lady Hillary Rodham Clinton; the proposal ultimately stalls in Congress.
* **May 26–July 31:** Oversees U.S. participation in Operation Restore Hope in Somalia; withdraws most forces after Black Hawk Down incident in October.
* **September 13:** Signs the Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act, instituting federal background checks for firearm purchases.
* **November:** Wins significant Democratic majorities in the House and Senate in the midterm elections, bolstering his domestic agenda.

**1994**

* **February 4:** Hosts South African President Nelson Mandela at the White House, celebrating the end of apartheid.
* **April 12:** Signs the North American Free Trade Agreement Implementation Act, enacting NAFTA with Canada and Mexico.
* **September 13:** Signs the Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act, the largest crime bill in U.S. history, including the “three strikes” provision and funding for police.
* **November 8:** Republicans, led by Newt Gingrich’s “Contract with America,” win control of both the House and Senate in midterms, marking the first GOP congressional majority in 40 years.

**1995**

* **March–April:** Faces opposition from the newly empowered Republican Congress; vetoes line-item pork appropriations.
* **November–December:** Two federal government shutdowns occur after budget impasses between Clinton and the Republican Congress over spending levels and policy riders. Both sides ultimately compromise in early 1996.

**1996**

* **February 29:** Signs the Telecommunications Act of 1996, deregulating broadcasting and telecommunications industries.
* **June 12:** Supports the Defense of Marriage Act, defining marriage for federal purposes as between one man and one woman (later partly overturned by Supreme Court).
* **September 29:** Signs the Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act, overhauling welfare by instituting work requirements and time limits.
* **November 5:** Wins re-election against Republican nominee Bob Dole, becoming the first Democrat since Franklin D. Roosevelt to win two consecutive terms.

**1997**

* **January 20:** Sworn in for second term; delivers inaugural address emphasizing opportunity and renewal.
* **March 12:** Unveils the Higher Education Act reauthorization, expanding student loans and grant programs.
* **April 24:** Orders cruise-missile strikes against Iraq in response to Saddam Hussein’s assassination attempt on former President George H. W. Bush.
* **October 9:** Chooses Madeleine Albright as the first female U.S. Secretary of State.
* **December:** Launches the Kyoto Protocol negotiations on climate change, though the U.S. will later decline to ratify.

**1998**

* **January 17:** Orders airstrikes against Iraqi air defense sites in Operation Desert Fox, in coordination with Britain.
* **January 27:** Monica Lewinsky scandal breaks; independent counsel Kenneth Starr expands investigation into Clinton’s conduct.
* **August 17:** Releases transcript of Lewinsky grand jury testimony, intensifying public scrutiny.
* **December 19:** The House of Representatives votes to impeach President Clinton on charges of perjury and obstruction of justice, making him only the second U.S. president to be impeached.

**1999**

* **February 12–16:** Senate trial acquits Clinton on both articles of impeachment (perjury and obstruction), failing to achieve the two-thirds needed for removal.
* **March 24:** Orders U.S. and NATO air campaign against Yugoslavia (Operation Allied Force) to halt ethnic cleansing in Kosovo.
* **June 10:** With NATO bombings paused, negotiates the Rambouillet Accords leading to Yugoslav withdrawal of forces from Kosovo.
* **June 11:** Signs the Gramm–Leach–Bliley Act, repealing parts of the Glass–Steagall Act and deregulating financial services.
* **December 12:** Signs the Good Friday Agreement, helping broker peace in Northern Ireland, a major diplomatic achievement.

**2000**

* **March 5:** Meets with Chinese President Jiang Zemin near Washington, enhancing U.S.–China relations and discussing trade.
* **June 2:** Endorses the creation of the Millennium Challenge Corporation to provide foreign aid contingent on governance reforms.
* **November:** Republican George W. Bush declared winner of the disputed presidential election; Supreme Court halts Florida recount on December 12, effectively deciding the election.

**2001**

* **January 7:** Delivers final State of the Union address, warning of potential terrorist threats.
* **January 20:** Concludes second term; leaves office with high approval ratings and a budget surplus, the first since 1969.

**Political Context and Legacy**
Clinton’s presidency is characterized by centrism—dubbed the “Third Way”—balancing market-oriented policies with social welfare goals. His administration oversaw sustained economic growth, budget surpluses, and expanded global trade, while navigating deep partisan divides that culminated in a Republican‐controlled Congress. Foreign policy successes in peace negotiations were counterbalanced by controversies over military interventions and domestic scandals. Clinton’s tenure set the stage for subsequent debates over globalization, welfare policy, and the scope of executive power.
