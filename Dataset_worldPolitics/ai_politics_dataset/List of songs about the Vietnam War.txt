**List of Songs About the Vietnam War**

The **list of songs about the Vietnam War** encompasses a wide range of musical works produced during and after the conflict (1955–1975), reflecting the cultural, political, and emotional dimensions of one of the most contentious wars in modern history. These songs represent various perspectives—from protest and opposition to support for troops and patriotic expression—and were a significant part of the broader public discourse surrounding the Vietnam War, particularly in the United States.

Music played a powerful role in shaping and expressing public sentiment. While many songs emerged from the anti-war movement and youth counterculture of the 1960s and 1970s, others supported the war effort or focused on the personal struggles of soldiers and veterans. The Vietnam War era marked a turning point in the relationship between popular music and political activism, and its musical legacy continues to influence contemporary art and historical understanding.

---

### Historical and Political Context

The Vietnam War, fought between communist North Vietnam (and its allies) and South Vietnam (backed primarily by the United States), provoked deep divisions within American society. As the conflict escalated—especially following the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution in 1964 and the Tet Offensive in 1968—public opposition grew, fueled by the draft, mounting U.S. casualties, and televised coverage of the war’s brutality.

Music became a key medium through which artists, activists, and ordinary citizens expressed their views. Protest songs became anthems of the anti-war movement, often performed at rallies, marches, and teach-ins. Meanwhile, other songs focused on the human toll of the war, honoring soldiers’ sacrifices or lamenting the trauma they endured. Both perspectives contributed to a rich and complex body of musical commentary on the conflict.

---

### Notable Anti-War Songs

#### **“Fortunate Son” – Creedence Clearwater Revival (1969)**

A widely recognized anti-establishment anthem, the song criticizes class privilege and the inequities of the draft system, highlighting how the wealthy often avoided combat service.

#### **“Give Peace a Chance” – Plastic Ono Band (1969)**

John Lennon’s minimalist chant became a rallying cry for the peace movement, frequently sung at anti-war protests across the U.S. and Europe.

#### **“I-Feel-Like-I’m-Fixin’-to-Die Rag” – Country Joe and the Fish (1965)**

A satirical and biting critique of the war and government propaganda, the song became famous after its performance at Woodstock and is remembered for its sarcastic “cheer” intro.

#### **“What’s Going On” – Marvin Gaye (1971)**

Though not explicitly about Vietnam, the song reflects the social turmoil of the era, with themes of war, police violence, and spiritual confusion. Inspired by conversations with his veteran brother, it became an enduring message of compassion and protest.

#### **“Ohio” – Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young (1970)**

Written in response to the Kent State shootings, where National Guardsmen killed four students protesting the war, the song became a poignant tribute to the anti-war movement’s sacrifices.

---

### Songs About Soldiers and Veterans

#### **“Goodnight Saigon” – Billy Joel (1982)**

Released after the war, this ballad offers a sympathetic portrayal of American soldiers in Vietnam, focusing on their shared suffering and camaraderie rather than political positions.

#### **“Still in Saigon” – Charlie Daniels Band (1982)**

This song explores the post-war trauma and disillusionment experienced by Vietnam veterans, including themes of PTSD and social alienation.

#### **“The Ballad of the Green Berets” – Staff Sgt. Barry Sadler (1966)**

One of the few pro-military hits during the height of the war, the song honors the bravery of U.S. Special Forces and became a patriotic anthem at the time.

---

### Songs Reflecting Broader War Themes

#### **“War” – Edwin Starr (1970)**

With its famous refrain “War, what is it good for? Absolutely nothing,” this song is a direct and powerful condemnation of militarism, becoming one of the most iconic anti-war tracks of the era.

#### **“Where Have All the Flowers Gone?” – Pete Seeger (1955)**

Originally written during the Korean War and later popularized during the Vietnam era, this folk song questions the cycles of war and loss. It became a staple at peace rallies.

#### **“Draft Morning” – The Byrds (1968)**

Captures the anxiety and dread associated with the military draft, evoking the loss of youth and innocence.

---

### Influence and Legacy

Songs about the Vietnam War became an integral part of the protest movement and of American cultural identity during the 1960s and 1970s. They were often used to mobilize opposition, humanize soldiers, and call for policy change. Radio broadcasts, live concerts, and underground circulation of politically charged music helped spread anti-war messages despite censorship or criticism.

The war also shaped the emergence of protest music as a recognized genre. Artists such as Bob Dylan, Joan Baez, and Phil Ochs were key figures in this tradition, blending folk music with activism. Their influence extended beyond Vietnam, informing later protest movements against nuclear weapons, apartheid, and the Iraq War.

Even decades after the war's end, Vietnam continues to inspire music addressing themes of trauma, memory, and national reconciliation. Many post-war songs revisit the emotional aftermath for veterans and their families, while others re-examine the political and moral dimensions of the conflict.

---

### Conclusion

The list of songs about the Vietnam War illustrates how music became a vital form of expression during one of the most polarizing episodes in U.S. history. These songs gave voice to a generation’s anxieties, hopes, and convictions, shaping public discourse and influencing the broader anti-war and civil rights movements. Today, they remain a powerful cultural legacy of resistance, reflection, and remembrance.
