**Milos Stankovic**

**Milos Stankovic** is a retired British Army officer, author, and former peacekeeper known for his work during the wars in the former Yugoslavia in the 1990s. Of Serbian and British heritage, Stankovic played a unique role as an interpreter, liaison officer, and intelligence operative during the Bosnian War, where he became a key figure in communication efforts between United Nations forces and Bosnian Serb leaders. His military service and subsequent legal troubles brought him significant attention in both military and political circles.

Stankovic is best known for his liaison work with General Ratko Mladić of the Army of Republika Srpska, his arrest and investigation under the Official Secrets Act, and his later writings that provided a personal account of British military operations during the Balkan conflicts.

---

### Early Life and Military Career

Milos Stankovic was born in the United Kingdom to a family with strong military and cultural ties. His father, a Serbian émigré, had fought with the Yugoslav Army during World War II, while his mother was British. Stankovic studied at Sandhurst and was commissioned into the British Army, serving with distinction in the **Parachute Regiment** and later in the **Intelligence Corps**.

Due to his fluency in Serbo-Croatian and understanding of Balkan culture, he was deployed to the former Yugoslavia in the early 1990s as the region descended into conflict following the breakup of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia.

---

### Role in the Bosnian War

During the **Bosnian War** (1992–1995), Stankovic served as a senior liaison officer with the United Nations Protection Force (**UNPROFOR**). His primary role was to facilitate communication between UN commanders and Bosnian Serb leaders, including General **Ratko Mladić**, who would later be indicted for war crimes by the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY).

Stankovic’s ability to navigate the complex and often hostile political environment made him a valuable asset. He was frequently involved in tense negotiations, hostage situations, and ceasefire discussions. His work required balancing military objectives with humanitarian concerns and navigating the limitations of UN peacekeeping mandates.

He operated in a highly charged atmosphere, where mistrust between the warring parties and between the UN and local forces was pervasive. His role placed him at the center of some of the most sensitive episodes of the war, including the sieges of Sarajevo and Srebrenica.

---

### Arrest and Investigation

In 1997, after returning to the UK and completing a tour with military intelligence, Stankovic was arrested by the British Ministry of Defence Police under the **Official Secrets Act**. He was held for several months and subjected to intense scrutiny on suspicion of espionage and improper disclosure of sensitive information related to his work in Bosnia.

Despite extensive investigations, **no charges were ever brought** against him, and the case was eventually closed. The ordeal drew criticism from legal and military experts, who saw the arrest as an example of the challenges facing individuals operating in complex intelligence environments with limited oversight and ambiguous legal protections.

The case highlighted concerns over how the British military and intelligence agencies handled internal investigations and treated personnel engaged in sensitive diplomatic and intelligence roles. Some commentators argued that Stankovic was a scapegoat for broader institutional failures in handling the Balkan conflicts and post-Cold War peacekeeping operations.

---

### Later Career and Writings

Following his departure from the military, Stankovic turned to writing and public speaking. He authored the memoir **“Trusted Mole: A Soldier’s Journey into Bosnia’s Heart of Darkness”** (2000), in which he detailed his experiences in Bosnia, including his work with Mladić, the complexities of UN operations, and his legal ordeal in Britain.

The book was praised for its candid portrayal of the challenges faced by peacekeepers and for shedding light on the bureaucratic and operational difficulties encountered by international forces in the former Yugoslavia.

Stankovic also became involved in leadership consulting and advocacy for veterans, emphasizing the importance of ethical leadership, cross-cultural communication, and mental health in post-conflict settings. His background made him a prominent voice in debates about post-military transition and the responsibilities of governments toward veterans.

---

### Legacy and Historical Significance

Milos Stankovic occupies a distinctive place in the history of post-Cold War peacekeeping and military diplomacy. His contributions during the Bosnian War represent a rare example of individual engagement across battle lines in a high-risk environment where traditional military roles were often blurred.

His arrest and eventual exoneration reflect the tensions inherent in intelligence work, especially when operating in legally and politically ambiguous theaters like the former Yugoslavia. His writings provide a first-hand account of the difficulties of implementing international mandates in fragmented, ethnically charged conflicts.

Stankovic’s story underscores the broader themes of trust, loyalty, and accountability in military and intelligence operations, particularly during a time when Western nations were grappling with their roles in humanitarian intervention and post-conflict reconstruction. His career continues to provoke discussion among scholars, soldiers, and policymakers about the limits and obligations of military service in the modern world.
