**Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina**

The **Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina** (Bosnian: *Armija Republike Bosne i Hercegovine*, ARBiH) was the official military force of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina during the Bosnian War (1992–1995). Formed in response to escalating ethnic conflict and the collapse of Yugoslavia, the ARBiH played a critical role in defending the multiethnic Bosnian state against aggression and asserting its sovereignty. Its establishment and operations were deeply shaped by the complex political, ethnic, and international dynamics of the war.

---

### Historical and Political Context

The ARBiH was created amid the disintegration of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia and Bosnia and Herzegovina’s declaration of independence in March 1992. The country’s population was ethnically diverse, primarily composed of Bosniaks (Bosnian Muslims), Serbs, and Croats. The declaration triggered violent resistance from Bosnian Serb forces, supported by the Yugoslav People’s Army (JNA) and Serbia, aiming to carve out ethnically Serb territories.

The international community’s recognition of Bosnia and Herzegovina’s sovereignty intensified the need for a unified defense force capable of resisting the insurgency and protecting the country’s territorial integrity.

---

### Formation and Organization

The Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina was officially established in April 1992, evolving from local defense groups, the Territorial Defense Forces, and other irregular militias loyal to the Bosnian government. It rapidly grew into a structured military force with an organized chain of command and distinct units.

The ARBiH was predominantly composed of Bosniak soldiers but also included volunteers from other ethnic groups, notably Croats and international fighters. Its structure consisted of infantry brigades, special forces, artillery units, and air components.

Command was centralized under the Presidency of Bosnia and Herzegovina and the Ministry of Defense, with General **Sefer Halilović** serving as the first Chief of Staff, followed by other commanders during the war.

---

### Role During the Bosnian War

The ARBiH was the principal defender of the internationally recognized Bosnian state throughout the conflict. It engaged in defensive and offensive operations against the Army of Republika Srpska (VRS) and, at times, against the Croatian Defence Council (HVO) during internal Bosniak-Croat hostilities.

The army faced significant challenges, including shortages of weapons, supplies, and international support, especially in the war’s early years. Despite this, the ARBiH maintained control over large portions of territory, including the capital Sarajevo, which endured the longest siege in modern history.

The ARBiH participated in key battles such as the defense of Sarajevo, operations in central Bosnia, and later offensives that helped regain territory in the war’s closing stages.

---

### Political and Ethnic Complexity

While the ARBiH was established as the official military of the Bosnian government, the war was complicated by shifting alliances and ethnic rivalries. The Bosniak-led ARBiH initially fought alongside the Croat forces but conflicts arose, notably during the Croat-Bosniak War (1992–1994), further fragmenting the Bosnian front.

Efforts such as the **Washington Agreement (1994)** helped reconcile Bosniak and Croat factions, leading to joint operations against Serb forces and the formation of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina as part of the post-war political settlement.

---

### International Involvement and Support

The ARBiH’s struggle attracted varying degrees of international attention and assistance. Early in the war, the army suffered from arms embargoes imposed by the UN, limiting its ability to defend against better-equipped Serb forces.

Gradually, humanitarian aid and military support, including from Western countries and Islamic states, increased. The presence of UN peacekeeping forces and NATO interventions in 1995, particularly during the **Srebrenica massacre** and the NATO bombing campaign against Serb targets, significantly influenced the conflict’s outcome.

The ARBiH also incorporated foreign volunteers, including the so-called **Bosnian mujahideen**, who contributed to the military effort but also added layers of complexity to the conflict.

---

### Postwar Demobilization and Integration

Following the **Dayton Peace Agreement** in December 1995, which ended the war and established the current constitutional framework of Bosnia and Herzegovina, the ARBiH was demobilized and merged with other former warring forces into a single, unified military—the **Armed Forces of Bosnia and Herzegovina**.

This integration was a key component of postwar reconciliation and state-building, aiming to foster interethnic cooperation and ensure the country’s defense under a unified command structure.

---

### Legacy and Historical Significance

The Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina symbolizes the Bosnian state’s efforts to defend its sovereignty during one of the most complex and brutal conflicts in post-Cold War Europe. Despite facing significant disadvantages, the ARBiH played a central role in resisting aggression and preserving a multiethnic Bosnia.

The army’s actions, along with those of other forces, have been subject to international scrutiny regarding compliance with international humanitarian law, with investigations into alleged war crimes committed during the conflict.

---

### Conclusion

The Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina was a crucial military and political institution during the Bosnian War, embodying the struggle to maintain a unified, sovereign state amidst ethnic conflict and external aggression. Its formation, wartime role, and eventual integration into a unified military structure reflect the broader processes of conflict, peacebuilding, and nation-building in Bosnia and Herzegovina.
