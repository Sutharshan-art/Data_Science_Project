**Aisha Mohammed (Ethiopian Politician)**

**Aisha Mohammed** is an Ethiopian engineer and politician who has held several high-ranking positions in the federal government of Ethiopia. She is known for her trailblazing role as one of the first women to hold ministerial office in traditionally male-dominated sectors such as defense and infrastructure. Aisha’s appointments to various cabinet posts reflect both her technical background and the political shift toward gender inclusion and reform under the administration of **Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed**.

### Early Life and Education

Aisha Mohammed was born in **1979** in **Afar Region**, one of Ethiopia’s most remote and least developed areas. She belongs to the **Afar ethnic group**, a community known for its strong cultural identity and historical marginalization in Ethiopian politics. Her ascent to national prominence marked a symbolic and practical inclusion of underrepresented regional and ethnic voices in the federal system.

She studied **civil engineering**, receiving her degree from **Mekelle University**, one of Ethiopia’s leading technical institutions. Her academic training in engineering formed the foundation for her early career and contributed to her credibility as a technocratic leader in infrastructure-related ministries.

### Early Career and Political Involvement

Before entering national politics, Aisha worked on development projects, primarily in the areas of **urban infrastructure**, **construction**, and **regional development**. Her background in civil engineering and public administration positioned her as a competent technocrat during a time when Ethiopia was pursuing ambitious national development goals, including infrastructure expansion and urbanization.

She entered politics as a member of the **Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front (EPRDF)** coalition, which governed the country from 1991 to 2019. She later aligned with the **Prosperity Party**, formed in 2019 as a successor to the EPRDF under Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed’s leadership.

### Minister of Defense (2018–2019)

Aisha Mohammed made history in **October 2018** when she was appointed **Minister of Defense**, becoming the **first woman in Ethiopia's history** to hold the position. Her appointment was part of a broader gender-balanced cabinet reshuffle announced by Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed, who pledged to advance women's representation in government.

As Defense Minister, Aisha oversaw the Ethiopian National Defense Force (ENDF) during a period of political liberalization and security realignment. Her tenure occurred amid widespread reforms, including the release of political prisoners, peace talks with neighboring Eritrea, and internal unrest triggered by ethnic tensions and demands for autonomy.

Though her time in the position was relatively brief—she was replaced in **April 2019**—her appointment signaled a departure from traditional gender roles in Ethiopia's security sector and served as a symbol of the administration's commitment to inclusion and reform.

### Minister of Urban Development and Construction (Multiple Terms)

Prior to and following her tenure in defense, Aisha served as **Minister of Urban Development and Construction**, a role well aligned with her engineering background. She was first appointed to the ministry in **2015** under Prime Minister Hailemariam Desalegn and was reappointed by Abiy Ahmed after her tenure at the Defense Ministry ended.

In this position, Aisha was responsible for formulating policies related to **housing**, **urban planning**, **construction regulation**, and **infrastructure development**, which were central to Ethiopia’s economic transformation agenda. Her ministry played a key role in Ethiopia’s **Growth and Transformation Plans (GTPs)**, aimed at expanding access to affordable housing and developing secondary cities to alleviate pressure on Addis Ababa.

She also focused on improving urban governance, modernizing construction codes, and promoting job creation through construction projects, which are a major source of employment in Ethiopia’s rapidly urbanizing society.

### Role in Gender and Regional Representation

Aisha Mohammed has been widely recognized for breaking barriers as a **female politician from a peripheral region**. Her appointments to senior government roles reflect a broader effort to make Ethiopian politics more representative of its diverse population. Under the Abiy administration, the federal government has taken steps to include more women and regional figures in governance, partly in response to long-standing critiques of centralization and exclusion.

She has also been involved in initiatives aimed at empowering women in engineering and public service, serving as a role model for younger generations of Ethiopian women aspiring to leadership roles.

### Political Context and Legacy

Aisha’s political career has unfolded during a period of significant transition in Ethiopian politics. The move from authoritarian developmentalism to a more open and pluralistic political environment under Prime Minister Abiy has created both opportunities and challenges for public officials. Aisha’s ability to navigate multiple cabinet roles reflects both her technocratic expertise and her political adaptability.

Her presence in high-profile ministries also serves as a barometer for Ethiopia’s ongoing experiment with gender parity and federal inclusiveness. While her impact on specific policies continues to be evaluated, Aisha remains a key figure in Ethiopia’s contemporary political landscape.

### Conclusion

Aisha Mohammed's career illustrates the intersection of technical competence, political reform, and gender inclusion in Ethiopia's evolving governance framework. As one of the few women to lead a defense ministry globally and a prominent voice in urban development, her role underscores the increasing visibility and importance of women and regional actors in Ethiopian federal politics.
