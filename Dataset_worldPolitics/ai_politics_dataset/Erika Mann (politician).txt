**Erika Mann** (born 2 November 1950, Leipzig) is a German politician and public policy expert who served as a Member of the European Parliament (MEP) for the Social Democratic Party of Germany (SPD) from 1994 to 2009. During her fifteen-year tenure in Strasbourg and Brussels, Mann played a leading role in shaping European trade, digital economy, and transatlantic relations. Following her parliamentary career, she transitioned to influential roles in industry associations and corporate public affairs, notably at the Computer & Communications Industry Association (CCIA) and Facebook’s Brussels office, before becoming a senior policy advisor in a major international law firm.

### Early Life and Education

Born in Leipzig in the German Democratic Republic, Mann grew up amid the Cold War division of Germany. After moving to West Germany, she pursued higher education in social pedagogy at the University of Hanover, earning a Diplom-Pädagogin degree. Her early exposure to the political landscape of a reunified Germany informed her decision to join the SPD in 1972, at a time when the party was redefining its platform under Willy Brandt’s Ostpolitik legacy.

### Entry into Politics and Local Engagement

In the 1970s and 1980s, Mann became active in local party structures in Lower Saxony. From 1990 to 1996 she served on the district council (Kreistag) of the Northeim district, focusing on education, social welfare, and cultural affairs. Her local experience laid the groundwork for her successful candidacy to the European Parliament in the 1994 elections, as the SPD sought to strengthen its representation in Brussels ahead of the EU’s post-Maastricht expansion.

### European Parliament Career (1994–2009)

Elected on the SPD list in 1994, Mann joined the Socialist Group in the European Parliament. She quickly established herself on the **Committee on International Trade (INTA)**, where she coordinated the Socialist Group’s positions and served as lead negotiator on key files. As rapporteur for the 2000 **eCommerce Directive**, Mann helped craft the legal framework that underpins cross-border digital services within the EU, balancing free-market principles with consumer protection and data privacy considerations.

In addition to her primary role on INTA, Mann was a substitute member of the **Committee on Industry, Research and Energy (ITRE)** and the **Committee on Budgetary Control**, where she contributed to oversight of EU research funding and industrial policy. Throughout the late 1990s and early 2000s, she championed legislation to harmonize telecommunication standards, promote investment in broadband infrastructure, and liberalize the digital marketplace.

Mann also held numerous delegation posts, reflecting her commitment to widening Europe’s external partnerships. She chaired the **Delegation for relations with Mexico** (2005–2009), steered parliamentary engagement with South-Asian countries, and served on joint parliamentary assemblies for the WTO. These roles underscored her long-standing advocacy for a “transatlantic market” concept, which later influenced negotiations on trade agreements between the EU and the United States.

### Political Context and Key Contributions

Mann’s parliamentary career spanned a period of deepening European integration: the introduction of the euro (1999), successive EU enlargements (up to 2004 and 2007), and the WTO’s Doha Round launch (2001). Against this backdrop, she emphasized that robust trade policy must be coupled with strong social and environmental safeguards. Her push for transparency in trade negotiations and for stakeholder consultations prefigured later reforms in EU decision-making.

The rise of the internet economy presented new regulatory challenges. Mann’s work on the eCommerce Directive and on early data protection discussions laid the foundation for subsequent digital single-market legislation. She argued that Europe needed to harness technological innovation to remain competitive globally, while preserving citizens’ rights.

### Later Career and Industry Engagement

After narrowly losing her seat in the 2009 European elections, Mann was appointed **Executive Vice President of the CCIA** in Washington, D.C., where she led the Brussels office (2009–2011). In this capacity, she represented major technology companies in discussions on trade, intellectual property, and cybersecurity.

In October 2011, Mann joined **Facebook’s Brussels public policy team** as Managing Director, serving until December 2015. She navigated the company through early debates on data portability, net neutrality, and the EU’s Digital Agenda. Under her leadership, Facebook strengthened its engagement with EU institutions and participated in multi-stakeholder dialogues on internet governance.

Since 2016, Mann has been a **senior policy advisor** at a leading international law firm’s EU Public Policy Practice Group, where she advises clients on regulatory strategy in trade, digital markets, and telecommunications. She also sits on the **GNSO Council of ICANN**, contributing to global domain name system policy, and participates in advisory boards for several think tanks focused on transatlantic relations.

### Legacy and Impact

Erika Mann’s career bridges politics, industry, and advocacy at a critical juncture in Europe’s evolution. In the European Parliament, she was a pioneer of digital trade policy and a steadfast proponent of social-market principles in EU external relations. Her subsequent roles in industry associations and corporate public affairs reflect a seamless move from policymaking to policy shaping, underscoring the interconnectedness of government, business, and civil society in the digital age. Today, her contributions continue to influence debates on Europe’s digital single market, data governance, and the future of transatlantic cooperation.
