**Ian James Marshall** (born 1968) is a farmer and former politician from Markethill, County Armagh, Northern Ireland. A dairy and beef farmer by profession, Marshall first gained prominence through his leadership of the Ulster Farmers’ Union (UFU) and later became the first unionist elected to Seanad Éireann in more than eighty years. Initially serving as an independent, he subsequently joined the Ulster Unionist Party (UUP) before reverting to independent status.

### Early life and education

Marshall was born in Markethill in 1968 into a third-generation farming family. He studied at Greenmount Agricultural College and later completed further agricultural and business management training at Ulster University. He also attended Babson College in the United States, where he focused on farm management and business development. His upbringing in a rural, unionist community shaped his lifelong commitment to agricultural advocacy and informed his belief in the importance of cross-border cooperation on the island of Ireland.

### Farming career and Ulster Farmers’ Union

Marshall ran a mixed dairy and beef enterprise on his family farm. In April 2014, he was elected President of the Ulster Farmers’ Union, a position he held until April 2016. During his presidency, he navigated challenges such as the Renewable Heat Incentive controversy, defending legitimate investment in agricultural renewables while condemning instances of fraud. In 2017, he led the UFU in a successful judicial review against the Department of Agriculture’s decision to reduce his Common Agricultural Policy grant following a nitrate pollution incident on his farm, arguing that the infraction had been unintentional.

### Institute for Global Food Security

After completing his term with the UFU, Marshall joined Queen’s University Belfast’s Institute for Global Food Security as Business Development Manager. In this role, he fostered partnerships between academia and industry, promoting sustainable agriculture practices and bolstering food supply resilience. His work involved coordinating research collaborations aimed at improving crop yields and reducing environmental impact.

### Seanad Éireann (2018–2020)

In April 2018, Marshall won a by-election for the Agricultural Panel of the 25th Seanad, filling the vacancy left by Denis Landy. His candidacy was notable for securing support across traditional party lines, including endorsements from both Fine Gael and Sinn Féin, reflecting his cross-community appeal. Sitting as an Independent Unionist, he was the first unionist member of the Oireachtas since the early 20th century. During his tenure, he championed rural interests, advocated for increased agricultural investment, and worked to strengthen North–South cooperation. He contested the subsequent Seanad election in February 2020 but was not re-elected.

### Party affiliation and later political activity

Long accustomed to independent status, Marshall joined the Ulster Unionist Party on 27 July 2021, citing its moderate stance and focus on economic development, healthcare, and housing over divisive identity politics. He stood as the UUP candidate for West Tyrone in the 2022 Northern Ireland Assembly election, securing 4.1% of first-preference votes but failing to win a seat. In August 2023, he resigned from the UUP and returned to independent unionist status, citing a desire to focus on cross-community agricultural and rural development issues free from party constraints.

### Political views

Marshall has maintained a broadly unionist outlook while emphasizing the necessity of cross-border collaboration. He was an outspoken opponent of Brexit, warning of its potential negative impact on agriculture and rural economies in both Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland. In the Seanad, he worked with representatives from across the political spectrum, promoting initiatives in agricultural innovation, environmental stewardship, and reconciliation between different communities on the island.

### Honours and recognition

In the 2022 New Year Honours, Marshall was appointed an Officer of the Order of the British Empire (OBE) for his public and political service. This accolade recognised his decades-long advocacy for farmers, his leadership during crises affecting the agricultural sector, and his efforts to foster dialogue between unionist and nationalist communities.

### Personal life

Marshall continues to reside in County Armagh, where he manages his family farm. He is married with children and remains actively involved in community and cross-border agricultural initiatives. Beyond farming and politics, he contributes as a speaker and advisor on topics such as food security, rural development, and sustainable agriculture.
