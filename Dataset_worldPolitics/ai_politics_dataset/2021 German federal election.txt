**2021 German Federal Election**

The **2021 German federal election** was held on **September 26, 2021**, to elect the **20th Bundestag**, Germany’s federal parliament. The election marked a historic transition: Chancellor **Angela Merkel**, who had served four consecutive terms since 2005, chose not to seek re-election, opening the door to a post-Merkel era. The vote took place amid significant global and domestic challenges, including the COVID-19 pandemic, climate change, and shifting geopolitical dynamics.

---

### Political Context

The election occurred after 16 years of Merkel-era leadership under a **grand coalition** between the **Christian Democratic Union/Christian Social Union (CDU/CSU)** and the **Social Democratic Party (SPD)**. While this arrangement ensured stability, both parties suffered declining support and faced mounting pressure to adopt clearer ideological identities and address emerging crises. The pandemic, especially around vaccine rollout and public health policy, intensified these debates.

On a broader scale, Germany confronted new global responsibilities: rising tensions with **Russia** following the annexation of Crimea, and internal discussions about Europe’s strategic future in light of Brexit and shifts in U.S. leadership.

---

### Major Parties & Leaders

* **Social Democratic Party (SPD)** – *Chancellor candidate: Olaf Scholz*. Positioned as a moderate, Scholz emphasized continuity, economic stewardship, and a socially responsible recovery from the pandemic.
* **CDU/CSU (Union)** – *Chancellor candidate: Armin Laschet*. Laschet aimed to maintain Merkel’s legacy but faced criticism for lacking clear vision and momentum.
* **Alliance 90/The Greens** – *Lead candidates: Annalena Baerbock and Robert Habeck*. The Greens ran on a climate-first agenda, advocating rapid green transition and technological modernization.
* **Free Democratic Party (FDP)** – *Chancellor candidate: Christian Lindner*. Center-right to libertarian, the FDP emphasized digital innovation, fiscal responsibility, and civil liberties.
* **Alternative for Germany (AfD)** – *Lead candidates: Alice Weidel and Tino Chrupalla*. The far-right AfD campaigned on anti-immigration and euroskeptic platforms, most strongly in the eastern states.
* **The Left (Die Linke)** – Positioned on the left spectrum with social justice and anti-militarist policies.

---

### Electoral System

Germany employs a **mixed-member proportional representation system**. Each voter casts two votes: one for a **direct candidate** in their district (first-past-the-post) and one for a party list. A party must exceed **5% of the second vote** or win at least three direct mandates to enter the Bundestag. The 2021 Bundestag grew to **736 seats**, due to overhang and leveling seats.

---

### Results

* **SPD**: 25.7% – 206 seats (largest party for first time since 2002)
* **CDU/CSU**: 24.1% – 198 seats
* **Greens**: 14.7% – 118 seats
* **FDP**: 11.4% – 91 seats
* **AfD**: 10.4% – 83 seats
* **The Left**: 4.9% – 39 seats (entered via direct mandates)
* **SSW** (South Schleswig Voters): 0.1% – 1 seat (exempt from threshold)

The election yielded a six-party Bundestag and a **76.6% voter turnout**.

The SPD surged by \~5 percentage points—their best showing since 2005—while Union suffered a historic decline. The Greens more than doubled their vote share, and the FDP returned to prominence. The AfD also made gains, while The Left narrowly missed the threshold but retained seats.

---

### Coalition Negotiations

Neither major party could govern alone; the CDU explicitly ruled out joining another grand coalition. Attention turned to smaller parties as potential partners. Two coalition models were debated:

* **Traffic-light coalition**: SPD (red), Greens (green), and FDP (yellow)
* **Jamaica coalition**: CDU/CSU (black), Greens, and FDP

Ultimately, the traffic-light model prevailed. Exploratory talks among SPD, Greens, and FDP led to formal negotiations. On **November 24**, they reached a coalition agreement. The new government was inaugurated on **December 8, 2021**, with **Olaf Scholz** elected Chancellor and heading a three-party coalition government.

---

### Historical Significance

The election marked the end of the Merkel era, ushering in a new political landscape. For the SPD, returning to the top spot revived its center-left influence. For the CDU, the result indicated a need for internal renewal and strategic reorientation.

The success of both the Greens and FDP reflected increased voter demand for environmental action, digital reform, and economic modernization. Simultaneously, the AfD’s performance highlighted enduring political polarization and the fragmented party system.

Germany entered the election with expectations focused on climate strategy, pandemic recovery, and a stronger European role. The emergent coalition reflected liberal, ecologist, and moderate perspectives, poised to advance these priorities.

---

### Immediate Post-Election Challenges

The new government faced significant tasks:

1. **Pandemic management** and vaccine rollout logistics.
2. **Climate policy implementation**, including achieving carbon neutrality.
3. **Digital transformation**, specifically broadband expansion and infrastructure.
4. **Social equity**, especially in housing, education, and public services.
5. **European and foreign policy**, including relations with Russia, China, and U.S. alliances.

---

### Legacy

The **2021 German federal election** stands as a pivotal turning point for Germany’s 21st-century trajectory. It signaled the transition from the Merkel era, reinforced a multi-party system, and set the country on a path toward coalition-driven policymaking with renewed focus on sustainability, technological progress, and social cohesion. The results reshaped Germany’s domestic agenda and positioned it in its evolving role within the European Union and global affairs.
