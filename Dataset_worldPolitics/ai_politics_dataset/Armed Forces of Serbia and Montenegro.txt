**Armed Forces of Serbia and Montenegro**

The **Armed Forces of Serbia and Montenegro** (Serbian: *Оружане снаге Србије и Црне Горе*, *Oružane snage Srbije i Crne Gore*) were the unified military forces of the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (FRY) from 1992 until 2003, and subsequently of the State Union of Serbia and Montenegro until their dissolution in 2006. These armed forces represented the combined military capabilities of the two constituent republics—Serbia and Montenegro—and were a key institution in the political and security landscape of the post-Yugoslav Balkans.

---

### Historical and Political Context

The Armed Forces of Serbia and Montenegro emerged from the breakup of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (SFRY) in the early 1990s. Following the declarations of independence by Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Macedonia, Serbia and Montenegro formed the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia in April 1992, seeking to continue the Yugoslav state in a reduced form.

The new federation inherited the bulk of the former Yugoslav People’s Army (JNA), which had been involved in the wars that engulfed the region during the 1990s. The military forces were restructured to reflect the new political reality of a two-republic federation facing international isolation, internal challenges, and regional conflicts.

---

### Formation and Structure

The Armed Forces were officially established under the name **Vojska Jugoslavije (VJ)**, or Yugoslav Army, encompassing ground forces, air forces, and naval components. The military structure integrated personnel, equipment, and bases from the remnants of the JNA located within Serbia and Montenegro.

The forces were organized into:

* **Ground Forces:** Comprising mechanized infantry, armor, artillery, and specialized units tasked with territorial defense and internal security.
* **Air Force and Air Defense:** Equipped with Soviet-era fighter jets, helicopters, and air defense missile systems.
* **Navy:** Primarily based in the Bay of Kotor, with a fleet of patrol boats and coastal defense assets.

Command and control were centralized in Belgrade, the federal capital, reflecting Serbia’s dominant position in the union.

---

### Role in Regional Conflicts and Security

Throughout the 1990s, the Armed Forces of Serbia and Montenegro were deeply involved in the Yugoslav Wars, including conflicts in Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and later Kosovo. The military supported Serbian paramilitary groups and government forces, often under the scrutiny and criticism of the international community for alleged involvement in war crimes and ethnic cleansing.

The 1999 **NATO bombing campaign** against the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia significantly affected the armed forces, targeting military infrastructure and compelling adjustments in defense posture and strategy.

Following the Kosovo conflict and the fall of Slobodan Milošević in 2000, the armed forces faced pressure to reform and professionalize amid a changing political environment and international engagement.

---

### Political Significance and Reform

The Armed Forces of Serbia and Montenegro were not only a defense institution but also a symbol of national sovereignty and political power during a period marked by upheaval and transition. Their role was closely tied to the policies of the federal government, which navigated between nationalist agendas and international pressures.

Post-2000 reforms aimed to modernize the military, improve civilian oversight, and align with Euro-Atlantic security standards. Cooperation with NATO’s Partnership for Peace program was initiated as part of efforts to rebuild international relations.

---

### Dissolution and Succession

In 2003, the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia was reconstituted as the **State Union of Serbia and Montenegro**, maintaining the joint armed forces under the new political framework. However, growing political divergence, especially Montenegro’s increasing push for independence, created tensions affecting military cohesion.

Following Montenegro’s referendum and declaration of independence in June 2006, the joint Armed Forces of Serbia and Montenegro were dissolved. Each new sovereign state established its own separate military forces: the **Serbian Armed Forces** and the **Armed Forces of Montenegro**.

---

### Legacy and Impact

The Armed Forces of Serbia and Montenegro played a crucial role during a complex and turbulent period in the Balkans. They were instrumental in both defending territorial claims and engaging in regional conflicts, with their actions shaping the course of the Yugoslav wars and subsequent peace processes.

The military’s transition from a large conscript force to a smaller, more professionalized army reflected broader political changes and the challenges of adapting to new national realities and international norms.

---

### Conclusion

The Armed Forces of Serbia and Montenegro were a pivotal institution in the post-Yugoslav era, reflecting the political ambitions, security challenges, and ethnic complexities of the region. Their evolution, involvement in regional conflicts, and eventual dissolution mirror the broader processes of state formation and realignment in the Western Balkans during the late 20th and early 21st centuries.
