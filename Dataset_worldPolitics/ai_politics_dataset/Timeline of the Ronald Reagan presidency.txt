**Timeline of the Ronald Reagan Presidency (1981–1989)**

Ronald W. Reagan, the 40th President of the United States, served two terms from January 20, 1981, to January 20, 1989. His administration was characterized by conservative domestic reforms—dubbed “Reaganomics”—and an assertive Cold War strategy that contributed to the Soviet Union’s eventual collapse.

---

### 1981

* **January 20:** Inaugurated; delivers inaugural address pledging to “restore us to ourselves.”
* **February 6:** Survives assassination attempt by John Hinckley Jr.; Reagan’s recovery bolsters public sympathy.
* **March 30:** Signs the Economic Recovery Tax Act (ERTA), cutting marginal income tax rates by 25% over three years to stimulate growth.
* **August 3:** Air Traffic Controllers Strike—Reagan fires over 11,000 striking PATCO workers for refusing to return, signaling a tougher stance on organized labor.

### 1982

* **January–July:** Deep recession; unemployment peaks at 10.8% in December.
* **August 13:** Signs the Tax Equity and Fiscal Responsibility Act, rolling back portions of ERTA to reduce deficit.
* **October:** Economic recovery begins, marking the end of stagflation and the start of sustained growth.

### 1983

* **March 23:** Delivers address proposing the Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI), a space‐based missile defense system aimed at neutralizing Soviet ICBMs.
* **October 25:** Orders U.S. invasion of Grenada (Operation Urgent Fury) to protect American students and prevent Marxist government consolidation in the Caribbean. The intervention receives mixed international reactions but high domestic approval.

### 1984

* **July 13–16:** Republican National Convention renominates Reagan and Vice President George H. W. Bush.
* **November 6:** Re‐elected in a landslide, defeating Democrat Walter Mondale with 525 electoral votes—the largest margin since 1820. Emphasizes strong defense and economic recovery.

### 1985

* **January 21:** Second inaugural address highlights “peace through strength” and expanding freedom globally.
* **September 30:** International espionage trial concludes when Jonathan Pollard, a U.S. Naval intelligence analyst, is arrested for spying for Israel—straining an important Middle East relationship.
* **November 19–20:** First Reagan–Gorbachev summit in Geneva lays groundwork for arms-control dialogue.

### 1986

* **February 27:** Federal budget agreement includes modest tax increases to control deficits, breaking Reagan’s “no new taxes” pledge.
* **April 26:** Signs the Comprehensive Anti-Apartheid Act, imposing sanctions on South Africa despite his veto, which is overridden by Congress.
* **October 11:** Signs the Tax Reform Act of 1986, broadening the tax base by eliminating deductions and lowering top rates from 50% to 28%.
* **October 23:** Iran–Contra scandal surfaces following an explosion of a contra supply plane in Nicaragua, revealing clandestine weapons-for-hostages deals in violation of congressional prohibition.

### 1987

* **March 8:** Delivers “Tear down this wall” speech at the Berlin Wall’s Brandenburg Gate, calling on Gorbachev to allow greater freedom in Eastern Europe.
* **June 12:** Signs the Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty with Gorbachev in Washington, D.C., eliminating an entire class of nuclear missiles and marking a major Cold War thaw.

### 1988

* **August 16–19:** Republican National Convention nominates Reagan’s Vice President, George H. W. Bush, who pledges “continuity” with Reagan policies.
* **September 1:** Signs the Civil Liberties Act of 1988, apologizing for and compensating Japanese‐American internment during World War II.
* **November 8:** George H. W. Bush elected President, winning 426 electoral votes, ensuring Republican control continues.

### 1989

* **January 11:** “Lame‐duck” State of the Union emphasizes support for freedom movements in Eastern Europe and the Soviet Union.
* **January 20:** Leaves office with mixed economic indicators—GDP growth averaging 3.5% annually but deficits tripled—and a Cold War winding down under Bush and Gorbachev’s détente.

---

**Political and Historical Context**
The Reagan presidency reshaped American politics through supply‐side economic policies aimed at reducing government’s role, deregulating industries, and combating inflation. His administration oversaw a sustained economic expansion following early recessions, though federal deficits rose significantly. On the global stage, Reagan adopted an assertive anti‐communist posture—escalating defense spending, promoting SDI, and forging alliances through NATO—while simultaneously engaging in landmark arms‐control agreements with the Soviet Union. His rhetorical emphasis on American exceptionalism and freedom bolstered dissident movements behind the Iron Curtain. Despite controversies—such as the Iran–Contra affair—Reagan’s legacy endures in debates over the role of government, taxation, and the United States’ global leadership at the close of the 20th century.
