**Peter Robinson (speechwriter)**

**Peter Mark Robinson** (born April 18, 1957) is an American author, political commentator, and former presidential speechwriter best known for writing the famous phrase “Mr. Gorbachev, tear down this wall!” delivered by President **Ronald Reagan** in a speech at the Brandenburg Gate in West Berlin in 1987. His work as a speechwriter placed him at the center of pivotal moments in Cold War history and reflected the Reagan administration’s ideological messaging and rhetorical style.

Following his tenure in the White House, Robinson pursued careers in academia and media, becoming a fellow at **Stanford University's Hoover Institution** and a prominent voice in conservative political commentary. His contributions to presidential rhetoric and his documentation of political history have had a lasting influence on public discourse.

---

### Early Life and Education

Peter Robinson was born on April 18, 1957, in Vestal, New York. He graduated from **Dartmouth College** in 1979, where he studied English literature and developed an early interest in politics and public policy. After completing his undergraduate degree, Robinson began working in media and communications before entering the Reagan White House in the early 1980s.

He later earned an MBA from the **Stanford Graduate School of Business** in 1990, signaling a transition toward policy research and academic engagement.

---

### Role in the Reagan Administration

Robinson joined the **White House Office of Speechwriting** in 1983 during President Ronald Reagan’s second term. As a junior speechwriter under chief speechwriter **Anthony Dolan**, he contributed to a variety of presidential addresses, including radio talks, public remarks, and major policy speeches. His writing was noted for its accessible tone, clarity, and ability to communicate complex policy ideas in a compelling narrative form.

Robinson's most enduring contribution came in 1987 with the drafting of President Reagan's speech at the **Brandenburg Gate** in West Berlin, commemorating the 750th anniversary of the city. At a time when Cold War tensions were easing but the **Berlin Wall** still physically and symbolically divided East and West Germany, the speech became a defining moment in Reagan’s foreign policy rhetoric.

---

### “Mr. Gorbachev, Tear Down This Wall!”

The Brandenburg Gate speech, delivered on **June 12, 1987**, became historically significant due to the direct and confrontational appeal to **Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev**. Robinson’s draft included the now-famous line: *“Mr. Gorbachev, tear down this wall!”*—a challenge for the Soviet Union to prove its commitment to openness and reform by dismantling the Berlin Wall.

Initially, the line was met with resistance from senior White House officials and diplomats, who feared it would provoke Soviet backlash or be diplomatically inappropriate. However, President Reagan insisted on keeping the phrase, believing it expressed the desires of the German people and the principles of American foreign policy.

The speech received moderate attention at the time but gained greater historical weight after the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989. Robinson’s authorship of the phrase solidified his reputation as one of the most impactful speechwriters of the Cold War era.

---

### Post-White House Career

After leaving the White House, Robinson returned to academia, attending Stanford University. In 1993, he joined the **Hoover Institution**, a public policy think tank affiliated with Stanford, where he became a research fellow. His work at Hoover has included analysis of presidential rhetoric, American political ideology, and the history of the Cold War.

Robinson has written several books, including:

* ***How Ronald Reagan Changed My Life*** (2003), a memoir reflecting on his time in the Reagan White House and the former president’s leadership style and principles.
* ***It’s My Party: A Republican’s Messy Love Affair with the GOP*** (2000), which explores shifts within the Republican Party and the conservative movement.

In addition to his writing, Robinson is the host of **“Uncommon Knowledge,”** a long-running interview program produced by the Hoover Institution. The show features in-depth discussions with scholars, policymakers, and public intellectuals on topics related to history, politics, economics, and culture.

---

### Political and Historical Significance

Peter Robinson’s career exemplifies the influence of presidential speechwriters in shaping public discourse and foreign policy messaging. His role in articulating the Reagan administration’s stance on communism, freedom, and democracy contributed to the ideological clarity that defined U.S. policy during the final decade of the Cold War.

The Brandenburg Gate speech is now considered one of the most memorable moments of 20th-century political oratory, and Robinson’s authorship of its central line remains a key reference point in discussions about political communication, presidential leadership, and rhetorical strategy.

---

### Legacy

Robinson continues to be a respected figure in conservative intellectual circles, blending policy analysis, historical commentary, and communication expertise. His firsthand experiences within the White House and his commitment to documenting that era have made him a valuable contributor to the study of modern American political history. His career illustrates the enduring impact of rhetoric in diplomacy and domestic affairs, as well as the significance of behind-the-scenes figures in shaping public memory and policy narratives.
