**William Henry Timmons Bush**

**Introduction**
William Henry Timmons Bush (April 14, 1938 – February 27, 2018), often known by his family nickname “Bucky,” was an American business executive and member of the Bush political dynasty. Though never an elected official, his career in finance and fundraising placed him at the intersection of business and politics. As the youngest brother of President George H. W. Bush and uncle of President George W. Bush and Governor Jeb Bush, William H. T. Bush played a significant supporting role in Republican Party politics and civic leadership in Ohio and beyond.

**Early Life and Education**
Born in Greenwich, Connecticut, William H. T. Bush was the youngest of six children of Prescott Sheldon Bush, a U.S. Senator from Connecticut, and Dorothy Walker Bush. Raised in Fairfield, Connecticut, and in Midland, Texas, he graduated from Phillips Academy in Andover, Massachusetts, in 1956. He attended Yale University—alma mater of his brother George H. W. Bush—earning a B.A. in American Studies in 1960. At Yale, he was a member of the Delta Kappa Epsilon fraternity and the Scroll and Key society.

**Business Career**
After serving in the U.S. Army from 1960 to 1962, Bush entered the financial sector. In 1964, he moved to Cleveland, Ohio, joining the Cleveland Trust Company. He rose through the ranks to become Executive Vice President of the holding company, Cleveland Trust Bank, by 1972. In 1983, after the bank’s merger into Ameritrust, he became President and CEO of the newly formed Worthington Trust Company, guiding it through expansion into private banking and wealth management.

In 1987, Bush joined the board of First Interstate Bancorp and was appointed Chairman of First Interstate Bancorp of Ohio. He oversaw operations in the Midwest, leading efforts to integrate California-based First Interstate’s practices with local banking traditions. Under his leadership, the Ohio division expanded its commercial lending and fiduciary services until its acquisition by Firstar Corporation in 1996.

**Political Fundraising and Civic Engagement**
Although not a candidate for public office, William H. T. Bush was a key fundraiser for Republican causes. During his brother George H. W. Bush’s 1988 presidential campaign, he hosted high-dollar events in Cleveland and contributed significantly to campaign finance efforts. In 2000, he served on the Finance Committee for nephew George W. Bush’s presidential campaign, leveraging his Midwest business connections to rally support among Ohio donors.

Bush also engaged in numerous civic and philanthropic initiatives. He was Chairman of the Greater Cleveland Growth Association, advocating for regional economic development. As a trustee of the Cleveland Clinic Foundation, he championed expansion of medical research facilities. He served on the board of the Cleveland Council on World Affairs, helping bring international conferences and diplomatic delegations to the city.

**Role in the United States Olympic Committee**
In 1992, William H. T. Bush was appointed to the board of the United States Olympic Committee (USOC). He became Treasurer in 1994 and Vice President in 1998. He played a central role in fundraising for athlete development programs ahead of the 1996 Atlanta and 2000 Sydney Games, working to expand corporate sponsorships and public-private partnerships. His tenure coincided with increased emphasis on corporate governance reforms within the USOC.

**Family and Personal Life**
Bush married Patricia Redfearn in 1964; the couple had four children: Tricia, Evan, Brian, and Craig. He was known for his quiet demeanor, business acumen, and loyalty to family. Though raised in the Protestant Episcopal Church, he supported various interfaith charitable activities in Cleveland. An avid outdoorsman, he enjoyed fly-fishing in Ohio’s streams and skiing in Colorado.

**Political Context and Influence**
William H. T. Bush’s career exemplifies the nexus of business leadership and political activism characteristic of the late 20th-century Republican establishment. His banking roles in Ohio—a key swing state—paralleled the party’s broader strategy of cultivating Midwest support. By facilitating campaign finance and fostering civic institutions, he contributed indirectly to the Bush family’s political successes: the election of George H. W. Bush in 1988, the contentious 2000 Ohio recount, and Jeb Bush’s prominence in Florida politics.

**Death and Legacy**
Bush died on February 27, 2018, at age 79, after a brief illness. His funeral at St. John’s Episcopal Church in Cleveland was attended by family, business colleagues, and political figures from across the Midwest. He was remembered for his mentorship of young bankers, his stewardship of civic organizations, and his discreet yet effective support of Republican campaigns.

William H. T. Bush’s legacy resides in the institutions he strengthened—regional banks, the Cleveland Clinic, and the US Olympic movement—and in the political infrastructure he helped sustain. As a scion of a political dynasty, he demonstrated how private-sector leadership and strategic fundraising can shape the civic and political landscape without holding elective office.
