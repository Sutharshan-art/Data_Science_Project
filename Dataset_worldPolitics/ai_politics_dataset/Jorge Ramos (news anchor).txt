**Jorge Gilberto Ramos Ávalos** (born March 16, 1958) is a Mexican‐American journalist, author, and news anchor, best known for his work at Univision, where his reporting and interviews have shaped coverage of immigration, U.S.-Latin American relations, and domestic policy issues affecting Hispanic communities. Over a career spanning more than four decades, Ramos has become an influential voice in Hispanic media, advocating for greater representation and accountability in political discourse.

### Early Life and Education

Jorge Ramos was born in Mexico City, the son of Isabel Ávalos and Jorge Ramos Hernández, a noted radio producer. He earned a bachelor’s degree in communications from Universidad Iberoamericana in 1978 and a master’s degree in journalism from Columbia University’s Graduate School of Journalism in 1981. While at Columbia, Ramos focused on comparative media systems and the role of the press in democratizing societies, themes that would inform his later work.

### Entry into Broadcast Journalism

Upon graduation, Ramos returned to Mexico and joined Televisa as a reporter covering political unrest during the late‐1980s student movements. In 1987, he was recruited by the newly launched Spanish‐language network Univision to help expand its news division. Ramos initially served as a general‐assignment correspondent, covering U.S. immigration policy, the war on drugs, and migrant labor issues across the Southwest border.

### Rise to Prominence: Noticiero Univision

In 1988, Ramos became the principal anchor and managing editor of *Noticiero Univision*, then a fledgling nightly news program. Under his stewardship, the broadcast grew into the highest‐ranked Spanish‐language news program in the United States. Ramos introduced in‐depth investigative segments and on‐the‐ground reports, including coverage of the 1994 Zapatista uprising in Chiapas, Mexico, and the 1996 U.S. welfare reform debate, which disproportionately affected Latino families.

### Political Coverage and Interviews

Ramos has conducted high‐profile interviews with U.S. presidents (Bill Clinton, George W. Bush, Barack Obama, and Donald Trump) and Latin American heads of state, pressing them on immigration reform, human rights, and economic inequality. His persistent questioning of President George W. Bush on undocumented migration in 2001 and his 2015 confrontation with Congressman Darrell Issa over the Venezuelan refugee crisis underscored his advocacy for Hispanic communities. His 2015 interview with President Barack Obama on the Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA) program helped elevate the issue nationally.

### Advocacy and Controversies

Beyond the newsroom, Ramos has advocated for comprehensive immigration reform and greater media diversity. In 2014, he led a *Noticiero Univision* campaign, “Somos un solo pueblo” (“We Are One People”), highlighting immigrant contributions to the U.S. economy. His insistence on activist‐style journalism has drawn criticism from conservative commentators, who accused him of bias after he publicly denounced the Trump administration’s family‐separation policy in 2018.

### Awards and Recognitions

Ramos has received numerous honors, including eight Emmy Awards, the Maria Moors Cabot Prize from Columbia University (1991) for promoting inter‐American understanding, and the Walter Cronkite Award for Excellence in Journalism (2013). He was named one of TIME magazine’s “100 Most Influential People” in 2018, cited for elevating Hispanic issues into the mainstream political debate.

### Writing and Publications

An accomplished author, Ramos has written several books on immigration and Hispanic identity in the United States. His 2005 memoir, *Una vida*: A Memoir, examines his journey from Mexico City to American television, while *Stranger: The Challenge of a Latino Immigrant in the Trump Era* (2018) reflects on shifting attitudes toward immigrants in national politics. His essays on press freedom and democracy have appeared in *The New York Times* and *Foreign Affairs*.

### Historical and Political Context

Ramos’s career coincided with the dramatic growth of the Hispanic population in the United States—from 22.4 million in 1990 to over 60 million by 2020—and the community’s increasing political significance. As the first Spanish‐language anchor to question U.S. leaders on prime‐time television, Ramos helped legitimize Hispanic concerns in mainstream media. His emphasis on accountability journalism reflects broader debates over media representation, demographic shifts, and the role of ethnic press in shaping policy on immigration, healthcare, and education.

### Legacy and Continuing Influence

Now based in Miami, Florida, Ramos continues to anchor *Noticiero Univision* and to speak on panels about the future of journalism and the rights of immigrants. He serves on the board of the International Center for Journalists and mentors young journalists through workshops facilitated by the National Association of Hispanic Journalists. His insistence on confronting power and amplifying marginalized voices has cemented his reputation as a leading figure in American political media, whose work has influenced both policy makers and the broader public discourse on issues central to the 21st‐century United States.
