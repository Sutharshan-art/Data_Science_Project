**George Walker Bush** (born July 6, 1946) is an American politician who served as the 43rd President of the United States from 2001 to 2009. A member of the Republican Party and scion of the Bush political family, his presidency was dominated by the global response to terrorism, military interventions in Afghanistan and Iraq, and significant domestic policy initiatives on taxation, education, and healthcare.

**Early Life and Career**
Born in New Haven, Connecticut, Bush is the eldest son of President George H. W. Bush and First Lady Barbara Bush. Raised in Texas, he graduated from Phillips Academy in Andover, Massachusetts, in 1964. After serving as a pilot in the Texas Air National Guard during the Vietnam era, he earned a Bachelor of Arts in history from Yale University in 1968 and an MBA from Harvard Business School in 1975. Bush spent the following decades in the oil and gas industry in Texas and co‐founded the Texas Rangers baseball franchise, gaining management experience and regional name recognition.

**Governor of Texas (1995–2000)**
Building on his family’s political legacy, Bush was elected Governor of Texas in 1994 and re‐elected in 1998. He pursued a platform of education reform—championing public‐school accountability measures and promoting standardized testing—and implemented tax cuts, tort‐reform measures, and budget surpluses. Under his leadership, Texas saw economic growth, although critics pointed to disparities in rural and urban school funding. His tenure as governor positioned him as a rising star within the Republican Party and laid the groundwork for a presidential bid.

**2000 Presidential Campaign**
Announcing his candidacy in June 1999, Bush narrowly secured the Republican nomination amid a competitive primary against Senator John McCain. He pledged “compassionate conservatism,” combining tax relief with investments in education and faith‐based social programs. In the general election, Bush faced Democratic nominee Al Gore. After Election Day, the outcome hinged on Florida’s electoral votes and a protracted recount. In **Bush v. Gore**, the Supreme Court halted the recount, effectively awarding Florida to Bush and, with it, the presidency by an Electoral College margin of 271 to 266 despite a popular‐vote deficit.

**Presidency (2001–2009)**

* **September 11 and the War on Terror**: Two months into his term, the 9/11 attacks reshaped U.S. priorities. President Bush launched the War on Terror, invoking the Authorization for Use of Military Force against those responsible. He oversaw the invasion of Afghanistan in October 2001 to dismantle al‐Qaeda and remove the Taliban from power.
* **Domestic Security and Policy**: Domestically, Bush signed the USA PATRIOT Act (2001), expanding law‐enforcement authority to counter terrorism. He established the Department of Homeland Security (2002) and implemented the controversial policy of “enhanced interrogation” of detainees at Guantánamo Bay, drawing widespread debate over civil liberties.
* **Iraq War**: Citing Iraq’s alleged weapons of mass destruction, Bush led a coalition to invade Iraq in March 2003. The initial military victory gave way to prolonged insurgency and sectarian violence. The absence of discovered WMDs and the human and financial costs of occupation eroded U.S. and international support, becoming a defining controversy of his presidency.
* **Domestic Legislation**: On the home front, Bush championed two major tax‐cut packages (2001, 2003), reducing income‐tax rates and capital‐gains taxes. He signed the No Child Left Behind Act (2002), reauthorizing the Elementary and Secondary Education Act with greater accountability through standardized testing. In 2003, he created the Medicare Part D prescription‐drug benefit, expanding federal entitlement programs despite Republican concerns about federal spending.
* **Hurricane Katrina**: In August 2005, Hurricane Katrina devastated the Gulf Coast. The federal response—criticized as slow and uncoordinated—became a focal point of public dissatisfaction, contributing to declining approval ratings.
* **Second Term and Financial Crisis**: Elected to a second term in 2004 against Senator John Kerry, Bush continued military operations in Iraq and Afghanistan. In his final year, the 2007–2008 global financial crisis erupted. His administration enacted the Emergency Economic Stabilization Act (2008), authorizing \$700 billion in Troubled Asset Relief Program funds to stabilize financial institutions.

**Post‐Presidency**
Leaving office in January 2009 with low approval ratings, President Bush returned to Dallas. He established the George W. Bush Presidential Center at Southern Methodist University, which houses his library, museum, and the Bush Institute policy arm. He has since engaged in veterans’ advocacy, global health initiatives (notably malaria prevention), and bipartisan efforts on immigration reform and civic education alongside former President Bill Clinton.

**Historical Significance**
George W. Bush’s presidency is marked by a definitive shift in American foreign policy toward preemptive action and nation‐building abroad, balanced against expanded domestic security measures. His administration’s decisions on the Iraq War and responses to terrorism and natural disaster continue to shape debates over executive power, international law, and the interplay between security and liberty. Domestically, his policy legacy in education and tax reform influenced subsequent legislative agendas, while the financial‐crisis interventions informed later economic‐stimulus debates.
