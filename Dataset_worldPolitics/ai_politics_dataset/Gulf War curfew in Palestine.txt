**Gulf War Curfew in Palestine**

During the 1991 Gulf War (2 August 1990 – 28 February 1991), the Palestinian territories of the West Bank and Gaza Strip—then under Israeli military occupation—were subjected to strict curfews and security measures. These restrictions were imposed in response to regional instability, missile threats, and concerns over civil unrest. The curfew periods disrupted daily life, exacerbated economic hardship, and became a focal point of political tension between Palestinian communities, the Israeli military government, and the broader international context of the Gulf conflict.

**Historical and Political Context**
Iraq’s invasion of Kuwait on 2 August 1990 precipitated a large-scale U.S.-led military response, including the deployment of Patriot missile batteries to Israel to intercept Iraqi Scud rockets. Fearing missile attacks and potential unrest, the Israeli government declared a state of readiness on 16 January 1991. Although Israel did not participate directly in combat operations, it endured 39 Scud attacks over the following weeks. Palestinian areas, densely populated and lacking civil defense infrastructure, were considered especially vulnerable.

Under the 1967 Six-Day War outcome, Israel administered the West Bank and Gaza through a military government headquartered in Jerusalem. Palestinian political life was heavily restricted, and the Israeli Defense Forces (IDF) retained authority over security, movement, and local governance. As regional hostilities escalated, Israeli authorities imposed emergency regulations to prevent sabotage, demonstrations, and panic.

**Implementation of the Curfew**
On 18 January 1991, the IDF commander in the West Bank and Gaza issued an order instituting a nightly curfew from 18:00 to 06:00. The curfew covered major urban centers—Ramallah, Nablus, Hebron, Jenin in the West Bank, and Gaza City, Khan Younis, Rafah in Gaza—as well as numerous smaller towns and refugee camps. Key features included:

* **Movement Restrictions**: All non-military traffic was barred. Palestinian civilians found outside their homes during curfew hours were subject to detention or fines.
* **Permit System**: Essential workers—medical personnel, utility technicians, and selected civil administrators—required military permits to travel during curfew hours. Checkpoints were reinforced, with IDF and Border Police conducting random stops.
* **Security Predicates**: The curfew was officially justified by the need to guard against infiltration by hostile elements allied with Iraq, prevent sabotage of Israeli infrastructure, and maintain public order amid missile alerts.

In practice, enforcement varied by location. In refugee camps, curfews were sometimes extended or imposed earlier in response to rumors of planned protests. In urban areas, limited exemptions allowed some humanitarian NGOs to operate under strict oversight.

**Humanitarian and Economic Impact**
The curfew severely disrupted everyday life in Palestinian communities already facing economic stagnation. Impacts included:

* **Access to Essential Services**: Hospitals operated under emergency staffing, often without reliable electricity or supplies. Curfew constraints complicated ambulance transfers during missile alerts and medical emergencies.
* **Food and Water Supplies**: Bakeries and water‐pumping stations, designated as “essential,” received limited curfew permits. Nonetheless, supply shortages arose when machinery broke down outside permitted hours or when checkpoint delays prevented timely deliveries.
* **Employment and Income**: Many Palestinians worked in Israeli cities or in seasonal agriculture; curfews halted these activities, further reducing household incomes. Informal markets partially mitigated food shortages but at higher prices.
* **Psychological Effects**: Frequent missile alarms and the threat of sudden curfew enforcement generated widespread anxiety. Social gatherings, religious services, and funeral processions were curtailed or conducted under military supervision.

**Political Reactions and Protests**
Palestinian political factions—primarily the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO)—condemned the curfew as a collective punishment that violated international humanitarian law. Local community committees organized petitions calling for lifting of nighttime restrictions, while moderate Palestinian leaders appealed to the United Nations for intervention. Sporadic protests occurred, notably in Hebron on 20 January, where youths clashed with Israeli troops enforcing an extended curfew. Such confrontations were quickly suppressed under the military government’s emergency regulations.

Israeli authorities, supported by a segment of the settler community, maintained that the curfew was a temporary security necessity. Defense Minister Moshe Arens stated that “in the face of Scud missile threats and uncertain regional loyalties, no government could afford to leave its civilian population unprotected.” International human‐rights organizations monitored the situation, urging both Israel and the PLO to minimize civilian suffering and ensure humanitarian access.

**Lifting of Curfew and Aftermath**
As the coalition air campaign (Operation Desert Storm) achieved air supremacy over Iraq and Scud attacks on Israel ceased after 29 January 1991, Israeli authorities began a phased lifting of curfews in Palestinian areas. By 5 February, most curfew restrictions were rescinded, though enhanced checkpoint controls and permit requirements remained in place for several weeks.

The Gulf War curfew highlighted the vulnerability of occupied populations during regional conflicts and became part of the broader discourse on occupation policy. Palestinian leaders leveraged the experience to argue for an end to military governance and unrestricted movement under the emerging Madrid peace process (October 1991). Israeli officials later acknowledged that some curfew measures were overly broad and called for improved coordination with local civil bodies in future emergencies.

**Legacy**
The 1991 Gulf War curfew in Palestine remains a case study in the intersection of occupation law, military necessity, and civilian protection. It contributed to evolving discussions on:

* **Protecting Civilians in Occupied Territories**: The need for clear guidelines balancing security and humanitarian considerations under the Fourth Geneva Convention.
* **Coordination Mechanisms**: Frameworks for operational cooperation between occupying forces, local authorities, and international agencies during crises.
* **Political Mobilization**: The role of collective hardship in galvanizing Palestinian support for political negotiations and greater autonomy.

While the curfew was lifted in the conflict’s aftermath, restrictions on Palestinian movement under Israeli military orders continued in various forms, shaping the protracted nature of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.
