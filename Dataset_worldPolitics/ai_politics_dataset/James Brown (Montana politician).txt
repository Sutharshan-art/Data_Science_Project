**James E. Brown** (born 1970/1971) is an American attorney and Republican politician serving as the 17th Montana State Auditor and Commissioner of Securities and Insurance since January 6, 2025. Prior to his election as State Auditor, Brown was president of the Montana Public Service Commission (PSC), where he regulated the state’s utilities. His career reflects Montana’s evolving political landscape, marked by debates over resource management, regulatory oversight, and the balance of rural and urban interests.

### Early Life and Education

James E. Brown was born and raised in Beaverhead County, Montana. He graduated from Beaverhead County High School before earning a Bachelor of Arts in political science from the University of Montana. Brown later obtained a Juris Doctor from Seattle University School of Law and a Master of Laws in administrative law from the University of Washington. His academic focus on public policy and administrative procedures laid the groundwork for a career at the intersection of law, regulation, and politics.

### Legal Career and Entry into Public Service

After law school, Brown returned to Montana and established a private legal practice in Dillon, where he specialized in land use, energy, and administrative law. He advised rural electric cooperatives, small businesses, and county governments on regulatory compliance, fostering relationships with local leaders and building a reputation as an advocate for Montana’s resource-based communities.

### Montana Public Service Commission (2021–2025)

In November 2020, Brown was elected to the five‐member PSC, the state’s constitutionally empowered body overseeing electric, natural gas, water, telecommunications, and transportation utilities. He took office in January 2021 and was chosen by his peers as commission president. During his tenure, the PSC faced high‐stakes decisions on utility rate increases, renewable energy integration, and grid resilience amid wildfires and extreme weather events. Brown championed transparency in rate cases and sought to balance consumer protection with the economic interests of utility providers, reflecting Montana’s broader discourse on resource stewardship and rural affordability.

Brown’s PSC presidency coincided with national debates over energy transition, federal infrastructure investment, and the role of state regulators in implementing federal climate goals. He advocated for expanded broadband access in rural Montana, citing its importance for economic development and telehealth, and supported pilot programs for community solar and advanced metering.

### 2022 Supreme Court Campaign

In 2022, Brown ran for an open seat on the Montana Supreme Court. Although judicial elections in Montana are officially nonpartisan, Brown’s campaign garnered early endorsements from Republican state leaders, illustrating the growing influence of partisan dynamics in down‐ballot races. His platform emphasized judicial restraint, respect for precedent, and the rule of law. He was defeated by incumbent Justice Ingrid Gustafson in a closely watched, high‐spending race that highlighted tensions over campaign finance and the politicization of judicial elections.

### Election as State Auditor (2024)

In early 2024, Brown announced his candidacy for State Auditor—an office constitutionally charged with overseeing the state’s insurance and securities industries, regulating captive insurance companies, and serving on the Montana Board of Land Commissioners. In the June Republican primary, he won a decisive victory, leveraging his PSC record and legal background. In the November general election, Brown defeated Democratic nominee John Repke by a margin of approximately 61.4% to 38.6%.

Brown campaigned on a platform of maintaining affordable insurance access, protecting consumers from fraud, and ensuring sound management of the state’s trust lands. The auditor position has historically served as a launchpad for higher office in Montana; Brown committed to serving his full term through 2029, pledging to focus on the auditor’s core responsibilities rather than immediate political advancement.

### Political and Regulatory Philosophy

Brown’s approach reflects Montana’s tradition of limited government balanced by robust regulation of critical services. He emphasizes the importance of transparent rulemaking, data‐driven decision making, and stakeholder engagement. On insurance issues, Brown advocates for competitive markets that offer Montanans a choice of providers while enforcing strict anti‐fraud measures. As a land board member, he supports sustainable timber harvesting and grazing leases to generate revenue for public schools, aligning with Montana’s reliance on trust‐land revenues for education funding.

### Impact and Significance

James Brown’s trajectory—from rural attorney to utility regulator and now State Auditor—mirrors Montana’s political evolution over the past decade, as the state grapples with shifting energy markets, broadband inequities, and the role of government in resource management. His tenure at the PSC and his election as auditor underscore the electorate’s emphasis on professional expertise and local roots in statewide offices. Brown’s policies and leadership will shape Montana’s insurance landscape and land‐trust revenues, with implications for consumer protection, public education funding, and rural‐urban economic divides.

As State Auditor, Brown inherits an office central to Montana’s regulatory framework and financial oversight. His performance in this role will influence debates on market regulation, fiscal stewardship, and the balance between provider viability and consumer rights in one of America’s most geographically vast and sparsely populated states.
