**Armed Forces of Bosnia and Herzegovina**

The **Armed Forces of Bosnia and Herzegovina (OSBiH)** are the unified military forces of the country, responsible for the defense and sovereignty of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Established in 2005, the OSBiH represents the culmination of post-war reforms aimed at integrating the formerly divided military forces from the country’s complex ethnic and political landscape. The creation and development of the OSBiH have been critical to Bosnia and Herzegovina’s political stability, state-building efforts, and Euro-Atlantic integration aspirations.

---

### Historical Background

The origins of Bosnia and Herzegovina’s armed forces are deeply tied to the country’s tumultuous history in the 1990s. Following the breakup of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, Bosnia and Herzegovina declared independence in 1992, triggering a brutal conflict marked by ethnic violence among Bosniaks, Croats, and Serbs.

During the **Bosnian War (1992–1995)**, three main military formations operated largely along ethnic lines: the **Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina (ARBiH)** predominantly Bosniak; the **Croatian Defence Council (HVO)** representing Bosnian Croats; and the **Army of Republika Srpska (VRS)** representing Bosnian Serbs. This fragmentation of military power reflected the deep ethnic and political divisions that shaped the war.

The **Dayton Peace Agreement** of 1995 ended the conflict and established a complex political system designed to maintain ethnic balance and peace. However, the existence of separate military forces continued to challenge the development of a cohesive state.

---

### Formation of the Unified Armed Forces

The consolidation of the country’s military forces into a single, unified structure was a major priority for postwar reconstruction and international efforts aimed at state-building and reconciliation. The process was heavily supported and overseen by the **Office of the High Representative (OHR)** and NATO-led forces.

In 2005, the three ethnically based armies were formally merged to create the **Armed Forces of Bosnia and Herzegovina**, unifying personnel, command structures, and training under a single defense ministry. This marked a significant milestone in Bosnia and Herzegovina’s political development, symbolizing a move towards functional state sovereignty and interethnic cooperation.

---

### Structure and Organization

The OSBiH is organized as a professional, volunteer force comprising land, air, and support components. It is structured under the **Ministry of Defence of Bosnia and Herzegovina** and commanded by the **Chief of Joint Staff**.

The force includes:

* **Army of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina (VFBiH):** Formerly the ARBiH and HVO combined, mainly Bosniak and Croat personnel.
* **Army of Republika Srpska (VRS):** Integrated into the unified structure but maintaining a distinct identity reflecting the Republika Srpska entity.

Despite integration, political and ethnic sensitivities influence recruitment, command, and operational dynamics. The OSBiH promotes joint training and interoperability to foster cohesion.

---

### Political and Security Context

The establishment and ongoing development of the OSBiH are central to Bosnia and Herzegovina’s efforts to consolidate sovereignty within a highly decentralized and ethnically divided political system. The armed forces serve as a symbol of national unity and a practical tool for defending the country’s territorial integrity.

The OSBiH also plays a role in supporting internal security and disaster response, cooperating with international peacekeeping and stabilization missions. The country’s complex constitutional framework, resulting from the Dayton Agreement, requires balancing entity autonomy with state-level authority, impacting military policy and reforms.

---

### International Cooperation and Euro-Atlantic Integration

Bosnia and Herzegovina’s aspirations for membership in the **European Union (EU)** and **NATO** have heavily influenced the reform and professionalization of the OSBiH. The country participates in NATO’s **Partnership for Peace** program and contributes personnel to international peacekeeping operations under the UN and NATO auspices.

NATO has supported modernization efforts, emphasizing interoperability, defense sector reform, and civilian oversight. The OSBiH’s development is seen as a prerequisite for deeper integration into Euro-Atlantic structures and enhanced regional security cooperation.

---

### Challenges and Progress

Despite significant advances, the OSBiH faces ongoing challenges rooted in the country’s ethnic divisions and political complexities. Disagreements between the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and Republika Srpska entities sometimes hinder defense planning and resource allocation.

Efforts continue to promote integration, increase transparency, and strengthen the military’s apolitical character. The gradual harmonization of standards, training, and command has improved operational effectiveness and public trust.

---

### Role in National Identity and Stability

The OSBiH contributes to the consolidation of Bosnia and Herzegovina’s national identity by providing a shared institution that transcends ethnic lines. Its existence supports the country’s sovereignty and helps maintain peace in a post-conflict environment where security remains a sensitive issue.

As Bosnia and Herzegovina navigates its complex political landscape, the armed forces remain a key factor in promoting stability and confidence among its diverse population.

---

### Conclusion

The Armed Forces of Bosnia and Herzegovina represent a vital institution in the country’s postwar reconstruction and political evolution. Their formation marked a significant step toward state-building, national integration, and regional security. While challenges persist due to the country’s unique constitutional arrangements and ethnic divisions, ongoing reforms and international support aim to strengthen the OSBiH as a professional, unified force capable of defending Bosnia and Herzegovina’s sovereignty and contributing to broader peace and stability in the Balkans.
