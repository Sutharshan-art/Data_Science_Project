**Peggy Noonan**

**Peggy Noonan** (born Margaret Ellen Noonan on September 7, 1950) is an American political commentator, author, and former speechwriter best known for her work as a special assistant and speechwriter for President **Ronald Reagan**. She has written extensively on U.S. politics, American culture, and the presidency, and is a long-time columnist for *The Wall Street Journal*. Noonan played a key role in crafting some of the most memorable speeches of the Reagan era and has remained an influential voice in American conservative thought and political discourse.

---

### Early Life and Education

Peggy Noonan was born and raised in **Brooklyn, New York**, in an Irish-American, Catholic family. She earned a Bachelor of Arts degree in English from **Fairleigh Dickinson University**. Early in her career, she worked as a producer and writer at CBS Radio, where she developed her skills in political communication and media.

Noonan's background in journalism and broadcasting laid the foundation for her later role in national politics and public commentary.

---

### Reagan Administration and Speechwriting

Noonan joined the **Reagan administration** in 1984 as a speechwriter and special assistant. She quickly became known for her ability to articulate themes of patriotism, optimism, and traditional American values—hallmarks of Reagan’s public persona.

Among her most famous contributions was her role in crafting Reagan’s **1986 address following the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster**, which included the widely quoted line: “They slipped the surly bonds of earth to touch the face of God.” The speech was praised for its solemn tone and emotional resonance.

She also helped write Reagan’s **farewell address in 1989**, which reflected on the president’s vision of America as a "shining city on a hill." Noonan’s rhetorical style was noted for its elegance, moral clarity, and evocative use of historical and cultural references.

Her work contributed to shaping the Reagan legacy and demonstrated the power of presidential rhetoric in framing national identity and political ideals.

---

### Political Writing and Commentary

After leaving the White House, Noonan transitioned to a career as a political author and commentator. She wrote speeches for **Vice President George H. W. Bush** during the 1988 presidential campaign, including the famous line from his convention speech promising a “kinder, gentler nation” and vowing “no new taxes.”

Noonan began writing columns and opinion pieces, and in 2000, she became a regular columnist for *The Wall Street Journal*, where she has since provided weekly political commentary. Her columns often focus on presidential politics, American institutions, cultural trends, and the challenges facing U.S. democracy.

Although broadly associated with **conservative and Republican politics**, Noonan has at times broken with party orthodoxy, criticizing both Democratic and Republican figures. Her tone has evolved over time to emphasize institutional stability, civic responsibility, and the importance of national unity.

---

### Books and Publications

Peggy Noonan is the author of several books that combine political analysis with personal reflections on leadership and public life. Notable works include:

* *What I Saw at the Revolution: A Political Life in the Reagan Era* (1990), a memoir of her time in the Reagan White House.
* *When Character Was King: A Story of Ronald Reagan* (2001), a biographical portrait of Reagan focused on his values and leadership.
* *Patriotic Grace: What It Is and Why We Need It Now* (2008), a call for civility and unity in American politics.

Her writings often emphasize the importance of character, tradition, and moral vision in political leadership.

---

### Awards and Recognition

Noonan has received several accolades for her contributions to journalism and political commentary. In 2017, she was awarded the **Pulitzer Prize for Commentary** for her *Wall Street Journal* columns, which were praised for capturing the political mood of the country during the 2016 presidential election.

She has also been recognized by media organizations and think tanks for her contributions to public discourse and the craft of political speechwriting.

---

### Political Views and Legacy

Peggy Noonan’s political views have evolved but remain grounded in traditional conservatism with an emphasis on civic virtue and American exceptionalism. While a supporter of the Republican Party’s historical platform, she has expressed criticism of its populist turn in recent years, particularly during and after the presidency of **Donald Trump**.

She has argued for restoring public trust in institutions and expressed concern over the erosion of political norms and national cohesion. Her commentary often appeals to a sense of shared American values and the need for thoughtful leadership.

---

### Conclusion

Peggy Noonan is a prominent figure in American political life, known for her eloquent writing, historical sensibility, and thoughtful analysis. As a speechwriter for Ronald Reagan and George H. W. Bush, and later as a columnist and author, she has shaped the language of American conservatism and provided a voice for moderation and principle in a polarized political landscape. Her work continues to influence discussions about leadership, patriotism, and the role of rhetoric in democratic governance.
