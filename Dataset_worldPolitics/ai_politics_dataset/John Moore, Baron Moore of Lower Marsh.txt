**John Michael Moore, Baron Moore of Lower Marsh** (23 November 1939 – 20 May 2019) was a British Conservative politician who held senior ministerial office under Prime Ministers Margaret Thatcher and John Major. Noted for his role in advancing market‐oriented reforms in public services, Moore served as Secretary of State for Transport (1987–1988), Secretary of State for Health (1988–1989), and Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster (1990–1992). He was elevated to the House of Lords as Baron Moore of Lower Marsh in 2005.

### Early Life and Parliamentary Career

Born in Kent, Moore was educated at Chatham House Grammar School and the London School of Economics, where he read economics. Before entering Parliament, he worked in the private sector in shipping and financial services. He was first elected as Member of Parliament for Croydon Central in February 1974, losing the seat in 1979, and returned to the Commons in 1983 as MP for Richmond and Barnes, a constituency he represented until 1992.

### Ministerial Offices under Margaret Thatcher

#### Secretary of State for Transport (1987–1988)

Following the Conservatives’ third electoral victory in June 1987, Moore was appointed Secretary of State for Transport. He oversaw several initiatives in the wake of the 1986 Transport Act, including further deregulation of bus services outside London and steps toward rail privatization. His tenure coincided with growing calls to introduce competitive tendering for public‐sector transport contracts—a Thatcherite priority aimed at reducing public spending and increasing efficiency.

#### Secretary of State for Health (1988–1989)

In July 1988, Moore succeeded Norman Fowler as Secretary of State for Health and Social Services—subsequently split into separate departments in April 1988, with Moore heading the newly created Department of Health. He inherited the reforms of the 1983 Griffiths Report, which recommended “management by objectives” and created the internal market within the National Health Service (NHS). Under his stewardship, legislation advanced the separation of purchasers (health authorities) and providers (hospital trusts), laying the groundwork for patient choice and performance measurement. Moore’s reforms were rooted in the belief that market mechanisms could curb cost inflation and improve service delivery, a stance that generated debate over the balance between professional autonomy and managerial accountability.

### Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster and Cabinet Office (1990–1992)

When John Major succeeded Margaret Thatcher in November 1990, Moore was appointed Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster with responsibility for the Cabinet Office. He played a coordinating role in implementing the Government’s “Next Steps” initiative, which sought to create executive agencies with defined performance targets across government departments. This model applied the internal‐market ethos—inspired by the NHS reforms—to wider public administration, seeking greater transparency and consumer‐style accountability in services ranging from the Passport Office to environmental regulators.

### Political Context and Impact

Moore’s ministerial career unfolded during the late Thatcher years when the Conservative Party pursued an agenda of deregulation, privatization, and public‐service reform. His transport and health portfolios were central theaters for the application of market principles to traditionally state‐run services. Though credited with improving managerial efficiency and introducing performance metrics, Moore’s reforms also faced criticism from healthcare professionals and trade unions who argued that market competition could undermine universal access and staff morale.

As Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster, Moore contributed to a broader reorientation of the civil service—shifting it toward delivery of measurable outcomes—while maintaining ministerial accountability. His work on “Next Steps” influenced subsequent New Labour administrations, which retained the agency model even as they reversed some market‐oriented policies.

### Later Career and Peerage

After leaving the Commons in 1992—choosing not to stand in that year’s general election—Moore returned to the private sector as a corporate director and adviser, notably in financial services and healthcare. In recognition of his public service, he was created a life peer on 19 July 2005 as Baron Moore of Lower Marsh, of Lower Marsh in the London Borough of Lambeth. In the House of Lords, he contributed to debates on public‐sector reform and healthcare financing until his retirement from the Lords in 2017.

### Legacy

John Moore’s ministerial tenure is remembered for embedding market‐based reforms in the NHS and across Whitehall. His transport deregulation policies reshaped local bus services and set the stage for later rail privatization, while his leadership in the Cabinet Office helped institutionalize performance management in government. Moore’s commitment to applying economic principles to public policy reflected the prevailing Conservative orthodoxy of his era and influenced both successors and critics across the political spectrum.

Baron Moore of Lower Marsh died on 20 May 2019 at the age of 79. His contributions to the modernization of public services remain significant reference points in debates over the role of markets in delivering government functions and the ongoing evolution of the British administrative state.
