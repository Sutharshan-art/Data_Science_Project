**Timeline of the Cold War**

The Cold War (circa 1945–1991) was a period of geopolitical tension between the United States and its Western allies (the “Western Bloc”) and the Soviet Union and its satellite states (the “Eastern Bloc”). Marked by ideological rivalry, arms races, proxy conflicts, and diplomatic confrontations, the Cold War shaped international politics for nearly half a century.

**Origins (1945–1947)**

* **February 1945 – Yalta Conference:** Allied leaders Roosevelt, Churchill, and Stalin meet to plan post–World War II order, sowing seeds of division over Eastern Europe’s fate.
* **July–August 1945 – Potsdam Conference:** Disputes over German reparations and Eastern Europe consolidation deepen mistrust.
* **March 1946 – “Iron Curtain” Speech:** Winston Churchill in Fulton, Missouri, declares that an “iron curtain” has descended across Europe, describing Soviet-controlled territories.
* **March 1947 – Truman Doctrine:** President Harry S. Truman pledges U.S. support to “free peoples” resisting subjugation, signaling the West’s policy of containment.
* **June 1947 – Marshall Plan:** U.S. announces aid for European economic recovery, which Stalin bars Eastern Bloc countries from accepting.

**Early Confrontations (1948–1953)**

* **June 1948–May 1949 – Berlin Blockade and Airlift:** Soviet blockade of West Berlin is countered by a massive Allied airlift, underscoring the city’s symbolic status.
* **April 1949 – NATO Founded:** Twelve Western nations establish the North Atlantic Treaty Organization as a collective defense pact.
* **October 1949 – Chinese Communist Victory:** Mao Zedong proclaims the People’s Republic of China, expanding communist influence in Asia.
* **June 1950 – Korean War Begins:** North Korean invasion of South Korea draws UN and U.S. forces, followed by Chinese intervention in late 1950.
* **July 1953 – Korean Armistice:** Fighting ends in a stalemate; Korea remains divided along the 38th parallel.

**Nuclear Arms Race and Crises (1954–1962)**

* **August 1955 – Warsaw Pact:** The Soviet Union and seven Eastern European states form a military alliance countering NATO.
* **October–November 1956 – Hungarian Uprising:** Soviet forces crush a popular revolt, demonstrating willingness to use force to maintain control.
* **May 1960 – U-2 Incident:** American spy plane shot down over Soviet airspace, derailing a planned summit between Eisenhower and Khrushchev.
* **January 1961 – Berlin Wall Construction:** East Germany erects a barrier dividing East and West Berlin, becoming the Cold War’s most potent symbol.
* **October 1962 – Cuban Missile Crisis:** Soviet deployment of nuclear missiles in Cuba brings superpowers to the brink of nuclear war; crisis resolves with mutual concessions and establishment of a direct “hotline.”

**Détente and Proxy Engagements (1963–1979)**

* **June 1963 – Limited Test Ban Treaty:** U.S., USSR, and United Kingdom agree to prohibit nuclear tests in the atmosphere, outer space, and underwater.
* **1964–1973 – Vietnam War Escalation:** U.S. involvement in South Vietnam intensifies, while the Soviet Union and China supply the North; the war becomes a focal Cold War battleground.
* **May 1972 – SALT I:** Strategic Arms Limitation Talks culminate in treaties limiting certain categories of nuclear weapons and establishing anti‐ballistic missile constraints.
* **1975 – Helsinki Accords:** Thirty‐five nations, including the U.S. and USSR, sign agreements on European security and human rights, enhancing East–West dialogue.
* **1979 – Soviet Invasion of Afghanistan:** Brezhnev orders troops into Afghanistan to support a communist government, ending détente and prompting renewed U.S. support for mujahideen fighters.

**Renewed Tensions and Reforms (1980–1985)**

* **November 1979 – Iran Hostage Crisis:** U.S. embassy staff taken hostage in Tehran exacerbate American global embattlement and contribute to Cold War volatility.
* **1981–1985 – Reagan’s “Peace through Strength”:** U.S. President Ronald Reagan increases defense spending, labels the USSR the “Evil Empire,” and initiates the Strategic Defense Initiative.
* **December 1981 – Martial Law in Poland:** Soviet‐backed Polish government suppresses the Solidarity movement, demonstrating continued Soviet willingness to contain dissent.

**Perestroika, Glasnost, and Collapse (1985–1991)**

* **March 1985 – Gorbachev’s Reforms:** Mikhail Gorbachev becomes General Secretary of the Communist Party, introducing **perestroika** (restructuring) and **glasnost** (openness).
* **October–November 1986 – Reykjavik Summit:** Reagan and Gorbachev explore radical nuclear arms reductions, laying groundwork for future treaties.
* **1989 – Revolutions of 1989:** Peaceful uprisings topple communist governments across Eastern Europe; the Berlin Wall falls on November 9, symbolizing Cold War end in Europe.
* **December 1990 – Conventional Forces in Europe Treaty:** NATO and Warsaw Pact agree to reduce conventional weaponry in Europe.
* **August 1991 – Soviet Coup Attempt:** Hard‐liners briefly seize power in an attempt to reverse Gorbachev’s reforms; failure accelerates dissolution.
* **December 1991 – Soviet Dissolution:** On December 25, President Gorbachev resigns; next day, the Soviet Union officially ceases to exist, marking the Cold War’s end.

**Legacy and Significance**
The Cold War profoundly shaped global alliances, military strategies, and ideological debates. Its legacy includes the nuclear deterrence doctrine, the proliferation of international organizations (NATO, UN peacekeeping), and the expansion of market democracies in Eastern Europe. The conflict’s conclusion ushered in a unipolar moment of U.S. dominance and initiated the post–Cold War era of globalization and regional conflicts.
