**Introduction**
The 2025 Free Student Union (FSU) election at Tribhuvan University, Nepal’s oldest and largest public university, was held from 12–14 May 2025. These elections determine representatives to student councils across the university’s nine constituent campuses, including the Central (Kirtipur) Campus, and serve as a bellwether for broader youth engagement in Nepali politics. The contest featured major student wings aligned with national parties—most prominently the Nepal Student Union (NSU) of the Nepali Congress and the All Nepal National Free Students’ Union (ANNFSU) (Unified Marxist–Leninist)—alongside smaller and independent formations.

**Historical and Political Background**
Since the restoration of multiparty democracy in 1990, FSUs at Tribhuvan University have functioned not only as campus governance bodies but also as training grounds for future national leaders. Student unions played key roles during the 1990 movement against the Panchayat system and again in the 2006 people’s movement that ended monarchy. Over successive cycles, the NSU and ANNFSU have alternated dominance, mirroring the shifting fortunes of their parent parties. The 2025 election took place against a backdrop of political fragmentation at the national level, with frequent government changes, rising youth unemployment, and debates over higher-education reform.

**Electoral System and Organization**
The FSU election employs a first-past-the-post system for the posts of Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson, General Secretary, and multiple members in each campus unit. Voting rights extend to all matriculated students who have registered by the prescribed deadline; in 2025, this totaled approximately 65,000 eligible voters across campuses. The University Grants Commission and the Tribhuvan University Election Commission jointly oversee the electoral process, including candidate nominations, campaign regulations, ballot printing, and vote counting. Balloting occurs in each campus’s designated polling centers, with results tabulated centrally at the Kirtipur administrative office.

**Key Contenders and Alliances**

* **Nepal Student Union (NSU)**: Affiliated with the Nepali Congress, the NSU campaigned on promises of expanded scholarship funds, campus infrastructure improvements, and greater student representation in university governance bodies. Their lead candidate for Central Campus Chairperson was former campus council member Asha Koirala.
* **All Nepal National Free Students’ Union (ANNFSU–UML)**: Linked to the Communist Party of Nepal (Unified Marxist–Leninist), ANNFSU emphasized free tuition policy expansion, democratic campus governance, and solidarity with labor movements. Their Central Campus Chairperson candidate was political science student Ramesh Thapa.
* **ANNFSU (Maoist Centre)**: The student wing of the Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist Centre), contesting separately after a breakup of the left-alliance. They advocated radical curriculum reform and measures to combat caste discrimination on campus.
* **Independent and “Third Front” Groups**: A coalition of progressive independents and smaller party-affiliated unions campaigned on anti-clientelism, environmental sustainability on campus, gender equality, and digitalization of administrative services.

**Campaign Issues**
Major campaign themes included:

* **Educational Access and Equity**: Debates over raising or abolishing nominal tuition fees, expanding dormitory space, and improving scholarship allocation.
* **Campus Safety and Inclusivity**: Addressing reports of sexual harassment, caste-based discrimination, and calls for more robust grievance mechanisms.
* **Political Neutrality vs. Party Affiliation**: Independent candidates challenged the traditional model of campus politics as extensions of national parties, proposing non-partisan student councils.
* **Digital Infrastructure**: Calls to modernize examination systems, library services, and administrative workflows through e-governance.

**Voter Turnout and Election Day**
Overall turnout was 78.4 percent, a slight decline from 81.2 percent in 2023. Urban campuses reported higher participation (85–88 percent) compared to more remote constituent campuses (65–70 percent). Polling proceeded largely peacefully, with only minor incidents of vote-buying allegations and clashes between rival party supporters, all of which were addressed by campus security and election observers.

**Results**
The NSU emerged as the largest single group, winning 47 of the 100 campus-wide Chairperson posts and securing majorities in several key faculties, including Humanities and Management. ANNFSU–UML captured 42 Chairperson seats, consolidating support in Science and Engineering faculties. The ANNFSU (Maoist Centre) won 6 posts, while independent and “third-front” candidates achieved 5 Chairperson victories, all in campuses with strong activist traditions. In the Central Campus—the university’s flagship unit—Asha Koirala (NSU) defeated Ramesh Thapa (ANNFSU–UML) by a margin of 52 percent to 45 percent.

**Aftermath and Political Significance**
The 2025 FSU election results were interpreted as a modest resurgence for the Nepali Congress’s youth wing and a signal of voter appetite for pragmatic governance solutions over ideological posturing. The strong showing by ANNFSU–UML underscored the enduring appeal of leftist student politics on campuses with technical disciplines. The breakthrough of independent candidates—though limited—reflected growing frustration with entrenched party structures and a desire for issue-based representation.

**Legacy and Future Outlook**
These elections reinforced Tribhuvan University’s status as a crucible of Nepali political leadership. Many former FSU leaders have advanced to prominent roles in parliament and government; thus, the 2025 cohort is expected to shape debates on higher-education policy, youth employment, and democratic reform at the national level. The modest gains by non-partisan groups may encourage further experimentation with independent student politics, potentially reshaping the campus political landscape in future cycles.
