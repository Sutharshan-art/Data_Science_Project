**List of Wars Involving Bosnia and Herzegovina**

The **list of wars involving Bosnia and Herzegovina** includes military conflicts that have directly involved the territory or the state of Bosnia and Herzegovina from the medieval period to the present day. Located in the western Balkans, Bosnia and Herzegovina has long been at the crossroads of empires, religions, and ethnic groups, which has contributed to its complex political history and frequent involvement in regional and international conflicts.

The region has experienced periods of foreign domination under the Ottoman and Austro-Hungarian Empires, wars of national liberation, and ethnic conflict during the dissolution of Yugoslavia. Since gaining independence in 1992, Bosnia and Herzegovina has maintained a fragile peace under a power-sharing arrangement established by the Dayton Accords.

---

### Medieval and Ottoman Period

#### **Bosnian Crusade (1235–1241)**

* **Belligerents:** Kingdom of Hungary and the Catholic Church vs. Bosnian heretics (Bogomils)
* **Context:** A series of campaigns initiated to suppress the Bosnian Church, viewed as heretical by the Roman Catholic Church. These crusades reflected broader religious and political struggles in medieval Europe.

#### **Ottoman Conquest of Bosnia (1463)**

* **Belligerents:** Ottoman Empire vs. Kingdom of Bosnia
* **Outcome:** The Kingdom of Bosnia fell to the Ottomans, and the territory became part of the Ottoman Empire. Islamization and integration into the Ottoman administrative system followed, shaping the region’s religious and cultural composition for centuries.

---

### Austro-Hungarian and World War I Era

#### **Austro-Hungarian Occupation of Bosnia (1878)**

* **Belligerents:** Austro-Hungarian Empire (with Ottoman acquiescence) vs. local resistance
* **Context:** Following the Congress of Berlin, Austria-Hungary was given the mandate to occupy and administer Bosnia and Herzegovina, though the region remained nominally under Ottoman sovereignty until annexation in 1908.

#### **World War I (1914–1918)**

* **Belligerents:** Central Powers vs. Allied Powers
* **Relevance:** The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo by a Bosnian Serb nationalist in 1914 triggered World War I. Bosnia was part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire during the war, with conscription and political repression affecting its population.

---

### Kingdom of Yugoslavia and World War II

#### **World War II (1941–1945)**

* **Belligerents:** Axis Powers vs. Yugoslav Partisans and Chetniks
* **Context:** Bosnia was incorporated into the fascist puppet state known as the Independent State of Croatia (NDH), under Axis occupation. The region witnessed severe atrocities, including genocide against Serbs, Jews, and Roma, as well as intra-Yugoslav civil war between Partisan and Chetnik forces.
* **Outcome:** Liberation by the communist Partisans led by Josip Broz Tito and incorporation into the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia.

---

### Socialist Yugoslavia (1945–1992)

Bosnia and Herzegovina was a constituent republic of socialist Yugoslavia and remained peaceful during this period. However, underlying ethnic and political tensions would later resurface amid Yugoslavia's breakup.

---

### Wars During and After the Breakup of Yugoslavia

#### **Bosnian War (1992–1995)**

* **Belligerents:** Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina vs. Bosnian Serb and Croat forces
* **Context:** Following Bosnia’s declaration of independence in 1992, war broke out among its main ethnic groups—Bosniaks (Bosnian Muslims), Croats, and Serbs. The conflict was marked by ethnic cleansing, mass atrocities, and the siege of Sarajevo.
* **International involvement:** NATO intervened in 1995 with airstrikes against Bosnian Serb forces.
* **Outcome:** The war ended with the Dayton Peace Agreement, which created a decentralized state with two main entities: the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and Republika Srpska.

---

### Post-War Conflicts and International Missions

#### **NATO Intervention and IFOR/SFOR (1995–2004)**

* **Belligerents:** NATO-led peacekeeping forces
* **Purpose:** Enforce the Dayton Agreement, maintain peace, and disarm warring factions.
* **Context:** NATO deployed the Implementation Force (IFOR), followed by the Stabilization Force (SFOR), to oversee post-war stabilization.

#### **EUFOR Althea (2004–present)**

* **Mission:** European Union-led military operation to maintain a safe environment.
* **Context:** Succeeded SFOR; supports the development of Bosnia’s armed forces and facilitates ongoing peace.

---

### Participation in International Peacekeeping

Though not engaged in active warfare since the 1990s, Bosnia and Herzegovina has participated in international peacekeeping missions under the UN and other multinational efforts, including in Lebanon and the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

---

### Conclusion

The **list of wars involving Bosnia and Herzegovina** reflects the country’s complex and turbulent history as a contested territory among empires, nations, and ethnic groups. From medieval religious conflicts and imperial struggles to modern ethnic warfare and peacekeeping operations, Bosnia’s military history is closely tied to broader European and global dynamics. The legacy of war continues to shape its political landscape and international relations in the 21st century.
