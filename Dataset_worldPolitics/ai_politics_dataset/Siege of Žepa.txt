**Siege of Žepa**

The **Siege of Žepa** was a military blockade and subsequent capture of the eastern Bosnian enclave of Žepa by the Army of Republika Srpska (VRS) during the Bosnian War. Lasting from **May to July 1995**, the siege occurred shortly after the fall of Srebrenica and formed part of a broader strategic offensive by Bosnian Serb forces to eliminate United Nations-declared “safe areas” held by the Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina (ARBiH). The fall of Žepa led to the forced evacuation and displacement of its Bosniak population and was accompanied by human rights abuses, although on a lesser scale than in neighboring Srebrenica.

Žepa was one of three eastern Bosnian enclaves—along with Srebrenica and Goražde—designated as UN “safe areas” in 1993, intended to protect Bosniak civilians under siege by Serb forces. Despite this designation, limited international military presence and lack of enforcement allowed the VRS to ultimately overrun the enclave.

### Background and Strategic Context

During the early stages of the **Bosnian War** (1992–1995), Serb forces gained control over large portions of eastern Bosnia, targeting Bosniak-majority areas for territorial consolidation. This strategy involved the siege and isolation of towns such as Srebrenica, Žepa, and Goražde, which became surrounded pockets of resistance in Serb-controlled territory. These areas were cut off from regular supply routes and became dependent on limited humanitarian aid and UN protection.

In April 1993, the United Nations Security Council passed Resolution 824, declaring several enclaves, including Žepa, as “safe areas” to be protected from armed attack. A small contingent of **Ukrainian UNPROFOR peacekeepers** was deployed to Žepa, but their mandate did not include offensive operations or robust defense against advancing forces.

By 1995, the Bosnian Serb leadership, under **Radovan Karadžić** and **General Ratko Mladić**, intensified military operations to eliminate these remaining enclaves and solidify the borders of Republika Srpska. Following the **fall of Srebrenica** on July 11, 1995, and the mass killing of over 8,000 Bosniak men and boys, Žepa became the next target.

### Military Offensive and Fall

The **siege of Žepa** escalated in mid-July 1995. The VRS began a coordinated artillery and ground assault against Bosniak defensive positions. Unlike Srebrenica, Žepa had limited external media presence and fewer international observers, which allowed the siege to proceed with relatively low visibility.

Žepa was defended by approximately 1,500 lightly armed members of the ARBiH 285th Light Mountain Brigade, under the command of **Colonel Avdo Palić**. The enclave lacked heavy weapons and faced a severe shortage of supplies. Despite its vulnerable position, local defenders attempted to resist the VRS offensive and maintain order among the trapped civilian population.

On **July 24, 1995**, after several days of shelling and negotiations, Žepa fell to the VRS. A partial evacuation had been negotiated between Bosnian Serb leaders and UN officials, facilitated by the Ukrainian peacekeepers. Unlike in Srebrenica, where mass executions occurred immediately following the town’s capture, the evacuation of Žepa was conducted under more closely monitored conditions, although it was still accompanied by significant abuses.

### Displacement and Human Rights Violations

After the fall of Žepa, the population was forcibly removed. Thousands of civilians, including women, children, and the elderly, were transported to government-held areas. Reports emerged of arbitrary detentions, disappearances, and mistreatment of prisoners. Many male civilians, including members of the local defense forces, were separated and reportedly killed or went missing.

Colonel **Avdo Palić**, who negotiated the evacuation with Serb forces, was taken into custody during the process and subsequently disappeared. His fate remained unknown for years, becoming a symbol of unresolved wartime disappearances. His remains were discovered in 2009, and forensic analysis confirmed he had been executed shortly after being detained.

Though the scale of killings in Žepa did not reach the level of genocide as in Srebrenica, the event is widely viewed as part of a systematic campaign of ethnic cleansing against Bosniaks in eastern Bosnia. The targeting of UN “safe areas” highlighted the failure of international protection mechanisms during the war.

### International Response and Legal Proceedings

The fall of Žepa, along with that of Srebrenica, triggered international outrage and contributed to a shift in global opinion regarding the conflict in Bosnia. The massacres and forced removals prompted NATO to intensify its involvement in the region, culminating in **Operation Deliberate Force** in August 1995—a large-scale bombing campaign against Serb military positions.

The **International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY)** later indicted and convicted several key figures for crimes related to the sieges of both Srebrenica and Žepa. **Radovan Karadžić** and **Ratko Mladić** were found guilty of genocide, crimes against humanity, and war crimes. While Žepa was not the primary focus of genocide charges, it was considered part of the same strategic plan to remove Bosniak populations from Serb-claimed territories.

### Legacy

The siege and fall of Žepa remain a significant, though less internationally recognized, chapter of the Bosnian War. Survivors and the families of missing persons continue to seek justice and remembrance. Memorials in Bosnia and Herzegovina commemorate the victims, and the events at Žepa are widely regarded as emblematic of the international community’s failure to protect vulnerable populations during the conflict.

Žepa was subsequently integrated into **Republika Srpska**, one of the two entities of post-war Bosnia and Herzegovina created by the **Dayton Peace Agreement**. The area remains sparsely populated, with only a small number of returnees in the post-war years, reflecting the long-term demographic and social impact of the siege.
