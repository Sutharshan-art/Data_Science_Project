**Larry Kudlow** (born August 20, 1947) is an American economic commentator and former government official known for his advocacy of free-market policies and supply-side economics. He served as Director of the National Economic Council under President Donald Trump from 2018 to 2021 and previously held senior economic posts in the Reagan administration. As a television host and syndicated columnist, Kudlow has influenced conservative economic discourse for more than four decades.

**Early Life and Education**
William Laurence “Larry” Kudlow was born in Englewood, New Jersey, and raised in Rockland County, New York. He earned a Bachelor of Arts in history from Princeton University in 1969 and completed graduate work in economics at the University of Rochester, focusing on monetary theory and business cycles. While in college and graduate school, Kudlow developed an interest in classical liberal and supply-side economic thought, influenced by economists such as Milton Friedman and Arthur Laffer.

**Early Career and Reagan Administration**
After a stint as an economist at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, Kudlow entered the financial sector, rising to become vice president and chief economist at the investment bank Bear Stearns by the late 1970s. In 1981, he joined the Reagan administration as Associate Director for Economics and Planning at the Office of Management and Budget, where he helped craft early Reagan-era budgets emphasizing tax cuts, deregulation, and reductions in nondefense spending. From 1987 to 1989, he served as Deputy Assistant to the President for Economic Policy, promoting the administration’s agenda of lower marginal tax rates, accelerated depreciation, and curbs on union power.

**Media Career**
Following Reagan’s second term, Kudlow transitioned to media and think-tank work. He became an executive at the libertarian-leaning Cato Institute before joining the Hudson Institute as a senior fellow. Beginning in the mid-1990s, Kudlow emerged as a regular commentator on CNBC, hosting “The Kudlow Report” (2005–2008) and later “Kudlow & Company.” His on-air style combined bullish market forecasts with unapologetic support for deregulation, tax reform, and balanced budgets. Kudlow also wrote a syndicated newspaper column and authored books that popularized supply-side economics among conservative audiences.

**Political Views and Influence**
A self-described “Reagan supply-sider,” Kudlow has long argued that lower marginal tax rates spur investment, job creation, and economic growth. He has championed the view that government deficits can be self-correcting in a dynamic economy, asserting that growth-induced revenue increases will offset initial revenue losses from tax cuts. Kudlow has consistently opposed large-scale welfare programs, stringent financial regulations, and aggressive monetary tightening, advocating instead for open capital markets, flexible labor laws, and a strong dollar. His commentary helped shape Republican economic platforms in the 2000s and early 2010s, particularly around debates over the Bush and Obama tax policies.

**Role in the Trump Administration**
In March 2018, Kudlow was appointed Director of the National Economic Council, succeeding Gary Cohn. In this capacity, he served as the president’s chief economic adviser, coordinating policy across Treasury, the Federal Reserve, and international financial institutions. Kudlow was a staunch defender of the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, crediting it with boosting GDP growth and labor-force participation. He supported President Trump’s use of tariffs on steel, aluminum, and Chinese goods as leverage in trade negotiations, arguing that targeted tariffs would protect domestic industries and redress unfair trade practices. Critics contended that these measures risked retaliatory tariffs and higher consumer prices.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Kudlow played a key role in shaping economic relief packages, endorsing loans and grants to businesses under the CARES Act. He maintained an optimistic outlook on economic recovery, frequently forecasting rapid rebounds, though some of his predictions proved overly sanguine amid persistent unemployment and supply-chain disruptions.

**Later Career and Legacy**
After leaving the White House in January 2021, Kudlow returned to media commentary and continued to advise conservative policymakers. His tenure at the National Economic Council marked a return to supply-side vis-à-vis emerging 21st-century challenges—globalization, technological disruption, and demographic shifts. Kudlow’s career underscores the enduring influence of supply-side economics on U.S. fiscal policy debates and highlights tensions between laissez-faire principles and pragmatic interventions in trade and monetary policy. As both a policymaker and pundit, he has remained a prominent voice for free markets, low taxes, and limited government intervention in the economy.
