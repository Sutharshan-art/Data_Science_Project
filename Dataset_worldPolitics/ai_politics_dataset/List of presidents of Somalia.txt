**List of Presidents of Somalia**

The **President of Somalia** is the head of state of the Federal Republic of Somalia. The presidency is the highest constitutional office in the country, responsible for upholding the constitution, representing Somalia in international affairs, and appointing the prime minister, who serves as head of government. The office has existed since Somalia gained independence in 1960 and has since experienced significant changes influenced by political instability, civil war, and efforts to restore national governance.

This list includes all individuals who have served as president of Somalia, including those who held the office under different regimes: the Somali Republic (1960–1969), the Somali Democratic Republic (1969–1991), the Transitional Federal Government (2004–2012), and the current Federal Government of Somalia (2012–present).

### Historical Background

Somalia achieved independence on July 1, 1960, following the unification of British Somaliland and Italian Somaliland. The new Somali Republic was founded as a parliamentary democracy, with the president serving primarily as a ceremonial head of state. However, the political landscape shifted dramatically following a military coup in 1969 that established a socialist regime under General Mohamed Siad Barre.

From the early 1990s, Somalia underwent a prolonged period of state collapse following the fall of Barre's government. A series of transitional administrations attempted to restore central authority amid ongoing conflict and the rise of regional warlords. A new permanent federal government was established in 2012, marking the beginning of a fragile but continuing effort toward democratic governance and political stability.

### List of Presidents

#### **1. Aden Abdulle Osman Daar (1960–1967)**

**Term:** July 1, 1960 – June 10, 1967
**Political Affiliation:** Somali Youth League
Aden Abdulle Osman Daar was the first president of Somalia and the first African head of state to be peacefully replaced through democratic elections. His presidency focused on building national institutions and promoting unity in the newly independent state.

#### **2. Abdirashid Ali Sharmarke (1967–1969)**

**Term:** July 6, 1967 – October 15, 1969
**Political Affiliation:** Somali Youth League
Sharmarke succeeded Osman Daar and emphasized regional cooperation and economic development. His presidency was cut short by his assassination in 1969, an event that precipitated a military coup.

#### **3. Mohamed Siad Barre (1969–1991)**

**Term:** October 21, 1969 – January 26, 1991
**Political Affiliation:** Supreme Revolutionary Council
Following the 1969 coup, Siad Barre led a military regime that declared Somalia a socialist state. His government nationalized major sectors of the economy and aligned with the Soviet Union before later shifting toward the West. Barre’s rule was marked by authoritarianism, repression, and conflict, including the Ogaden War with Ethiopia. His regime collapsed in 1991, plunging the country into civil war.

#### **4. Interim and Transitional Presidents (1991–2012)**

After Barre's ousting, Somalia lacked a functioning central government. Several interim and transitional authorities attempted to reestablish national governance:

* **Ali Mahdi Muhammad** (1991–1997): Recognized by some as president, but lacked effective control beyond parts of Mogadishu.
* **Abdiqasim Salad Hassan** (2000–2004): President of the Transitional National Government (TNG), based in Djibouti.
* **Abdullahi Yusuf Ahmed** (2004–2008): First president of the Transitional Federal Government (TFG), supported by Ethiopian military intervention.
* **Adan Mohamed Nuur Madobe** (Acting, 2008–2009): Interim president during a political transition.
* **Sharif Sheikh Ahmed** (2009–2012): Oversaw the TFG’s final phase and the adoption of a new provisional constitution.

#### **5. Hassan Sheikh Mohamud (2012–2017; 2022–present)**

**First Term:** September 16, 2012 – February 16, 2017
**Second Term:** May 15, 2022 – present
**Political Affiliation:** Peace and Development Party
Hassan Sheikh Mohamud was the first president elected under Somalia’s new federal system. His administration focused on rebuilding state institutions and countering the al-Shabaab insurgency. He was re-elected in 2022, becoming the first Somali president to return to office for a non-consecutive term.

#### **6. Mohamed Abdullahi Mohamed (2017–2022)**

**Term:** February 16, 2017 – May 15, 2022
**Political Affiliation:** Tayo Party
Commonly known as "Farmaajo," Mohamed emphasized national sovereignty and security. His term was marked by disputes over election delays and federal relations. His tenure ended amid political tension, but he peacefully ceded power following his defeat in the 2022 election.

### Constitutional Framework

Under the 2012 Provisional Constitution, the president of Somalia is elected by both houses of the Federal Parliament and serves a four-year term, renewable once. The president appoints the prime minister, who forms a government subject to parliamentary approval. The presidency is largely ceremonial compared to the prime minister's executive authority, though the president retains significant influence in foreign affairs, defense, and national unity.

Somalia’s indirect electoral system, based on clan representation and parliamentary voting, has drawn criticism for lacking universal suffrage. Reforms to implement direct elections have been repeatedly delayed due to security challenges and political disagreements.

### Political Challenges

The presidency in Somalia has often reflected the broader volatility of the Somali state. Issues such as clan dynamics, federal-state relations, insurgency, and foreign intervention have shaped presidential authority and legitimacy. While recent transitions have been peaceful, challenges persist in consolidating democracy, ensuring security, and achieving inclusive governance.

### Conclusion

The list of Somali presidents provides a window into the country’s complex political history, from early democratic aspirations to decades of conflict and recent efforts at state reconstruction. Despite ongoing instability, the peaceful transfers of power since 2012 offer cautious optimism for Somalia’s future as a functioning federal republic.
