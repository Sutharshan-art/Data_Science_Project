**Biljana Plavšić**

Biljana Plavšić (born July 7, 1930) is a Bosnian Serb politician and former biologist who played a prominent role during the Bosnian War (1992–1995). She was a leading figure in the political leadership of the Republika Srpska, the self-declared Serb entity within Bosnia and Herzegovina. Plavšić's political career is closely linked to the complex and violent conflict that engulfed the former Yugoslavia in the 1990s, as well as the subsequent international efforts to bring accountability for war crimes.

---

### Early Life and Academic Career

Biljana Plavšić was born in Tuzla, then part of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia. She pursued a scientific career specializing in biology, eventually becoming a respected professor and researcher. Before entering politics, Plavšić held academic positions at the University of Sarajevo and later at the University of Banja Luka. Her scientific background set her apart from many other political figures in the region.

---

### Entry into Politics and the Bosnian War

Plavšić entered the political arena in the early 1990s, a period marked by the disintegration of Yugoslavia and rising ethnic nationalism. She joined the Serbian Democratic Party (SDS), which was led by Radovan Karadžić and became the dominant political force among Bosnian Serbs.

In 1992, Bosnia and Herzegovina declared independence from Yugoslavia, triggering a brutal conflict among Bosniaks (Bosnian Muslims), Croats, and Serbs. Plavšić became an influential figure in the emerging Republika Srpska government. She was appointed Vice President of Republika Srpska in 1992 and later became its President in 1996, following the Dayton Agreement that ended the war.

---

### Political Role During the War

During the Bosnian War, the leadership of Republika Srpska, including Plavšić, was involved in the conduct of military and paramilitary operations. These actions included ethnic cleansing campaigns aimed at securing territory and creating ethnically homogeneous areas. The war resulted in widespread atrocities, including massacres, mass displacement, and other serious violations of international humanitarian law.

Plavšić’s role has been examined in light of her association with these policies. She initially was considered a hardline nationalist but later distanced herself from some of the extreme policies advocated by other leaders.

---

### Post-War Political Career and Controversy

After the war, Plavšić served as President of Republika Srpska from 1996 to 1998. During this time, she began advocating for cooperation with international peace efforts and hinted at a more moderate stance, calling for reconciliation and peaceful coexistence among the ethnic groups in Bosnia.

However, her past associations with wartime policies led to international scrutiny. The International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY) indicted her on charges including crimes against humanity related to the ethnic cleansing campaigns.

---

### Trial and Conviction

In 2001, Biljana Plavšić voluntarily surrendered to the ICTY and was charged with persecution, extermination, deportation, and other crimes committed during the Bosnian War.

In 2003, she pleaded guilty to one count of persecution as a crime against humanity in a plea bargain agreement. The tribunal acknowledged her cooperation and remorse, which contributed to a reduced sentence.

Plavšić was sentenced to 11 years in prison and served her sentence in Sweden. She was released in 2009 after serving two-thirds of her term.

---

### Historical and Political Context

Biljana Plavšić’s political career cannot be separated from the broader context of the Yugoslav Wars, ethnic nationalism, and the collapse of socialist Yugoslavia. Her involvement illustrates the complex roles played by political leaders in conflicts characterized by severe ethnic violence and international intervention.

Her trial at the ICTY was part of a larger effort to address war crimes and promote accountability, marking a significant development in international criminal justice.

---

### Legacy

Biljana Plavšić remains a controversial figure. Some view her as a nationalist leader complicit in ethnic violence, while others note her later cooperation with the ICTY and calls for peace. Her conviction and imprisonment represent a landmark case in the prosecution of high-ranking political figures for wartime atrocities.

Her life and career continue to be studied in discussions on transitional justice, post-conflict reconciliation, and the political dynamics of the Balkans.

---

### Conclusion

Biljana Plavšić’s trajectory—from academic to wartime politician and subsequently convicted war criminal—reflects the turbulent and tragic history of Bosnia and Herzegovina in the 1990s. Her role in the Bosnian War, subsequent trial, and legacy are integral to understanding the political and humanitarian consequences of the conflict and the international community’s response to crimes committed during this period.
