**President William Jefferson Clinton Birthplace Home National Historic Site**

The **President William Jefferson Clinton Birthplace Home National Historic Site** is a unit of the United States National Park Service located in **Hope, Arkansas**. It preserves the early childhood home of **William Jefferson Clinton**, the 42nd President of the United States, who served from 1993 to 2001. The site commemorates Clinton's formative years in a small Southern town and seeks to contextualize his personal and political development within the broader themes of mid-20th-century American life.

The house, a two-story wood-frame structure built in 1917, was the residence of Clinton (then William Jefferson Blythe III) from his birth in 1946 until the age of four. The property was designated a **National Historic Site** in 2010, recognizing its significance in the early life of a U.S. president who played a major role in post–Cold War politics and domestic policy during the 1990s.

---

### Historical Background

William Jefferson Clinton was born on **August 19, 1946**, in Hope, Arkansas, at Julia Chester Hospital. He was named after his father, **William Jefferson Blythe Jr.**, who died in a car accident before his son’s birth. Clinton spent the early years of his life in the home of his maternal grandparents, **Eldon** and **Edith Cassidy**, along with his mother, **Virginia Cassidy Blythe**.

The house at 117 South Hervey Street served as the family residence during Clinton’s earliest years and was where he learned foundational values that shaped his later political identity. Clinton has spoken and written extensively about the influence of his grandparents, who were small-business owners with a strong sense of civic responsibility and belief in education and equality. His time in Hope laid the groundwork for his lifelong interest in public service and social justice.

---

### Architectural and Cultural Features

The house is a modest, two-story frame structure typical of small-town American homes of the early 20th century. It contains a living room, dining area, bedrooms, and kitchen, all restored to reflect the furnishings and atmosphere of the late 1940s. The restoration was informed by archival materials, photographs, and interviews with family members and contemporaries of the Clinton family.

In addition to its architectural significance, the home reflects the social and economic dynamics of the American South in the post–World War II era. The interpretive exhibits at the site explore themes such as race relations, small-town life, and the emergence of middle-class values during a period of rapid national change.

---

### Designation as a National Historic Site

The site was officially designated as a **National Historic Site** on **December 14, 2010**, through an act of Congress and was signed into law by President **Barack Obama**. Prior to its designation, the house had been maintained by a private foundation dedicated to preserving Clinton’s legacy in Hope.

The transition to National Park Service management involved historical renovations, expanded interpretive programming, and increased public access. The site now includes a **visitor center**, educational exhibits, and guided tours that provide insight into Clinton’s early environment and the values instilled in him by his family and community.

---

### Political Significance

The President William Jefferson Clinton Birthplace Home is one of a select group of properties recognized for their direct association with a U.S. president. Like other presidential birthplaces, it serves as both a memorial and an educational resource. However, the site’s political significance is amplified by Clinton’s complex and transformative presidency.

Clinton, a **Democratic** president, served during a time of major economic expansion, welfare reform, and budget surpluses, while also facing political polarization and impeachment proceedings. His presidency was marked by efforts to reposition the Democratic Party toward the political center, known as the "New Democrat" approach, blending market-oriented policies with traditional liberal values.

By preserving his birthplace, the site offers an opportunity to explore the roots of a figure who would later become a key actor in domestic policy debates, international diplomacy, and the post–Cold War global order. The home’s interpretation connects Clinton’s modest beginnings with the larger American narrative of opportunity and mobility.

---

### Public Engagement and Educational Outreach

The site attracts visitors from across the country and abroad, including school groups, historians, and those interested in presidential history. Educational materials and interpretive programs emphasize the connection between personal history and public leadership.

The National Park Service partners with local organizations and scholars to ensure that the site remains a vibrant center for public learning. Through tours, exhibits, and commemorative events, the birthplace home fosters discussion on the nature of leadership, the role of upbringing in shaping political values, and the enduring relevance of small-town America in national life.

---

### Conclusion

The President William Jefferson Clinton Birthplace Home National Historic Site is more than a preserved structure; it is a portal into the early life of a man who rose from modest origins in rural Arkansas to become one of the most consequential political figures of the late 20th century. As a public institution, it contributes to a broader understanding of American history, democracy, and the diverse paths to national leadership.
