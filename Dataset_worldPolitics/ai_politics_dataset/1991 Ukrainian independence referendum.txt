**1991 Ukrainian Independence Referendum**

The **1991 Ukrainian independence referendum** was held on **December 1, 1991**, and marked a pivotal moment in the dissolution of the **Soviet Union** and the emergence of **Ukraine** as a sovereign, independent state. The referendum followed the **Declaration of Independence** adopted by the **Verkhovna Rada** (the Ukrainian parliament) on **August 24, 1991**, in response to the failed **August coup** in Moscow. Ukrainian citizens were asked to confirm or reject the declaration through a nationwide vote.

The results were overwhelming: more than **90% of voters** approved independence, with majorities in every region, including traditionally pro-Soviet and ethnically Russian areas such as **Crimea** and the **Donbas**. The referendum provided a powerful democratic mandate for independence and was immediately recognized by many foreign governments, including the **Russian Federation** and the **United States**. It also played a decisive role in hastening the **dissolution of the USSR**, which was formally declared on **December 26, 1991**.

---

### Background

Throughout the 1980s, Ukraine—like other Soviet republics—experienced rising nationalist sentiment and growing dissatisfaction with centralized rule from Moscow. The **Chernobyl disaster** of 1986 had a lasting psychological and political impact on the Ukrainian population, intensifying calls for greater transparency, environmental safety, and autonomy. As **Mikhail Gorbachev’s** reforms of *glasnost* and *perestroika* gained momentum, Ukrainian activists and politicians pushed for more radical change.

In **July 1990**, the Ukrainian parliament adopted a **Declaration of State Sovereignty**, asserting the supremacy of Ukrainian laws over Soviet ones and laying the groundwork for eventual independence. On **August 24, 1991**, following the collapse of the hardline coup attempt in Moscow, the Verkhovna Rada declared full independence from the Soviet Union. The decision passed by an overwhelming majority of 346 votes in favor out of 450 and was supported even by many Communist deputies, reflecting a broad consensus across the political spectrum.

To lend democratic legitimacy to this declaration, the parliament scheduled a **nationwide referendum** to be held on December 1, 1991, allowing citizens to directly approve or reject the independence decision.

---

### The Referendum

The wording of the referendum was simple and direct:

> “Do you support the Act of Declaration of Independence of Ukraine adopted by the Verkhovna Rada of the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic on August 24, 1991?”

A single “Yes” or “No” checkbox appeared on the ballot. The vote coincided with the **first direct presidential election** in Ukrainian history, in which **Leonid Kravchuk**, the former chairman of the parliament and a moderate nationalist, was elected president with over 60% of the vote.

Out of a total eligible electorate of roughly **37.9 million**, **84.2%** participated in the referendum. According to official results:

* **Yes**: 28,804,071 votes (90.32%)
* **No**: 2,417,554 votes (7.58%)

Majorities supported independence in all **27 administrative regions**, including the **Autonomous Republic of Crimea**, **Donetsk** and **Luhansk oblasts**, and the **city of Sevastopol**. In Crimea, 54.2% voted in favor; in Donetsk and Luhansk, 83.9% and 83.9% voted "Yes," respectively.

---

### Political Significance

The referendum provided a **clear and democratic mandate** for Ukrainian independence, affirming that the desire for sovereignty extended beyond nationalist elites to the general population. It also effectively ended the debate over whether Ukraine should remain within a restructured Soviet federation—a proposal that had been floated by Gorbachev and some leaders of the other republics.

The referendum had immediate political consequences. On **December 2, 1991**, **Russia**, under **President Boris Yeltsin**, formally recognized Ukrainian independence, as did other major states including **Canada** and **Poland**. The recognition of Ukraine by Russia was particularly significant, as it undermined any lingering hopes of preserving the Soviet Union in a confederal form.

The vote also influenced the **Belavezha Accords**, signed on **December 8, 1991**, by the leaders of **Russia**, **Ukraine**, and **Belarus**, which declared the **USSR effectively dissolved** and established the **Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS)**. Gorbachev, who had continued to serve as President of the Soviet Union despite the diminishing power of his office, resigned on **December 25, 1991**. The Soviet Union formally ceased to exist the next day.

---

### Legacy

The **1991 Ukrainian independence referendum** is widely regarded as a **foundational moment** in modern Ukrainian statehood. It marked the transition from a Soviet republic to an independent nation through a peaceful and democratic process. The vote also demonstrated the **national unity** of Ukraine at that time, with support for independence cutting across regional, ethnic, and linguistic lines.

Over the following decades, Ukraine's path toward democratic consolidation, market reform, and European integration would be marked by political upheaval, economic challenges, and geopolitical tensions, particularly with Russia. However, the December 1991 referendum continues to serve as a **legitimizing cornerstone** of Ukraine’s sovereignty, reaffirmed by later political movements such as the **Orange Revolution** (2004) and the **Euromaidan protests** (2013–2014).

In contemporary Ukrainian political discourse, the referendum is commemorated as a **national holiday**, and its anniversary remains a powerful symbol of the country’s **right to self-determination and independence**.
