**Congress of Soviets of the Soviet Union**

The **Congress of Soviets of the Soviet Union** was the highest legislative body of the **Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR)** from its founding in 1922 until the adoption of the 1936 Soviet Constitution, which replaced it with the **Supreme Soviet of the USSR**. Functioning as a representative assembly of local soviets (workers’, peasants’, and soldiers’ councils), the Congress served as the formal sovereign authority, ratifying major policy decisions and constitutional changes within the framework of the Communist Party’s leadership.

The Congress of Soviets played a crucial role in the early institutionalization of Soviet power, reflecting the Bolshevik vision of proletarian democracy during the revolutionary and formative years of the Soviet state. Over time, its function became increasingly ceremonial as real political power centralized within the Communist Party apparatus.

---

### Historical Context and Establishment

The concept of the soviet (council) originated during the **Russian Revolution of 1905** as grassroots bodies representing workers and soldiers. The Bolsheviks adopted soviets as organs of revolutionary authority, culminating in the October Revolution of 1917 when the **All-Russian Congress of Soviets** became the supreme governing body of Soviet Russia.

Following the establishment of the USSR in December 1922, the Congress of Soviets of the Soviet Union was created to act as the union-wide supreme legislative authority. It was composed of delegates elected by the soviets of the constituent republics—workers’, peasants’, and soldiers’ deputies—forming a hierarchical system of representation intended to embody the principle of direct proletarian democracy.

---

### Structure and Composition

The Congress of Soviets consisted of delegates from the soviets of the **union republics**, including the Russian SFSR, Ukrainian SSR, Belarusian SSR, and Transcaucasian SFSR. Representation was based on the soviet system at the local, regional, and republican levels.

Delegates were elected to represent various social groups, predominantly workers, peasants, and Red Army soldiers, reflecting the class-based ideology of the regime. The Congress convened infrequently, typically once or twice a year, and its sessions often lasted only a few days.

Between sessions, legislative authority was vested in the **Central Executive Committee (CEC)** of the Soviet Union, which acted as the permanent governing body. The CEC executed the decrees and policies approved by the Congress.

---

### Powers and Functions

As the supreme legislative organ, the Congress of Soviets formally held authority to:

* Adopt or amend the Soviet Constitution
* Approve national economic plans and budgets
* Establish laws regulating social, political, and economic life
* Elect members of the Central Executive Committee and other key institutions
* Ratify treaties and declarations of war

In practice, the Congress’s role was largely to **endorse decisions** already made by the Communist Party leadership, particularly the Politburo. The CPSU maintained tight control over delegate selection and agenda-setting, ensuring that the Congress functioned as a rubber-stamp legislature rather than an independent body.

---

### Major Congresses and Political Developments

Key sessions of the Congress of Soviets marked important constitutional and political developments:

* **First Congress (1922):** Officially established the USSR and ratified the Treaty on the Creation of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics.
* **Fourth Congress (1924):** Adopted the 1924 Soviet Constitution, formalizing the federal structure and codifying the roles of soviets.
* **Tenth Congress (1929):** Marked increased centralization under Stalin and approval of the first Five-Year Plan, signaling the beginning of rapid industrialization and collectivization.
* **Thirteenth Congress (1934):** Last congress before the 1936 Constitution; occurred amid the Great Purge, underscoring the consolidation of Stalin’s authority.

Following the adoption of the **1936 Constitution** (also known as the “Stalin Constitution”), the Congress of Soviets was replaced by the **Supreme Soviet of the USSR**, a more regularly convened legislative body structured to appear more democratic and representative but still firmly controlled by the Communist Party.

---

### Decline and Replacement

The Congress of Soviets’ infrequent meetings and limited independent authority made it increasingly symbolic. The 1936 constitutional reforms reflected a desire to modernize Soviet governance structures, projecting an image of institutional legitimacy to both domestic and international audiences.

The **Supreme Soviet** convened more regularly and was divided into two chambers—the **Soviet of the Union** and the **Soviet of Nationalities**—designed to better represent the USSR’s diverse population. However, like its predecessor, the Supreme Soviet functioned largely as a formal ratifier of decisions made by the party elite.

---

### Political and Historical Significance

The Congress of Soviets represents a foundational phase in Soviet political organization, embodying early Bolshevik ideals of proletarian democracy and soviet power. It institutionalized the soviet system as the framework for governing the newly formed USSR and reflected the attempt to create a socialist state based on workers’ councils.

Despite its theoretical sovereignty, the Congress’s limited practical power illustrates the authoritarian tendencies of the Soviet system, where the Communist Party monopolized political authority and suppressed genuine democratic pluralism.

Its replacement by the Supreme Soviet marked a shift toward more formalized state institutions, while maintaining party dominance. The evolution from the Congress of Soviets to the Supreme Soviet exemplifies the broader trajectory of Soviet political development from revolutionary council democracy to bureaucratic authoritarianism.

---

### Conclusion

The Congress of Soviets of the Soviet Union was a central institution in the early Soviet state, serving as the nominal highest legislative authority from 1922 to 1936. While its political power was circumscribed by Communist Party control, the Congress symbolized the revolutionary transformation of governance and laid the groundwork for subsequent Soviet political structures. Its legacy is integral to understanding the development of Soviet constitutionalism and the role of party-state relations in the USSR.
