**George Prescott Bush** (born April 24, 1976) is an American attorney, military officer, and Republican Party politician who has served as the 28th Land Commissioner of Texas since January 2015. A member of the Bush–Walker political family, he is the son of former Florida Governor Jeb Bush and the nephew of President George W. Bush. His tenure as Land Commissioner and his 2022 gubernatorial campaign illustrate both the continuity and evolution of conservative politics in Texas during the early 21st century.

**Early Life and Education**
Born in Houston, Texas, George P. Bush is the eldest son of Jeb Bush—Governor of Florida from 1999 to 2007—and Columba Bush, née Garnica Gallo. He is the grandson of President George H. W. Bush and First Lady Barbara Bush, and a nephew of President George W. Bush. Raised partly in Florida and Texas, he graduated from Gulliver Preparatory School in Miami in 1994. Bush earned a Bachelor of Arts in Hispanic studies at Rice University in 1998, during which he interned in the Texas Secretary of State’s office. He received his Juris Doctor from the University of Texas School of Law in 2003 and later completed a Master of Science in Geospatial Intelligence at Texas A\&M University in 2018.

**Early Career and Military Service**
After law school, Bush joined the global law firm Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld in Dallas, focusing on business litigation and real estate law. In 2007, he co-founded Pennybacker Capital, a private equity firm that invests in real property and venture-stage enterprises, reflecting the intersection of his legal training and interest in land development.

In 2007 he was commissioned as an intelligence officer in the U.S. Navy Reserve. Assigned to the Navy Information Operations Command in San Antonio, Bush completed deployments to Afghanistan (2009–2010) and aboard the USS Ponce in the Persian Gulf (2012–2013), rising to the rank of lieutenant commander. His military service bolstered his credentials as a public-service leader and positioned him within the post-9/11 generation of veterans entering politics.

**Texas Land Commissioner (2015–Present)**
In 2014, Bush entered electoral politics, winning the Republican nomination for Texas Land Commissioner—the constitutional officer responsible for managing 13 million acres of state trust lands, veteran land grant programs, and the Alamo grounds. He defeated Democratic nominee John Cook in the general election and was sworn in on January 2, 2015. He was re-elected in 2018 with over 50 percent of the vote and again in 2022.

As Land Commissioner, Bush modernized the agency’s outreach to veterans, expanding low-interest land-loan programs and partnering with nonprofit lenders to reduce home-financing barriers. He launched initiatives to map and lease state lands for renewable energy projects—wind, solar, and geologic carbon sequestration—to diversify Texas’s traditional fossil-fuel revenue streams. Under his leadership, the agency digitalized land-title records and enhanced coastal-erosion monitoring, reflecting the growing importance of environmental resilience in state policy.

**2022 Gubernatorial Campaign**
In May 2021, Bush announced his candidacy for the Republican nomination for Governor of Texas, challenging two-term incumbent Greg Abbott. Framing his campaign around “New Generation Conservatism,” he emphasized pragmatic solutions to border security, public-education reform, and pandemic recovery. Despite significant fundraising—bolstered by national name recognition—Bush finished a distant second in the March 2022 primary, receiving approximately 15 percent of the vote to Abbott’s 64 percent. The outcome underscored the challenges faced by establishment-aligned candidates in a party increasingly dominated by Abbott’s brand of conservative populism.

**Political Positions**
Bush’s policy stances combine mainstream Republican priorities with occasional departures from prevailing Texas GOP orthodoxy. He opposes increases in the state sales tax and supports school-choice programs, while advocating for expanded access to affordable housing through veteran land grants. On immigration, he has backed enhanced border security measures and investment in legal ports of entry, but has also called for streamlined pathways to legal residency for long-term undocumented workers. On energy and the environment, his support for renewable-energy development on state lands represents a strategic diversification approach uncommon among Texas Republicans.

**Family and Personal Life**
George P. Bush married law school classmate Meredith Leith in 2007; the couple has three children. A polyglot fluent in English and Spanish, he has emphasized bilingual outreach in his public engagements. He serves on the boards of veterans’ charities and educational nonprofits and has lectured on leadership and public-service ethics at several Texas universities.

**Legacy and Influence**
As a scion of one of America’s most prominent political families, George P. Bush’s career illustrates both the advantages and constraints of political lineage in the 21st century. His tenure as Texas Land Commissioner has modernized a longstanding state office, aligning traditional resource management with emerging economic and environmental challenges. His unsuccessful gubernatorial bid highlighted the evolving dynamics within the Republican Party, where grassroots-driven conservatism sometimes resists establishment figures. Whether in public service, military duty, or civic engagement, Bush’s trajectory reflects the interplay between heritage and innovation in contemporary American politics.
