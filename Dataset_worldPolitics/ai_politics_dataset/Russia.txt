**Russia**

**Overview**
Russia, officially the Russian Federation, is a transcontinental country spanning Eastern Europe and Northern Asia. With a land area of approximately 17.1 million square kilometers, it is the world’s largest nation by territory. Russia has a population of around 146 million and is the world’s ninth most populous country. Its capital and largest city is Moscow. A permanent member of the United Nations Security Council, Russia wields significant influence in global affairs.

**Historical Context**
The territory of modern Russia was home to East Slavic tribes in the 9th century, coalescing into the Kievan Rus’ federation. After Mongol domination (13th–15th centuries), the Grand Duchy of Moscow expanded to form the Tsardom of Russia (1547). Under Peter the Great and Catherine the Great, the Russian Empire (1721–1917) became a major European power. The Russian Revolution of 1917 overthrew the monarchy, leading to the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic (RSFSR) and, in 1922, the formation of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR). Following the USSR’s dissolution in December 1991, the RSFSR emerged as the independent Russian Federation.

**Political System**
Russia is a federal semi-presidential republic. The President, elected for six-year terms (reduced to five from 2024), serves as head of state and holds substantial executive power, including authority over foreign policy, defense, and the appointment of the Prime Minister (head of government), subject to the State Duma’s approval. The Federal Assembly is Russia’s bicameral legislature, consisting of the State Duma (450 deputies elected for five-year terms) and the Federation Council (two representatives from each of the 85 federal subjects).

Since 2000, Vladimir Putin—alternating between the presidency and premiership—has dominated Russian politics. Constitutional amendments approved in 2020 reset presidential term limits, potentially allowing Putin to serve until 2036. Critics contend these changes and controls over media, civil society, and electoral processes have consolidated power and weakened checks and balances.

**Administrative Divisions**
Russia comprises 85 federal subjects: 46 oblasts, 22 republics (each with its own constitution and official language), nine krais, four autonomous okrugs, one autonomous oblast, and three federal cities (Moscow, Saint Petersburg, and Sevastopol). Subjects possess varying degrees of autonomy, but federal authority remains strong, especially under centralized governance since the early 2000s.

**Economy**
Russia has a mixed economy with abundant natural resources, particularly oil and natural gas, which account for a significant share of exports and state revenue. It is among the world’s largest producers of hydrocarbons and metals. After market reforms in the 1990s, rapid growth in the early 2000s under high energy prices improved living standards, but wealth remains unevenly distributed. Western sanctions following the 2014 annexation of Crimea and the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, as well as Russian countersanctions, have impacted investment, the financial sector, and trade. In response, Russia has pursued import substitution, closer economic ties with China, and efforts to de-dollarize its reserves.

**Foreign Policy and Security**
Russia seeks to maintain regional hegemony in the former Soviet space and to project power globally. It is a founding member of the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS), the Collective Security Treaty Organization (CSTO), and the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU). Russia’s 2014 annexation of Crimea, support for separatists in Eastern Ukraine, and full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 prompted widespread international condemnation, NATO reinforcement of Eastern Europe, and Russia’s diplomatic isolation in the West.

Russia’s military modernization, nuclear arsenal (one of the world’s largest), and intervention in Syria (2015–present) underscore its strategic ambitions. It maintains a permanent seat on the UN Security Council, through which it exercises veto power, often in coordination with China.

**Society and Culture**
Russia’s population is ethnically diverse, with Russians comprising around 80 percent and over 190 other ethnic groups recognized officially. The Russian Orthodox Church plays a prominent role in national identity, while Islam, Buddhism, and other faiths have significant followings. Urbanization is high, with over 75 percent living in cities. Demographic challenges include a low birth rate, high mortality rate among working-age males, and population decline, which the government addresses through pronatalist programs and controlled labor migration from Central Asia and the Caucasus.

Russia boasts a rich cultural heritage in literature (Tolstoy, Dostoevsky), music (Tchaikovsky, Stravinsky), ballet (Bolshoi), visual arts (Kandinsky), and space exploration (first human in space, Yuri Gagarin). State support for culture remains strong, though censorship and emphasis on patriotic themes have increased under recent administrations.

**Human Rights and Governance Challenges**
International observers and domestic critics cite constraints on political freedoms, media pluralism, and civil society. Laws against “foreign agents” and “extremism” have targeted NGOs, independent media, and opposition figures. High-profile cases include the imprisonment of Alexei Navalny, a leading opposition activist, and suppression of protests following the 2021 Duma elections.

Corruption and lack of judicial independence are persistent issues. Transparency International ranks Russia low on its Corruption Perceptions Index. Economic inequality and regional disparities also pose governance challenges.

**Recent Developments**
In 2022, Russia’s invasion of Ukraine triggered unprecedented sanctions, heightened military tensions, and debates over Europe’s energy security. Domestically, the government has tightened political controls, while economic growth slowed amid sanctions and demographic headwinds. Russia continues to navigate relations with major powers—balancing confrontation with the West against deepening ties with China and seeking new markets in Asia and Africa.

**Conclusion**
Russia’s evolution—from Tsarist empire to Soviet superpower and, since 1991, a federal republic—reflects its pivotal role in global history. Today, Russia’s political system combines strong presidential authority with limited pluralism, shaping its domestic policies and international posture. As it confronts economic sanctions, demographic shifts, and geopolitical rivalry, Russia remains a central actor in 21st-century international affairs.
