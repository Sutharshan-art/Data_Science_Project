**Prime Minister of Latvia**

The **Prime Minister of Latvia** (Latvian: *Latvijas Ministru prezidents*) is the head of government of the Republic of Latvia. The prime minister leads the Cabinet of Ministers, formulates government policy, and represents the government domestically and internationally. Appointed by the President of Latvia and confirmed by the Saeima (parliament), the prime minister holds significant executive authority within the framework of a parliamentary democracy.

The position has existed during both the interwar Republic of Latvia (1918–1940) and since the restoration of Latvian independence from Soviet rule in 1991. The office has evolved in response to shifts in Latvia’s political system, regional challenges, and membership in international organizations such as the European Union and NATO.

---

### Historical Origins

The position of Prime Minister was first established following the proclamation of Latvian independence on **18 November 1918**, in the aftermath of World War I and the collapse of the Russian Empire. The first head of government was **Kārlis Ulmanis**, who also played a key role in organizing the provisional government during the Latvian War of Independence (1918–1920).

During the interwar period, Latvia was a parliamentary democracy, and the prime minister served as the chief executive. The political landscape was characterized by coalition governments and frequent changes in leadership. In 1934, Ulmanis staged a coup d'état, establishing an authoritarian regime and abolishing parliamentary democracy. He ruled as both prime minister and president until the Soviet occupation in 1940.

---

### Soviet and Nazi Occupation (1940–1991)

From 1940 to 1991, Latvia was occupied successively by the Soviet Union, Nazi Germany, and again the USSR. During this period, the office of prime minister ceased to exist, and governance was conducted under the framework of the Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic, a constituent republic of the Soviet Union. De facto power was held by the Communist Party and the First Secretary of the Latvian Communist Party.

---

### Restoration of Independence and Modern Role

Latvia declared the restoration of its independence on **4 May 1990**, a process completed in **August 1991** following the failed Soviet coup. The modern office of the prime minister was re-established as part of the reintroduction of a parliamentary democratic system, grounded in the **1990 Declaration of Independence** and the **Satversme** (Latvian Constitution), which was originally adopted in 1922 and reinstated after independence.

Since 1991, the prime minister has been nominated by the **President of Latvia** and must be confirmed by a majority in the **Saeima**, Latvia’s unicameral parliament. The prime minister appoints ministers, leads cabinet meetings, and coordinates national policy. Although formally subordinate to the president, in practice the prime minister holds greater day-to-day authority over government operations.

---

### Political Dynamics and Coalition Governance

Latvia’s multi-party political system often results in coalition governments, requiring prime ministers to navigate complex negotiations among diverse parties. This dynamic has led to both political instability and frequent cabinet reshuffles.

Despite these challenges, Latvia has maintained democratic governance since 1991. Prime ministers have played central roles in major national developments, including Latvia’s accession to the **European Union** and **NATO** in 2004, economic reforms in the aftermath of the 2008 global financial crisis, and responses to regional security concerns related to Russia.

---

### Notable Prime Ministers

* **Kārlis Ulmanis** (1918–1934; 1934–1940): A founding figure of Latvian independence, Ulmanis led multiple governments before consolidating authoritarian rule in 1934.

* **Valdis Dombrovskis** (2009–2014): Oversaw major austerity and economic recovery measures following the 2008 financial crisis; later became a European Commissioner.

* **Laimdota Straujuma** (2014–2016): The first woman to serve as prime minister of Latvia, leading during a time of regional tension after the annexation of Crimea.

* **Krišjānis Kariņš** (2019–2023): Led a centrist, pro-European coalition and guided Latvia through the COVID-19 pandemic and energy diversification efforts after the Russian invasion of Ukraine.

---

### Current Prime Minister

As of 2025, the office is held by **Evika Siliņa**, who took office in **September 2023**. A member of the center-right *New Unity* party, Siliņa previously served as Minister of Welfare. Her government continues to emphasize EU integration, defense cooperation, and digital innovation while managing domestic challenges such as inflation and social inequality.

---

### Institutional Role

The prime minister’s powers are defined by the **Satversme**, which enshrines the principle of parliamentary supremacy. While the president has formal authority to nominate the prime minister and approve cabinet appointments, the Saeima ultimately determines the government's composition and continuation through votes of confidence or no-confidence.

The prime minister must maintain the confidence of the Saeima, requiring continual coalition management. Executive decisions are made collectively within the Cabinet, with the prime minister acting as first among equals.

---

### Conclusion

The Prime Minister of Latvia plays a pivotal role in guiding the country's domestic and foreign policy within a framework of democratic governance and parliamentary accountability. From its inception during Latvia’s first independence to its current function in a modern European democracy, the office reflects the evolving political landscape and challenges of a small state at the crossroads of Europe and Russia.
