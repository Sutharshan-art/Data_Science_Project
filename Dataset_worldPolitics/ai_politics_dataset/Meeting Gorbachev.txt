**Meeting Gorbachev**

*Meeting Gorbachev* is a 2018 documentary film directed and narrated by German filmmaker **Werner Herzog**, co-directed by British documentarian **André Singer**. The film centers on a series of interviews conducted by Herzog with **Mikhail Gorbachev**, the last leader of the **Soviet Union**, offering a reflective portrait of Gorbachev’s political career, personal philosophy, and historical legacy. Through archival footage and candid conversation, the film explores Gorbachev’s role in reshaping the political landscape of the late 20th century, particularly his leadership during the final years of the Cold War and the eventual dissolution of the USSR.

Presented through Herzog’s characteristic narrative style, *Meeting Gorbachev* serves not only as a personal examination of a world leader but also as a broader meditation on power, reform, and the collapse of empires. The documentary contributes to the historical understanding of the Soviet Union’s transformation and the emergence of a post-Cold War global order.

---

### Background and Production

The idea for *Meeting Gorbachev* emerged from Herzog’s long-standing interest in political figures who have had a profound impact on world history. Herzog, known for both fictional and documentary films that explore human resilience and extremity, saw Gorbachev as a unique subject whose political decisions altered the course of the 20th century. Herzog conducted three extensive interviews with Gorbachev in 2017 and 2018 in Moscow, during which Gorbachev was in declining health but remained intellectually engaged.

The film was produced by Spring Films and Werner Herzog Film, in association with A\&E IndieFilms. It premiered at the **Telluride Film Festival** in September 2018 and was later screened at other international festivals before receiving a limited theatrical release in 2019.

---

### Synopsis and Themes

*Meeting Gorbachev* is structured around Herzog’s direct interviews with Gorbachev, interwoven with historical footage of key events from his tenure as General Secretary of the **Communist Party of the Soviet Union** (1985–1991). The documentary traces Gorbachev’s early life, political rise, and the major reforms he implemented—most notably **perestroika** (restructuring) and **glasnost** (openness).

Key themes include:

* **Reform and Resistance**: The film details how Gorbachev’s domestic reforms aimed to modernize the Soviet economy and political system but faced resistance from hardliners within the Communist Party.

* **Arms Control and Diplomacy**: Gorbachev’s relationship with Western leaders, particularly U.S. President **Ronald Reagan**, is explored in depth. The film highlights the significance of the **Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty (INF)** and other efforts at de-escalating the Cold War.

* **German Reunification**: Gorbachev’s pivotal role in enabling the peaceful reunification of Germany in 1990 is presented as one of his most consequential international decisions, marking a turning point in post-war European history.

* **Dissolution of the USSR**: The film addresses the internal political turmoil that led to the fragmentation of the Soviet Union, including the failed 1991 coup attempt and Gorbachev’s resignation in December of that year.

Herzog’s commentary frames Gorbachev as a tragic figure—admired abroad for ending the Cold War without violence, but often criticized at home for presiding over the disintegration of the Soviet state.

---

### Interviews and Archival Footage

Beyond Gorbachev himself, the film includes insights from other global figures and historians. Interviewees include **Horst Teltschik**, a former advisor to German Chancellor **Helmut Kohl**, and **Lech Wałęsa**, the Polish trade union leader and Nobel Peace Prize laureate. Archival clips feature U.S. President **George H. W. Bush**, British Prime Minister **Margaret Thatcher**, and footage from major summits and public addresses.

The combination of personal reminiscence and geopolitical context gives viewers a multifaceted understanding of Gorbachev’s era. Gorbachev reflects on both his achievements and regrets, including his wife Raisa’s illness and death, which he discusses with visible emotion.

---

### Reception and Critical Response

*Meeting Gorbachev* received generally positive reviews from critics, who praised Herzog’s intimate approach and the historical importance of the subject. Commentators highlighted the contrast between Herzog’s typically existential style and the more restrained tone adopted for this project.

The film was noted for its sympathetic portrayal of Gorbachev, often casting him in a humanistic light in contrast to the typical depiction of Cold War leaders. However, some critics pointed out that the documentary offered limited critical analysis of the economic failures and political chaos associated with Gorbachev’s reforms.

---

### Historical and Political Context

The documentary situates Gorbachev within a unique historical moment at the intersection of domestic upheaval and global transformation. As the last Soviet leader, Gorbachev presided over a superpower in decline and attempted to reform it from within—only to see it dissolve. His policies hastened the end of the Cold War but also unintentionally triggered a chain of events that led to economic collapse, the rise of nationalist movements, and the re-emergence of Russian authoritarianism in the decades that followed.

Herzog’s portrayal emphasizes Gorbachev’s legacy as a reformer and a peacemaker, aligning with a view held more broadly in the West than in post-Soviet Russia. In doing so, the film contributes to ongoing debates about leadership, accountability, and the unintended consequences of transformative politics.

---

### Legacy

*Meeting Gorbachev* stands as a significant documentary portrayal of one of the 20th century’s most influential political figures. Through its blend of biography, political history, and reflective dialogue, the film offers an accessible entry point into understanding the end of the Cold War and the complex legacy of Mikhail Gorbachev.
