**David Young, Baron Young of Graffham**

David Ivor Young, Baron Young of Graffham, PC (born 22 April 1932) is a British businessman and Conservative politician who held senior ministerial office in Margaret Thatcher’s government. Over the course of his career he served as Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster (1984–1985), Secretary of State for Employment (1985–1987), and Secretary of State for Trade and Industry (1987–1989), playing a key role in the implementation of Thatcherite economic and industrial policies during the late Cold War era.

## Early Life and Business Career

David Young was born in Chalfont St Giles, Buckinghamshire, to a family active in local commerce. He was educated at Charterhouse School and read Modern History at New College, Oxford, graduating with upper-second-class honours in 1953. After National Service in the Royal Air Force (1953–1955), Young embarked on a career in business. He joined Unilever in 1956 and rose to become a director of the company’s margarine division by the early 1970s.

In 1978, Young left Unilever to take up the chairmanship of the food group Tate & Lyle, where he presided over diversification into sugar trading and industrial sweeteners. His success in revitalizing traditional manufacturing sectors brought him to the attention of Conservative Party leaders seeking managerial talent to translate into public-sector reform.

## Entry into Politics and Life Peerage

In recognition of his business acumen—and his contributions as co-chairman of the Conservatives’ Business and Industry Committee—Young was created a life peer on 5 September 1984 as Baron Young of Graffham, of Graffham in the County of West Sussex. He entered the House of Lords and was appointed Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster in October 1984, a sinecure post that carried responsibility for City of London policy and Whitehall efficiency reviews.

## Ministerial Offices

### Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster (1984–1985)

As Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster, Young oversaw the government’s initiative to reduce public-sector borrowing and streamline administration. He chaired the “Young Committee” on local authority housing finance in 1985, which led to the introduction of the Housing Revenue Account Subsidy system aimed at creating a more transparent funding model for council housing.

### Secretary of State for Employment (1985–1987)

In June 1985, Young was appointed Secretary of State for Employment. His tenure coincided with high unemployment—peaking above three million—and ongoing industrial disputes. He championed legislation to curb secondary picketing and to tighten trade-union ballots, reinforcing Thatcher’s drive to reduce union power. The Employment Act 1985, introduced under his watch, outlawed closed-shop agreements and extended restrictions on union activities.

### Secretary of State for Trade and Industry (1987–1989)

After the 1987 general election, Young became Secretary of State for Trade and Industry. In this capacity he led efforts to privatize remaining state-owned enterprises, including the sale of British Airways shares to the public, and to deregulate key sectors. He also presided over the White Paper “Competition and Choice,” which laid the groundwork for further liberalization of telecommunications and energy markets. During his term, the Department of Trade and Industry negotiated Britain’s entry into the European Economic Community’s single market, implementing the Single European Act’s directives on mutual recognition and competition policy.

## Political and Historical Context

Young’s ministerial career unfolded during a period of intense ideological contest between state interventionism and free-market liberalism. Operating against the backdrop of Cold War tensions, the Thatcher government viewed industrial modernization as essential for Western competitiveness. Young’s policies reflected the broader Conservative commitment to roll back the post-war welfare consensus, promote privatization, and diminish the power of trade unions—all key elements in the UK’s transition to a service-oriented economy.

## Later Career and Public Service

Resigning from the Cabinet in July 1989, Young returned to the private sector as chairman of British Aerospace (1989–1992), overseeing its merger with Marconi Electronic Systems in a landmark consolidation of the UK defence industry. In 1993 he was appointed chairman of the privatized Lloyd’s of London insurance market during its post-“Lloyd’s crisis” restructuring. He also served on the boards of the Financial Times, the Carlyle Group, and several nonprofit organisations, including as chairman of the Henry Jackson Society.

## Honours and Personal Life

Lord Young was appointed to the Privy Council in 1985. He has received honorary doctorates from the University of Sussex and the London School of Economics for his contributions to public policy and business. Married since 1959 to Mary Elizabeth Miranda Peto, they have two children. An active philanthropist, he has supported educational initiatives aimed at fostering entrepreneurship and leadership in young people.

## Legacy

David Young’s blend of corporate leadership and high-level ministerial responsibility embodies the Thatcherite integration of private-sector expertise into government. His reforms in employment law and industry policy contributed to reshaping the United Kingdom’s economic landscape in the late 20th century, influencing the balance between market forces and state regulation. His subsequent stewardship of major defence and financial institutions further underscored the revolving-door relationship between government and business that became characteristic of the era.
