**Anti-Fascist Council for the National Liberation of Yugoslavia**

The **Anti-Fascist Council for the National Liberation of Yugoslavia** (Serbo-Croatian: *Antifašističko vijeće narodnog oslobođenja Jugoslavije*, AVNOJ) was the political body established during World War II to lead the resistance movement against Axis occupation and to lay the foundation for the postwar federal Yugoslav state. Formed by the **Yugoslav Partisans**, led by **Josip Broz Tito**, AVNOJ became the de facto provisional government of Yugoslavia and played a pivotal role in shaping the country's political future.

---

### Historical Background

Following the Axis invasion and subsequent dismemberment of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia in April 1941, the country was divided between German, Italian, Hungarian, Bulgarian, and puppet regimes. The occupation sparked a multi-front resistance, with two major factions emerging: the royalist **Chetniks** and the communist-led Partisans.

The Partisans, organized under the **Communist Party of Yugoslavia (KPJ)**, quickly gained support due to their commitment to national liberation and social revolution. Recognizing the need for political legitimacy and coordination, the Partisans sought to establish a political body to unify various ethnic groups and anti-fascist forces across the fragmented territory.

---

### Formation and First Session (1942)

The first session of AVNOJ took place in **Bihać** in November 1942. It brought together representatives from all major Yugoslav ethnic groups and regions under Partisan control. The council declared itself the supreme legislative and executive authority of the liberated territories and rejected the authority of the royal government-in-exile based in London.

AVNOJ emphasized principles of federalism and equality among Yugoslavia's constituent nations, aiming to overcome the ethnic tensions that had historically plagued the country. The council also outlined plans for a postwar socialist federation, asserting the right of self-determination for national groups within Yugoslavia.

---

### Second Session (1943) and Political Consolidation

The second, more decisive AVNOJ session was held in **Jajce** in November 1943. This gathering further solidified the council’s authority, declaring itself the supreme legislative body of Yugoslavia. It formally rejected the government-in-exile’s claims and prohibited King **Peter II** from returning without approval of the new government, signaling a break with the monarchy.

AVNOJ’s resolutions established the framework for a federal Yugoslavia composed of six republics: Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, Macedonia, Montenegro, Serbia, and Slovenia. The council adopted the principle of equality for these republics, setting the stage for the postwar socialist federation.

---

### Role During the War

Throughout World War II, AVNOJ functioned as the political leadership of the Partisan movement, coordinating military resistance and diplomatic efforts. It served as the basis for international recognition of the Partisans as the legitimate representatives of Yugoslavia, gaining support from the Allied powers, especially after the **Tehran Conference** in 1943.

The council worked alongside the Partisan military command to organize liberated territories and to prepare for postwar reconstruction. It fostered cooperation among diverse ethnic groups and sought to build a Yugoslav identity transcending prewar divisions.

---

### Postwar Transition and Legacy

After the war, AVNOJ’s authority transitioned into the new government of the **Federal People’s Republic of Yugoslavia**, formally proclaimed in 1945 with Tito as prime minister. The political and territorial framework laid down by AVNOJ informed the 1946 Yugoslav constitution, establishing a federal socialist state.

The principles of federalism, ethnic equality, and socialist governance enshrined by AVNOJ shaped Yugoslavia’s political structure for decades. The council’s decisions also legitimized the exclusion of the monarchy and consolidated the Communist Party’s control.

---

### Political and Historical Context

AVNOJ emerged from the complex interplay of resistance against fascist occupation, ethnic nationalism, and socialist revolution. It sought to address the failures of the interwar Yugoslav state by promoting a new federal order aimed at managing ethnic diversity within a socialist framework.

The council’s assertive rejection of the royal government-in-exile reflected broader geopolitical shifts during World War II, as the Allies increasingly supported communist-led resistance movements in Eastern Europe.

AVNOJ also embodied the growing influence of communist ideology in the region, advocating social and political transformation alongside national liberation.

---

### Conclusion

The Anti-Fascist Council for the National Liberation of Yugoslavia was a foundational institution in the creation of socialist Yugoslavia. It played a critical role in coordinating the wartime resistance, establishing political legitimacy for the Partisans, and shaping the federal structure of postwar Yugoslavia. AVNOJ’s legacy is closely tied to the development of Yugoslav socialism and the complex management of ethnic and national identities within the federation.
