**George Herbert Walker III** (March 16, 1931 – January 19, 2020) was an American businessman, diplomat, and civic leader who served as the United States Ambassador to Hungary from 2003 to 2006. A scion of the Walker–Bush family, he was a first cousin of President George H. W. Bush and first cousin once removed of President George W. Bush. Walker’s career spanned finance, politics, and philanthropy, reflecting the evolving role of business leaders in American public life during the late 20th and early 21st centuries.

**Early Life and Education**
Born in St. Louis, Missouri, Walker was the son of George Herbert Walker Jr.—cofounder of the New York Mets—and Mary Carter Walker. His paternal grandfather, George Herbert Walker, founded G. H. Walker & Co., later part of Merrill Lynch ([en.wikipedia.org][1]). Walker graduated from Yale University in 1953, where he was a member of the Skull and Bones society, and earned a J.D. from Harvard Law School in 1955 ([en.wikipedia.org][1]). He served two years as a U.S. Air Force staff judge advocate at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio.

**Business Career**
After military service, Walker returned to St. Louis to join his family’s firm, G. H. Walker & Co., before moving to Stifel Nicolaus in 1977. As President and later Chairman and CEO of Stifel Nicolaus, he led the firm through its initial public offering and expansion on the New York Stock Exchange, exemplifying the trend of regional investment houses growing into national brokerages in the late 20th century ([en.wikipedia.org][1]).

**Entry into Politics**
In 1992, amid the Republican realignment under Presidents Reagan and his cousin George H. W. Bush, Walker sought the GOP nomination for Missouri’s 2nd Congressional District. He was defeated in the Republican primary by State Representative Jim Talent (58 percent to 32 percent), reflecting the competitive shift toward conservative grassroots candidates in the post–Cold War era ([en.wikipedia.org][1]).

**Diplomatic Service**
Walker’s political involvement and family connections led President George W. Bush to nominate him as U.S. Ambassador to Hungary. Confirmed in 2003, he presented his credentials on October 6, 2003, and served until August 4, 2006. His tenure coincided with Hungary’s deeper integration into NATO and the European Union, tasks requiring adept navigation of post–Soviet Central European politics and U.S. strategic interests in the region ([en.wikipedia.org][1], [hu.usembassy.gov][2]).

**Philanthropy and Civic Engagement**
A committed philanthropist, Walker served on the Board of Trustees of Webster University from 1974. His 2005 gift endowed the George Herbert Walker School of Business and Technology, bolstering regional higher education ([en.wikipedia.org][1]). In 2016, he and his wife Carol established the Walker Leadership Institute at Eden Theological Seminary, underscoring his interest in “common good” leadership blending business acumen with faith-based values ([en.wikipedia.org][1]).

**Family Legacy and Personal Life**
Walker married Carol Banta, with whom he had eight children and fifteen grandchildren. His son, George Herbert Walker IV, continues the family tradition in finance as CEO of Neuberger Berman. Walker’s close ties to the Bush political dynasty—campaign finance roles for Presidents Nixon and Reagan, and active support for his cousin’s campaigns—highlight the interplay between family networks and American political power in the late 20th century ([bettertogetherstl.com][3]).

**Death and Legacy**
Walker died on January 19, 2020, in St. Louis at age 88 after complications from a stroke. His career bridged private-sector growth and public service, illustrating the expanding role of business executives in diplomacy and civic leadership. As ambassador, party activist, and philanthropist, George Herbert Walker III embodied the post–Cold War generation of American elites who navigated shifting political, economic, and international landscapes.
