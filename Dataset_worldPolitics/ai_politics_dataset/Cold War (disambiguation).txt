**Cold War (disambiguation)**

The term **Cold War** primarily refers to the prolonged period of geopolitical tension and ideological rivalry between the **United States** and the **Soviet Union**, along with their respective allies, following **World War II**. It is generally dated from the end of the war in **1945** to the **dissolution of the Soviet Union** in **1991**. This period, though it did not result in direct large-scale warfare between the two nuclear superpowers, was marked by proxy wars, nuclear arms races, political subversion, espionage, and diplomatic confrontation on a global scale.

However, “Cold War” has also been used more broadly in other historical, political, and cultural contexts. These include similar periods of non-combat conflict, as well as various books, films, and concepts inspired by or reflective of Cold War dynamics. Below is a disambiguation of the term in political, historical, and cultural usage.

---

### Primary Historical Usage

**Cold War (1947–1991):**
The original and most recognized meaning of the term refers to the post-World War II conflict between the United States (representing Western capitalist democracies) and the Soviet Union (representing Marxist-Leninist socialism). It was defined by:

* **Military alliances**: NATO vs. Warsaw Pact
* **Nuclear deterrence and arms race**
* **Proxy wars** in Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan, and parts of Africa and Latin America
* **Intelligence operations**, ideological propaganda, and espionage
* **Space Race** and scientific competition
* **Economic and technological rivalry**
* **Domestic anti-communism** and censorship policies in both blocs

Despite the lack of direct warfare between the two main powers, the Cold War deeply influenced international relations, domestic politics, and global security architecture for nearly five decades.

### Alternate Historical and Political Usages

**Cold War (term):**
The phrase “cold war” has been used generically since at least the 19th century to describe prolonged periods of hostility short of open warfare. The term gained prominence in the mid-20th century with American financier **Bernard Baruch** and British author **George Orwell** using it in political discourse to describe the emerging postwar order.

**Second Cold War (1979–1985):**
A specific phase of renewed Cold War tensions following the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan, characterized by deteriorating U.S.–Soviet relations, increased military buildup, and the rhetoric of confrontation, especially under U.S. President **Ronald Reagan**.

**Cold War II:**
A contemporary concept used by some scholars, analysts, and politicians to describe escalating rivalry—primarily between the **United States** and the **People’s Republic of China** (and to a lesser extent, Russia) in the 21st century. The analogy is debated and refers to trade wars, cyberconflict, technological competition, and military posturing in the **Asia-Pacific** and **Indo-Pacific** regions.

**Cultural Cold War:**
A term used to describe the use of **soft power** by both the U.S. and USSR to win hearts and minds through media, literature, academic exchanges, and cultural diplomacy. It involved entities such as the **Congress for Cultural Freedom**, and highlighted how the arts became arenas of ideological contest.

### Arts and Media

**Cold War (2012 film):**
A Hong Kong political action thriller directed by **Longman Leung and Sunny Luk**, involving high-level police corruption and institutional crisis. While not about the historical Cold War, the title metaphorically references internal tension and power struggles.

**Cold War (2018 film):**
A Polish historical romantic drama directed by **Paweł Pawlikowski**, set in the 1950s–60s Europe. It portrays a complex love story between two artists divided by ideological and geographical barriers of the Cold War era. The film won international acclaim and was nominated for multiple Academy Awards.

**Cold War (Doctor Who):**
An episode from the British science fiction series *Doctor Who*, aired in 2013, involving a Soviet submarine and an extraterrestrial threat. The episode plays with Cold War tropes of paranoia and global tension.

**Cold War (video games):**
Several video games have adopted the Cold War as a setting, most notably:

* *Call of Duty: Black Ops Cold War* (2020), which blends historical events with fictional narratives rooted in espionage and military operations during the 1980s.
* *Cold War* (2005), a stealth-action video game involving a journalist caught in political conspiracies in the Soviet Union.

### See Also

* **Iron Curtain** – Symbolic and physical division between Western Europe and Eastern Bloc countries.
* **Detente** – Periods of eased geopolitical tension during the Cold War, especially in the 1970s.
* **Glasnost and Perestroika** – Reforms under Gorbachev that contributed to the Cold War’s end.
* **Non-Aligned Movement** – A group of states that sought to avoid alignment with either the U.S. or Soviet blocs.

---

### Conclusion

While the Cold War originally referred to a specific period of geopolitical struggle between the United States and the Soviet Union, the term has since evolved into a broader metaphor for non-violent conflict, ideological rivalry, and strategic competition in global affairs. It has also permeated popular culture, media, and academic discourse, reflecting its lasting impact on the modern world.
