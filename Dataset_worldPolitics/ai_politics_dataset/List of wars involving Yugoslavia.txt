**List of Wars Involving Yugoslavia**

The **list of wars involving Yugoslavia** encompasses military conflicts that took place under the various political incarnations of Yugoslavia during the 20th century. Created in the aftermath of World War I and dissolved in the early 2000s, Yugoslavia existed as a multi-ethnic state in Southeast Europe. Throughout its history, the country experienced both foreign invasions and internal conflicts, including wars of resistance, civil wars, and separatist movements that ultimately led to its fragmentation.

Yugoslavia underwent three major political phases: the **Kingdom of Yugoslavia (1918–1941)**, the **Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (1945–1992)**, and the **Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (1992–2003)**, which was later reconstituted as Serbia and Montenegro. Each phase was marked by distinct conflicts reflecting broader geopolitical and ethnic dynamics in the Balkans and beyond.

---

### Kingdom of Yugoslavia (1918–1941)

#### **Formation and Interwar Tensions**

The Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes was formed in 1918 after World War I, uniting several South Slavic peoples. It was renamed the **Kingdom of Yugoslavia** in 1929. The interwar period saw rising nationalist tensions, particularly between Serbs and Croats, and growing authoritarianism under King Alexander I.

#### **World War II and Axis Invasion (1941)**

* **Belligerents:** Axis Powers vs. Kingdom of Yugoslavia
* **Context:** In April 1941, Axis forces invaded and quickly defeated Yugoslavia after a military coup opposed to alignment with the Axis.
* **Outcome:** The country was dismembered; puppet regimes were installed, most notably the Independent State of Croatia (NDH), which carried out genocidal policies against Serbs, Jews, and Roma.

---

### Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (1945–1992)

After World War II, Yugoslavia was reestablished as a socialist federation under **Josip Broz Tito**, a former leader of the Partisan resistance.

#### **World War II Resistance (1941–1945)**

* **Belligerents:** Yugoslav Partisans vs. Axis occupiers and collaborationist forces
* **Context:** The Partisans, a communist-led resistance movement, fought against Axis forces and domestic collaborators like the Chetniks and Ustaše.
* **Outcome:** The Partisans emerged victorious and established the Federal People’s Republic of Yugoslavia in 1945.

#### **Tito–Stalin Split and Internal Security Operations**

* While not involving formal war, the post-war years included crackdowns on perceived internal enemies and alleged Soviet sympathizers, such as the 1948 split with the Soviet Union.
* Yugoslavia also maintained a large military force prepared to defend its non-aligned status during the Cold War.

---

### Federal Republic of Yugoslavia and the Yugoslav Wars (1991–2001)

Following Tito’s death in 1980, Yugoslavia faced economic decline and rising nationalism. The federation began to unravel in the early 1990s, leading to a series of **Yugoslav Wars** that accompanied the breakup of the country.

#### **Ten-Day War (1991)**

* **Belligerents:** Yugoslav People’s Army (JNA) vs. Slovenian Territorial Defense
* **Context:** Slovenia declared independence; the JNA attempted to stop secession.
* **Outcome:** Slovenia retained independence after a brief conflict and the Brioni Agreement.

#### **Croatian War of Independence (1991–1995)**

* **Belligerents:** Croatia vs. JNA and Serb paramilitaries
* **Context:** Croatia’s secession triggered conflict with ethnic Serbs backed by Belgrade.
* **Outcome:** Croatia eventually gained control over most of its territory after major operations in 1995.

#### **Bosnian War (1992–1995)**

* **Belligerents:** Bosnia and Herzegovina vs. Bosnian Serb, Croat, and Muslim forces
* **Context:** Following Bosnia's independence, war broke out among its ethnic groups, with major involvement from Serbia and Croatia.
* **Notable events:** Siege of Sarajevo, Srebrenica massacre
* **Outcome:** Dayton Accords ended the war, establishing Bosnia as a single state with two entities.

#### **Kosovo War (1998–1999)**

* **Belligerents:** Federal Republic of Yugoslavia vs. Kosovo Liberation Army (KLA) and later NATO
* **Context:** Ethnic Albanian demands for independence led to conflict and human rights abuses.
* **NATO intervention:** NATO launched airstrikes against Yugoslavia in 1999 without UN authorization.
* **Outcome:** Yugoslav forces withdrew; Kosovo was placed under UN administration.

---

### Post-War and Dissolution

#### **Insurgency in the Preševo Valley (1999–2001)**

* **Belligerents:** Yugoslav forces vs. ethnic Albanian insurgents in southern Serbia
* **Context:** Part of post-Kosovo tensions; eventually resolved through international mediation.

#### **Macedonian Conflict (2001)**

* Though not directly involving Yugoslavia, tensions in neighboring Macedonia were influenced by fallout from the Kosovo War, involving ethnic Albanians and the Macedonian government.

---

### Final Dissolution

In 2003, the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia was restructured into **Serbia and Montenegro**. This union lasted until **2006**, when Montenegro declared independence, marking the end of Yugoslavia as a political entity.

---

### Conclusion

The **list of wars involving Yugoslavia** reflects the dramatic shifts in Southeast Europe across the 20th century. From monarchy and foreign occupation to socialist federation and eventual dissolution into multiple states, Yugoslavia's military history mirrors its political evolution. The legacy of these wars continues to shape regional relations, international justice, and collective memory in the Western Balkans and beyond.
