**Veljko Ražnatović**

**Overview**
Veljko Ražnatović (20 May 1920 – 17 June 1986) was a Montenegrin officer in the Yugoslav Partisan movement during World War II and later a Colonel in the Yugoslav Air Force. He served as a political commissar and staff officer, playing a key role in both the wartime resistance against Axis occupation and the postwar establishment and professionalization of Yugoslavia’s military aviation. He is also known as the father of Željko “Arkan” Ražnatović, the paramilitary leader active during the Yugoslav Wars of the 1990s.

**Early Life and Communist Party Involvement**
Born in the village of Rijeka Crnojevića near Cetinje in what was then the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, Ražnatović became active in communist youth circles just before World War II. In 1939 he joined the League of Communist Youth of Yugoslavia and, in May 1941, became a full member of the Communist Party. His early underground activities in Montenegro laid the foundation for his wartime service.

**World War II Service**
Following the Axis invasion of Yugoslavia in April 1941, Ražnatović joined the Partisan uprising in Montenegro. He quickly rose through the ranks, serving as both deputy commander and company commander in frontline units. He later served in the political departments of several major Partisan formations, including the Tenth Krajina Strike Brigade and the 23rd and 46th Serbian Divisions, where his duties focused on political education, morale, and coordination with local civilian populations.

**Postwar Military Career**
After the war, Ražnatović was transferred to the newly formed Yugoslav Air Force. In August 1945 he became the Political Commissar of the Air Force Military School, overseeing the creation of new regiments, the organization of air bases, and the development of aviation training programs. Over the next decade, he held commissar roles at key air force installations in Brežice, Zagreb, and Pančevo, and from 1950 to 1953 served as Political Commissar of the 37th Aviation Division, one of the Air Force’s primary combat units.

**Political and Military Context**
His career epitomized the dual military–political leadership model of socialist Yugoslavia, in which political commissars ensured the loyalty of the armed forces to the ruling Communist Party. His work took place during a critical period that included Yugoslavia’s split from the Soviet Union in 1948, after which maintaining a politically reliable officer corps became paramount. Ražnatović’s trusted positions within both the Partisan movement and the postwar military hierarchy reflected his standing within the party.

**Decorations and Honors**
Throughout his service, Ražnatović received several Yugoslav military honors, including medals commemorating his early Partisan activity, orders recognizing his bravery and leadership, and awards for his contributions to the development of the Yugoslav Air Force.

**Personal Life**
Ražnatović married Slavka Josifović, with whom he had four children: Mirjana, Jasna, Biljana, and Željko. His youngest son, Željko, later became known as “Arkan” and led the Serb Volunteer Guard during the conflicts in the former Yugoslavia, an association that added a complex posthumous dimension to Veljko Ražnatović’s legacy.

**Death and Legacy**
Veljko Ražnatović died on 17 June 1986 in Belgrade, Socialist Republic of Serbia. He is remembered for his significant contributions to Yugoslavia’s wartime resistance and the postwar establishment of its air force. His career illustrates the integration of military and political roles in socialist Yugoslavia and highlights the importance of political commissars in maintaining state and party control over the military. His legacy is further shaped by the later notoriety of his son, linking his family name to two very different chapters of Yugoslav and post-Yugoslav history.
