**World War II**

**Overview**
World War II (1 September 1939 – 2 September 1945) was a global conflict involving most of the world’s nations, organized into the Axis (led by Germany, Italy, and Japan) and the Allies (chiefly the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, the United States, and the Republic of China). It was the deadliest conflict in human history, resulting in 70–85 million fatalities—including military personnel and civilians—and reshaping international politics, economics, and society.

**Origins and Causes**
The war’s origins lie in unresolved tensions from World War I and the interwar period. The Treaty of Versailles (1919) imposed punitive reparations on Germany and redrew European borders, fueling German revanchism. The Great Depression (1929) undermined democracies and spurred extremist movements. Militarist regimes in Germany (under Adolf Hitler’s Nazi Party), Italy (under Benito Mussolini), and Japan pursued territorial expansion: Germany annexed Austria (Anschluss, 1938) and seized the Sudetenland (Munich Agreement, 1938); Italy invaded Ethiopia (1935–36); Japan occupied Manchuria (1931) and launched a full-scale invasion of China (1937). Failed policy of appeasement by Britain and France emboldened aggressors.

**Major Alliances**

* **Axis Powers:** Germany, Italy, Japan; later joined by Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, and others under Tripartite Pact (September 1940).
* **Allied Powers:** Initially France, the United Kingdom, and Poland. The Soviet Union and the United States joined in June 1941 and December 1941 respectively. China (from 1937) and Free French forces also formed part of the Allies.

**European Theater**

* **Invasion of Poland (September 1939):** Germany’s Blitzkrieg conquered Poland, prompting Britain and France to declare war. The Soviet Union invaded eastern Poland under the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact.
* **Fall of France (May–June 1940):** German forces bypassed the Maginot Line via the Ardennes and occupied northern France; the Vichy regime governed the unoccupied south.
* **Battle of Britain (July–October 1940):** The Royal Air Force repelled the German Luftwaffe, preventing invasion.
* **Operation Barbarossa (June 1941):** Germany invaded the Soviet Union, opening the Eastern Front. Intense battles at Leningrad, Moscow, and Stalingrad (1942–43) depleted German forces.
* **Italian Campaign (1943–45):** Allied landings in Sicily (July 1943) and mainland Italy led to Mussolini’s fall and Italy’s armistice, though German forces continued fighting.
* **Normandy Invasion (D-Day, June 1944):** Allied landings in Normandy initiated the liberation of Western Europe; Paris was liberated in August 1944.
* **Final Push (1944–45):** Soviet offensives from the east and Allied advances from the west converged on Germany; Berlin fell in May 1945, leading to Germany’s unconditional surrender on 8 May (V-E Day).

**Pacific Theater**

* **Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–45):** Japan’s invasion of China became part of WWII.
* **Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941):** Japanese attack on U.S. Pacific Fleet drew the United States into the war.
* **Island-Hopping Campaign:** U.S. and Allied forces seized strategic Pacific islands (Guadalcanal, Iwo Jima, Okinawa) en route to Japan.
* **Atomic Bombings and Japanese Surrender:** The United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima (6 August 1945) and Nagasaki (9 August 1945). The Soviet Union declared war on Japan on 8 August and invaded Manchuria. Japan surrendered on 2 September 1945 (V-J Day).

**Holocaust and War Crimes**
The Holocaust, orchestrated by Nazi Germany, resulted in the systematic murder of six million Jews and millions of Roma, disabled individuals, and political opponents. Japan’s forces committed atrocities such as the Nanjing Massacre. The Moscow, London, and Yalta conferences laid groundwork for postwar justice; the Nuremberg and Tokyo tribunals prosecuted major war criminals and codified crimes against humanity.

**Political and Economic Outcomes**

* **Decolonization:** European colonial empires weakened; movements for independence gained momentum across Asia, Africa, and the Middle East.
* **Cold War Beginnings:** Ideological and geopolitical rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union emerged, dividing Europe with the Iron Curtain and leading to the formation of NATO (1949) and the Warsaw Pact (1955).
* **United Nations:** Founded in October 1945 to replace the League of Nations, aiming to prevent future global conflicts and promote human rights.
* **Economic Reconstruction:** The Marshall Plan (1948–52) aided Western Europe’s recovery. The Bretton Woods system (1944) established the IMF and World Bank, underpinning postwar economic order.

**Legacy**
World War II reshaped the global balance of power, accelerated technological and social change, and prompted universal human-rights norms. Its memory continues to inform international relations, peacebuilding efforts, and collective commemorations worldwide.
