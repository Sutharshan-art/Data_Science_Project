**Alfred Roberts**

**Alfred Roberts** (19 May 1892 – 18 December 1970) was a British grocer and local politician, best known as the father of **Margaret Thatcher**, the first female Prime Minister of the United Kingdom. Though Roberts himself never held national office, his life and career embodied the values and social milieu that influenced his daughter’s political ideology. His roles in local governance and his active involvement in community affairs reflected the political climate of early 20th-century Britain and the rising importance of grassroots political engagement.

---

### Early Life and Career

Alfred Roberts was born in 1892 in **Leigh, Lancashire**, into a working-class family. After training as a grocer, he established a small grocery business in the town of **Grantham**, Lincolnshire, where he became a respected local figure. His business acumen and community involvement helped build a stable middle-class lifestyle for his family, despite the economic challenges of interwar Britain.

Roberts’s career as a grocer coincided with a period of significant social and economic change in Britain, including the aftermath of **World War I**, the **Great Depression**, and the gradual expansion of political participation beyond the traditional elite. His experiences as a small businessman navigating these challenges informed his conservative outlook on economics and governance.

---

### Local Political Involvement

Roberts was actively engaged in local politics as a member of the **Conservative Party**. He served as a **councillor on Grantham Borough Council** for many years and was appointed **Mayor of Grantham** for the term 1945–1946. His tenure as mayor came just after World War II, during a period of national reconstruction and political realignment.

In local government, Roberts advocated for fiscal responsibility, public service improvement, and community development. His political stance was characterized by a commitment to individual enterprise, self-help, and traditional social values, aligning with the conservative ideology of the time.

---

### Influence on Margaret Thatcher

Alfred Roberts’s most enduring political significance is linked to his role as a father and early influence on **Margaret Thatcher** (née Roberts). He encouraged her academic pursuits, debating skills, and interest in politics, fostering an environment that emphasized discipline, moral rectitude, and public service.

Thatcher frequently cited her father’s example in her speeches and writings, crediting him with shaping her beliefs in free enterprise, personal responsibility, and the importance of community. The values he instilled in her formed a foundation for her later political career and her conservative policies during her premiership from 1979 to 1990.

---

### Historical and Political Context

Alfred Roberts’s life spanned a transformative period in British political history. The early 20th century saw the decline of the British Empire, the rise of the Labour movement, and the increasing democratization of political institutions. As a local Conservative politician, Roberts operated within a framework that sought to preserve traditional social hierarchies while adapting to changing economic realities.

His participation in local governance exemplifies the role of grassroots politics in shaping national discourse. The postwar years, in particular, witnessed expanded state intervention and welfare provision, which Roberts and many conservatives viewed with caution. This environment influenced the ideological development of a generation of politicians, including his daughter, who reacted against what they perceived as excessive government control.

---

### Legacy

Alfred Roberts died in 1970, several years before Margaret Thatcher became Prime Minister. His legacy is largely intertwined with her political narrative, representing the values of small-town Britain and the aspirations of the British middle class in the 20th century.

While Roberts himself did not achieve national prominence, his life and work provide insight into the social and political fabric that influenced one of Britain’s most consequential political figures. His commitment to public service and conservative principles continues to be recognized as a formative influence on Thatcher’s approach to politics and governance.

---

### Conclusion

Alfred Roberts stands as a representative figure of local political engagement in early 20th-century Britain. Through his business career and municipal service, he embodied the values of responsibility, community involvement, and conservative principles that shaped British political culture in the interwar and postwar periods. His influence on Margaret Thatcher further cements his place in British political history as a foundational figure behind a transformative era of governance.
