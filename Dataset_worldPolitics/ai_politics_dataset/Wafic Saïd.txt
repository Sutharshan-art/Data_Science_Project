**Wafic Saïd**

**Introduction**
Wafic Rida Saïd (born 1948) is a Syrian-Saudi businessman and philanthropist best known for his role as an intermediary in major Middle Eastern arms and oil transactions, and for his substantial contributions to higher education in the United Kingdom. Over several decades, Saïd has cultivated close ties with members of the Saudi royal family, senior British politicians, and international business leaders, positioning him as a significant figure in the geopolitics of energy and defense procurement.

**Early Life and Education**
Saïd was born in Latakia, Syria, into a family engaged in regional trade and agriculture. He attended local schools before studying physics at Damascus University. In the early 1970s, following the discovery of vast oil reserves in the Gulf, he relocated to Saudi Arabia. There, he joined the emerging cadre of expatriate traders and advisers who facilitated the kingdom’s rapid expansion of oil production and export infrastructure.

**Business Career and Oil Trading**
In Saudi Arabia, Saïd established Wafic Saïd Ltd., a trading and consultancy firm specializing in oil logistics, financing, and refinery construction. Through his company, he negotiated contracts between Saudi state-owned enterprises and Western oil majors, advising on the development of onshore and offshore facilities. His technical background, fluency in Arabic and English, and personal relationships within the Saudi monarchy enabled him to secure commissions on transactions totaling billions of dollars.

**Role in the Al-Yamamah Arms Deal**
Saïd’s international prominence rests largely on his intermediary role in the Al-Yamamah arms deal, first negotiated in 1985 between the United Kingdom and Saudi Arabia. Acting as a conduit for back-channel communications, Saïd helped arrange successive agreements under which the UK supplied Tornado jets, helicopters, missiles, and support services to the Saudi armed forces. The deal, one of the largest export contracts in British history, spanned more than two decades and included extensive offsets in infrastructure, training, and technology transfer. Saïd’s involvement drew attention for his close rapport with then-Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher and senior Saudis, and for substantial commissions he received—reportedly amounting to hundreds of millions of pounds.

**Political Connections and Controversies**
Saïd’s proximity to power made him a focal point for both praise and scrutiny. In the UK, questions were raised over transparency and the propriety of arms-broker fees. In 2006, the British Serious Fraud Office launched an inquiry into alleged corruption in the Al-Yamamah transactions but suspended it at the government’s direction, citing national security and diplomatic concerns. Critics accused authorities of placing commercial and political interests above legal accountability. In Saudi Arabia, Saïd was viewed as a trusted adviser on defense procurement and energy policy, though details of his official standing remained opaque.

**Philanthropy and Saïd Business School**
In 1996, Saïd donated £20 million to the University of Oxford to establish the Wafic Saïd Business School, reflecting his commitment to fostering global leadership and entrepreneurship. The school opened in 2001 and has since grown into a leading centre for management education, offering MBA, executive education, and research programs. Saïd’s gift enabled construction of a purpose-built campus on Oxford’s west side and endowed scholarships for students from developing countries.

**Investments and Cultural Patronage**
Beyond oil and defense, Saïd diversified into real estate, finance, and the arts. He acquired interests in European hotels, Italian vineyards, and Parisian galleries. In London, he funded restoration of historic buildings and supported contemporary art exhibitions. His cultural patronage extended to donations to museums and theatre companies, often emphasizing Arab art and cross-cultural dialogue.

**Later Career and Legacy**
Entering the 21st century, Saïd scaled back intermediary work but continued as a consultant on Middle Eastern energy strategy. His memoirs and occasional lectures have shed light on the intersection of commerce and diplomacy in the Gulf. While his role in the Al-Yamamah deal remains contentious, his philanthropic impact endures through the Saïd Business School and other charitable endeavors. He is widely regarded as a pioneer in leveraging private networks to bridge East-West transactions, even as debates over accountability and transparency in such deals persist.

**Personal Life**
Saïd has maintained residences in London, Geneva, and Riyadh. He is married with children, some of whom are active in managing the family’s diversified business interests and philanthropic foundations. He holds dual Syrian and Saudi citizenship and has been honored by academic institutions for his contributions to education and international understanding.
