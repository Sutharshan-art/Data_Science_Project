**List of Serbian Soldiers**

The **list of Serbian soldiers** includes notable individuals who served in the military forces of Serbia or Serbian-led states throughout history. These soldiers played significant roles in the defense, expansion, and political transformation of Serbian territories across various historical periods, including the medieval Serbian states, the Ottoman and Habsburg eras, the Balkan Wars, both World Wars, the Yugoslav conflicts, and modern-day Serbia.

This list encompasses soldiers from royal armies, national liberation movements, guerrilla and partisan groups, and formal state militaries. Their contributions reflect Serbia’s complex military history, shaped by shifting alliances, foreign domination, nationalist movements, and global conflicts.

---

### Historical Context

Serbian military history stretches back to the medieval period, when the **Kingdom and later Empire of Serbia** fielded organized armies in defense of sovereignty and Orthodox Christianity. The Ottoman conquest in the 15th century suppressed Serbian statehood, but Serbian soldiers continued to serve in both Ottoman and Habsburg forces. During the **19th century**, the modern Serbian army emerged as the Principality and later Kingdom of Serbia gained autonomy and then independence.

Serbia's strategic position in the Balkans made it a key player in the **Balkan Wars (1912–1913)** and **World War I**, where it gained international recognition for its military endurance. In **World War II**, Serbs fought in various factions, including royalist Chetniks and communist Partisans. During the **Yugoslav Wars of the 1990s**, Serbian soldiers participated in complex and controversial regional conflicts. Today, the **Serbian Armed Forces** continue to serve under a professional, peacetime structure and have contributed to international peacekeeping missions.

---

### Notable Serbian Soldiers by Era

#### **Medieval and Early Modern Period**

* **Stefan Lazarević (1377–1427)**
  A Serbian despot and military commander, Stefan distinguished himself at the Battle of Ankara (1402) as an Ottoman vassal and later led efforts to consolidate the Serbian Despotate. He was known for combining martial prowess with cultural patronage.

* **Đurađ Branković (1377–1456)**
  Successor to Stefan Lazarević, Đurađ faced repeated Ottoman invasions and was a key figure in the last years of medieval Serbian independence. His military leadership was pivotal during sieges and diplomacy.

* **Petar Tekelija (1720–1792)**
  A Serbian general in the Habsburg service, Tekelija rose to high rank in the Russian Imperial Army and contributed to Serbia's military traditions in exile.

---

### 19th Century – Independence and State Formation

* **Miloš Obrenović (1780–1860)**
  A leader of the Second Serbian Uprising (1815), Miloš established the foundation of the modern Serbian state. While primarily a political leader, his military role in resisting Ottoman rule was critical.

* **Stepa Stepanović (1856–1929)**
  One of Serbia’s most respected generals, he played a central role in the Balkan Wars and World War I, including the victory at the Battle of Cer, the first Allied victory in WWI.

* **Živojin Mišić (1855–1921)**
  A field marshal, Mišić led Serbian forces during critical WWI battles such as Kolubara. He is remembered for strategic brilliance and resilience under pressure.

---

### World War II

* **Draža Mihailović (1893–1946)**
  Leader of the royalist Chetnik resistance movement, Mihailović was initially supported by the Allies but later marginalized in favor of Tito’s Partisans. After the war, he was captured and executed by the communist government.

* **Peko Dapčević (1913–1999)**
  A Montenegrin Serb and senior commander in the Yugoslav Partisans, Dapčević was the first to enter Belgrade during its 1944 liberation. He later became Chief of the General Staff of the Yugoslav People's Army.

* **Koča Popović (1908–1992)**
  A commander and intellectual, Popović was instrumental in Partisan military operations and post-war Yugoslav diplomacy. He fought in the Spanish Civil War before joining the resistance in Yugoslavia.

---

### Yugoslav Wars (1990s)

* **Ratko Mladić (born 1942)**
  A general in the Bosnian Serb army during the Bosnian War, Mladić was convicted by the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY) for war crimes, including the Srebrenica massacre.

* **Željko Ražnatović “Arkan” (1952–2000)**
  A paramilitary leader of the Serbian Volunteer Guard, Arkan was indicted for war crimes but assassinated before standing trial. His role in the wars in Croatia and Bosnia remains controversial.

* **Momčilo Perišić (born 1944)**
  Former Chief of the General Staff of the Yugoslav Army. Perišić was initially convicted for aiding and abetting war crimes, though the conviction was later overturned on appeal.

---

### Modern Era and Peacekeeping

* **General Ljubiša Diković (born 1960)**
  Served as Chief of the General Staff of the Serbian Armed Forces from 2011 to 2018. Under his leadership, the Serbian military participated in international peacekeeping missions and modernization efforts.

* **Zoran Đorđević (born 1970)**
  Former Minister of Defence and contributor to the post-conflict professionalization and NATO cooperation of Serbia’s armed forces.

---

### Legacy and Recognition

Serbian soldiers have been commemorated for their sacrifices in defense of national sovereignty, often becoming national symbols. However, especially in the context of the Yugoslav Wars, the legacy of some figures is highly contested, reflecting differing historical interpretations and unresolved regional grievances. In recent decades, Serbia has undertaken military reforms, demobilized irregular units, and contributed to peacekeeping missions under the United Nations and European Union frameworks.

Understanding the role of Serbian soldiers across centuries offers insights into the evolution of military identity, national struggles, and Serbia’s role in broader regional and global conflicts.
