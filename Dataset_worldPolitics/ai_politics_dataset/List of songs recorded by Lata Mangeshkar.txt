**List of Songs Recorded by Lata Mangeshkar**

The **list of songs recorded by Lata Mangeshkar** includes thousands of Hindi, regional, and foreign-language tracks spanning over seven decades. Lata Mangeshkar (1929–2022), often referred to as the “Nightingale of India,” was one of the most prolific and iconic playback singers in the history of Indian cinema. Her voice became synonymous with the golden era of Bollywood, and her songs have had a lasting cultural and political impact across South Asia.

With a repertoire estimated at over 25,000 songs in more than 30 languages, Mangeshkar’s recordings not only shaped the musical aesthetics of Indian film but also reflected the evolving political, social, and national identity of India in the 20th and early 21st centuries.

---

### Historical and Political Context

Lata Mangeshkar’s career began in the 1940s, shortly before India gained independence from British rule. As the newly independent nation grappled with questions of identity, unity, and cultural expression, film and music emerged as powerful tools of nation-building. Mangeshkar’s voice, often used to represent the idealized Indian woman, became central to this process.

Her rise coincided with the consolidation of Bollywood as a major cultural industry. Through songs that depicted themes of patriotism, romance, loss, and resilience, Mangeshkar’s music played a role in articulating the aspirations and emotional narratives of a post-colonial society. In times of national crisis or celebration, her recordings were frequently invoked to express collective sentiment.

Mangeshkar also participated directly in moments of political significance. Her rendition of “Aye Mere Watan Ke Logon,” commemorating Indian soldiers killed in the 1962 Sino-Indian War, moved then-Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru to tears and became a defining patriotic anthem.

---

### Career Overview and Collaborations

Lata Mangeshkar began recording songs in the early 1940s and rose to prominence with hits like “Aayega Aanewala” from *Mahal* (1949). She soon became the most sought-after playback singer in Indian cinema, working with leading composers such as:

* **Naushad** (e.g., *Baiju Bawra*, *Mughal-e-Azam*)
* **S.D. Burman** (e.g., *Guide*, *Bandini*)
* **Madan Mohan**, **Shankar–Jaikishan**, and later **R.D. Burman**, **Laxmikant–Pyarelal**, and **Anu Malik**

Her voice was lent to leading actresses across generations—from Nargis and Meena Kumari to Sridevi and Madhuri Dixit—creating a continuity of sound that transcended cinematic eras.

She also recorded numerous devotional, classical, and non-film songs, further broadening her reach. Her work in regional languages, including Marathi, Bengali, Gujarati, and Malayalam, contributed to her pan-Indian appeal and helped bridge linguistic divides in a diverse country.

---

### Notable Patriotic and Political Songs

#### **“Aye Mere Watan Ke Logon” (1963)**

Composed by C. Ramchandra and written by Kavi Pradeep, this non-film patriotic song became a landmark in Indian cultural history. Its performance at the Republic Day function in 1963, in the aftermath of the India-China war, cemented Mangeshkar’s role as a national icon.

#### **“Vande Mataram”**

Mangeshkar recorded several renditions of India’s national song “Vande Mataram,” composed by Bankim Chandra Chatterjee. Her versions are often played during national ceremonies and continue to evoke a strong patriotic response.

#### **Songs in National Integration Films**

She contributed to films sponsored by the Indian government or public service initiatives, promoting themes like unity in diversity, communal harmony, and women’s empowerment. Songs from such films often played a role in shaping public opinion and civic identity.

### Legacy and Honors

Lata Mangeshkar’s recordings span political regimes, linguistic divides, and generational shifts. Her contribution to India’s cultural and political life was recognized with numerous honors, including:

* **Bharat Ratna** (India’s highest civilian award, 2001)
* **Padma Vibhushan**, **Padma Bhushan**
* **Dadasaheb Phalke Award** for lifetime achievement in cinema

Her death in 2022 was marked by a national day of mourning, and her music continues to serve as a touchstone for Indian identity and sentiment.

---

### Conclusion

The list of songs recorded by Lata Mangeshkar reflects not only a musical legacy of extraordinary breadth and depth but also a chronicle of modern Indian history. Her voice captured the joys, sorrows, and hopes of a nation in transition and became an enduring symbol of Indian cultural unity and resilience. Her vast catalog continues to inspire generations, both within India and in the global South Asian diaspora.
