**Ron Reagan**

**Early Life and Family Background**
Ronald Prescott “Ron” Reagan was born on May 20, 1958, in Los Angeles, California, the youngest child of President Ronald Reagan and First Lady Nancy Reagan. His parents divorced Reagan’s first marriage in 1948; Ron’s half‐brother, Michael Reagan, was born in 1945. Raised in the public eye—his father a prominent Hollywood actor, later Governor of California, and ultimately the 40th President of the United States—Ron experienced both the privileges and the pressures of life in America’s political spotlight.

**Education and Early Career**
After graduating from Yale University in 1979 with a Bachelor of Arts in public policy, Ron began his professional life at the classical‐music recording label RCA. He later worked as a film critic and appeared as a guest host on public television panels. Unlike his father’s conservative alignment, Ron did not pursue public office or Republican Party activism; instead, he gravitated toward cultural commentary and advocacy on social issues, nurturing a reputation as an independent‐minded public figure.

**Political Views and Activism**
Ron Reagan emerged publicly as a vocal liberal and secularist. In 1989, he delivered the keynote address at the inaugural meeting of Americans United for Separation of Church and State, declaring: “I am—and always have been—a staunch atheist.” He has since championed church‐state separation, reproductive rights, LGBT equality, and civil liberties. His political evolution—from the son of a conservative icon to a prominent progressive voice—has drawn media attention and occasional controversy, illustrating the ideological diversity within prominent political families.

**Media Career**
In 2003, Ron joined MSNBC as a commentator and occasional host, providing political analysis during presidential campaigns and national events. He hosted several MSNBC specials, including **“The Big Ideas”** series (2006–2008), which interviewed leading thinkers on topics ranging from atheism to economic policy. His on‐air presence combined personal anecdotes—often reflecting on his father’s legacy—with advocacy for liberal policy positions, positioning him as a distinctive voice in cable news.

**Role in Presidential Politics**
Although never a candidate himself, Reagan has actively participated in Democratic presidential campaigns. He endorsed Senator John Kerry in 2004, Senator Hillary Rodham Clinton in 2008 and 2016, and Senator Bernie Sanders in 2016’s Democratic primary after Clinton’s withdrawal. His public endorsements leveraged both his Reagan surname and his contrasting political convictions to appeal to moderate Republicans and independents disillusioned with the GOP’s direction.

**Advocacy and Public Speaking**
Beyond television, Reagan has spoken at universities, civic forums, and national conventions on issues of secularism, government transparency, and nuclear disarmament. In 2005, he testified before Congress against constitutional amendments to ban same‐sex marriage, arguing that “the government has no business defining marriage.” He has also served on the board of People for the American Way, a progressive advocacy organization, and partnered with the Secular Coalition for America to advance policies protecting non‐believers.

**Relationship with Ronald Reagan’s Legacy**
Ron’s critiques of contemporary Republican policies have sometimes extended to his father’s record. While acknowledging Ronald Reagan’s achievements—such as economic recovery and the end of the Cold War—Ron has expressed disagreement with elements of his father’s social conservatism and nuclear‐arms buildup. In interviews and his 2016 memoir *My Father at 100*, Ron portrays a nuanced understanding of his father as both a political leader and a private individual, contributing to historiographical debates on Reagan’s impact.

**Public Perception and Criticism**
Reagan’s political stances have generated mixed reactions. Supporters praise his courage in diverging from family tradition and his articulate advocacy for secularism and progressive causes. Critics—particularly within conservative circles—accuse him of exploiting his family name to lend unwarranted credibility to his views. Despite such criticism, Ron Reagan remains a sought‐after speaker and commentator, valued for his candor and unique vantage point.

**Personal Life**
Ron Reagan has been married twice, first to Doria Palmieri (1990–2003) and later to Doria’s former husband, Matt Palmieri (2006–2012), before their divorce. He has one daughter, Elizabeth, born in 1994. A father himself, Reagan frequently cites his family experiences in discussions of health care policy and educational reform.

**Legacy and Influence**
Ron Reagan’s public life exemplifies the intersection of personal conviction and political heritage. By charting a path distinct from his father’s conservative legacy, he has contributed to broader conversations about ideological diversity and the evolving role of religion in American public life. His career in media and activism continues to influence debates on secular governance, civil rights, and the dynamics of partisan identity in the United States.
