**Timeline of the George W. Bush Presidency (2001–2009)**

George W. Bush, the 43rd President of the United States, served two terms from January 20, 2001, to January 20, 2009. His presidency was defined by the terrorist attacks of September 11, 2001, and the ensuing global “War on Terror,” major domestic policy initiatives, two overseas wars, and significant economic challenges.

---

## 2001

* **January 20**: Inaugurated; calls for “a thousand points of light” of community service.
* **February 27**: Nomination of Colin Powell as Secretary of State and Condoleezza Rice as National Security Advisor signals experienced foreign-policy team.
* **March 20**: Signs the Economic Growth and Tax Relief Reconciliation Act, enacting large individual and business tax cuts.
* **September 11**: Al-Qaeda terrorists hijack four airliners; crash into the World Trade Center, the Pentagon; one plane crashes in Pennsylvania. Nearly 3,000 killed.
* **September 14**: Addresses joint session of Congress; declares “War on Terror” and demands Taliban hand over Osama bin Laden.
* **October 7**: Launches Operation Enduring Freedom—U.S. and British forces invade Afghanistan to topple the Taliban and dismantle al-Qaeda.
* **November 19**: Signs the Authorization for Use of Military Force (AUMF), granting broad authority to combat terrorism.

## 2002

* **January 29**: Delivers State of the Union; labels Iraq, Iran, and North Korea the “Axis of Evil.”
* **October 16**: Signs the Homeland Security Act, creating the Department of Homeland Security, the largest federal reorganization since 1947.
* **October 26**: Signs the Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act (McCain–Feingold), overhauling federal campaign finance laws.
* **November 25**: Addresses the UN General Assembly; presents Iraq’s alleged WMD threat, seeking international support for disarmament.

## 2003

* **March 19**: Launches Operation Iraqi Freedom; U.S.-led coalition invades Iraq to overthrow Saddam Hussein.
* **April 9**: “Mission Accomplished” speech aboard USS *Abraham Lincoln* following rapid military advance to Baghdad, though insurgency intensifies.
* **May 1**: Declares end of major combat operations in Iraq, though violence escalates.
* **June 6**: Signs the Jobs and Growth Tax Relief Reconciliation Act, accelerating tax cuts to stimulate the economy.
* **September 24**: Signs the Medicare Prescription Drug, Improvement, and Modernization Act, creating Medicare Part D for prescription drugs.

## 2004

* **January–February**: Administration pursues constitutional amendments banning same-sex marriage; proposed amendment fails to gain required support.
* **November 2**: Re-elected, defeating Senator John Kerry in a closely contested election emphasizing national security and social conservatism.

## 2005

* **January 20**: Inaugurated for second term; emphasizes compassion and freedom in inaugural address.
* **February 2**: Unveils national energy policy, promoting domestic production and renewable sources.
* **March 20**: Signing of the USA PATRIOT Improvement and Reauthorization Act, extending surveillance powers under the PATRIOT Act.
* **August 29**: Hurricane Katrina makes landfall near New Orleans; federal response criticized as slow, prompting debate over FEMA’s structure.
* **October 26**: Signs the Deficit Reduction Act, including reforms to Medicaid and student loans.

## 2006

* **January 30**: Midterm elections result in Democratic control of both the House and Senate, interpreted as public rebuke of Iraq War policies.
* **November 8**: Attorney General Alberto Gonzales testifies on U.S. interrogation techniques and firing of U.S. attorneys, intensifying the “U.S. attorneys scandal.”

## 2007

* **January 10**: Delivers State of the Union; announces “surge” of 21,500 additional troops to Iraq.
* **March 2007**: Faces mounting opposition to the Iraq surge and rising death toll; Congress votes to require reporting on troop progress.
* **September 27**: Addresses the United Nations; declares “soft power” diplomacies essential but reaffirms U.S. readiness to act militarily.

## 2008

* **February 19**: Delivers State of the Union; labels nation at “crossroads,” proposes spending freeze and deficit reduction.
* **September 7**: Stockton, California, declares bankruptcy amid foreclosure crisis; signals deepening economic malaise.
* **September 15**: Investment bank Lehman Brothers collapses; Treasury Secretary Paulson announces Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) to stabilize financial system.
* **October 3**: Bush signs the Emergency Economic Stabilization Act, authorizing \$700 billion for bank bailouts, marking the largest federal intervention since the Great Depression.
* **November 4**: Barack Obama is elected President, signaling a change in political leadership amid economic crisis.

## 2009

* **January 14**: Delivers farewell address in Washington, DC; reflects on administration’s achievements amid unprecedented challenges.
* **January 20**: Concludes presidency; Barack Obama is inaugurated as the 44th President.

---

**Historical and Political Context**
Bush’s presidency opened at a pivotal moment: the dawn of the 21st century, exponential globalization, and a sense of American unipolar dominance after the Cold War. The terrorist attacks of September 11, 2001, reshaped U.S. foreign and domestic policy, leading to interventions in Afghanistan and Iraq under the doctrines of pre-emption and counterterrorism. Domestically, Bush enacted historic tax cuts, expanded Medicare, and reorganized Homeland Security. His tenure also grappled with debates over civil liberties, environmental regulation, and the balance between security and freedom. The economic collapse of 2008 culminated his presidency, framing the transition to the Obama era with urgent calls for financial reform and economic stabilization. Bush left office with mixed public approval and a legacy defined by both the assertion of American power and the complexities of a changing global and domestic landscape.
