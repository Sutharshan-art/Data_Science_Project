**Ukrainka (air base)**

**Overview**
Ukrainka is a long-range aviation air base of the Russian Aerospace Forces, located in Amur Oblast in the Russian Far East, approximately 50 km northwest of the town of Svobodny. Established during the early 1960s, it serves as a key element of Russia’s strategic bomber force, hosting heavy bombers on nuclear-capable missions and enabling power projection across the Asia-Pacific region. Over six decades of operation, Ukrainka has played a prominent role in Cold War deterrence, post-Soviet force restructuring, and contemporary strategic signalling.

**Location and Infrastructure**
Situated on the eastern bank of the Zeya River floodplain, Ukrainka occupies a remote site chosen for its distance from potential adversaries and its capacity for dispersal. The base features a 3,500 m concrete runway capable of accommodating the heaviest Soviet and Russian aircraft, including Tu-95MS “Bear” turboprops and Tu-22M3 “Backfire” supersonic bombers. Hardened hangars, extensive fuel storage, and munitions bunkers support sustained high-readiness operations. A network of taxiways and dispersal pads allows rapid deployment of multiple aircraft under wartime conditions. The facility is complemented by a dedicated personnel garrison town, complete with housing, schools, and medical services.

**Historical Development**
Construction of Ukrainka began in 1963 amid escalating superpower tensions, as the Soviet Union sought to augment its strategic bomber reach beyond European participants of the Cold War. Initial deployments included Tu-95 “Bear” bombers operating under Long-Range Aviation, capable of Arctic and trans-Pacific patrols. By the 1970s, the base expanded to accommodate the newer Tu-22M “Backfire,” whose supersonic dash profile and maritime strike capability enhanced Soviet naval deterrence in the Pacific Ocean.

**Cold War Operations**
Throughout the 1960s–1980s, Ukrainka hosted routine patrols and training flights aimed at demonstrating Soviet bomber presence near North American and East Asian airspace. The base maintained a continuous alert posture, with aircraft armed for dual nuclear and conventional missions. During seasonal exercises, formations from Ukrainka flew escorted overflights into the Bering Sea and along Alaska’s Aleutian Islands, testing U.S. air defenses. In response to heightened Sino-Soviet tensions of the late 1960s and early 1970s, the base also served as an operational hub for joint bomber-fighter patrols along the Sino-Soviet border, underscoring the USSR’s capacity to project force across its vast eastern frontier.

**Post-Soviet Restructuring**
Following the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991, Ukrainka experienced personnel reductions and budget constraints that led to intermittent maintenance challenges. Nevertheless, the 79th Guards Heavy Bomber Aviation Regiment remained active, transitioning to new command structures under the Russian Air Force. Modernization efforts in the 2000s included upgraded navigation and refueling systems to extend operational range. In 2009, as part of a broader military reform, the 200th Guards Heavy Bomber Aviation Regiment was reactivated at Ukrainka, introducing the modernized Tu-95MSM variant.

**Political and Strategic Significance**
Ukrainka occupies a central role in Russia’s strategic balance in East Asia. Its bomber sorties support nuclear deterrence, signaling resolve to both regional powers and treaty partners. Frequent long-distance flights demonstrate Russia’s ability to reinforce Pacific theater operations without reliance on forward bases. The base has been used for overflight training in conjunction with Russian naval drills in the Sea of Japan and down to Australian waters, projecting a message of strategic reach to the United States, China, Japan, and Australia. In multilateral contexts, Ukrainka-operated missions form part of Russia’s contribution to joint exercises with China and India, reflecting evolving defense partnerships.

**Units and Aircraft**
Key units based at Ukrainka include:

* **79th Guards Heavy Bomber Aviation Regiment** (Tu-95MS “Bear-H”)
* **200th Guards Heavy Bomber Aviation Regiment** (Tu-95MSM “Bear-HM”)
  Historically, the base also hosted the 182nd Heavy Bomber Aviation Regiment (Tu-22M3 “Backfire”) until its relocation in the 2010s. Rotational detachments of Tu-160 “Blackjack” supersonic bombers have operated from Ukrainka during Asia-Pacific patrols, underscoring the base’s ability to support the full spectrum of Russia’s strategic aviation fleet.

**Recent Developments**
Since 2018, Ukrainka has undergone runway refurbishments, installation of modern air-defense radars, and expansion of underground fuel and weapons storage. Joint flight planning with the Northern Fleet’s maritime aviation component has increased, integrating bomber sorties with anti-submarine warfare exercises. In response to rising tensions over Ukraine and NATO’s pivot to the Indo-Pacific, Ukrainka-based bombers have conducted more frequent long-range patrols, including missions approaching the Alaskan air defense identification zone and transit near contested maritime chokepoints. Enhanced coordination with China’s People’s Liberation Army Air Force, via reciprocal overflight exercises, signifies the base’s role in shaping a trilateral security dynamic among Russia, China, and the United States.

**Legacy**
Over sixty years, Ukrainka air base has remained a cornerstone of Russia’s ability to wield air-launched strategic deterrents in the Pacific theater. Its evolution—from Cold War outpost to modernized hub for long-range aviation—reflects broader trends in Russian military doctrine, emphasizing flexibility, power projection, and partnership with emerging regional powers. As geopolitical competition intensifies in East Asia, Ukrainka’s strategic value continues to grow, anchoring Russia’s role in shaping the security architecture of the 21st-century Pacific.
