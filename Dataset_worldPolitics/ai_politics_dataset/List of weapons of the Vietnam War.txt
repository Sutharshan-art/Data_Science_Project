**List of Weapons of the Vietnam War**

The **list of weapons of the Vietnam War** includes a broad range of firearms, vehicles, aircraft, artillery, and unconventional weapons used by both sides during the conflict, which lasted from 1955 to 1975. The war pitted the communist forces of North Vietnam and the Viet Cong against the anti-communist South Vietnamese government, supported by the United States and its allies. The variety and scale of the weaponry employed reflected the war's status as a Cold War proxy conflict, with both the Soviet Union and the United States supplying arms and equipment to their respective allies.

The Vietnam War was marked by asymmetrical warfare. The North Vietnamese Army (NVA) and the Viet Cong relied on guerrilla tactics, booby traps, and limited but strategically deployed conventional weapons. In contrast, U.S. and South Vietnamese forces employed advanced military technology, including helicopters, jet aircraft, and precision-guided weapons, though often with limited success in counterinsurgency operations.

---

### Historical and Political Context

The Vietnam War took place during a period of intense global rivalry between the capitalist West, led by the United States, and the communist bloc, led by the Soviet Union and China. U.S. involvement escalated after the Gulf of Tonkin incident in 1964, leading to full-scale deployment of troops and weaponry. North Vietnam received significant military aid from its communist allies, creating a battlefield where modern Western and Soviet-designed weapons were tested in one of the 20th century’s most high-profile conflicts.

Due to the nature of the terrain, with dense jungles, mountains, and rural hamlets, the Vietnam War also became a testing ground for new military tactics and technologies, including air mobility and chemical defoliants. The conflict witnessed both traditional infantry engagements and unconventional warfare that blurred the lines between combatants and civilians.

---

### Weapons Used by the United States and South Vietnam

#### **Small Arms**

* **M16 Rifle**: Standard U.S. military rifle; lightweight and capable of automatic fire. Early models had reliability issues.
* **M14 Rifle**: Used in the early stages of the war; eventually replaced by the M16.
* **M60 Machine Gun**: General-purpose machine gun used for squad-level fire support.
* **M1911 Pistol**: Standard-issue sidearm for U.S. forces.
* **M79 Grenade Launcher**: Single-shot launcher effective for indirect fire against concealed targets.
* **Thompson Submachine Gun**: Used by some ARVN and U.S. special forces, especially in the early years.

#### **Artillery and Explosives**

* **105mm and 155mm Howitzers**: Provided mobile and static fire support for U.S. and ARVN units.
* **Claymore Mines**: Directional anti-personnel mines used in defensive setups.
* **Napalm and White Phosphorus**: Incendiary weapons used controversially to flush out entrenched enemy forces.

#### **Aircraft**

* **Bell UH-1 “Huey” Helicopter**: Iconic symbol of the war; used for troop transport, medevac, and gunship roles.
* **F-4 Phantom II**: Multi-role fighter-bomber widely used in air-to-ground and air-to-air missions.
* **AC-47 “Spooky” Gunship**: Modified transport plane equipped with side-firing machine guns for close air support.
* **B-52 Stratofortress**: Strategic bomber used in carpet bombing campaigns such as Operation Rolling Thunder and Linebacker.

#### **Armored Vehicles**

* **M48 Patton Tank**: Main battle tank deployed in armored operations.
* **M113 Armored Personnel Carrier**: Widely used for troop transport and convoy protection.

#### **Naval Weapons**

* **Riverine Patrol Boats (PBRs)**: Small boats used in the Mekong Delta for interdiction and support.
* **Aircraft Carriers and Naval Gunfire Support Ships**: Provided offshore support for air strikes and artillery fire.

---

### Weapons Used by North Vietnam and the Viet Cong

#### **Small Arms**

* **AK-47**: Iconic Soviet-designed assault rifle; robust and effective in jungle conditions.
* **SKS Carbine**: Semi-automatic rifle used by NVA regulars and militia.
* **PPSh-41 Submachine Gun**: Soviet World War II-era weapon used by North Vietnamese forces.
* **Type 56 (Chinese AK-variant)**: Most common assault rifle among Viet Cong and NVA troops.

#### **Guerrilla Weapons and Tactics**

* **Booby Traps**: Punji stake pits, tripwire grenades, and bamboo spikes widely used to inflict casualties and demoralize U.S. troops.
* **Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs)**: Crafted from unexploded U.S. ordnance or locally made materials.

#### **Artillery and Rockets**

* **82mm Mortars and 122mm Rockets**: Portable indirect fire systems used for ambushes and attacks on bases.
* **Recoilless Rifles and RPG-2/RPG-7**: Anti-tank weapons effective against lightly armored vehicles and bunkers.

#### **Anti-Aircraft Weapons**

* **ZPU and DShK Heavy Machine Guns**: Deployed extensively to target low-flying helicopters and aircraft.
* **SA-2 Surface-to-Air Missiles**: Supplied by the Soviet Union to protect key targets in North Vietnam from air strikes.

---

### Chemical and Psychological Warfare

* **Agent Orange**: A defoliant used by the U.S. to destroy jungle cover and crops; later linked to severe health problems and environmental damage.
* **Propaganda Leaflets and Loudspeakers**: Used by both sides to demoralize enemy troops and encourage defection.

---

### Conclusion

The **weapons of the Vietnam War** reflected the broader ideological, technological, and tactical clashes of the Cold War era. U.S. reliance on high-technology firepower contrasted with the resourceful, guerrilla-based strategy of the North Vietnamese and Viet Cong. Despite the superior military hardware of the United States and its allies, the war ultimately demonstrated the limitations of conventional military might in a politically and culturally complex environment. The legacy of these weapons—particularly the humanitarian and ecological impact—continues to shape the post-war history of Vietnam and American military doctrine.
