**Presidency of George W. Bush**

The **presidency of George W. Bush** began on January 20, 2001, and ended on January 20, 2009. A member of the **Republican Party**, Bush served as the 43rd president of the United States. He assumed office after defeating Democratic Vice President **Al Gore** in the controversial 2000 presidential election and was re-elected in 2004 by defeating Democratic Senator **John Kerry**. His presidency was dominated by the **September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks**, the subsequent **War on Terror**, and major domestic challenges, including **Hurricane Katrina** and the **2008 financial crisis**.

Bush’s leadership style and policy decisions were widely debated, with supporters praising his decisiveness and moral clarity, while critics pointed to his handling of the Iraq War, civil liberties, and economic management.

---

### 2000 Election and Inauguration

Bush, the former governor of Texas and son of former President **George H. W. Bush**, secured the Republican nomination in 2000 and ran on a platform of “compassionate conservatism,” tax cuts, and education reform. The general election against Al Gore was one of the closest in U.S. history. The result hinged on the state of **Florida**, where a prolonged recount and legal battle culminated in the Supreme Court decision **Bush v. Gore**, effectively awarding Bush the presidency despite losing the popular vote.

He was inaugurated on January 20, 2001, with **Dick Cheney** serving as vice president. His early domestic agenda focused on tax relief and education reform.

---

### September 11 and the War on Terror

The defining moment of Bush’s presidency occurred on **September 11, 2001**, when al-Qaeda terrorists hijacked and crashed four commercial airplanes, killing nearly 3,000 people. The attacks prompted a global shift in U.S. foreign and domestic policy.

In response, Bush launched the **War on Terror**, beginning with **Operation Enduring Freedom** in **Afghanistan**, aimed at toppling the Taliban regime and dismantling al-Qaeda. The **USA PATRIOT Act** was enacted to expand domestic surveillance and law enforcement powers, raising long-term debates about civil liberties.

---

### Iraq War

In 2003, Bush led the United States into the **Iraq War**, citing the threat posed by **weapons of mass destruction (WMDs)** and alleged ties between Iraq and terrorism. **Saddam Hussein** was deposed, but the failure to find WMDs and the onset of a prolonged insurgency drew widespread international and domestic criticism.

The war significantly affected global perceptions of the U.S., strained alliances, and led to a protracted American military presence in Iraq. Bush’s **"Mission Accomplished"** speech in May 2003, declaring major combat operations over, was later viewed as premature.

---

### Domestic Policy

Bush's domestic policy emphasized tax reform, education, and healthcare. He signed a series of tax cuts between 2001 and 2003 aimed at stimulating the economy. While these were credited with promoting growth in the early 2000s, they also contributed to growing budget deficits.

In 2002, Bush signed the **No Child Left Behind Act**, a major education reform bill that introduced standardized testing and accountability measures for public schools. Critics argued it placed undue pressure on schools without adequate funding.

Bush also attempted, unsuccessfully, to reform **Social Security** by introducing private retirement accounts. His proposals met strong opposition in Congress and among the public.

---

### 2004 Re-Election

In 2004, Bush won re-election against John Kerry, campaigning on national security and the need to stay the course in Iraq and the broader War on Terror. The election was again closely contested, with Bush winning both the electoral and popular vote—a first for him, and the first Republican to do so since 1988.

---

### Second Term Challenges

Bush’s second term was marked by rising dissatisfaction with the Iraq War, revelations of prisoner abuse at **Abu Ghraib**, and criticism over the administration’s handling of **Hurricane Katrina** in 2005. The federal response to the disaster was widely seen as inadequate, especially in New Orleans, and eroded public confidence in Bush’s leadership.

His administration also faced controversy over **warrantless wiretapping**, the dismissal of U.S. attorneys, and debates over the use of torture and detainee rights at **Guantanamo Bay**.

---

### 2008 Financial Crisis

In the final year of Bush’s presidency, the U.S. was hit by the worst financial crisis since the Great Depression. Triggered by a collapse in the housing market and banking sector, the crisis led to widespread economic instability.

Bush responded with the **Emergency Economic Stabilization Act of 2008**, which created the **Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP)** to bail out financial institutions. Though unpopular at the time, the intervention was seen by many economists as necessary to prevent broader economic collapse.

---

### Legacy

George W. Bush left office with low approval ratings, primarily due to the Iraq War and the financial crisis. However, his presidency has undergone some reassessment in later years, particularly in light of his efforts to combat HIV/AIDS globally, promote education reform, and his calls for tolerance and bipartisanship.

His administration reshaped U.S. foreign policy, expanded presidential powers in national security, and redefined the Republican Party’s direction in the early 21st century. The long-term impact of his decisions, especially in Iraq and Afghanistan, continues to influence U.S. policy and political discourse.
