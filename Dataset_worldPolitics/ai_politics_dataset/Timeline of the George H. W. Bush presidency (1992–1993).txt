**Timeline of the George H. W. Bush Presidency (1992–1993)**

The final stretch of George H. W. Bush’s presidency—from January 1, 1992, through his departure on January 20, 1993—was defined by domestic economic difficulties, international peacekeeping challenges, and a hard-fought re-election campaign that culminated in defeat.

---

**January–March 1992**

* **January 17**: President Bush announces withdrawal of U.S. troops from Somalia, ending participation in Operation Restore Hope after U.S. casualties and rising domestic opposition.
* **January 31**: The Bush administration formally invites Congress to vote on the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA), negotiated jointly with Canada and Mexico, framing it as vital for U.S. competitiveness.
* **February 7**: Labor Department reports unemployment at 7.4 percent—Bush’s highest since the early 1980s—undercutting his “no new taxes” pledge amid an economic slowdown.
* **February 21**: In State of the Union address, Bush emphasizes deficit reduction, crime control, and education reform, but criticism of his economic stewardship grows.
* **March 3**: At a summit in Lisbon, Bush meets with European Community leaders to discuss enlargement and common market reforms; Europe’s mixed reception reflects post–Cold War uncertainty.

**April–June 1992**

* **April 29–May 4**: Los Angeles riots erupt following the Rodney King verdict on police brutality. Bush authorizes federal assistance, including FBI riot suppression teams, and meets civil rights leaders to urge calm.
* **May 8**: General Dynamics announces job cuts in Oklahoma City, symbolizing the broader defense-industry contraction after the Cold War.
* **June 2**: Third-party candidate Ross Perot formally enters the presidential race, pledging to “stop the gravy train” and highlighting the national deficit. Polls show Perot at 20–25 percent, signaling a challenge to both major parties.
* **June 17**: Bush reads “My Pet Goat” to schoolchildren in Florida during news of the first World Trade Center bombing, underscoring increasing terrorism concerns.

**July–September 1992**

* **July 16**: Nominations for the Republican convention in Houston conclude with Vice President Dan Quayle and Secretary of State James A. Baker III exerting party unity after intra-party debates over economic policy.
* **August 11–14**: Republican National Convention nominates Bush and Quayle. Bush’s acceptance speech touts his foreign-policy successes—particularly the Gulf War—and warns of a “New Isolationism.”
* **September 3–6**: Democratic National Convention in New York City nominates Governor Bill Clinton and Senator Al Gore. Rising economic anxiety and Clinton’s “it’s the economy, stupid” message frame the general-election discourse.
* **September 25**: Senate fails to ratify the Convention on the Rights of the Child, reflecting conservative skepticism of international human-rights treaties.

**October–December 1992**

* **October 1**: NAFTA Implementation Act passes the House (234–200) but later stalls in the Senate, as protectionist sentiment rises amid campaign politics.
* **October 3**: The Bush administration rescinds most trade restrictions on China, reversing post-Tiananmen sanctions and marking a strategic pivot toward economic engagement.
* **October 8**: Vice President Quayle’s debate gaffe—correcting a student’s spelling of potato—becomes a meme, undermining public confidence in the ticket.
* **November 3**: Presidential election: Bill Clinton defeats Bush (43 percent to 37 percent), with Ross Perot garnering 19 percent of the popular vote. Economic recession and the perceived disconnect between Bush and domestic concerns contribute to his defeat.
* **November 25**: In one of his final acts, Bush grants clemency to retired Air Force Lieutenant Colonel Oliver North for his role in the Iran–Contra affair, sparking debate over accountability.
* **December 17**: The National Performance Review—chaired by Vice President Quayle—releases recommendations to streamline the federal government, reflecting Bush’s commitment to management reform.

**January 1993**

* **January 15**: Bush delivers a farewell radio address, reflecting on a presidency that “made history” abroad but struggled with economic headwinds at home.
* **January 20**: George H. W. Bush concludes his term at noon; Bill Clinton is inaugurated as the 42nd President. Bush departs the White House with an approval rating near 30 percent, according to Gallup, the lowest of his presidency.

---

**Political and Historical Context**
Bush’s final year illustrated the limits of a post–Cold War foreign-policy triumphalism in insulating a president from domestic economic woes. While his leadership during the Gulf War, détente with the USSR, and international diplomacy underscored U.S. global primacy, a deepening recession, rising unemployment, and perceptions of inattention to middle-class concerns eroded public support. The emergence of Ross Perot as a significant third-party candidate emphasized popular discontent with both major parties and fiscal deficits. Ultimately, the 1992 election signaled a shift toward domestic economic priorities under the incoming Clinton administration, while Bush’s farewell underscored the transition from a bipolar world to a focus on globalization and internal reform.
