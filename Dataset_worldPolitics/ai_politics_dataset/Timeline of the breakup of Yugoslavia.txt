**Timeline of the Breakup of Yugoslavia**

The dissolution of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (SFRY) between 1991 and 1995 followed decades of ethnic tensions, nationalist revivals, and political crises. This timeline highlights key events that led to the disintegration of the six-republic federation and subsequent wars.

---

**1980s: Seeds of Disintegration**

* **May 1980:** Death of longtime leader Josip Broz Tito removes the unifying figure whose authority had held Yugoslavia’s diverse nationalities together.
* **Early–Mid 1980s:** Economic stagnation, rising external debt, and austerity measures provoke public discontent. Slobodan Milošević’s 1987 “anti-bureaucratic revolution” in Serbia consolidates Serbian control over the autonomous provinces of Vojvodina and Kosovo and emboldens Serbian nationalism.

**1989–1990: Rising Nationalism and Political Reform**

* **June 1989:** Milošević’s Gazimestan speech on the 600th anniversary of the Battle of Kosovo signals aggressive Serbian nationalism.
* **1989–1990:** All six republics and two autonomous provinces introduce multiparty elections, ending the League of Communists’ monopoly. Nationalist and reformist parties gain prominence in Serbia, Croatia, Slovenia, and elsewhere.

**1990: First Multiparty Elections**

* **April–May:** Croatia and Slovenia hold competitive elections; Croatia elects Franjo Tuđman’s Croatian Democratic Union (HDZ), and Slovenia elects Janez Drnovšek’s Liberal Democratic Party, both advocating greater republican autonomy.
* **November:** In Bosnia and Herzegovina, the Party of Democratic Action (SDA), the Social Democratic Party (SDP), and the Croatian Democratic Union of Bosnia and Herzegovina win plurality, reflecting multiethnic yet fractious politics.

**1991: Secession and the Outbreak of War**

* **June 25:** Slovenia and Croatia declare independence. The Ten-Day War erupts in Slovenia when the Yugoslav People’s Army (JNA) intervenes; withdrawn by early July.
* **July 7:** Brioni Agreement (European Community–mediated) imposes a three-month moratorium on secession, which Slovenia largely ignores; Croatia delays full independence until October.
* **August 1991:** The JNA re-engages in Croatia, triggering full-scale war over Serb-populated areas.
* **October 8:** Croatia proclaims full independence.
* **December 1991:** Former Yugoslav capitals Belgrade and Sarajevo submit bids to host the 1997 World Expo, symbolizing competing visions for post-Yugoslav Europe.

**1992: Bosnia’s Breakaway and International Recognition**

* **March 1:** Bosnia and Herzegovina holds a referendum on independence; majority of Bosniaks and Croats vote “Yes,” Bosnian Serbs boycott.
* **April 6:** The European Community (EC) and United States recognize Bosnian independence; Bosnian Serb forces, backed by the JNA, begin siege of Sarajevo and ethnic cleansing in eastern Bosnia.
* **April 7:** United Nations Security Council imposes an arms embargo on all former Yugoslav republics, advantaging better-armed Serb forces.

**1993: Internationalization and Humanitarian Crisis**

* **January:** UN Protection Force (UNPROFOR) deploys to Croatia to safeguard “United Nations Protected Areas” (UNPAs) inhabited by Serbs.
* **March:** UN declares “safe areas” in Srebrenica, Žepa, and Goražde in Bosnia, but fails to prevent atrocities.
* **April:** United Nations imposes sanctions on the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (Serbia and Montenegro) for its support of Bosnian Serb forces.

**1994: Stalled Peace and Shifting Alliances**

* **February:** Washington Agreement brokers federation between Bosniaks and Bosnian Croats, creating the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and isolating Bosnian Serbs.
* **July–September:** NATO initiates limited air strikes against Bosnian Serb positions for shelling UN safe areas.

**1995: War’s Climax and the Dayton Accords**

* **May 1995:** Operation Flash and Operation Storm by Croatian forces retake most Serb-held territories in Croatia’s Krajina region, causing Serb civilian exodus.
* **July–August:** Bosnia’s Army (ARBiH) and Croatian forces conduct Operation Summer ’95, regaining key territories in western Bosnia.
* **August 1995:** NATO’s Operation Deliberate Force, large-scale air campaign against Bosnian Serb military infrastructure, follows the Srebrenica massacre (July) and Markale marketplace shelling (August).
* **November 1–21:** Dayton Peace Conference, led by U.S. diplomat Richard Holbrooke in Dayton, Ohio, produces the General Framework Agreement for Peace, dividing Bosnia and Herzegovina into the Federation and Republika Srpska and ending open hostilities.

---

**Aftermath and Legacy**

* **December 14, 1995:** Signing of the Dayton Accords in Paris formally ends the Bosnian War.
* **1996–1999:** Implementation of civilian and military parts of the Dayton settlement under NATO’s IFOR/SFOR missions and the Office of the High Representative.
* **2003:** Serbia and Montenegro rename the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia the State Union of Serbia and Montenegro, preceding Montenegro’s 2006 independence.
* **July 5, 2006:** Montenegro declares independence, formally dissolving the last union of the former Yugoslavia.
* **February 17, 2008:** Kosovo unilaterally declares independence from Serbia; over 100 UN member states eventually recognize its sovereignty, though Serbia and several others do not.

**Political Significance**
The disintegration of Yugoslavia transformed Southeast Europe’s political map, ending the Cold War-era federation and birthing seven independent states. The conflicts tested international law—particularly on ethnic cleansing and state sovereignty—and led to the first-ever ICTY prosecutions for war crimes. European integration of former Yugoslav republics and NATO enlargement eastward are direct legacies of the wars. The region’s post-conflict transition underscores challenges of constitutional engineering, minority rights protection, and reconciliation in ethnically divided societies.
