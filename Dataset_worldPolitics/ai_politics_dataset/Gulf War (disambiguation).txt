“Gulf War” is a term applied to several distinct military conflicts in and around the Persian (Arabian) Gulf region. While most commonly referring to the 1990–1991 U.S.-led intervention against Iraq’s invasion of Kuwait, the phrase also appears in descriptions of earlier and later wars. This disambiguation page outlines the principal uses of the term and provides brief historical and political context for each.

**1. Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988)**
Often called the **First Gulf War** in Middle Eastern historiography, the Iran–Iraq War pitted Iraq, under President Saddam Hussein, against the newly established Islamic Republic of Iran.

* **Origins**: Iraqi leaders feared the export of Iran’s revolutionary Shiʿite ideology to Iraq’s Shiʿa majority and sought territorial gains by invading the oil-rich Khuzestan province in September 1980.
* **Conduct**: The war devolved into a bitter stalemate characterized by trench warfare reminiscent of World War I, extensive use of chemical weapons by Iraq, and attacks on shipping in the Persian Gulf (the “Tanker War”).
* **International Involvement**: Although officially neutral, both superpowers and Gulf Arab states provided intelligence, weaponry, and financial support—ultimately prolonging the conflict.
* **Outcome**: A U.N.-brokered ceasefire took effect in August 1988, restoring pre-war borders but leaving nearly a million casualties and deep regional animosities.

**2. Persian Gulf War (1990–1991)**
Referred to in Western media as the **Gulf War** or **First Gulf War**, this conflict erupted when Iraq invaded neighboring Kuwait on 2 August 1990.

* **Coalition Formation**: Under U.S. President George H. W. Bush, the United States assembled a 35-nation coalition mandated by U.N. Security Council resolutions to expel Iraqi forces and restore Kuwaiti sovereignty.
* **Operation Desert Shield**: U.S. forces and allies rapidly deployed to Saudi Arabia to deter further Iraqi aggression, marking the largest peacetime mobilization of Western military power since World War II.
* **Operation Desert Storm**: Beginning on 17 January 1991, a sustained air campaign targeted Iraqi command‐and‐control, infrastructure, and Republican Guard units. A 100-hour ground offensive in February swiftly liberated Kuwait.
* **Aftermath**: The war curtailed Iraq’s regional ambitions but left Saddam Hussein in power under strict U.N. sanctions. Environmental and humanitarian concerns arose from Iraq’s scorched-earth tactics—setting fire to hundreds of oil wells—and civilian displacement.

**3. Iraq War (2003–2011)**
Sometimes labeled the **Second** or **Third Gulf War**, the 2003 invasion of Iraq by a U.S.-led “Coalition of the Willing” sought to topple Saddam Hussein’s regime.

* **Casus Belli**: The United States and United Kingdom cited Iraq’s alleged possession of weapons of mass destruction (WMDs) and its purported links to terrorism, though subsequent inspections found no active WMD programs.
* **Major Combat Operations**: “Shock and awe” air strikes on 20 March 2003 were followed by a rapid armored thrust from Kuwait to Baghdad, culminating in the fall of the Iraqi capital on 9 April 2003.
* **Occupation and Insurgency**: The disbanding of the Iraqi Army and the de-Baʿthification policy fueled an insurgency and sectarian conflict. U.S. and coalition forces conducted extensive counterinsurgency and nation-building efforts amid mounting casualties.
* **Withdrawal**: A U.S.–Iraq Status of Forces Agreement set a deadline for full U.S. withdrawal, completed by December 2011. The war’s legacy includes prolonged instability, the rise of ISIL, and deep regional divisions.

**4. War Against the Islamic State (2014–Present)**
In the aftermath of the 2003–2011 conflict, segments of the Iraqi insurgency coalesced into the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL). The subsequent military campaign to defeat ISIL is sometimes informally referred to as a **Gulf War** by media focusing on the Gulf Cooperation Council’s (GCC) involvement.

* **Rise of ISIL**: Exploiting sectarian disenfranchisement, ISIL captured Mosul in June 2014 and declared a caliphate spanning parts of Iraq and Syria.
* **International Coalition**: A U.S.-led coalition—supported by Arab states including Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Bahrain—launched air strikes in August 2014. Combined with local ground forces (Iraqi Security Forces, Kurdish Peshmerga, and various militias), the coalition reclaimed most territory by late 2017.
* **Continuing Operations**: While ISIL’s territorial “caliphate” collapsed, counter-terrorism operations continue to address sleeper cells, border security, and regional political stabilization.

**5. Other Uses**

* **Yemeni Civil Conflict (2015–Present)**: Some analysts dub aspects of the Saudi-led intervention in Yemen a “Gulf War” due to its geographic and political involvement of GCC states, though it more accurately represents a proxy conflict between Saudi Arabia and Iran.
* **GCC Security Operations**: Periodic GCC naval exercises and show-of-force operations in the Strait of Hormuz—often framed as demonstrating readiness to defend Gulf maritime security—are occasionally colloquially called “Gulf Wars” in defense reporting.
