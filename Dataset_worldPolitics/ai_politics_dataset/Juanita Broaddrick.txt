**Juanita Broaddrick** (born Juanita Smith; August 29, 1946) is an American nurse and former nursing home administrator who came to public attention in 1999 when she accused President Bill Clinton of having raped her in a Little Rock, Arkansas, hotel room in 1978. Broaddrick’s allegations resurfaced during the Clinton impeachment proceedings and again in the 2016 U.S. presidential campaign, contributing to broader debates over sexual assault, power dynamics, and credibility in American politics.

### Early Life and Career

Juanita Smith was born on August 29, 1946, in Shreveport, Louisiana. She graduated from nursing school and worked as a nurse and nursing home administrator in Arkansas. In the 1970s, she was employed at St. Francis Hospital in Little Rock, where she met Bill Clinton—then Arkansas Attorney General and later Governor of Arkansas—during his speeches to the hospital’s staff.

### 1978 Alleged Assault

Broaddrick has stated that in January 1978 she met Clinton at an event at the Camelot Hotel in Little Rock. According to her account, Clinton offered her a private ride to her car after the event. She alleges that in the hotel room he forcibly raped her. Broaddrick did not report the incident to the police at the time, citing fear of retaliation given Clinton’s political power.

### Public Disclosure and 1999 Testimony

Broaddrick’s allegations first became public during the 1992 presidential campaign in a private letter to Hillary Clinton, which became part of the Whitewater investigation documents. In 1999, during testimony before Independent Counsel Robert B. Fiske, Broaddrick recounted her allegation under oath, stating that Clinton’s assault caused a vaginal injury requiring medical treatment. Clinton denied the allegation, characterizing it as false.

### Media Coverage and Political Reaction

The initial media response in 1999 was muted, with major news outlets reluctant to press the story amid the ongoing Lewinsky scandal and impeachment trial. Broaddrick’s credibility was challenged by Clinton supporters and commentators who questioned her motives and the delay in reporting. However, in subsequent years, her case became a reference point in discussions about the treatment of sexual‐assault survivors, particularly those involving powerful public figures.

### Renewed Attention in Later Political Contexts

* **2008–2016 Democratic Primaries**: Broaddrick publicly endorsed Hillary Clinton’s opponent, John McCain in 2008, and Donald Trump in 2016, framing her support as a response to perceived dismissiveness toward sexual‐assault allegations by the Clintons.
* **#MeToo Movement**: In the wake of the #MeToo movement beginning in 2017, Broaddrick’s allegations were revisited as part of a broader examination of systemic issues in reporting sexual misconduct. Media outlets and commentators debated whether her account should have been given greater credence in the 1990s.

### Legal and Social Impact

No criminal charges ensued. Broaddrick pursued no civil litigation, citing the statute of limitations and the difficulties of lawsuits against a sitting or former president. Her case has been cited in scholarly and policy discussions on:

* **Statute of Limitations Reform**: Advocacy to extend or eliminate limitations for sexual‐assault cases.
* **Victim Credibility**: Analysis of how social stigma and power disparities influence survivors’ willingness to report assault.
* **Political Accountability**: Debates over how allegations involving elected officials should be investigated and adjudicated.

### Personal Life

Juanita Broaddrick has been married three times and has two sons. She has lived in Van Buren, Arkansas. In interviews and public appearances, she has described ongoing emotional and physical health struggles stemming from the alleged assault.

### Legacy and Continuing Relevance

Juanita Broaddrick’s allegations against Bill Clinton remain one of the most prominent cases of an American public figure accused of sexual assault by a private citizen. Her story illustrates the complex interplay between sexual‐assault reporting, media priorities, and political power. As discussions about consent and accountability evolve, Broaddrick’s experience continues to inform debates on how society and the justice system address claims against powerful individuals.
