**List of Vietnam War Games**

The **list of Vietnam War games** includes board games, tabletop simulations, video games, and role-playing scenarios that depict or are inspired by the Vietnam War (1955–1975). These games range from strategic simulations and tactical shooters to political management games and alternate-history scenarios. The Vietnam War, with its complex asymmetrical warfare, controversial political dimensions, and cultural resonance, has posed unique challenges and opportunities for game developers across various media.

Game portrayals of the Vietnam War have reflected shifting historical interpretations and cultural attitudes, especially in the United States. While earlier games often focused on military tactics and battlefield realism, later works have explored themes such as guerrilla warfare, civilian impact, political dissent, and moral ambiguity. Vietnam War games serve not only as entertainment but also as vehicles for public memory, political critique, and educational exploration.

---

### Historical and Political Context

The Vietnam War was a Cold War-era conflict involving communist North Vietnam and its allies—including the Viet Cong and the Soviet Union—against South Vietnam, supported by the United States and other anti-communist nations. Escalating U.S. military involvement peaked in the late 1960s, followed by a gradual withdrawal and the fall of Saigon in 1975.

Unlike the more conventionally depicted World Wars, the Vietnam War remains deeply controversial, especially in American and Vietnamese popular memory. Its political complexities, unclear objectives, and media coverage influenced a generation’s perception of war. This ambivalence has extended into how the conflict is represented in games, with some products emphasizing realism and strategy, while others portray the war as a chaotic and morally ambiguous setting.

In gaming, the Vietnam War emerged as a theme in the 1970s and 1980s, particularly in tabletop wargames. Digital representations gained popularity in the 1990s and 2000s, often paralleling film and television portrayals.

---

### Board and Tabletop Games

#### **Vietnam (1971)** – *Simulations Publications, Inc. (SPI)*

One of the earliest and most detailed strategic-level simulations of the conflict. Players manage both political and military aspects, including U.S. domestic support and pacification efforts.

#### **Firepower (1980)** – *Avalon Hill*

A squad-level tactical combat game that includes Vietnam War scenarios alongside other modern conflicts. Known for its detailed mechanics and attention to firepower and movement.

#### **Hearts and Minds (2010)** – *Worthington Games*

A strategic-level board game that emphasizes political will, insurgency, and the shifting balance of power. It reflects the dual nature of the Vietnam War as both military and ideological.

#### **Fields of Fire (2008)** – *GMT Games*

A solitaire tactical game in which players command U.S. infantry through various historical campaigns, including Vietnam. It focuses on command challenges, communication delays, and real-time decision-making.

---

### Video Games

#### **Battlefield: Vietnam (2004)** – *Electronic Arts*

Part of the popular *Battlefield* series, this multiplayer first-person shooter emphasizes vehicular combat and squad tactics. Known for its immersive environments and soundtrack evoking the 1960s and 1970s.

#### **Vietcong (2003)** – *Illusion Softworks*

A tactical shooter offering both single-player and multiplayer modes, *Vietcong* portrays U.S. Special Forces operations against the Viet Cong. The game received praise for its realism and atmosphere.

#### **Rising Storm 2: Vietnam (2017)** – *Tripwire Interactive*

A multiplayer tactical shooter known for its authenticity and asymmetrical gameplay. It allows players to take the roles of both U.S. and Vietnamese forces, emphasizing realism over arcade-style action.

#### **Line of Sight: Vietnam (2003)** – *nFusion Interactive*

A squad-based tactical shooter focusing on small unit operations and jungle warfare. It presents Vietnam from the perspective of U.S. Army special forces.

#### **Conflict: Vietnam (2004)** – *Pivotal Games*

Part of the *Conflict* series, this third-person tactical shooter centers on a U.S. squad's struggle to survive behind enemy lines during the Tet Offensive.

---

### Strategy and Political Simulations

#### **Vietnam ’65 (2015)** – *Slitherine Ltd.*

A counter-insurgency strategy game where players manage military and civilian operations in South Vietnam. It incorporates political and logistics dynamics, simulating the challenges of winning "hearts and minds."

#### **Radio Commander (2019)** – *Serious Sim*

An innovative real-time strategy game where players issue commands via radio rather than controlling units directly. The game emphasizes limited information, confusion, and the “fog of war,” mirroring real U.S. command challenges.

#### **Wargame: Red Dragon (2014)** – *Eugen Systems*

While not exclusively about Vietnam, this Cold War-era strategy game includes scenarios and factions based on Vietnam War-era conflicts, including the People’s Army of Vietnam and U.S. Marines.

### Educational and Role-Playing Uses

Vietnam War-themed games have been used in academic and military settings to examine topics such as irregular warfare, decision-making under stress, and civil-military relations. Some role-playing games and scenarios allow for a deeper exploration of soldier psychology, anti-war sentiment, or interactions with local populations.

---

### Criticism and Reception

Vietnam War games have often been the subject of criticism regarding historical accuracy, ethical portrayal of violence, and simplification of complex events. Some critics argue that early games romanticized U.S. military action or trivialized the suffering of Vietnamese civilians. Others have praised newer titles for addressing moral ambiguity and psychological trauma.

The cultural legacy of the war, particularly in the United States, means that Vietnam War games are often subject to intense scrutiny, especially when released during periods of active U.S. military engagement elsewhere.

---

### Conclusion

The **list of Vietnam War games** reflects the evolving ways in which the conflict has been interpreted, contested, and remembered through interactive media. Whether simulating battlefield tactics, political strategy, or personal narratives, these games contribute to ongoing discourse about war, memory, and national identity. As technology and storytelling methods advance, Vietnam War games continue to serve as a medium for both entertainment and reflection on one of the most complex conflicts of the 20th century.
