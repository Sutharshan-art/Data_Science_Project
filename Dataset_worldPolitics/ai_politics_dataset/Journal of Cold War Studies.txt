**Journal of Cold War Studies**

The **Journal of Cold War Studies** is a peer‐reviewed academic journal dedicated to the multidisciplinary examination of the Cold War era, broadly defined as the geopolitical, ideological, economic, and cultural confrontation between the United States and its allies and the Soviet Union and its allies, approximately between 1945 and 1991. Launched in 1999, the journal provides a forum for historians, political scientists, sociologists, and other scholars to analyze the origins, conduct, and aftermath of Cold War conflicts and policies, as well as their enduring legacies in international relations.

### Founding and Institutional Affiliation

The journal was established by the Cold War Studies Project at the Harvard University Belfer Center for Science and International Affairs in collaboration with the Massachusetts Institute of Technology Press. Its inception reflected growing scholarly interest in the Cold War following the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991 and the subsequent declassification of archival materials in former Warsaw Pact countries. These sources enabled fresh archival research, comparative studies, and methodological innovations in the rapidly evolving field of Cold War history.

### Editorial Mission and Scope

Aiming to foster rigorous, empirically grounded scholarship, the Journal of Cold War Studies publishes three issues annually. Its scope encompasses:

* **Political and Diplomatic History**: Research on summit diplomacy, alliance formation, nuclear arms control negotiations, and interstate crises such as the Berlin Blockade, Korean War, Cuban Missile Crisis, and Vietnam War.
* **Military Strategy and Intelligence**: Analyses of deterrence theory, covert operations, espionage practices, and the military‐industrial complex in both superpower and peripheral theaters.
* **Economic and Technological Competition**: Studies of economic aid programs (e.g., the Marshall Plan), trade blocs, resource diplomacy, and the role of science and technology in the space race and arms development.
* **Social and Cultural Dimensions**: Investigations into propaganda, cultural diplomacy, ideological mobilization, popular perceptions, and the transnational movements (e.g., decolonization, human rights campaigns) shaped by Cold War rivalries.
* **Post–Cold War Legacies**: Reflections on the Cold War’s impact on contemporary security dilemmas, regional conflicts, international institutions, and collective memory.

### Editorial Leadership and Peer Review

The journal’s editorial board comprises senior scholars from leading universities in North America, Europe, and Asia, reflecting the international nature of Cold War studies. Manuscripts undergo a double‐blind peer‐review process, ensuring that authors address both archival evidence and theoretical frameworks. Special issues often accompany anniversaries of key events or emerging subfields, such as Cold War environmental diplomacy and digital history approaches to state security archives.

### Historical and Political Context

The Journal of Cold War Studies emerged during a period of intense reappraisal of the Cold War in the 1990s. The opening of Soviet, Eastern Bloc, and Chinese archives allowed scholars to challenge earlier “orthodox” Western narratives that had emphasized Soviet expansionism and Western containment. “Revisionist” and “post‐revisionist” historiographical schools debated the relative weight of economic imperatives, internal politics, and mutual misperceptions in driving superpower conflict. The journal has been instrumental in publishing research that integrates multiple national perspectives—from U.S. and Soviet primary‐source analyses to studies of Third World agency in Cold War alignments.

### Impact and Notable Contributions

Since its inaugural issue, the Journal of Cold War Studies has published groundbreaking articles that have reshaped understanding of the era. Notable contributions include:

* Analyses of Soviet decision‐making during the Cuban Missile Crisis based on newly available Politburo minutes.
* Comparative studies of decolonization in Africa and Asia, highlighting how superpower competition influenced local nationalist movements.
* Research on the role of nonaligned states at the Bandung Conference (1955) and their strategic navigation between blocs.
* Interdisciplinary work on Cold War cultural exchanges, such as the U.S. Symphony Orchestra tours in the Soviet Union and Soviet ballet tours in the United States, as instruments of soft power.
* Digital‐history projects mapping nuclear command and control networks through declassified communications records.

The journal’s articles are frequently cited in monographs, policy analyses, and graduate‐level coursework on international history, underscoring its authority in the field.

### Accessibility and Publication Details

Published by MIT Press on behalf of Harvard University’s Belfer Center, the journal is available in print and online via academic databases. Subscriptions are held by university libraries and research institutions worldwide. Each issue typically includes original research articles, book review essays, and occasional symposium discussions featuring responses by leading scholars.

### Continuing Relevance

As contemporary challenges—such as great‐power competition between the United States, Russia, and China—resonate with Cold War dynamics, the Journal of Cold War Studies remains a vital resource for understanding the historical roots of current international tensions. Its multidisciplinary approach provides policymakers, educators, and the public with nuanced insights into how ideological confrontations, strategic rivalries, and diplomatic negotiations of the mid‐20th century continue to influence global affairs in the 21st century.
