**Army of Republika Srpska**

The **Army of Republika Srpska** (Serbian: *Vojska Republike Srpske*, VRS) was the military force of the Republika Srpska, a self-proclaimed Serb entity within Bosnia and Herzegovina during the Bosnian War (1992–1995). Formed in 1992, the VRS played a central role in the conflict and was involved in numerous military operations, as well as controversial acts that later became the subject of international war crimes investigations. The army’s establishment, conduct, and dissolution are deeply intertwined with the complex political and ethnic dynamics of the former Yugoslavia.

---

### Historical and Political Context

The Army of Republika Srpska emerged amid the violent breakup of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, which led to the independence declarations of Slovenia, Croatia, and Bosnia and Herzegovina. The Bosnian War began in April 1992 following Bosnia and Herzegovina’s declaration of independence, a move rejected by Bosnian Serbs who sought to remain united with Serbia.

The Republika Srpska was proclaimed in January 1992 as the political entity representing the Bosnian Serb population, backed by the leadership of the Serbian Democratic Party (SDS) and supported by the government of Serbia under Slobodan Milošević. The VRS was established to defend and assert Republika Srpska’s territorial claims during the ensuing conflict.

---

### Formation and Structure

The VRS was officially formed in May 1992, inheriting many personnel and equipment from the Yugoslav People’s Army (JNA) stationed in Bosnia. It consisted of infantry, armored units, artillery, and special forces, organized under a centralized command structure with General Ratko Mladić appointed as its chief commander.

The army was responsible for military operations across Republika Srpska-controlled territory, which covered roughly 49% of Bosnia and Herzegovina at the war’s end. Its command was closely linked to political leaders, notably Radovan Karadžić, the President of Republika Srpska.

---

### Role in the Bosnian War

The VRS engaged in major military campaigns throughout the Bosnian War, including sieges, offensives, and territorial defense. The most notable and devastating military operation conducted by the VRS was the **Siege of Sarajevo (1992–1996)**, during which the city endured prolonged bombardment and blockade.

The army was also responsible for the **Srebrenica massacre** in July 1995, recognized as the worst atrocity in Europe since World War II, where over 8,000 Bosniak men and boys were systematically executed. This event, along with other acts of ethnic cleansing and atrocities, has been the subject of extensive investigations and prosecutions by the **International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY)**.

---

### Political Significance

The Army of Republika Srpska was not only a military force but also a political instrument used to consolidate Bosnian Serb territorial claims and political control during the war. The VRS’s actions significantly shaped the course of the conflict and the post-war territorial settlement.

Its existence underscored the ethnic fragmentation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and the challenges of sovereignty and governance in a deeply divided society. The military was integral to the Republika Srpska’s assertion of autonomy and subsequent negotiations at the **Dayton Peace Agreement** in 1995.

---

### Post-War Demobilization and Integration

Following the Dayton Agreement, which ended the Bosnian War, the VRS underwent significant changes. Under international supervision, the army was demobilized and reorganized as part of the new **Armed Forces of Bosnia and Herzegovina**, a unified military force integrating former warring factions.

The transformation aimed to promote reconciliation and build a cohesive defense structure for the multiethnic state. The former VRS personnel were gradually integrated or retired, with the entity of Republika Srpska retaining no separate military force.

---

### International Legal Proceedings

Senior VRS commanders, including Ratko Mladić and political leaders such as Radovan Karadžić, were indicted and tried by the ICTY for war crimes, crimes against humanity, and genocide. These trials highlighted the army’s involvement in grave violations of international law and significantly influenced post-conflict justice and reconciliation processes in the region.

The legacy of the VRS remains contentious, with differing perspectives among ethnic groups and political actors in Bosnia and Herzegovina regarding its role in the war and its impact on national identity.

---

### Legacy and Contemporary Relevance

The Army of Republika Srpska’s history is integral to understanding the Bosnian War’s military and political dynamics. It exemplifies the complex interplay between ethnicity, nationalism, and military power in the breakup of Yugoslavia.

The legacy of the VRS continues to influence Bosnian politics, particularly in Republika Srpska, where debates over wartime history and identity remain central to political discourse. The army’s past underscores the challenges of post-conflict reconstruction and the ongoing efforts to build peace and unity in Bosnia and Herzegovina.

---

### Conclusion

The Army of Republika Srpska was a pivotal military force during the Bosnian War, shaping the conflict’s trajectory and post-war political arrangements. While it served as the defender of Republika Srpska’s territorial ambitions, its involvement in severe human rights violations has left a lasting imprint on regional and international perceptions. The dissolution of the VRS and integration into Bosnia and Herzegovina’s unified armed forces represent a crucial step toward peace and reconciliation in the post-war era.
