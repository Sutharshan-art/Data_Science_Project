**Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia**

The **Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia** (Serbo-Croatian: *Autonomna Pokrajina Zapadna Bosna*) was a self-proclaimed and short-lived political entity during the Bosnian War (1992–1995). Established in 1993 in northwestern Bosnia and Herzegovina, it was led by Fikret Abdić, a former Bosnian politician and businessman. The province existed as a semi-autonomous region under Abdić’s control and was involved in complex alliances and conflicts with other factions during the war. Its history exemplifies the fragmented and multi-layered nature of the Bosnian conflict.

---

### Historical and Political Background

The Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia arose amid the dissolution of Yugoslavia and the violent conflict in Bosnia and Herzegovina. After Bosnia declared independence in 1992, the war erupted between Bosniaks, Croats, and Serbs, each pursuing political and territorial goals.

Fikret Abdić, once a prominent member of the **Party of Democratic Action (SDA)** and a successful businessman in the Agrokomerc food company, broke away from the Bosnian government led by Alija Izetbegović. In opposition to the central government, Abdić established his own power base in the Velika Kladuša region.

---

### Formation and Governance

In 1993, Abdić declared the formation of the Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia, claiming authority over a territory of approximately 500 square kilometers in northwestern Bosnia, centered around Velika Kladuša.

The province operated with its own administration, security forces, and economic structures. Abdić’s government cooperated with Serb and Croat forces at times, contrasting with the Bosnian government forces that opposed it. The Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia was never internationally recognized and was considered a breakaway entity.

---

### Military and Political Role in the Bosnian War

The Autonomous Province’s forces, often referred to as the **Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia Army** or the **Green Berets**, fought both Bosnian government forces and Serb paramilitaries at different points in the conflict.

Abdić’s alliance with Serb forces against the Bosnian government led to intense fighting in the region, with the Autonomous Province serving as a buffer zone for Republika Srpska and the Croatian Defence Council (HVO).

The territory was subject to several offensives and sieges. Bosnian government forces attempted multiple times to regain control over the area but faced strong local resistance supported by external allies.

---

### Collapse and Reintegration

By mid-1995, as the Bosnian government forces gained strength and international pressure increased, the Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia began to lose territory. The decisive **Operation Tiger** and other military campaigns resulted in the fall of Velika Kladuša and the reintegration of the territory into the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

Following the Dayton Peace Agreement in late 1995, the Autonomous Province was formally dissolved, and its territory was placed under the jurisdiction of the postwar Bosnian state structures.

---

### Political and Social Legacy

The Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia remains a controversial episode in Bosnian history. Fikret Abdić’s leadership and collaboration with Serb forces are viewed by many as acts of betrayal against the Bosnian government and the Bosniak cause.

Abdić was later tried and convicted by Bosnian courts for war crimes and collaboration. However, his local support base in Velika Kladuša has persisted, reflecting enduring political divisions.

The province’s existence exemplifies the fragmentation and complexity of the Bosnian War, highlighting how local and personal rivalries intersected with ethnic and national conflicts.

---

### Conclusion

The Autonomous Province of Western Bosnia was a self-declared political and military entity that played a significant, though contentious, role in the Bosnian War. Its formation and actions underscore the internal divisions within Bosniak society and the multifaceted nature of the conflict. The province’s eventual defeat and reintegration marked a step toward the consolidation of Bosnia and Herzegovina’s postwar political order.
