**Frank Charles Carlucci III** (February 18, 1930 – June 3, 2018) was an American diplomat, national security official, and defense secretary who played key roles in U.S. foreign and defense policy during the Nixon, Ford, Carter, and Reagan administrations. A career foreign service officer turned senior policymaker, Carlucci’s tenure spanned the late Cold War era, marked by détente, the Iran hostage crisis, and the resurgence of American defense posture.

### Early Life and Career

Born in Scranton, Pennsylvania, Carlucci graduated from Princeton University in 1951 and served as a U.S. Navy officer during the Korean War. After receiving a master’s degree in public affairs from Princeton’s Woodrow Wilson School in 1956, he entered the U.S. Foreign Service. His early postings included the U.S. Embassy in Brussels (1957–1962) and the State Department’s Office of French and Italian Affairs. Proficient in multiple languages, Carlucci developed expertise in European diplomacy and NATO affairs.

### National Security Council and Ambassador to Portugal

In 1971, National Security Adviser Henry Kissinger selected Carlucci to serve on the National Security Council (NSC) staff, where he advanced from director for NATO policy to senior director for European and NATO policy. In 1974, President Gerald Ford appointed him U.S. Ambassador to Portugal amid the aftermath of the Carnation Revolution and Portugal’s decolonization of Africa. Carlucci managed U.S. relations during Portugal’s transition to democracy and negotiated continued U.S. base access in the Azores, vital for Atlantic surveillance.

### Deputy CIA Director and Director of the Office of Management and Budget

Returning to Washington in 1977 under President Jimmy Carter, Carlucci became Deputy Director of the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA). He oversaw counterintelligence reforms following revelations of domestic abuses and improved analytic coordination. In 1979, Carter appointed him Director of the Office of Management and Budget (OMB), where Carlucci implemented spending restraints amid rising federal deficits and the economic pressures of stagflation and the Iran hostage crisis.

### Deputy Secretary of Defense and National Security Adviser

Under President Ronald Reagan, Carlucci was named Deputy Secretary of Defense in 1981. In this capacity, he supervised the rapid expansion of defense spending—the Reagan defense buildup—including strategic forces modernization and the development of the Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI). In early 1987, amid the Iran–Contra scandal and tensions with Congress, Carlucci succeeded Robert “Bud” McFarlane as National Security Adviser. He restored stability to the NSC process, reasserted cabinet-level policy coordination, and oversaw arms-control negotiations, including the Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty implementation.

### Secretary of Defense

In November 1987, Reagan nominated Carlucci as Secretary of Defense following the resignation of Caspar Weinberger. Carlucci was confirmed unanimously by the Senate and sworn in on November 23. His tenure focused on:

* **Arms Control and Soviet Relations**: Carlucci managed INF Treaty verification measures and facilitated the commencement of Strategic Arms Reduction Talks (START), balancing deterrence with dialogue.
* **Regional Conflicts**: He oversaw U.S. military support for coalition operations in the Persian Gulf—Operation Earnest Will—escorting reflagged Kuwaiti tankers during the Iran–Iraq War.
* **Defense Reform**: Carlucci promoted acquisition reform to curb cost overruns on major programs (e.g., the F-117 stealth fighter) and initiated the Bottom-Up Review process to align force structure with strategic objectives.
* **Post–Reagan Transition**: He prepared the Department of Defense for the transition to the George H. W. Bush administration, ensuring continuity in contingency planning for potential operations in Panama and the Middle East.

Carlucci left office on January 20, 1989, concluding a two-year tenure noted for bipartisan support and managerial effectiveness.

### Post–Government Career and Legacy

After leaving public office, Carlucci served as chairman of The Carlyle Group, a global private-equity firm, where he helped expand its defense and government services investments. He chaired the board of trustees of the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) and was a director of several corporations, including Westinghouse Electric Corporation.

Carlucci’s career is characterized by:

* **Bipartisan Leadership**: He served under presidents of both parties, earning reputations for competence and integrity.
* **Cold War Management**: His stewardship spanned key arms-control milestones and defense modernization initiatives.
* **Crisis Stabilization**: As National Security Adviser, he restored credibility to U.S. foreign policy during the Iran–Contra aftermath.

Frank Carlucci died on June 3, 2018, in McLean, Virginia, at the age of 88. He is remembered for his calm demeanor, institutional knowledge, and dedication to public service during an era of significant geopolitical transition.
