**1991 Soviet Coup Attempt**

The **1991 Soviet coup attempt**, also known as the **August Coup**, was an unsuccessful effort by members of the Soviet Union’s government and security services to take control of the country from **President Mikhail Gorbachev**. The coup began on **August 19, 1991**, and lasted for three days before collapsing on **August 21**. It was orchestrated by hardline Communist Party officials, military leaders, and KGB figures who opposed Gorbachev’s political and economic reforms, as well as the impending **New Union Treaty**, which they believed would lead to the dissolution of the USSR.

The coup failed in part due to widespread popular resistance in **Moscow**, particularly led by **Russian Federation President Boris Yeltsin**, and a lack of military and political cohesion among the conspirators. Although Gorbachev was restored to power, the event significantly **weakened the central Soviet government**, accelerated the **collapse of the Communist Party**, and hastened the **dissolution of the Soviet Union** in December 1991.

---

### Background

By mid-1991, the Soviet Union was experiencing profound political and economic turmoil. **Gorbachev’s reforms**—notably *perestroika* (economic restructuring) and *glasnost* (political openness)—had loosened state control but failed to prevent economic decline, shortages, and public unrest. Meanwhile, nationalist movements were gaining strength across the USSR’s republics, with several, including the **Baltic states**, already moving toward full independence.

To preserve the union, Gorbachev had negotiated the **New Union Treaty**, which would have transformed the USSR into a more decentralized federation known as the **Union of Sovereign States**. The treaty was scheduled to be signed on **August 20, 1991**, by several republics. However, hardliners within the **Communist Party**, the **military**, and the **KGB** saw this as the final step in the disintegration of Soviet power and sought to prevent it.

---

### The Coup

On the morning of **August 19, 1991**, a group calling itself the **State Committee on the State of Emergency (GKChP)** announced that Gorbachev had become ill and was being relieved of his duties. In fact, Gorbachev was under house arrest at his vacation home in **Foros, Crimea**, where his communications were cut.

The GKChP, led by **Vice President Gennady Yanayev**, **KGB Chairman Vladimir Kryuchkov**, **Defense Minister Dmitry Yazov**, and other senior officials, declared a state of emergency and sent tanks into **Moscow** to assert control. They suspended political activity, imposed censorship, and attempted to isolate key political figures, including Boris Yeltsin.

However, resistance emerged quickly. Yeltsin, speaking from the **Russian White House (the Russian Parliament building)**, condemned the coup as unconstitutional and called for mass protests and a general strike. Images of Yeltsin standing atop a tank and rallying the public became symbolic of the defiance to the coup.

Thousands of civilians surrounded the White House, building barricades and defending it from possible assault. Several elite military units, including the **Taman Division**, refused to follow orders or defected to Yeltsin’s side. The coup plotters failed to arrest key opposition figures, shut down independent media, or maintain discipline among the armed forces.

On **August 21**, the coup collapsed. Gorbachev was released and returned to Moscow, and the plotters were arrested or fled. The GKChP leaders were charged with treason and other crimes, although most were later granted amnesty or released.

---

### Political Consequences

The failed coup had immediate and far-reaching consequences:

* **Collapse of the Communist Party**: The coup discredited the **Communist Party of the Soviet Union (CPSU)**. Yeltsin banned its activities in **Russia**, and support for the party collapsed nationwide. Gorbachev resigned as General Secretary days later.

* **Rise of Boris Yeltsin**: Yeltsin emerged as the key political figure in post-coup Soviet politics. His defiance solidified his legitimacy as the leader of the **Russian Federation**, and he rapidly moved to assert the authority of the Russian republic over that of the union government.

* **Acceleration of Republican Independence**: The coup attempt prompted many republics to declare full independence. Within weeks, **Ukraine**, **Belarus**, and others moved toward secession. By **December 1991**, the **Belavezha Accords** formally dissolved the Soviet Union.

* **End of the Soviet Union**: The coup marked the de facto end of the USSR’s centralized authority. While Gorbachev resumed his role as president, his power had been fatally undermined. On **December 25, 1991**, Gorbachev resigned, and the Soviet Union was officially dissolved the next day.

---

### Legacy

The 1991 Soviet coup attempt is widely seen as a **turning point in global history**. It marked the final failure of Communist hardliners to reverse the course of democratization and decentralization, and it ensured the collapse of one of the 20th century’s most powerful states.

Domestically, the coup's failure set the stage for **Russia’s transition to a market economy** and a new political order under Yeltsin, though the years that followed were marked by economic crisis and political instability.

Internationally, the end of the USSR shifted the **global balance of power**, effectively ending the **Cold War** and establishing the **United States** as the sole global superpower. The coup remains a subject of study for scholars of democratization, civil resistance, and the fragility of authoritarian systems during periods of reform.
