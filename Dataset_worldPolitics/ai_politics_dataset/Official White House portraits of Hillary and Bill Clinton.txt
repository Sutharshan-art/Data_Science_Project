**Official White House Portraits of Hillary and Bill Clinton**

The **official White House portraits of President Bill Clinton and First Lady Hillary Rodham Clinton** are the formal oil paintings commissioned to commemorate their service in the Executive Mansion from 1993 to 2001. Unveiled in separate ceremonies in 2004 and 2007, respectively, the portraits occupy places of honor in the White House complex and reflect both artistic trends and the political legacies of the Clintons.

## Commissioning and Selection Process

Under the White House Historical Association’s auspices, official portraits of U.S. presidents and first ladies are commissioned following each administration. In 2002, the association invited several artists to submit designs for President Clinton’s portrait. After review by a selection committee—including White House curators, historians, and former presidential portrait subjects—artist John Howard Sanden was chosen. Sanden, noted for his ability to blend realism with a dignified sense of personality, based his composition on photographic sittings and meetings with Clinton over a three-month period in 2003.

For First Lady Hillary Clinton’s portrait, commissioned later and unveiled in 2007, the White House Historical Association selected Simmie Knox, the first African American artist to paint an official presidential portrait (of President George H. W. Bush in 1990). Knox had previously captured Mrs. Clinton’s likeness in a private commission for Chelsea Clinton’s graduation gift; his style—warm, detailed, and reflecting the First Lady’s poise—led to his selection in 2005 after consultations with the Clinton family and White House curators.

## Bill Clinton Portrait (Unveiled 2004)

### Artist and Style

John Howard Sanden’s portrait of Bill Clinton presents the 42nd president seated in a Windsor chair upholstered in blue silk, situated in the Red Room of the White House. Dressed in a dark blue suit, white shirt, and striped tie, Clinton gazes directly at the viewer with a slight smile. Sanden employed a muted palette to harmonize with the room’s crimson draperies and gilded furnishings, emphasizing both formality and approachability.

### Unveiling Ceremony

The portrait was unveiled on April 12, 2004, in the East Room, with President Clinton and his successor, President George W. Bush, in attendance. Chief Justice William Rehnquist administered the proceedings. In her remarks, Laura Bush lauded the portrait’s dignified portrayal and the precedent of bipartisan support for presidential legacies. Clinton responded by reflecting on his administration’s achievements and acknowledging the symbolic weight of joining the White House’s formal collection.

### Reception and Placement

Critics praised Sanden’s skillful rendering of likeness and environment, noting the portrait’s balance between formality and personal warmth. The painting was installed in the State Dining Room’s hallway, alongside portraits of other modern presidents, where it remains accessible to official tours and state functions.

## Hillary Rodham Clinton Portrait (Unveiled 2007)

### Artist and Style

Simmie Knox’s portrait of Hillary Rodham Clinton depicts the former First Lady standing in a white evening gown against a neutral background accented with deep green curtains. Her right hand rests on the back of a chair draped with an American flag, symbolizing her public service. Knox focused on integrating a sense of confidence and elegance, using smooth brushwork and rich tonal contrasts to convey both approachability and strength.

### Unveiling Ceremony

On February 9, 2007, the portrait was unveiled in the East Room. Attendees included Secretary of State Condoleezza Rice—who praised Mrs. Clinton’s diplomatic contributions—Vice President Dick Cheney, former presidents Jimmy Carter and George H. W. Bush, and members of the Clinton family. Secretary of State Rice’s remarks emphasized Mrs. Clinton’s global advocacy and the importance of commemorating first ladies’ evolving public roles.

### Reception and Placement

The portrait garnered acclaim for capturing Mrs. Clinton’s multifaceted public persona—lawyer, advocate for health-care reform, and diplomat—while adhering to the ceremonial conventions of official portraiture. It was installed in the State Dining Room’s northwest alcove, adjacent to portraits of other first ladies, reinforcing the evolving recognition of first ladies’ political influence.

## Historical and Political Significance

The official portraits of Bill and Hillary Clinton reflect broader trends in White House commemorative culture:

* **Bipartisanship and Continuity:** The joint presence of President George W. Bush and Secretary Rice at the unveiling ceremonies underscored the tradition of cross‐party respect for presidential legacies.
* **Evolving Role of First Ladies:** Knox’s portrait highlights the transition of the first lady’s role from a domestic hostess to a public policy advocate and diplomat, mirroring Hillary Clinton’s subsequent tenure as U.S. Senator and Secretary of State.
* **Artistic Diversity:** The selection of Sanden and Knox—an established White House portraitist and the first African American presidential portrait artist—signals growing inclusivity and recognition of diverse artistic contributions.

## Legacy and Public Access

Both portraits form part of the White House’s permanent collection, managed by the White House Historical Association and the White House Curator’s Office. They are visible to millions of annual visitors and serve as educational tools in tours, exhibitions, and online archives. The images appear in official publications, including the *White House Historical Association Cookbook* and commemorative brochures, cementing their role in shaping public memory of the Clinton years.
