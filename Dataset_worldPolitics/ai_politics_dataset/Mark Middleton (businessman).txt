**Mark Middleton (businessman)**

**Mark Middleton** (1961–2022) was an American businessman and political aide who served in the administration of President **Bill Clinton** during the 1990s. He is best known for his role as a special assistant to the president and for his work within the **White House Office of Presidential Liaison**, where he helped facilitate relationships between the Clinton administration and the business community. Middleton's tenure intersected with several high-profile political and fundraising controversies, notably involving campaign finance practices during the 1996 U.S. presidential election cycle.

Following his government service, Middleton returned to the private sector in Arkansas, where he was involved in various business ventures. His name later reemerged in media coverage in connection with notable public figures and investigations into donor influence and lobbying activities.

Mark Middleton was born in 1961 in Arkansas. He graduated from the **University of Arkansas**, where he earned a degree in business. Before entering politics, Middleton built a career in investment banking and energy development, eventually becoming a prominent figure in Arkansas’s business community.

### Service in the Clinton Administration

Middleton joined the **White House Office of Presidential Liaison** early in President Bill Clinton's first term. In this role, he was tasked with coordinating relationships between the White House and business leaders, especially those with ties to trade, international investment, and campaign finance.

As **Special Assistant to the President** under Clinton, Middleton had access to senior officials and was involved in managing visits and interactions between high-level donors and administration figures. His work often involved navigating the complex boundaries between fundraising, policymaking, and executive access.

Middleton’s activities came under scrutiny during the **1996 campaign finance controversy**, in which congressional and media investigations examined the role of foreign nationals and large donors in U.S. political fundraising. Though never charged with wrongdoing, Middleton was cited in several reports as a contact point for individuals linked to questionable donation practices, including figures later associated with foreign influence allegations.

After his departure, Middleton's White House access was reportedly restricted, reflecting broader efforts within the administration to manage the fallout from donor-related controversies.
